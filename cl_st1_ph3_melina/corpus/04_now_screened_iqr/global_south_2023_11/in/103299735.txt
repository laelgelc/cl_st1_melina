<h> ' My Son Was Killed And I Could Not Hear A Thing ' <h> A Palestinian writer shares his grief from a hospital bed <p> I write these words from my hospital bed . I am beginning to have some energy to hold my phone and use my fingers after 10 days here . <p> I write in grief . <p> I woke up in my family house in Rafah , in the south of Gaza on Tuesday morning , 24 October . <p> The house is -- was -- made up of three levels . <p> My father , his wife , my married siblings and their children all live there . My children come to visit their grandfather 's house to play with their cousins . <p> I was sitting with my children , and they began what has become a regular conversation in a time of genocide . <p> How do we find bread and water for the day ? And where can we find a solar power panel to charge our phones ? We sometimes managed to charge our phones in the school next door . <p> We were sitting in the living room @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , 13 . He had just come back from the corner shop and had bought some biscuits . <p> Abdullah -- I called him Abboud -- loved to share what he bought with his allowance . <p> I told him : " Whatever you want habibi , next time I will buy it . " <p> Abboud had bought biscuits instead of bread . There was no bread . <p> There is no electricity or fuel . Israel has cut off all food , fuel and electricity supplies to Gaza . We had to find substitutes , and we told our children to buy biscuits to sate their hunger for bread . <p> Abboud , my other son Hammoud and my daughter Batool were sitting with me . Abdulrahman , my fourth child , was at that moment out of the house . <p> Abboud said he was going to get my phone , which was charging in the school at that point . He and his cousin had agreed among themselves to take turns to go . It was his turn . <p> It was the last conversation I @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ sound . I do not remember what happened . <p> I must have been unconscious , but I do n't remember for how long . Maybe it was just five minutes . <p> When I opened my eyes , I felt a dizziness that I can not properly describe . There was dust and rubble everywhere . <p> I knew the house had been bombed but I could n't hear anything . My hearing was gone . <p> I looked next to me , and saw Hammoud and Batool sticking to me , screaming , and pointing to Abboud . I looked , and Abboud was lying in front of me . <p> I looked the other way , and I saw the body of a woman and then the body of another woman . <p> Both my aunts , one of their daughters and my stepmom were nearby . <p> My elderly aunt was staying with us . Her son and three grandkids had been martyred just the day before , and my father had brought her to stay with us so we could care for her . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ so she could console her sister . The rest of the family was all on the eastern side of the house . <p> It was clear that the missile had struck the part of the house that me and my children were sitting in . <p> I looked around and saw my father trying to hold on to the children . <p> I saw that the house was destroyed . I saw we were in the rubble . <p> I saw that people had gathered . I began yelling to them despite not being able to hear my own voice : " Come fast , there are children and women lying on the floor ... " <p> They took me to a hospital . My neighbor took me there on foot . <p> He felt my injury was relatively minor in comparison to others and anyway , emergency responders were working under extreme pressure . As I was walking , I kept telling people to save the children . <p> The image of Abboud would not leave my mind . <p> I received first aid at the clinic . So had my aunt 's daughter and my stepmom . <p> Hammoud and Batool and my three sisters were all injured , and I learned a week later that our neighbor was martyred too as he was hit with shrapnel . Abboud and my niece Joud , 10 , were both in critical condition . <p> After a day in intensive care , Abboud died . Joud followed the next day . <p> My injuries , and those of my other children , were classified as second degree burns . People kept coming to tell me that I was lucky and that I would recover . <p> Alhamdulillah . <p> But my mind was filled with Abboud , my sweet son , who was closer to me than anyone and I to him . <p> I know how much innocence , love , kindness and generosity his heart holds , and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and innovation . He and I had a spiritual connection . <p> His eyes alone could tell me everything . And he could sense what I was feeling without us having to talk . <p> He was my companion .
