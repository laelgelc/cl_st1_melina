<p> Palestinians in the occupied West Bank city of Bethlehem marked a sombre Christmas for the second consecutive year in solidarity with Gaza , where the Israeli onslaught has killed 45,000 people in over a year . <p> No festive lights or Christmas trees were put up in the city as the holy day for Christians was limited to religious rituals and prayers . <p> Manger Square , the centre of Bethlehem traditionally believed to be the birthplace of Jesus , was unusually quiet . <p> " Every year , there are scouts , a choir , decorations , a tree is raised , carols are sung , and celebrations take place , " Father Issa Thaljieh , a Greek Orthodox parish priest in Bethlehem , told Middle East Eye . <p> " But this year , Christmas arrives in a sad and gloomy manner , without any signs of joy . " <p> However , he said holding Christmas prayers was an important message of defiance . <p> " Our determination to continue with Christmas , even without the tree and scout music , is our resistance , " Isaac , who has been outspoken against the war , told MEE . <p> " It is our way of saying that we are here , and we will not leave . The policy of the occupation is clear : it is to displace us . But we are determined to stay here . " <h> Gaza 's Christians face erasure <p> The nearly 15-month-long Israel war on Gaza has devastated the besieged territory and threatens to wipe out its 2,000-year-old Christian heritage completely . <p> With fewer than 1,000 Christians remaining in Gaza , most currently live in either the Holy Family Church or St Porphyrius , a Greek Orthodox church in Gaza City . <p> These two churches are the last sanctuaries for Christians in the war-torn territory . <p> The community faces a grim future , threatened not only with physical destruction but also with the erasure of its cultural legacy . <p> The number of Christians sheltering in Gaza 's churches has dwindled this year , with many fleeing through the Rafah crossing into Egypt before Israel seized control of it in May . <p> Those left behind - around 650 people - are enduring dire conditions and are on the brink of annihilation , said George Akroush , an official at the Latin Patriarchate in Jerusalem , speaking to the Financial Times . <p> In his Christmas address last week , Pope Francis condemned the killing of children in Gaza by Israel as " cruelty " as he became more open with his criticism of Israel . <p> " Yesterday , children were bombed . This is cruelty ; this is not war , " he said . <p> Earlier this year , he told the CBS network 's 60 Minutes : " I speak every night at seven to the Gaza parish ... They tell me about what happens there . It 's very tough , very tough ... Sometimes , they go hungry , and they tell me things . There is a lot of suffering .
