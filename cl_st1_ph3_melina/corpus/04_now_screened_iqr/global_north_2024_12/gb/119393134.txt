<p> Binyamin Netanyahu visited Dublin years before he became Israeli prime minister , in a bid to establish a diplomatic presence in the capital . <p> The Israeli embassy is now in the process of closing under the direction of his administration . <p> In February 1990 Netanyahu was the deputy foreign minister in the Likud government while Ireland held the presidency of the European Community . <p> Files released by the National Archives in Dublin show that the Department of Foreign Affairs prepared a brief resum ? of Netanyahu 's career , so that officials could familiarise themselves with a man who would become a defining figure in Israeli politics . His meeting with the Irish foreign affairs minister , Gerry Collins , took place in Dublin on February 21 , 1990 . <p> Netanyahu suggested that there was a " natural feeling of sympathy towards Israel among the Irish people " , but relations had not been helped by Irish soldiers who had been killed in Lebanon while serving with the UN mission . <h> Advertisement <p> Many of these attacks had been blamed on Lebanese militias supported by Israel . <p> Netanyahu told Collins that 95 per cent of the Israeli people were against Palestinian aspirations and defended Israel 's refusal to withdraw from the territories it had occupied since 1967 . <p> Israel is closing its embassy in response to ' anti-Israel policies of the Irish government ' <p> CILLIAN SHERLOCK/PA <p> He was also questioned about settlements in the occupied territories and responded by saying only a fraction of a percentage of Jewish immigrants to Israel lived there . He maintained they were not being incentivised or encouraged to live there . <p> Collins cited a statement by the Israeli prime minister Yitzhak Shamir in the Knesset the previous week , in which he said he would strengthen Jewish settlements through " Judea , Samaria , Gaza and the Golan " . Judea and Samaria are names used by Israel to refer to the West Bank . <p> The report of the meeting said when Shamir 's statement was put to Netanyahu , he " made no reply " . <h> Advertisement <p> At the time of the meeting , Ireland was one of the few states in Europe which did not have an ?
