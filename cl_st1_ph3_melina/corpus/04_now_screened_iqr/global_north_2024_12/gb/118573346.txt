<h> Get the latest Birmingham Live breaking news on WhatsApp <p> Birmingham activists have formed a football club to help to support young people living in deprived areas . Palestine Academy aims to build up aspiring footballers whilst steering them away from drugs , gangs and anti-social behaviour . <p> The club also provides a safe space for young Muslims to express their support for Palestine in the ongoing Israel/Gaza war . Founded by Birmingham activist Abu Umaymah and youth worker Isa Ahmed , the academy officially opened in September welcoming young lads from across Birmingham . <p> Based in Small Heath , boys aged 14-17 can develop their football skills and possibly be directed to larger clubs to pursue a career in the sport . It 's more than just football , however , as Mr Umaymah argues young people from inner city areas lack opportunity . <p> As one of the city 's most deprived areas , child poverty and crime are rampant in Small Heath , he says . Palestine Academy is one of many community groups trying to tackle these issues . <p> Mr Umaymah , 20 , said : " Kids who do n't have a lot of support at home and we are trying to work with parents to help these kids . We also want to instil discipline so if someone is late for a session they will be dropped from the squad . <p> Mohsin Dahir ( left ) ( Image : Abu Umaymah , Palestine Academy ) <p> " Some kids would turn up at the end of the session and were capable players , so they were dropped to the substitute bench for example . It is not your local kick about in the park , we are giving opportunities so take advantage of it . <p> " We are a family . we do a lot of youth work and noticed young people feeling emotional about what 's happening in Palestine . <p> " The best thing to keep young people sane is physical activity . It was a no brainer to link sports to the Palestinian movement but also help young people develop further in their careers , confidence and teamwork . It is more than a football team . " <p> Co founder Mr Ahmed , 18 , knows this better than anyone . He voted for the first time in the general election spurred by the government 's perceived inaction to the Israel-Gaza conflict . <p> He also criticised the lack of investment in youth services and the violent crime he witnessed growing up in Alum Rock . Neglect of inner city Birmingham and Gaza were major issues for the Muslim vote , however Mr Umaymah claims some Muslims feel afraid to express sympathy for Gaza . <p> He claimed he worries about his employment or safety when expressing support for Gaza . Many Muslims tell him they fear far-right retaliation and being accused of being ' terrorist sympathisers ' . <p> He believes the academy allows a safe space to talk about the conflict whilst nurturing aspiring footballers . He said : " We want to see what kids we are dealing with and it could be mental health , drugs , gangs or kids who just want to learn to play football . <p> " We had over 25 applicants from Small Heath , Hodge Hill , Sparkbrook and Alum Rock come to a training session . " <p> One star player , 15-year-old Mohsin Dahir won most inspirational player at the The Black &amp; Asian Police Association awards . He said : " I felt proud of my community and myself . <p> " We feel we are not encouraged to talk politically especially about Palestine , we have raised awareness however .
