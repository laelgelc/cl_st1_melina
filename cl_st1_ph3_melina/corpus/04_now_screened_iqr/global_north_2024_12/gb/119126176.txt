<p> Gordon Brown ordered the Chilcot Inquiry in June 2009 to look into the Labour government and particularly his predecessor Tony Blair 's decision-making when it came to the UK 's involvement in the war in Iraq . <p> Civil servant John Chilcot , who died in 2021 , led the inquiry <p> The seven-year-long inquiry ultimately ruined Blair 's reputation , exposing his subservient relationship to US president George W Bush and found that the UK had not properly exhausted the peace process . <p> The war led to the loss of around 150,000 Iraqi lives as well as those of 179 Brits . <p> Now , Corbyn has said that Keir Starmer and the new Labour Government should expect a new one -- particularly given the UK 's continued arms sales to Israel . <p> While Foreign Secretary David Lammy announced a partial arms embargo earlier this year due to fears UK-made weapons are being used to commit war crimes , parts for F-35 fighter jets -- used to bomb Gaza -- are exempt . <p> The war on Gaza has so far led to the deaths of at least 45,028 Palestinians -- the majority of whom are women and children , according to the Gaza health ministry . <p> " I hope we 're not going to have to go through years of parliamentary wrangling about the effectiveness and nature of the inquiry and then end up with a legally independent judge-led inquiry into the whole process of decision making , " the Independent MP for Islington North said . <p> " But if we have to do that , then we have to do that because we need to get to the truth of it . " <p> Corbyn added that there has to be an inquiry specifically into the " complications of demanding adherence to international law " . <p> " The UK prides itself on that and then is clearly continuing very normal relations with a state that clearly does not adhere to international law in any way whatsoever , " he said . <p> The grants to the likes of BAE Systems , Thales and Leonardo -- which have totalled around ? 3 million since 2023 -- have been criticised by campaigners and charities including Amnesty International , who told the Sunday National it risked " complicity with the most serious of international humanitarian law violations " . <p> Scottish Enterprise , meanwhile , has also defended the grants -- insisting that human rights diligence checks are conducted and are taken " seriously " . <p> Corbyn said all support for arms manufacturers ought to end " particularly in the light of what 's happening in Gaza " . <p> " I think the Scottish Government has a good and correct policy on the whole issue , but there seems to be something lacking in this semi-detached approach that is taken by investors and Scottish Enterprise on how these policies are actually enacted , " he said . <p> " And so that is the danger . With arms-length operations by governments , you 've got to be completely on it to make sure the policy is carried out . " <p> Corbyn added : " I 'm sure the intention of the Scottish Government is not to support or invest in arms industries , particularly those supplying Israel , but they have to make sure it actually happens .
