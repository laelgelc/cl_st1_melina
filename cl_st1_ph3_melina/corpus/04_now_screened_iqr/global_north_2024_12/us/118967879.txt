When my children died , he was the one who comforted me every day , " she says , her voice breaking as she scrubs tears off her face with her hand . <p> Eight-year-old Ahmed sat beside his mother , bursting into tears whenever she wept , only calming down as she stopped or reached a black-clad arm to comfort him . <p> " Grandpa 's gone , " he repeated tearfully , over and over . <p> In an overcrowded home where she has taken refuge with Ahmed , Maysa has little space to grieve her dad , who inadvertently became an icon of Gaza 's suffering a little more than a year ago . <h> ' Soul of my soul ' <p> At 2am on November 29 , 2023 , in the shattered remains of Deir al-Balah , Khaled Nabhan cradled his granddaughter 's small , lifeless body . <p> An Israeli air strike had killed three-year-old Reem and her five-year-old brother Tarek , the two youngest children of his eldest daughter , Maysa . <p> Advertisement <p> Gently kissing Reem 's closed eyes , he whispered that she was " Ruh al-ruh " ( soul of my soul ) and the moment was caught on camera , making the 54-year-old grandfather an icon of Gaza 's agony . <p> It was a moment of peaceful surrender to God 's will that captured hearts everywhere . <p> Since that moment , more videos were shared of Khaled Nabhan as he navigated his loss and worked to help as many people as he could . <p> He focused on comforting others , even consoling people calling from around the world to offer their condolences . <p> When they would bemoan their inability to do anything to stop the bloodshed , he would ask them to pray for Gaza . <p> " There 's nothing more valuable than your prayers ... pray for Allah to be with us , " he told a tearful caller . <p> Khaled Nabhan loved his children and grandchildren . " <p> After his farewell to Reem went viral , Khaled " turned into a one-man relief agency " , Diaa said . <p> Advertisement <p> As love and compassion for him flooded in from around the world , he funnelled that support to those in need , collecting tents , food , and clothing for those left with nothing . <p> Khaled Nabhan in February , moving his family to their second place of refuge , Rafah , which they had to flee again shortly afterwards Mohamed Solaimane/Al Jazeera <p> On the rare occasions when Khaled complained , it was about life in displacement and the humiliation it brought upon others as Israel continued to obstruct the entry of nearly all aid to Gaza . <p> " There is no greater indignity than this , " he said in February from the back of a horse-drawn cart that had his family 's possessions piled on it as he moved them to Rafah , their second displacement location that they eventually had to flee . <p> " People reach out to me for help who do n't even have the bare minimum of clothes to shield them from the elements , " he said .
