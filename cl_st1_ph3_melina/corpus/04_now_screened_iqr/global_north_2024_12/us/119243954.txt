<p> The sense of hollowness of the Church of the Nativity is deeper than absent tourists . <p> The chants and prayers are pain-stricken and desperate . <p> Down in the manger by the enshrined spot where baby Jesus was said to be born , a priest solemnly swings incense into the corners . <p> Image:Bethlehem 's Christian community struggles to celebrate amid ongoing war in Palestine <p> There is no beautifully-lit tree in the square outside for the second year in a row . Even in their homes , the Palestinian Christian community in Bethlehem is struggling to celebrate . <p> The empty spaces and lack of tourism are the shadow of 14 months of war in Gaza and a daily reminder of the ongoing devastation . <p> And around their family homes , Israeli settlements in the West Bank - illegal under international law - are inching closer . <p> ' Our country is shrinking ' <p> " Normally we spend Christmas in Bethlehem and Jerusalem but this year there are no celebrations because of what is happening with the war . It is not nice to celebrate while people are dying , " says Alice Kisiya . <p> Alice is from Beit Jala , Bethlehem . For five years , her family have waged legal battles with settlers over their generational home . <p> " Each Christmas we had demolition because each time they come and demolish . Last year , we were celebrating Christmas there and they came and demolished our small tent , " says Alice . <p> She and her family are waiting for a Supreme Court ruling in January on whether they can return . <p> " Our country is shrinking . As Palestinian Christians , we can not really have our freedom to move freely and it 's getting worse . " <p> Her words resonate as we drive along the West Bank wall on the edges of Bethlehem . <p> A stark reminder of the political divide that is tearing through the Holy Land . <p> ' It seems destiny of Holy Land is to stay divided ' <p> On the other side of the wall from the sacred site where Jesus was born is where he was crucified in Jerusalem . <p> We hear that the procession of the Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem is soon arriving through a checkpoint inside the wall into Bethlehem to the Church of Nativity . <p> A crowd of eager Catholics are waiting for him by Rachel 's Tomb , a sacred site for Orthodox Jews . <p> School children visiting the tomb are ushered off the street by elders aware of the arriving procession . <p> They start to sing defiantly as older students are forcefully removed from the road by Israeli police . <p> There is a hushed sense of anticipation and awe from those waiting for Cardinal Pierbattista Pizzaballa to arrive from the Church of Holy Sepulchre - where Jesus was crucified - to the Church of the Nativity , where he was born in refuge . <p> " This is an important more than a thousand-year-old tradition , " says Tony Marcos , Dean of the Catholic Action Foundation . <p> As a Palestinian resident of the West Bank , Tony is not permitted to make the procession between the two holy sites . <p> " It seems the destiny of this Holy Land is to stay divided and these are difficult times we are living in , " he adds . <p> " Christmas is the season of love and the season of hope . There is big pain and there is instability - a lot of sacrifices and a lot of blood , " he says . <p> Image:Tony Marcos <p> ' We want next year to be full of light ' <p> The anxiety seems to dissipate as Cardinal Pizzaballa arrives . <p> He shakes hands with a queue of people eager to get close to the leader of the Church they can not visit . <p> " This Christmas , we want people not to lose hope . It is possible to break down the hatred , the division , the contempt and the lack of justice and dignity we are experiencing here , " says Cardinal Pizzaballa . <p> " The prayer is to raise our gaze and to look forward , not backwards . We want the next year to be full of light - lighted in the darkness . "
