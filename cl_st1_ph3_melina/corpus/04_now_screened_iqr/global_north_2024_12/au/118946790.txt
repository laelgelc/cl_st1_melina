The government cited a rise in antisemitic incidents across Australia and Victoria -- including the arson attack on the Adass Israel synagogue in the heart of Melbourne 's Jewish community -- as the catalysts for the additional bans . <p> The crackdown will place a blanket ban on symbols and flags of listed terrorist organisations -- including Hamas , Hezbollah and white nationalist groups -- which extend beyond existing restrictions in federal counter-terrorism laws . It will also prohibit protests outside places of worship . <p> Jewish Community Council of Victoria ( JCCV ) chief executive Naomi Levin cautiously welcomed the reforms . Zionism Victoria president Elyse Schachna described them as an important step to address rising levels of antisemitism in Melbourne . <p> " Victorians have had enough of their city being overtaken by often hostile and aggressive protesters , " Schachna said . <p> A Hezbollah flag displayed at a rally in Melbourne.Credit : AAP <p> Liberty Victoria vice president Sam Norton said the proposed changes would lead to people being prosecuted for non-crimes , and that the prohibition on protests outside places of worship could unintentionally prevent advocacy groups from bringing public attention to institutional abuse . <p> " What would their answer be to the protests that occurred , for example , outside Catholic churches in Ballarat ? Are you going to prevent victims of child sex abuse from tying ribbons on the fence of the church ? " he said . <p> Advertisement <p> " There 's about 700 things wrong with this proposal . <p> Loading <p> " Firstly , it 's being done without one scrap of consultation . " What 's not going to lead to a decrease in tension is criminalising behaviour that is not criminal . What will assist is a mature exchange of views . " <p> The proposed ban on face masks at protests revives legislation introduced by the then-Andrews government in 2016 , which it subsequently abandoned after concerns raised by civil libertarian groups . <p> At the time , the proposed laws were aimed at far-right extremists who wore face masks to shield their identity from police . <p> Pro-Palestinian activists have staged weekly protests in Melbourne 's CBD since October 7 , 2023 , when Hamas ' attack on Israel ignited the war in Gaza . <p> They believe the state government 's revived proposal is aimed squarely at them . Some protesters wear keffiyeh scarves to cover their faces . <p> Islamic Council of Victoria president Adel Salman , who has spoken at the Melbourne rallies , said the proposed new powers were an overreaction . <p> " It all presents a view that somehow , there is something insidious about the protests , " he told The Age .
