Photo : Reuters <p> Katie Cox , Christian Aid <p> Katie Cox <p> Yesterday at 03:30 <p> After more than a year of relentless Israeli military bombardment , there are fewer places in the world as deadly for civilians as Gaza . Of the nearly 45,000 Palestinians killed in Gaza , nearly 70pc have been women and children . It is estimated that 37 mothers in Gaza are killed every day . <p> Irish international aid agency Christian Aid has received heart-wrenching accounts from women in Gaza collected by its local partner , the Women Affairs Centre , which shed light on what life has been like . <p> Here are some of these stories . <p> Twenty-three-year-old Heba got married at the end of September last year . She then moved into her new husband 's family home , only for her life to turn completely upside down just one week later . <p> " On the morning of October 7 , we woke up to strange sounds . I thought it was thunder . I could n't imagine it was rockets and that the war had started , and our lives would end , " she said . <p> Heba and 35 of her husband 's extended family fled central Gaza and were displaced a number of times before arriving to Rafah where they all crammed into a friend 's apartment . Later that month while Heba was out , a missile hit the building . <p> He was gone , and everything beautiful in life was gone with him <p> With the help of rescue teams , Heba searched the rubble for over seven hours but she was unable to find her husband . <p> " I had my phone with me and kept calling him . We could hear the ringing but could n't find him . " <p> It was later confirmed that her husband had been killed in the airstrike . <p> " He was gone , and everything beautiful in life was gone with him . " <p> Two women embrace after an Israeli airstrike on a school sheltering displaced people . Photo : Reuters <p> Soon after the war broke out , another woman Walaa , her husband and their son Kareem fled their home in Gaza city to her uncle 's home in Jabalia refugee camp to escape airstrikes . However , a few days later , a missile struck her uncle 's home . <p> " I felt the ceiling collapsing on me . I kept thinking I was dead , " Walaa said . <p> Her husband managed to get out from under the rubble and with the help of neighbours and rescue teams , they eventually rescued Walaa , but could n't find Kareem . <p> Walaa was rushed to a nearby hospital for surgery , where her husband set about looking for Kareem . The devastating truth was revealed later at the hospital when he found a shroud with Kareem 's name covering the body of a small child . <p> I was left for days lying on my back on the floor without treatment , food or drink <p> The day before the airstrike had been Kareem 's fifth birthday . <p> " He had asked me to prepare a birthday party as usual and invite everyone . I promised him I would celebrate it again after the war , " Walaa said . <p> The airstrike robbed Walaa of the chance to fulfil that promise to her son . " He was my whole life . I am a prisoner of my pain and grief over the loss of my only child . " <p> Earlier this year , 60-year-old Fatima 's home in central Gaza was suddenly hit by an airstrike , killing her husband and two of their four children . <p> Fatima was taken to a nearby hospital amid heavy gunfire . Her condition was critical and doctors did not expect her to survive . With hospitals in Gaza short of the electricity , medicine and beds needed to treat the numbers injured , Fatima 's situation deteriorated . Her shattered feet were amputated without anaesthetic during a surgery that lasted several hours . <p> Palestinians at the site of an Israeli strike on a school sheltering displaced people . Photo : Reuters <p> " After the operation , I started feeling for my feet , but could n't find them . I cried a lot . The beds in the hospital were full . I was left for days lying on my back on the floor without treatment , food or drink , " she said . <p> Rosamond Bennett , chief executive of Christian Aid Ireland , said : " These stories are an utterly heart-wrenching insight into what life is like for women in Gaza .
