<h> Banning signs part of dangerous trend <p> As a member of the grassroots North Country Peace Group , I attended the Dec. 17 general meeting and saw the signs calling for a cease-fire and an end to genocide in Gaza and find it hard to imagine why anyone would find such pleas " inciting , " as one legislator implied . <p> Presiding Officer Kevin McCaffrey said that speakers holding signs was not an issue until recently . It 's interesting that signs protesting war and genocide apparently are an issue . These current signs apparently got under the skin of legislators . <p> This is a dangerous trend and a slap in the face of free speech . What will be next ? When the signs are gone , will legislators ban residents from expressing opinions with which they disagree ?
