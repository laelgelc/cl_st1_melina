<p> CAIRO - A new process for inspecting aid for Gaza at the Kerem Shalom crossing is being tested , but efforts to get permission for trucks to enter through the crossing and ramp up relief are still ongoing , a senior U.N . official told Reuters on Saturday . <p> Under the new system , trucks would come to the Kerem Shalom crossing on the border between Israel , Gaza and Egypt for the first time from Jordan , before entering Gaza from Rafah , about 3 km ( 1.86 miles ) away . <p> But the trucks would need to be allowed to enter Gaza directly through Kerem Shalom to alleviate an increasingly desperate situation in the coastal enclave , said Carl Skau , deputy executive director of the U.N . World Food Programme . <p> Israel has so far rebuffed pleas from the United Nations and others to open Kerem Shalom , but they both signalled on Thursday that Kerem Shalom could soon help process delivery of humanitarian supplies into Gaza . <p> Until now , limited quantities of aid have been delivered from Egypt through the Rafah crossing , which is ill-equipped to process large @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ than 40 km ( 24.85 miles ) south to Egypt 's border with Israel before returning to Rafah , leading to bottlenecks and delays . <p> A process to test the inspection system at Kerem Shalom for trucks arriving from Jordan is underway , said Skau , who visited Gaza on Friday . <p> " It 's good , it 's useful because it would also be the first time that we can then bring in a pipeline from Jordan .
