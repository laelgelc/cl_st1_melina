<p> The faces are haunting : Or Levy , Eli Sharabi , and Ohad Ben Ami . Gaunt and hollow , their bodies frail , tell the story of 491 days in Hamas captivity . Sunken eyes , protruding bones , and skeletal frames ring an instant bell for Israelis and Jews everywhere : survivors of the Holocaust , the Muselmann of the concentration camps , and those who starved to the brink of death . <p> But when these hostages came home precisely 80 years after the fall of the Nazi regime , the world 's response was unimaginably detached . <p> The BBC , ever selective in its moral outrage , conveniently omitted their suffering from its homepage . CNN , on the other hand , saw fit to highlight the " emaciated " condition of Palestinian prisoners released in exchange-prisoners who , it must be said , received three meals a day , medical care , and family visits . The comparison is not only absurd ; it is obscene . <p> The myth of Palestinian ' prisoner suffering ' <p> Let 's set the record straight @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ they are convicted criminals , many guilty of terrorism and murder . There are still 76 hostages left in Gaza , some of them dead , all of them subjected to inhumane conditions . The haunting images of these released captives make one thing clear : Every moment they remain in Hamas 's grip is another moment of irreversible physical and psychological damage . <p> And yet , as Israeli families weep for their loved ones , as a nation wrestles with the horror of these images , politicians continue to play their games . <p> Opposition leader Yair Lapid seized @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to act sooner . The hostages ' skeletal frames stand as a living indictment of Hamas 's barbarism , an undeniable crime against humanity . The fact that some still equivocate , still seek to " both sides " this horror , is a stain on the conscience of the world . <p> We must be clear : Hamas does not take hostages .
