For one , haredi Orthodox synagogues , which draw many of the most frequent synagogue-goers in the country , are not represented at all . Neither are Reconstructionist congregations . The researchers also did not distinguish between ideological factions within the movements -- for example , liberal Modern Orthodox vs. the more traditionalist Young Israel movement . Conservative synagogues that decided not to record their sermons on Shabbat because of Jewish legal prohibitions are likewise not reflected . The rabbis who gave the sermons are not listed . <p> There are some broad takeaways , though . Sermons that spoke about Israel did so in overwhelmingly positive terms , especially after Oct. 7 . <p> Criticism of Israel did spike , especially in Reform sermons , during the year or so when Israel 's current government was in office prior to Oct. 7 , as the government was pursuing a program of weakening the judiciary . During that period , more than 40% of Reform sermons included some criticism of Israeli politics . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ mentioned in sermons was Israel 's hostage crisis .
