<p> A group of Jewish journalists have appealed to Israel and Egypt to allow international media into Gaza . <p> Seventeen Jewish British journalists have signed a letter calling on the two countries to lift the ban on foreign press , saying the refusal to allow access was " unjustified and unjustifiable , " and placed " an unacceptable restriction on our professional responsibility to inform readers , viewers and listeners accurately and impartially " . <p> Since the beginning of the Gaza War , only locals or foreign press accompanied by the IDF have been able to report from inside the Strip . <p> " Journalists are the first line of accountability . Refusal of access is therefore a denial of the voices of all in Gaza . We owe it to the victims of this conflict -- and all future conflicts -- that journalists can report on what 's happening and tell the story to the fullest extent possible . That is not possible without being on the ground , " the letter , which was published in Press Gazette on Thursday , states . <p> " With @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ aid , and troops on the ground , it is critical that reporters are able to fulfil their important responsibility to report accurately , faithfully and independently , and to exercise the normal editorial judgements according to the highest ethical standards and journalistic practices . " <p> It went on : " We note that local media have done an extraordinary job reporting on events while themselves being displaced , hungry and at constant risk , but they should not carry this burden alone . " <p> Palestinian journalists and social media influencers have reported from inside Gaza since the start of the war , but many have been killed or injured , while others have been revealed to have connections with Hamas . <p> The letter claimed that " unfetted " media access to Gaza would mean " permitting credentialed , trusted and respected international journalists to work inside Gaza with immediate effect , " adding : We ask our elected representatives , humanitarian colleagues and our own community to demand such access . <p> " It is time for Israel and Egypt to stop using the pretext of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and foreign correspondents to be in situ to cover events , " it added . <p> Some journalists have been permitted into the territory under tightly controlled conditions with an IDF escort . Israel has maintained that the safety of journalists will be at risk if they enter Gaza unrestricted . <p> However , the letter dismisses this as a reason to prohibit access : " To suggest that the safety of journalists can not be guaranteed is not a reason to deny access . International journalists are aware of the risks . These risks are for them to manage as they do all too frequently in conflict zones around the world . That is their job and that is their calling : to report from some of the most troubled places in the hope that humanity will not avert its eyes . " <p> The signatories are calling for international journalists to be allowed " direct in-person contact " in Gaza to write " the first draft of this baleful history " . <p> They add that the request is " respectful " and would allow journalists to " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " . <p> The letter follows a similar intervention in February 2024 , when more than 50 journalists , including the BBC 's Jeremy Bowen , Lyse Doucet and Mishal Husain , sent an open letter calling on Israel and Egypt to provide " free and unfettered access to Gaza for all foreign media " . <p> The Foreign Press Association previously filed a petition with the Israeli court seeking to force the IDF to open a crossing into Gaza so international media could enter . The High Court refused the request , citing ongoing security concerns .
