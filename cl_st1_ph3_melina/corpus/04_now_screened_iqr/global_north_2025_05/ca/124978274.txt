<p> A day after they attended the inauguration of Pope Leo XIV in Rome last weekend , the elected leaders of France , Britain and Canada released a joint statement whose words of moral outrage and calls to action were aimed predominantly at Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu . <p> This was n't a routine diplomatic statement . With its threats of economic sanctions against Mr. Netanyahu and the extreme-right members of his cabinet if they did not allow food into Gaza and bring a stop to the military assault there ( and its demand that Hamas release the remaining hostages ) , it marked a noticeable turn away from the comparative disengagement on Middle East issues previously expressed by the governments of Prime Ministers Mark Carney of Canada and Keir Starmer of Britain . <p> But their decision to join forces with French President Emmanuel Macron in such a direct and active engagement marked a dramatic break from decades-old traditions of international politics in two more important ways . <p> First was their decision to act together , without involving or consulting the United States , on an initiative @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ attempt to compel a ceasefire and peace . This marked the most visible manifestation yet of a new Canada-Europe realignment , forged over the last months in response to President Donald Trump . It foreshadows more dramatic actions in coming months , especially around military co-operation and the defence of Ukraine . <p> Aside from the sanctions threat -- which , considering the economies involved , is largely symbolic -- the statement names further actions . The three countries will join dozens of others , it says , in preparing to officially recognize a Palestinian state at a June 17-20 United Nations conference in New York on the two-state solution . <p> The decision by a majority of Western democracies to pre-emptively recognize a Palestinian state ( Ireland , Norway and Spain did so last year ) would mark a sharp break from the " peace process " convention in place since the 1990s , which holds out statehood as a final reward for Palestinian acceptance of negotiated conditions , including the banishment of the terrorist organization Hamas .
