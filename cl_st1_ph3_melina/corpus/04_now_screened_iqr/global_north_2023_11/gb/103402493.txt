<h> Police picture people wanted for questioning over Pro-Palestine and far-right clashes <p> Several people wanted for questioning over suspected offences including hate crimes and a racially aggravated altercation have been pictured after Armistice Day tensions were stirred by Suella Braverman <p> A number of people wanted by police in relation to alleged crimes at a pro-Palestine march in London yesterday ( November 11 ) have been pictured . <p> Officers from both the Metropolitan Police and the British Transport Police say they want to speak to the individuals following the rally , which saw hundreds of thousands of people take to the streets to demand a ceasefire in Gaza in one of the biggest political marches in British history . Nine cops were injured amid clashes - the " vast majority " with far-right counter-protesters - as they guarded the Cenotaph while a Remembrance service took place . <p> Whilst at least 126 arrests were made on the day , police are continuing to probe footage of the event to prosecute more people . Metropolitan Police took to X , formerly Twitter , to share images of people wanted in relation to hate crimes over offensive signs and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ four men wanted for a " racially aggravated altercation " at Waterloo Station . <p> It comes as pressure continues to mount on Rishi Sunak to sack Suella Braverman after she was accused of sparking the ugly scenes during the protests by labelling ? pro-Gaza supporters as " hate marchers " . Around 2,000 members of the far-right turned up alongside ex-EDL chief Tommy Robinson . Videos showed the group clashing with police with an outbreak of violence before the 11am minute 's silence . <p> London Mayor Sadiq Khan said the unrest - including scuffles with some in the far-crowd chanting anti-Islamic songs - was a " direct result " of Ms Braverman 's dangerous rhetoric . He told the Mirror : " The scenes we witnessed involving the far right were a direct result of the Home Secretary 's words and behaviour . The vast majority of the over 300,000 pro-Palestinian protesters estimated to have marched were peaceful , but the police also had to deal with completely unacceptable behaviour from some , including antisemitism .
