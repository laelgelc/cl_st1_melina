## Recommended label

### **Civilian precarity, displacement, and transnational fallout**

This pole foregrounds the human and political consequences of the conflict beyond general battlefield reporting. It combines discourse about material deprivation and restricted livelihoods with forced displacement, occupation-related violence, hostage experiences, and the conflict’s repercussions in international politics, diaspora communities, media, and popular culture.

## Basis for the interpretation

### 1. Humanitarian and material precarity

A substantial lexical cluster concerns the conditions needed for civilian survival:

- **healthcare**
- **emaciate**
- **desalination**
- **meal**
- **dehydration**
- **airdrop**
- **stampede**
- **underdeveloped**

These words evoke damaged infrastructure, hunger and thirst, humanitarian aid, and bodily deterioration. They suggest a discourse in which Gaza is represented through the material consequences of war and blockade rather than only through military strategy.

The September 2023 export-ban excerpt illustrates an earlier form of this precarity. It describes fish spoiling, workers losing jobs, farmers and fishermen being affected, and the closure aggravating an already fragile economy. Although most of its vocabulary does not exactly reproduce the loading list, its emphasis on blocked goods, ruined livelihoods, and collective punishment is consistent with the loading cluster’s wider humanitarian-material orientation.

The February 2025 Dearborn excerpt makes the connection more directly through **healthcare**, contrasting US expenditure on Gaza-related policy with investment in healthcare and schools. The lexical evidence therefore extends from immediate survival in Gaza to political arguments about resources and responsibility.

### 2. Displacement, reconstruction, and territorial control

The loading **redevelopment** is especially important. In the February 2025 German election excerpt, Trump’s proposal to relocate Gaza’s population and **redevelop** the territory is explicitly discussed and condemned as contrary to international law. Related excerpts address:

- proposals to remove Palestinians from Gaza;
- Saudi rejection of locating a Palestinian state outside Palestine;
- Palestinian-American memories of the Nakba;
- settler expansion and forced expulsion;
- Palestinian attachment to land and demands for sovereignty.

Thus, “redevelopment” functions not as neutral reconstruction vocabulary but as part of a contested discourse about who controls Gaza, whether its population can be transferred, and whether post-war planning entails dispossession. The pole consequently encodes both physical destruction and the political projects proposed in its aftermath.

### 3. Individualized consequences of conflict and occupation

The excerpts repeatedly organize the conflict around identifiable people and particular incidents:

- workers affected by border and export closures;
- Palestinians killed in West Bank raids;
- a soldier’s death attributed to “mistaken identification,” semantically corresponding to **misidentification**;
- Thai agricultural workers returning after nearly 500 days as hostages;
- Palestinian-American families recounting displacement and loss;
- a performer displaying a Sudan–Palestine flag at the Super Bowl.

The loadings **unintentional**, **misidentification**, **kneecap**, and possibly **seeker** similarly point toward incident-level accounts of injury, disputed responsibility, victims, and vulnerable individuals. This is therefore not exclusively a generalized humanitarian pole: it also contains episodic reporting that makes the consequences of violence concrete through individual cases.

### 4. International, media, and cultural repercussions

Another, more heterogeneous loading cluster includes:

- **selfie**
- **newsfeed**
- **advertisment**
- **dnc**
- **oscar**
- **impartiality**
- **chef**
- **yacht**

These items suggest that Palestine/Gaza discourse enters reporting through social media, electoral politics, journalism, celebrity culture, protests, and high-profile public events. This pattern is supported by excerpts concerning:

- a Gaza–Sudan solidarity intervention during the Super Bowl;
- Palestinian-American electoral responses in Dearborn;
- criticism of Keir Starmer’s hostage statement;
- German election debate over US Gaza policy;
- a BBC documentary about Israeli settlers.

The pole thus includes the conflict’s circulation through institutions and arenas geographically removed from Gaza. The term **impartiality** may additionally index disputes over media or institutional positioning, although the excerpts do not provide enough direct evidence to make media impartiality the principal label.

Some of the strongest loadings—**requalify**, **nication**, **sev**, and **lim**—appear to be truncated or malformed tokens. They should not be assigned a confident discourse meaning. Their presence, together with several highly specific lexical items, indicates that this is a relatively heterogeneous, event-sensitive pole.

## Groups driving the pole

Because this is the negative pole, the relevant groups are those with the **lowest mean Factor 2 scores**, even though all reported means are positive.

The two strongest groups are:

- **global_south_2023_09**: mean **2.90**, \(N=10\)
- **global_north_2025_02**: mean **2.95**, \(N=1,649\)

These are also precisely the two groups represented in the supplied excerpts.

The September 2023 Global South result is highly uncertain because it contains only ten texts and has a large SD of 4.77. Nevertheless, its excerpts show a coherent pre-7 October discourse of blockade, export restrictions, West Bank raids, border protests, and occupation-related violence. It may therefore capture a historically specific emphasis on the chronic structures of occupation and economic restriction immediately before the Gaza war.

The February 2025 Global North result is much more robust in sample size. Its excerpts concentrate on:

- ceasefire-era hostage releases;
- Trump’s Gaza relocation and redevelopment proposal;
- international rejection of population transfer;
- Palestinian sovereignty and diaspora responses;
- solidarity performances and media representations;
- settler expansion.

This indicates a later phase in which humanitarian consequences became closely connected to post-war territorial planning and transnational political controversy.

Other relatively low-scoring groups include:

- **global_north_2025_04**: 3.24
- **global_north_2025_03**: 3.28
- **global_north_2025_05**: 3.39
- **global_north_2025_01**: 3.58
- **global_south_2025_02**: 3.59
- **global_south_2025_04**: 3.87

This distribution suggests a broader concentration in early 2025, especially in Global North reporting, but not an exclusive North/South distinction. Both geopolitical groupings move toward this pole in 2025. The pattern is better understood as temporal and event-driven—particularly by ceasefire developments, hostage releases, reconstruction proposals, and displacement debates—than as a stable regional division. Uneven group sizes and the IQR filtering mean that sampling structure may also contribute.

## Alternative labels

1. **Humanitarian precarity and contested displacement**  
   Best if emphasis is placed on deprivation, blockade, aid, population transfer, and territorial rights.

2. **Civilian consequences and post-war territorial politics**  
   Captures the movement from individual suffering and damaged livelihoods to reconstruction, relocation, sovereignty, and settlement.

3. **Episodic human impacts and global political repercussions**  
   Gives greater weight to the individualized incidents and their circulation through elections, diaspora politics, journalism, documentaries, and popular culture.

Overall, **Civilian precarity, displacement, and transnational fallout** is the most inclusive label because it accommodates both the humanitarian lexical cluster and the excerpts’ strong emphasis on territorial dispossession, individualized experiences, and international political-cultural responses. The interpretation should nevertheless be treated as moderately tentative because the loading list contains malformed tokens, several highly event-specific words, and limited direct lexical overlap with the selected excerpts.
