## Factor f3 positive pole: interpretation

### Preferred label  
**Institutional and state control of pro-Palestinian campus activism**

This pole encodes news discourse in which Palestine solidarity activism is situated within universities and becomes the object of institutional regulation, police intervention, legal proceedings, and—especially in the later corpus—immigration enforcement.

### Justification

#### 1. University protest and divestment discourse

A central lexical cluster identifies higher education as the principal arena:

- *campus, student, university, college, school, faculty, academic, graduate, doctoral, postdoctoral, phd*
- *encampment, protest, protester, demonstrator, activist, demonstration, chant, organize*
- *divest, divestment*

The examples strongly instantiate this cluster. They report student encampments, building occupations, walkouts and demonstrations demanding that universities divest from companies connected to Israel or its military campaign. Columbia, MIT, Brown, Northwestern and the University of California system recur as institutional sites.

The pole therefore concerns not pro-Palestinian advocacy in general, but its organization through university communities, facilities, investments and governance structures.

#### 2. Policing, discipline and disruption

A second prominent cluster represents official attempts to control protest:

- *crackdown, arrest, detention, detain, police, officer, riot, gear*
- *barricade, trespassing, disruption, unrest, pre-dawn*
- *administrator, terminate, proceeding, hearing*

These words are also pervasive in the excerpts. Police clear encampments, dismantle barricades, arrest demonstrators for trespassing, and confront protesters in riot gear. University administrations justify intervention through the language of safety, unlawful activity, blocked access and disruption to campus operations. Disciplinary measures include suspension, expulsion threats and restrictions on campus access.

The discourse is not uniformly evaluative. Some reports adopt a critical vocabulary such as *crackdown*, while others reproduce official descriptions of encampments as “unsafe,” “unlawful” or “disruptive.” The factor therefore appears to identify a field of institutional conflict rather than one consistent ideological stance.

#### 3. Graduation and the academic calendar as contested settings

Loadings such as *graduation, commencement, spring, class* and *lawn/green* mark the spatial and temporal form taken by the 2024 protest wave. The examples describe:

- cancelled or altered commencement ceremonies;
- protest walkouts and chants during graduation;
- remote or disrupted classes and examinations;
- encampments on university lawns and campus greens;
- alternative or “people’s” graduations.

Thus, routine academic spaces and ceremonies are represented as politically contested. Commencement becomes both an administrative pressure point and a symbolic platform for Palestine solidarity.

#### 4. Legal and immigration enforcement

The full loading set extends beyond the campus-policing material emphasized by the excerpts:

- *customs, ice, immigration, visa, revoke, deport, deportation*
- *agent, federal, homeland, constitutional*
- *attorney, lawyer, judge, court, case, ruling, jurisdiction, legal*
- *card, residence, application*

Because loadings represent the whole corpus, this cluster must be included in the interpretation even though it is largely absent from the supplied May 2024 examples. It suggests a related discourse in which student or academic activism intersects with immigration status, visa revocation, detention and deportation, accompanied by constitutional challenges and court proceedings. The accompanying academic-status vocabulary—*doctoral, postdoctoral, graduate, phd*—reinforces the likely connection between higher education populations and immigration enforcement.

Accordingly, the pole is broader than **campus protest** alone: it covers the movement of pro-Palestinian activism into systems of university discipline, policing, federal authority and legal adjudication.

### Groups driving the pole

The strongest substantial group means are:

- **global_north_2024_05:** 5.79  
- **global_north_2025_03:** 5.77  
- **global_south_2025_03:** 4.30  
- **global_north_2025_04:** 4.29  
- **global_north_2024_04:** 3.51  
- **global_north_2024_06:** 3.38  
- **global_north_2024_07:** 3.15  

The examples all come from **global_north_2024_05**, directly associating that peak with the April–May 2024 university encampment wave, police clearances and commencement disruptions. The rise begins in April 2024, peaks in May, and remains comparatively elevated in the Global North through the following months.

A second, nearly equal peak occurs in **March 2025**, in both the Global North and Global South, followed by elevated April 2025 scores. Given the full loading inventory, this later peak may be especially associated with visa, deportation, ICE/customs and legal-proceeding coverage involving students or academics. This remains a cautious inference because no March–April 2025 excerpts are supplied.

Global North means exceed their Global South counterparts in every well-populated matched month shown, often substantially. This suggests that the discourse is especially prominent in Global North reporting, plausibly because many salient campus confrontations and immigration controversies concern North American or other Global North institutions. However, the pattern may also reflect uneven corpus sizes, source composition and event concentration. The very high standard deviations in May 2024 and March–April 2025 indicate that the pole is likely driven by clusters of highly focused articles rather than being equally characteristic of all reporting in those groups. The September 2023 means should be treated cautiously because their group sizes are very small.

### Alternative labels

1. **Campus Palestine protests, policing and immigration enforcement**  
   Most explicit about the two major lexical components, though somewhat list-like.

2. **Regulation and criminalization of pro-Palestinian academic activism**  
   Captures arrests, disciplinary action, deportation and legal proceedings, but *criminalization* may overstate texts that report negotiated settlements or neutral administrative action.

3. **University encampments and institutional enforcement**  
   Closely fits the supplied excerpts, but underrepresents the visa, deportation and federal legal vocabulary in the full loading set.

4. **Academic Palestine solidarity under institutional and legal scrutiny**  
   A more stance-neutral label encompassing university administration, policing, civil-rights hearings and immigration litigation.

Overall, **Institutional and state control of pro-Palestinian campus activism** is the most inclusive label because it accommodates both the 2024 encampment-and-policing discourse demonstrated by the excerpts and the immigration/legal enforcement discourse indicated by the full factor loadings and the 2025 group peaks.
