## Interpretation of Factor f4 (negative pole)

### Preferred label  
**Immediate crisis and digitally mediated human-impact reporting**

This pole appears to encode event-driven reporting that presents conflict through rapidly circulating accounts of violence, abduction, personal danger, public reaction, and political controversy. The discourse is characteristic of the immediate aftermath of a major crisis, when information is obtained from eyewitness testimony, social media, mobile recordings, official statements, and reports about missing or endangered civilians.

### Evidence from the excerpts

The examples are remarkably concentrated in **Global North October 2023**, and most concern the first days after 7 October. Recurring discourse elements include:

- **Hostages and missing civilians:** families search for abducted relatives, identify them in videos, track phones, submit DNA samples, and appeal for assistance.
- **Embodied fear and suffering:** bloodied hostages, shootings, bombing, thirst, burning homes, frightened children, and relatives killed or trapped.
- **Eyewitness and first-person narration:** telephone interviews, testimony from survivors and relatives, and experiential descriptions of rockets and bombing.
- **Digital mediation:** videos shared on Telegram or other social media, mobile-phone tracking, online posts, footage captured on camera, and disputes conducted on X.
- **Fast-moving uncertainty:** phrases such as “we don’t know,” “unsure whether they are still alive,” and “almost impossible to predict” reflect the provisional character of breaking-news reporting.
- **Immediate public and political reaction:** protests, accusations, condemnation, arguments about responsibility, demands for resignation, and controversy over reactions on university campuses.

Thus, the pole is not limited to battlefield reporting. It encompasses the wider communicative environment of an unfolding crisis: personal testimony, viral visual evidence, public emotion, and rapidly developing political disputes.

### Evidence from the lexical loadings

The loadings are heterogeneous, but several loose clusters support this interpretation:

1. **Portable and digital news mediation**
   - *hand-held, wireless, newsfeed, selfie*
   
   These terms suggest news and experience conveyed through mobile devices, images, feeds, and informal visual documentation. This is consistent with the excerpts’ repeated reliance on videos, social media, Telegram, camera footage, and phone tracking.

2. **Explosion, bodily harm, and emotional impact**
   - *detonation, bodily, kneecap, heartbroken, healthcare, prevention*
   
   This cluster points towards embodied injury, medical consequences, emotional suffering, and attempts to manage danger. It corresponds to the excerpts’ accounts of bombing, gunfire, deaths, wounded or abducted civilians, and families experiencing fear and grief.

3. **Movement, transport, and international circulation**
   - *passenger, voyage, flotilla*
   
   These items may relate to evacuation, maritime movement, aid missions, or internationally mobile civilians. Evacuation and entrapment are prominent in the Irish-Palestinian example, although the specific loading words do not occur there.

4. **Peripheral international and economic references**
   - *Taiwanese, Polish, dirham, riyal, tola, gram*
   
   These suggest that the pole may also include miscellaneous international reporting, national identification, currencies, and measurements. Their connection to the Gaza excerpts is weak, indicating that the factor extends beyond the illustrated conflict narratives or contains a residual news-register component.

Several loadings—especially *ing, tion,* and *nication*, as well as less interpretable items such as *sev* and *lim*—look like tokenisation or text-processing fragments. Their presence reduces the semantic coherence of the pole and cautions against assigning it a narrowly defined ideological meaning.

### Important limitation of the examples

None of the listed f4-negative loading words is actually identified in the excerpts. Consequently, the excerpts and loadings provide only **indirect convergence**. The excerpts strongly establish an immediate-crisis discourse, while the most interpretable loadings contribute themes of mobile mediation, embodied harm, emotion, healthcare, and movement. Because direct lexical overlap is absent, the label should remain broad and provisional. This may be a relatively diffuse or residual factor rather than a sharply unified discourse dimension.

## Groups driving the pole

Because this is the negative pole, the relevant groups are those with the **lowest mean Factor 4 scores**, even though all reported means are numerically positive.

The principal groups are:

- **global_north_2023_10:** mean 3.12, *N* = 2,864  
- **global_south_2023_09:** mean 3.50, *N* = 10  
- **global_south_2023_10:** mean 3.54, *N* = 1,361  
- **global_north_2023_09:** mean 3.84, *N* = 25  

The strongest substantive evidence comes from **October 2023**, particularly the Global North group. It has the lowest mean, a large sample, and supplies all ten excerpts. Global South October 2023 also scores relatively low and has a substantial *N*, suggesting that the pole may characterize the opening phase of the war across both geopolitical divisions, although no Global South excerpts are supplied to verify whether it takes the same textual form there.

The September groups should be treated cautiously because their sample sizes are extremely small. Their low means may reflect corpus composition rather than a stable pre-October discourse tendency.

There is also a weaker recurrence among groups such as **Global North June 2025** and **Global South October 2024**, but the clearest temporal concentration is the opening crisis of October 2023. By contrast, January–March 2025 have much higher means and therefore do not drive this negative pole. Overall, the pattern is more clearly **diachronic than geopolitical**: the pole is associated most strongly with the immediate outbreak and initial reporting cycle, rather than consistently with either the Global North or Global South. Nevertheless, unequal group sizes and the exclusive selection of Global North examples mean that this conclusion must remain cautious.

## Alternative labels

Other defensible labels include:

- **Breaking-news discourse of violence, abduction, and public reaction**
- **Digitally witnessed civilian crisis reporting**
- **Eyewitness, hostage, and social-media crisis discourse**
- **Event-driven human-interest reporting on the opening war crisis**

Of these, **Immediate crisis and digitally mediated human-impact reporting** is the most inclusive: it captures the excerpts’ dominant discourse while also accommodating the loading clusters related to mobile media, bodily harm, emotion, healthcare, and movement. It should, however, be regarded as a cautious interpretation of a lexically heterogeneous pole.
