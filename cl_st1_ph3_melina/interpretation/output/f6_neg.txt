## Interpretation of Factor f6 (negative pole)

### Preferred label  
**Humanitarian public-health and civilian-protection reporting, with strong outlet-boilerplate marking**

This pole most plausibly encodes reporting that presents Gaza through threats to civilian survival, especially the destruction or failure of healthcare, displacement, inadequate shelter, and the protection of civilians and professionals. However, it is also substantially marked by recurring publisher-specific and webpage language, so it should not be treated as a purely thematic dimension.

### Evidence from the loadings

The clearest coherent lexical cluster concerns **public health and care**:

- *vaccinate* (−.65), *vaccination* (−.57), *virus* (−.44), *polio* (−.41), *healthcare* (−.39), *caregiver* (−.38), *vaccine* (−.38), and *caretaker* (−.33).

These words point particularly to vaccination campaigns, disease prevention, medical provision, and vulnerable populations. In the Gaza context, this likely includes reporting on polio vaccination and the difficulties of maintaining healthcare under war conditions.

A secondary cluster concerns **civilian infrastructure, destruction, and recovery**:

- *residence* (−.33), *rebuilding* (−.34), *detonation* (−.33), *walkie-talkie* (−.43), *wireless* (−.38), *hand-held* (−.33), and *simultaneous* (−.34).

This may extend the pole beyond healthcare to technical reporting on attacks, communications devices, damaged homes, and post-attack reconstruction. The walkie-talkie/wireless/detonation cluster may reflect geographically adjacent reporting on the Lebanon communications-device explosions rather than one unified Gaza-specific discourse.

Several other loadings appear less thematically coherent and may represent source, regional, or genre effects: *Malay*, *Taiwanese*, *Lim*, *DNC*, *congresswoman*, *postdoctoral*, *doctoral*, *advertisment*, *Versa*, *gram*, and *tola*. Their presence suggests that f6_neg is partly a heterogeneous lexical bundle rather than a completely clean substantive dimension.

### Evidence from the excerpts

The excerpts strongly instantiate a **civilian humanitarian emergency and protection discourse**:

- attacks on hospitals and the collapse of Gaza’s healthcare system;
- the deaths and detention of healthcare workers;
- forced displacement and the destruction of residences;
- flooded tents, freezing temperatures, hypothermia, and infant deaths;
- the killing of journalists;
- demands for investigations and accountability under international law;
- criticism of evacuation orders, “safe zones,” and alleged war crimes or genocide;
- Israeli soldiers’ testimony about the destruction and looting of Palestinian homes.

The examples therefore frame Palestinians primarily as civilians exposed to institutional collapse and cumulative, preventable harm. Medical and humanitarian institutions—hospitals, the UN, WHO, ICRC, human-rights organizations, and legal tribunals—provide much of the evidential and evaluative authority.

Nevertheless, the actual loading words found in these examples reveal an important qualification. The recurrent terms are mainly:

- *kindly*, appearing in Countercurrents fundraising appeals;
- *well-rounded*, appearing in PKKH’s standardized self-description;
- *Malay*, as part of the outlet name *Malay Mail*;
- *residence*, used in substantive references to civilian or diplomatic residences.

Thus, many of the sampled texts receive negative scores partly because of **publisher templates, promotional material, fundraising appeals, and outlet identification**, not only because of their humanitarian content. The excerpts contain only one or two negative-loading words each, and the highest negative scores are modest (−1 or −2). The thematic interpretation should consequently give substantial weight to the broader loading inventory—especially the vaccination and healthcare cluster—while recognizing that the examples expose a strong source-format component.

## Groups driving the pole

Because this is the negative pole, the relevant groups are those with the **lowest mean f6 scores**. The clearest driver is:

- **global_south_2025_01: 0.228**, the lowest mean in the table.

All ten supplied examples come from this group, confirming its importance. The next-lowest means are also predominantly Global South groups:

- global_south_2024_12: 0.273  
- global_south_2024_09: 0.364  
- global_south_2025_02: 0.365  
- global_south_2024_10: 0.370  
- global_south_2024_11: 0.393  

The Global North also approaches this pole in late 2024 and early 2025, particularly:

- global_north_2024_10: 0.425  
- global_north_2024_09: 0.427  
- global_north_2024_11: 0.430  
- global_north_2025_02: 0.431  

This suggests a broad late-2024/early-2025 tendency, but one that is more pronounced in the Global South strata. The especially low Global South January 2025 score plausibly reflects the salience of healthcare collapse, winter displacement, ceasefire developments, retrospective casualty reporting, and human-rights documentation at that moment. It may also reflect the concentration of particular outlets—especially Countercurrents, PKKH, and Malay Mail—whose boilerplate supplies several of the observed loading words.

Scores rise sharply in both regions by May–June 2025, so those months do not drive this pole. Since every group mean remains positive, f6_neg is best understood as a **relative concentration** in the lowest-scoring groups rather than a pole that characterizes entire monthly strata with strongly negative average scores. Unequal group sizes also require caution: global_south_2025_01 has a substantial but smaller sample than many Global North groups, while September 2023 groups are too small to support firm comparisons.

## Other defensible labels

1. **Medical vulnerability, displacement, and civilian survival discourse**  
   This foregrounds the healthcare, vaccination, shelter, and mortality vocabulary, as well as the excerpts’ focus on hospitals, infants, displaced families, and damaged homes.

2. **Humanitarian-health crisis and institutional accountability reporting**  
   This captures both the medical lexicon and the examples’ reliance on UN assessments, human-rights reports, international law, investigations, and demands for accountability.

3. **Public-health intervention and war-damaged civilian infrastructure discourse**  
   This gives more direct weight to the full loading set, including vaccination, polio, healthcare, residences, rebuilding, communications devices, and detonations.

Of these, **“Humanitarian public-health and civilian-protection reporting, with strong outlet-boilerplate marking”** is the most cautious label because it captures the coherent discourse in both the loadings and excerpts without overlooking the clear influence of source-specific webpage language.
