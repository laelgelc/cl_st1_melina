## Interpretation of Factor 1, negative pole

### Preferred label: **Routinized institutional-administrative framing of Israel/Palestine**

This pole appears to encode reporting in which Palestine/Israel is mediated through **diplomatic, regulatory, governmental, economic, educational, or civic institutions**. Rather than representing one narrowly defined topic, it brings together routine news about visa rules, diplomatic recognition, border administration, economic restrictions, official investigations, university discipline, and public commemoration.

The examples support this interpretation:

- The September 2023 Global South texts report prospective **normalisation agreements**, foreign-ministerial statements, the US **Visa Waiver Program**, border and export restrictions, World Bank assessments, and officially reported military raids.
- The February 2025 Global North texts relocate the issue into US institutional settings: university disciplinary proceedings, statements by the attorney general and college administrators, and a synagogue memorial involving local political and religious figures.
- Conflict is therefore often presented through procedures and institutional actors: ministries, armies, international organisations, universities, visa programmes, permits, crossings, and formal statements.

The lexical loadings reinforce this administrative and public-institutional component. Items such as **reciprocity, waiver, congresswoman, Arab-American, nominee, DNC, inauguration, swearing-in, tariff, ownership, redevelopment**, and **then-foreign** point toward regulation, elite politics, diplomacy, public office, and policy administration. Their occurrence is visible in the excerpts: the visa-waiver reports discuss **reciprocity**, Arab-American advocates, congresswomen, and formal eligibility requirements, while the normalisation report contains **“on the cusp”** and references a former foreign minister.

This is not simply a discourse of military violence or Palestinian hardship. Although raids, deaths, blockade, unemployment, healthcare access, and export losses are prominent in several examples, they are consistently organised through **official attribution and administrative consequences**: health-ministry figures, army statements, World Bank reports, work permits, export regulations, and crossing closures.

### Alternative label: **Diplomatic, regulatory, and civic mediation of the conflict**

This label gives greater prominence to the different institutional domains represented:

- **Diplomatic:** Arab–Israeli normalisation, foreign ministers, UN General Assembly activity.
- **Regulatory:** visa reciprocity, permits, border access, trade restrictions, tariffs and waivers.
- **Civic and educational:** campus protest discipline, memorialisation, religious institutions, and local political responses.

It is particularly suitable if the dimension is understood as identifying how Palestine/Gaza discourse is incorporated into broader domestic and international institutional agendas.

### More cautious label: **Institutional news reporting with news-feed and webpage residue**

The pole also contains a substantial technical or corpus-source component. Several highly weighted items—**newsfeed, nication, footer, advertisment, promotional, opt-out, intro**—look like webpage furniture, truncated forms, subscription language, or scraped adjacent material rather than semantically central discourse features. Other items such as **selfie, yacht, cocktail, Oscar, estate, riyal**, and **dirham** suggest mixed news sections or appended content.

The examples demonstrate this directly:

- The Pakistani visa-waiver articles are followed by unrelated currency and gold-price material containing **riyal** and **dirham**.
- The February 2025 Fox News example ends with subscription boilerplate containing the loading words **promotional** and **opt-out**.
- “Advertisement” and “related topics” material also occur within the extracted texts.

Consequently, the pole should not be treated as a wholly clean substantive discourse. It likely combines an institutional-administrative news register with **outlet formatting, aggregation, syndication, and scraping characteristics**. The opaque strongest loadings—especially **requalify, newsfeed**, and the truncated **nication**—make this qualification important.

## Groups driving the pole

Because this is the negative pole, the relevant groups are those with the **lowest Factor 1 means**.

The lowest mean is for **global_south_2023_09** (5.80), and eight of the ten examples come from this group. These texts establish a clear pre-October-2023 cluster involving normalisation diplomacy, visa arrangements, economic restrictions, healthcare administration, and West Bank raids. However, this group has only **N = 10**, so its extreme mean is unstable and may partly reflect the particular articles retained after IQR filtering.

The strongest well-sampled contributor is **global_north_2025_02** (6.28, N = 1,649), represented by the university-discipline and memorial excerpts. Other relatively low groups include:

- **global_north_2024_09**: 6.63  
- **global_south_2025_02**: 7.03  
- **global_north_2024_08**: 7.07  
- **global_north_2025_03**: 7.20  
- **global_north_2024_07**: 7.29  

Thus, February 2025 is a notable shared low point across both geopolitical groupings, while the Global North also shows relatively strong association with this pole from July to September 2024.

## Geopolitical and temporal tendencies

In most matched months, Global North means are lower than Global South means, suggesting that this pole is somewhat more characteristic of the sampled Global North reporting. This is a tendency rather than a categorical divide: the September 2023 Global South group is the overall minimum, but its very small sample makes direct comparison unsafe.

Diachronically, the pole is least evident in the higher-scoring months around late 2023 and early 2024, especially February–March 2024, and becomes more prominent in:

1. the small pre-war September 2023 stratum;
2. Global North reporting in mid-to-late 2024; and
3. both North and South reporting in February 2025.

These movements may reflect changing news agendas—from pre-October diplomacy and administrative restrictions to later domestic institutional controversies and memorial practices—but they may also be influenced by outlet composition and the uneven presence of boilerplate-heavy or aggregated pages.

## Overall assessment

The most defensible label is therefore:

> **Routinized institutional-administrative framing of Israel/Palestine**

with **“Diplomatic, regulatory, and civic mediation of the conflict”** as a more topical alternative. Any label should acknowledge that the pole is partly shaped by **news-site boilerplate and mixed scraped content**, so it represents a combination of discourse and source-format effects rather than a fully unified ideological theme.
