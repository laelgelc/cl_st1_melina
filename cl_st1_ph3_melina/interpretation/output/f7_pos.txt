## Recommended label

**Formal diplomatic-administrative management of Israel–Palestine relations, with source-linked market-news bundling**

### Other possible labels

- **Institutional diplomacy, reciprocity, and regional normalisation**
- **Bureaucratised mobility and international-policy discourse**
- **Procedural US–Israel and Arab–Israel diplomacy**
- **Diplomatic-policy reporting with Pakistani financial bulletin residue**

## Interpretation

The coherent discourse at this pole concerns the treatment of Israel–Palestine through **formal diplomatic, legal, and administrative mechanisms**. Rather than concentrating primarily on events on the ground, high-scoring texts discuss visa rules, reciprocal rights, diplomatic recognition, international resolutions, policy memoranda, permit applications, and negotiations over regional normalisation.

This interpretation is supported by a substantial procedural-policy cluster in the loadings:

- **Mobility and administrative eligibility:** *waiver, reciprocity, application, requalify, customs*
- **Formal instruments and international procedure:** *memorandum, resolution, non-binding, abstention, veto, roadmap, provisional*
- **Diplomatic relations and negotiation:** *saudi, then-foreign, caretaker, conduit, cusp, Arab-American*
- **Humanitarian-policy language:** *unhindered, eight-month, basis*

The excerpts give these terms a more specific discursive context. Excerpts 1–3 centre on Israel’s admission to the US Visa Waiver Program. *Waiver, reciprocity, memorandum,* and *application* frame Palestinian-American mobility and alleged discriminatory treatment as matters of regulatory compliance and bilateral administration. Excerpt 7 similarly presents Palestinian healthcare access through the bureaucratic language of permit *applications*. Thus, one component of the pole is a discourse of **rights and movement mediated through institutional procedures**.

Excerpts 4–6 develop a related diplomatic component: possible Saudi–Israeli normalisation, Pakistan’s position on recognition, Abraham Accords expansion, and unofficial diplomatic *conduits*. The words *Saudi, cusp,* and *then-foreign* occur in reporting that treats Palestine as a condition, constraint, or negotiating consideration within wider interstate realignment. This supports the label’s emphasis on **regional normalisation and diplomatic management**.

Excerpt 10 extends the same discourse into US foreign policy and UN procedure. The *memorandum* conditioning military support and the US *veto* of Security Council *resolutions* represent Gaza through institutional decisions, formal oversight mechanisms, and diplomatic leverage. Across the examples, therefore, Palestine/Gaza is repeatedly embedded in administrative and geopolitical processes: visa eligibility, diplomatic recognition, aid conditions, UN voting, and negotiated regional arrangements.

## Important lexical and corpus qualification

The pole is not entirely semantically unified. Its strongest loadings—*riyal* (.90), *dirham* (.89), *tola* (.71), together with *gram, fluctuate,* and *currency*—come from financial-market material appended to the two highest-scoring Pakistani texts. Those excerpts combine Israel visa-waiver reporting with exchange-rate and gold-price bulletins. Consequently, part of the factor appears to encode a **publication or text-extraction bundle**, especially Pakistani news pages containing adjacent market content, rather than a Palestine-related discourse in its own right.

Other items such as *doctoral, postdoctoral,* and *PhD*, as well as fragments including *tion, ing,* and *nication*, further suggest mixed page content or extraction artefacts. The safest label should therefore retain the coherent diplomatic-administrative interpretation while acknowledging that the statistical pole is strengthened by source-specific non-political material. A narrow label such as “visa-waiver discourse” would fit the top excerpts but would underrepresent the broader loading set; an exclusively economic label would fit the highest loadings but not the central discourse of most examples.

## Groups driving the pole

The pole is overwhelmingly associated with **global_south_2023_09**, whose mean is **2.50**, the only positive group mean and by far the highest score. Nearly all of the September examples come from this stratum, particularly Pakistani outlets, and several reproduce or closely duplicate the same visa-waiver, Saudi-normalisation, and attached financial-market material.

This result requires strong caution:

- The group contains only **10 texts**, compared with hundreds or thousands in most later strata.
- Its SD is high (**3.24**), indicating substantial internal variation.
- The two highest-scoring texts contain nearly identical appended exchange-rate and gold-price passages.
- The Global North September 2023 group is also small (**N = 25**).

The exceptional mean may therefore reflect the historical salience of late-September 2023 diplomacy—Israel’s US visa-waiver admission and anticipated Saudi normalisation—but it is also likely amplified by source concentration, duplication, and page bundling.

Outside this small outlier, Global South groups generally remain closer to the positive pole than corresponding Global North groups. The next-highest means include:

- **global_south_2024_11:** −0.304
- **global_south_2024_03:** −0.366
- **global_south_2023_12:** −0.379

The November 2024 result is substantively supported by excerpt 10, where Trump’s return, Saudi normalisation, US policy memoranda, and Security Council vetoes reactivate the pole’s institutional-diplomatic vocabulary. Global North groups are consistently more negative, with their closest approaches occurring around February–April and November 2024. This may indicate that Global South reporting more frequently embeds Palestine in questions of diplomatic recognition, Arab or Muslim-state policy, mobility rights, and international procedure. Nevertheless, because all sizeable strata have negative means, the pole appears to be a **concentrated discourse found in particular articles and sources**, not a dominant register across the corpus.

Overall, the best interpretation is **formal diplomatic-administrative management of Israel–Palestine relations**, especially visa reciprocity, Palestinian mobility, Arab–Israeli normalisation, and international-policy procedure, while explicitly recognising the strong source-linked financial-news residue that helps define the statistical pole.
