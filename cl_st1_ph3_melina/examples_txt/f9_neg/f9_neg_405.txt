<h> Children 's book on Palestine gets blowback <p> Illustrated by South African artist Nathi Ngubane , From the River to the Sea : A Colouring Book has sparked controversy , drawing sharp criticism from Zionist groups who have labelled it " anti-Semitic " . <p> The colouring book , designed to be both educational and engaging for children , aims to introduce young readers to Palestine 's history , culture and notable figures through an interactive format . <p> Ngubane explained to the Mail &amp; Guardian that the inspiration for it came after South African lawyers brought charges against Israel for genocide at the International Court of Justice early this year . <p> Following these events , his publisher approached him with the idea of creating a book focused on Palestine , particularly in light of its work on Covid-related publications . <p> " Social Bandit Media steps in during critical moments , " Ngubane said . " In 2020 , we addressed Covid , and now we are responding to the ongoing conflict in Gaza . Our goal was to create an accessible activity book for children that also educates them about Palestine 's struggle for freedom @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ global solidarity movements , including the widespread protests supporting Palestinian rights . <p> As an activist , Ngubane sees the book as a tool for political education , raising awareness about the Palestinian cause . <p> The book 's title , From The River To The Sea , has been particularly contentious . Pro-Israel lobbyists claim the slogan calls for ethnic cleansing against Israeli Jews . <p> Ngubane acknowledges its controversial nature but clarifies his intentions . <p> " To me , From the River to the Sea is an inspirational chant for freedom , coexistence and human rights . <p> " It signifies hope and the pursuit of justice . " <p> Despite this , the book has faced severe backlash . Exclusive Books , a major chain , has removed it from its shelves . Attempts to reach Batya Bricker , the general manager of books and brand , for comments on the removal and the store 's definition of " controversial " content , were unsuccessful . <p> Ngubane expresses his dismay at the negative reactions and the hateful messages he has received . <p> The Cape @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ alleged on Facebook the book promoted " the obliteration of Jews from our historical and rightful homeland -- Israel " . <p> " The pushback highlights a stark double standard , " said Ngubane . " On one hand , we see children being burnt alongside their parents in Gaza , suffering unimaginable horrors , with over 26 ? 000 Palestinians killed since 7 October . On the other hand , people are outraged by a colouring book . " <p> Ngubane asserts his belief in the Palestinian quest for freedom and refuses to succumb to censorship , echoing Nelson Mandela 's words , " We are not free until the people of Palestine are free . " <p> Funds from the book 's sales will be used to support humanitarian relief in Gaza where Israel 's insurgency continues .
