<h> Cloudy year ahead for airlines <h> Yearender : Of smooth flights and rough skies <p> MANILA , Philippines -- As the global demand for air travel surges , it appears safe to assume that airlines are on their way back to cruising altitude in 2024 . <p> Quite the opposite . They are now more cautious than ever . <p> Philippine Airlines ( PAL ) president and chief operating officer Stanley Ng believes that the risks to be faced next year are as many and as serious as the opportunities that could be taken . <p> He warned that the brewing tensions in Gaza and Ukraine pose one of the biggest threats toward regaining pre-pandemic stability . <p> The man steering the flag carrier to post-pandemic recovery said prices of jet fuel would keep on fluctuating in the face of geopolitical conflicts in Gaza and Ukraine . Data gathered by the International Air Transport Association showed that jet fuel in Asia and Oceania averaged $102.87 per barrel as of Dec. 8 . <p> Cebu Pacific chief finance officer Mark Julius Cezar expects prices of jet fuel to stay above $100 per barrel in 2024 . In response , Ng said the flag carrier would expand its fleet of jets powered by other engines to fill in the gap to be left behind by the parked units . <p> " PAL will work aggressively on mitigating any negative effect from the Pratt &amp; Whitney engine issues . Whereas PAL is finalizing its spending guidance for next year , Cebu Pacific is increasing capital expenditures by nearly a fifth to P50 billion . <p> PAL is working with Airbus for the delivery of two batches of aircraft : first , for 13 narrow-body jets and second , for nine wide-body units .
