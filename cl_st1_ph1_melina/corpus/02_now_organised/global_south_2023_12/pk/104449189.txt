<h> Dying for a better life <p> By Editorial Board <p> December 20 , 2023 <p> There are two kinds of migrants in today 's world . There are those that have the money , skills and education , with the latter two more often than not a by-product of the first , necessary to cross the invisible lines of red tape that divide our world -- and those that do not . <p> Sadly , the red tape is only getting thicker as a wave of xenophobia sweeps across many advanced countries in the West which attracts migrants of all kinds seeking to improve their standard of living or flee war , natural disasters , and persecution . In this latest tragedy at sea , the migrants are reportedly mostly from African countries and around 25 survived the shipwreck . The boats used in these illicit Mediterranean crossings are often too small and overcrowded , making them prone to accidents . Unfortunately , the desperation of many in the developing world is such that they will pay exorbitant sums to human smugglers in order to reach European countries despite the risks involved . Rather than putting up barriers to keep poor migrants out , the rich countries of Europe ought to take some ownership of the problem . No one leaves their home behind and puts their life on the line if they have better options . These migrants are a by-product of the climate change , wars , and predatory economics that the West has fuelled -- a case in point would be the ongoing war in Gaza . If not refuge , the European countries at least owe these migrants better rescue and rehabilitation services . And this does not mean that leaders in the developing world are off the hook .
