<p> As we do every year , IndieWire reached out to the cinematographers whose films are showing at the 2025 Cannes Film Festival to find out which cameras and lenses they used and , crucially , why their choices were the right tools to create the look and visual language of their films . <p> The following list includes both documentaries and fiction films playing throughout the festival . There are exciting titles from both veteran directors and first-time filmmakers , ambitious documentary projects , and pared-down character studies looking to do the most painterly and/or naturalistic work with limited resources . There is a pleasing mix of 16mm and 35mm in and amongst the digital , and also clever workarounds for how to achieve film looks on digital . <p> Each film at Cannes is its own universe ; each required its own set of innovations and compromises to bring to life . Bertrand Mouly , the cinematographer of " Dites Lui Que Je L'Aime , " shot through a Pyrex glass ( yes , the oven-safe trays ) in order to pull off the stilted vision of a character 's flashback . Keisuke Imamura , who shot " Exit 8 " mostly in underground spaces , needed to create both dynamic senses of reality and unreality within them . Mia Cioffi Henry , the DP of " Sorry , Baby " needed to maintain a sense of intimacy over the sense of five years of time passing . <p> If there 's a theme that emerges out of the 2025 Cannes films , it is a search for imperfection , for , as cinematographer Simon Beaufils who shot " Her Will Be Done " put it , " We loved the idea of getting , in 2025 , an " unclean " complex living image , full of flaws and lacking in precision . An image that hesitates , that questions itself , that has large grey areas . A human image , in a way . " <p> Likewise , Amin Jafari , the cinematographer of " A Simple Accident , " told IndieWire that " in films like ' A Simple Accident , ' where guerrilla filmmaking is the priority , mood and emotional presence are often more important than technical perfection , " Jafari said . " Ultimately , these tools did n't just do the job -- they helped us feel it , shape it , and see it . In this film , the camera was n't just a recording device -- it was part of how we saw the world .
