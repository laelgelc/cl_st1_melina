( AP Photo/Jose Luis Magana , file ) <p> It was a meaningful night that is now memorable for all the wrong reasons . <p> As the associate director of the American Jewish Committee 's Washington , D.C. , regional office , part of my job is to plan events . On Wednesday , May 21 , I was excited as we prepared to welcome diplomats , young leaders and community advocates to our Young Diplomats Reception at the Capital Jewish Museum . <p> The evening 's theme was " Turning Pain into Purpose , " a reflection on how grassroots diplomacy can transform the Middle East through humanitarian aid and cross-cultural collaboration . Speakers from the Multifaith Alliance and IsraAID shared how humanitarian diplomacy @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is painstakingly tedious to build , and is -- at the same time -- one of the most valuable and vital commodities in this work . <p> As we wrapped up the event , we were feeling inspired , hopeful even . That soon changed . <p> Sarah Milgrim and Yaron Lischinsky , may their memory forever be a blessing , were murdered by a fervently antisemitic terrorist outside the museum , targeted because they were believed to be Jewish , leaving a Jewish event , held in a Jewish space . <p> I keep trying to make sense of the fragmented scenes from that evening replaying in my head . <p> I remember standing by the exit as the group with Sarah and Yaron left . I hugged some of them goodbye , saying we 'd see each other soon . <p> Then my colleague , who had gone outside to catch a ride home , came rushing back inside , after calling 911 . And this community we had built over years -- and some who we had first welcomed that night -- came together to keep each @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ sure everyone was OK , that we had water . Women took off their heels . We moved away from the doors and took note of places we could hide . <p> I tried to keep myself from assuming the worst -- yes , we were at a Jewish event , in a Jewish space , but we were also in a city hardly immune to gun violence . I tried to convince myself it was just that , hoping to abate the feeling in the pit of my stomach that knew otherwise , that a shooting outside of a Jewish museum could n't be just a coincidence . <p> We knew there were two victims . We did n't know if they were alive or dead . Then a few of us learned that it was Sarah and Yaron -- and we could n't tell anyone because the information was n't yet public . There was no time to grieve -- there were people to take care of and questions from law enforcement to answer . <p> As the news began to break across the DMV , the country @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ asking if we were safe -- including from family members of hostages still held captive in Gaza . The irony was not lost on us that our Israeli friends were terrified for our safety . In those moments of darkness and fear , this community -- in that room in D.C. and beyond -- was the light and humanity . <p> There is a Jewish teaching , elu v'elu -- these and these -- which tells us that it is possible for two seemingly contradictory things to be true at the same time . AJC 's Young Diplomats event was a stark reminder of this . One hundred deeply committed people -- Jews and non-Jews , diplomats representing 30 countries , and volunteers , came together to hear about turning pain into purpose . <p> They left having experienced the depravity of an act whose purpose was to terrorize , isolate and " globalize the intifada " -- stoking the fires of antisemitism , and the goal of annihilating Jews across the world simply because we are Jews . <p> Sarah and Yaron are dead ; their friends and families @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ this vile ideology , a stark example of what happens when hateful , antisemitic rhetoric festers and erupts with deadly consequences . Their murders are the manifestation of what the Jewish community has been warning about for years -- and they are not the first . Sarah and Yaron 's murders are now part of the list of antisemitic tragedies including Pittsburgh , Poway , Monsey , Paris and Oct. 7 , 2023 . It 's up to us to make sure it 's the last addition . <p> This week , Jews will celebrate Shavuot , the holiday commemorating the receiving of the Torah at Mount Sinai . The Torah is the centrality of what has held our people together for 5,000 years . And even with a written document at its core , Judaism is a religion of action . When our ancestors received the Torah , they said , " Na'aseh v'nishmah " -- widely interpreted to mean , first we will act and then we will believe . <p> Believing is an important component for any community of faith , but action is what helps us @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> We will turn this pain into purpose , ensuring Sarah and Yaron 's names are truly for a blessing . <p> We owe it to them , to ourselves , to future generations . Today , centered in optimism for the future and with the conviction that a world of more people coming together to build bridges is possible , we act so that tomorrow we can believe this world is achievable . <p> Alexis Schwartz is associate director of the American Jewish Committee regional office in Washington , D.C.
