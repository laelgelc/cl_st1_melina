These people have spent months away from their families , jobs , and lives , and finally got a short break . Now , with little notice , they are being called back -- some for another 100 days of service . <p> And yet , they show up . <p> Despite predictions that motivation would plummet after more than a year and a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ round of call-up orders is remarkably high . It is no longer the 150% response we saw in the immediate aftermath of October 7 , but still in the 90s , well above expectations . It is a testament not just to resilience , but to the national ethos of responsibility and commitment that exists here in Israel . <p> But that resilience was matched this week by an incredible political slap in the face . As reservists prepared to return to the battlefield , the Knesset opened its summer session with one key item on its agenda : passing a new IDF draft law that would , once and for all , codify the exemption of haredim ( ultra-Orthodox ) from military service . Is the war about returning the hostages or eliminating Hamas ? Or is it both ? If it is about removing Hamas from power , there needs to be a new governing entity in place , but the government refuses to decide . <p> It moves between conflicting goals , based on political interest rather than what is right for the nation . As a result , Hamas has managed to rearm , and 59 hostages remain in captivity . <p> Could this have been handled differently ? Almost certainly .
