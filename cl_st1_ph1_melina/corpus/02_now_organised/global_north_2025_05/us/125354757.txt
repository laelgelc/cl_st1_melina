<h> Rising from the ashes , Beirut rebuilds after years of strife <p> Alley with graffiti in Beirut 's trendy Gemmayzeh neighborhood of cafes and nightclubs . <p> Jim Doyle , Special to the Post-Dispatch <p> BEIRUT -- Looking beyond this city 's war scars , it 's still possible to see why decades ago Lebanon 's capital acquired the nickname " the Paris of the Middle East . " This cosmopolitan mecca on the Mediterranean Sea served during its Golden Age , from the 1950s to the 1970s , as a financial center and trading hub , a cultural oasis and travelers ' way station . Then this nation took a detour , falling into a prolonged civil war . <p> Today , it 's a city straining to be reborn . Construction cranes and gleaming skyscrapers have taken up residence next to tenement apartments and abandoned buildings in tatters as this ancient city attempts to rebuild itself . Through the grim years , the Lebanese held fast to their passion for music , art , dance , fashion , exquisite cuisine and the Beqaa Valley 's homegrown wines @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ romantic vistas or its eternal spirit . Transcending the chaos , they hung on to a form of " joie de vivre " -- celebrating life 's moments and nuances . <p> But it 's also a city in an unrelenting rush . Locals seem driven by a frenetic pace to survive , get ahead and make up for lost time . Day and night , the shrill honks of speeding cars , motorcycles and scooters reverberate through Beirut 's crowded streets . With few traffic lights , stop signs or crosswalks , the careening motorists create a high-stakes obstacle course for pedestrians . <p> The Al-Omari Grand Mosque , known as Jami ' Al-Kabir , in central Beirut . <p> Jim Doyle , Special to the Post-Dispatch <p> Built in the 1930s atop Roman and Ottoman ruins , one can see beyond the former French colonial outpost , visit the remains of a Roman bathhouse and detect the Moorish influences of ornate arches and stately columns in Beirut 's eclectic architecture , historic mosques and cathedrals . Some structures are renovated . Others are demolished . Beirut is still mending its war-ravaged infrastructure . Temporary electrical outages occur across the city several times a day . <p> Lebanon 's civil war from 1975 to 1990 claimed more than 100,000 lives as the country became consumed by fear , religious prejudice , ethnic hatred and violence . Military occupations by Israel and Syria further tore apart the nation 's fabric . An Islamic Jihad bombing in 1983 of barracks in Beirut housing U.S. Marines and French peacekeeping forces claimed 299 lives . Amid this carnage , Israel -- which in the early 1980s raided Palestinian militias in Lebanon 's refugee camps -- occupied southern Lebanon until 2000 . Syria controlled portions of Lebanon until 2005 . And too , a massive explosion of ammonium nitrate in 2020 at Beirut 's port caused more than 200 fatalities , 7,000 injuries , $15 billion in damage , displaced 300,000 people and blew out windows six miles away . Lebanon 's political power-sharing pact empowers leaders from both of these faiths to oversee this fragile nation . Political posters framed by plexiglass on advertising billboards herald Lebanon 's coalition leaders . The unity government is not without its woes . Some of Beirut 's suburbs , along with portions of southern Lebanon , are controlled by Hezbollah , a Shia Islamist political party aligned with Hamas terrorists in Gaza who massacred some 1,200 Israeli citizens and foreign nationals in October 2023 . Israel has tried in the past year to drive Hezbollah out of Lebanon , and many Lebanese support this goal . <p> But it 's also a city of seaside views , stylish cafes and bountiful vegetation -- olive and fruit trees , bougainvillea vines , Persian cyclamen flowers and other tropical blooms .
