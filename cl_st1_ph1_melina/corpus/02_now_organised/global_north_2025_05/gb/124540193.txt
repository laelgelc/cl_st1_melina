<p> When six young women gathered in central London to discuss the climate crisis and the war in Gaza , the setting could not have been more appropriate . The building in which they sat was a Quaker meeting house , the home of a movement whose centuries-long history is rooted in protest and a commitment to social justice . On the table were cups of jasmine tea , ginger biscuits and a selection of vegan cheese straws . <p> But the events that brought this apparently convivial gathering to an abrupt end have sparked protests of a different kind and raised questions about how justice is administered by the UK 's largest and most embattled police force . <p> Talk among the youth activists that evening had turned to the 1963 Children 's March in Birmingham , Alabama , when a flash of blue light interrupted the chatter . Seconds later up to 30 Metropolitan police officers , some armed with stun guns , smashed down the door of the Grade II-listed building and arrested the young women inside . <p> The Quaker meeting house in London .
