It does not necessarily reflect the view of The Herald . <p> IN today 's landscape of geopolitical conflict and cultural accountability , there is a stark contrast between the consequences faced by individuals for harmful speech and the impunity with which states enact large-scale violence . Nowhere is this more apparent than in discourse surrounding Israel and Gaza since October 2023 . <p> Recently , BBC presenter Gary Lineker retweeted a post critical of Zionism that included a harmful antisemitic image . Upon realising this , he deleted the tweet and offered an unequivocal apology . Despite this , some cast him as antisemitic . <p> Meanwhile , senior members of the Israeli government make no apologies , no retractions , and show no signal that the ongoing mass displacement and killing of Palestinians is anything but intended . It must instead be seen through the lens of power and discourse -- concepts articulated by postmodern thinker Michel Foucault . <p> Foucault argued that power is not only exercised through institutions , but through the framing of truth itself : who may speak , which narratives are legitimised , and what forms of violence are normalised . In this context , an individual 's mistake -- even when corrected -- is publicly condemned , while a state 's catastrophic actions are cloaked in legitimacy by diplomatic backing and media framing . <p> One of the clearest examples of this is the weaponisation of antisemitism allegations . Antisemitism is real , dangerous , and demands serious resistance . Yet the term is increasingly used not to protect Jewish communities , but to shield Israeli state policy from critique . This conflation undermines the fight against actual antisemitism while enabling atrocities to proceed behind a smokescreen of historical trauma . Palestinian moderates , Israeli human rights activists , and Jewish anti-Zionists are discredited or dismissed . In their place , polarising voices dominate -- whether from Zionist camps that merge theology with nationalism , or anti-Zionist corners that mirror the same zero-sum logic . The middle ground still exists , but it is starved of space and breath . <p> This erosion of proportionality mirrors the logic of totalitarian regimes : a world where language is twisted so power appears just and dissent is framed as threat . History is applied selectively . <p> Thus , we see a moral inversion : a deleted tweet becomes unforgivable , while starvation is recast as strategy . Apology is weakness ; impunity is resolve . Accountability is demanded of the powerless , while the powerful are permitted to act without restraint or remorse . <p> This is why we must go beyond balance . We need a re-examination of how power is framed and resisted . This is not a conflict of equals .
