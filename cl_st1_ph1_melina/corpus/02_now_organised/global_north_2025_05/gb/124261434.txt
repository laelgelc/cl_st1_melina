<p> For 30 years , Keith McNally was the king of New York restaurants . A working-class boy from the East End of London who moved to the city hoping to become an actor , he found his calling in hospitality in the mid-1970s . <p> Acting took a back seat as he opened a series of era-defining restaurants : Odeon , Balthazar , Lucky Strike , Minetta Tavern . They were bustling , informal places inspired by the great Parisian brasseries , where the cuisine was secondary to the vibe . New Yorkers -- old , young , famous , not-so-famous -- flocked to them . By 2004 , TheNew York Times could describe McNally as " the restaurateur who invented downtown " . He was a millionaire many times over . The rich and famous vied for his favour and feared his disapproval . Rivals around the world looked on with envy . <p> " Keith takes the greatest joy from seeing people happy , from people seeing his restaurants being the canvas on which people paint their lives , " says Jeremy King , the hospitality legend @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ The Park .
