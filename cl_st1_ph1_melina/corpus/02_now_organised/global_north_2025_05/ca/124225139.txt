<h> Controversial protest bylaw left in limbo after city council adjourns <p> ' Democracy is under siege , ' one delegate warned , as deep divisions emerged during public input on a potential law to limit protests near religious sites - leading to an early end of Oakville 's council meeting <p> With 30 delegates signed up to comment on a possible bylaw , Mayor Rob Burton initially took a heavy-handed approach to keeping speakers away from comments on world affairs , including the situation in Gaza . <p> " We 're discussing the bylaw , we 're not discussing the war , " he said , as he silenced microphones and argued with delegates . <p> But after a few delegations , he appeared to give up the battle , allowing speakers to deliver prepared remarks . <p> A deep division emerged , with delegates split between those seeking greater protection for religious institutions under a possible bylaw , and those wanting no bylaw at all . <p> That division reflected what the town heard in public surveys earlier this year , where about 60 per cent of residents @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ per cent said it is not necessary . <p> Representatives from Oakville 's Hindu and Jewish communities told councillors they would like to see a protection zone of 100 metres around places of worship , significantly larger than the 20 to 50 metres proposed in various forms of the bylaw . <p> That viewpoint was echoed by Richard Robertson , director of research and advocacy with B'Nai Brith Canada . <p> He said the congregation at Oakville 's Shaarei-Beth El synagogue was recently forced to seek a peace bond to put an end to nine months of " intimidating harassment " from a protester targeting people attempting to attend services . <p> But councillors also heard from several delegates arguing that peaceful protests are an essential part of democracy . <p> " When the right to protest is under attack , democracy is under siege , " said Samreen Hasan , who described the bylaw as authoritarian
