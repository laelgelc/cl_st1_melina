<h> Mark Carney was right to stand up to Benjamin Netanyahu <p> Michael Byers holds the Canada Research Chair in global politics and international law at the University of British Columbia . <p> During the recent federal election campaign , Prime Minister Mark Carney embraced a hockey term : elbows up . It describes a defensive stance that also signals a readiness to fight back -- and during the campaign , it was about opposing U.S. President Donald Trump 's tariffs and his aspirations to make Canada the " 51st state . " <p> Now that his Liberals have been returned to government , Mr. Carney has decided to play offence in global politics , by picking a fight with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu . <p> On Monday , Mr. Carney joined French President Emmanuel Macron and British Prime Minister Keir Starmer in condemning the intensified bombing of Gaza and the continued blocking of all humanitarian aid , while reasserting support for a ceasefire and a two-state solution . " <p> These strong words represent a departure from the policies of Justin Trudeau 's government , which muted its criticism of Israel . <p> To be clear : Mr. Carney did not need to join the French and British leaders in their statement . Unlike his meeting with Mr. Trump , he could have watched this play out from the bench . <p> So why did he pick this fight ? <p> Taking part in the statement signals that Canadian foreign policy is becoming more independent from the United States , and closer to that of our major European allies . It 's a timely message , as the Carney government seeks to diversify Canada 's trade , investment and military procurement . <p> Taking part also represents a claim to international influence . Up to 100 more trucks are reportedly on their way . <p> But thousands more truckloads will be needed if widespread starvation is to be averted .
