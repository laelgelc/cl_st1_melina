<p> A new program introduced to provide security services to election candidates was used by nearly two dozen people during the last federal campaign , according to the government department that administered it . <p> Privy Council Office spokesperson Daniel Savoie said in a statement that 22 candidates accessed its security program . The initiative , which was first announced in April , offered private-sector security at events and at home to candidates who applied for the help . <p> Candidates had access to an unarmed security guard while they attended campaign events and during daily activities . They could also have a guard monitor their residence and report any serious incidents to local police . <p> Of the 22 candidates who accessed the program , Savoie said , 15 had close protection services and two had residential security services .
