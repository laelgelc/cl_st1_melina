<p> Ukraine has finally signed its minerals agreement with the US . The deal states that Washington will eventually receive a share of the profits from the sale of Ukrainian natural resources , providing an economic incentive to continue investing in Ukraine 's defense and reconstruction . <p> On the surface , there is nothing surprising about the deal . The idea that natural resource extraction can play a role in building peace has been around for a decade or two , and has been promoted by the World Bank , the UN and the mining industry itself . <p> But what is surprising is how the conversation about mining and peace has changed . It used to be about increasing prosperity in war-torn countries , rather than the " who gets what " that has been associated with this deal . <p> The idea that mining can contribute to peace emerged somewhat paradoxically from the demonstrated capacity of natural resources to drive conflict in places like Afghanistan , the Democratic Republic of Congo ( DRC ) and Sierra Leone .
