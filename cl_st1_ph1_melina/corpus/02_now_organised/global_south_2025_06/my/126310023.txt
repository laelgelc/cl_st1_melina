<p> JUNE 25 -- Eighty years ago this month , the Charter of the United Nations was signed in San Francisco , turning the page on decades of war and offering hope for a better future . For 80 years , the United Nations has stood as the highest expression of our hopes for international cooperation , and as the fullest embodiment of our aspiration to end the " scourge of war . " Even in a world steeped in cynicism , this is a milestone worth acknowledging . <p> The United Nations remains the only organisation of its kind , and the only one to have endured for so long . That longevity is remarkable when we consider the context of its founding : assembled from the rubble of not one , but two global cataclysms . Its predecessor , the League of Nations , had collapsed in disgrace . <p> No organisation is flawless . But to paraphrase the second Secretary-General , Dag Hammarskj ? ld : the United Nations was created not to take humanity to heaven but to save us from hell . The recent escalation between Iran and Israel is a stark reminder of the fragility of peace , particularly in the tension-prone Middle East region . <p> Yet amid the violence , we have managed to avert a third global war . In a nuclear age , that is an achievement we can never take for granted . It is one we must preserve with the full force of our efforts . <p> Over the past eight decades , much of human development also bears the direct imprint of the United Nations . Consider the success of the Millennium Development Goals , adopted in 2000 by 189 Member States and more than 20 international organisations , which gave the world a shared roadmap for action . <p> By 2015 , compared to 1990 , extreme poverty had been more than halved . Child mortality had fallen by nearly 50 percent . And millions of children -- especially girls who had long been denied the right -- had entered school for the first time . <p> Now @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , we must build on that legacy of progress . We must continue efforts to eradicate poverty and hunger , achieve universal health coverage , and produce and consume sustainably . <p> There is another story of progress , often overlooked : the dismantling of the empire . Eighty years ago , colonialism cast its shadow over much of the world . Today , more than 80 former colonies across Asia , Africa , the Caribbean , and the Pacific have gained independence and joined the United Nations . That transition , supported and legitimised by this Organisation , reshaped the global order . The United Nations has always shown its worth in times of crisis . Its founders had witnessed humanity at its most destructive and responded not with despair , but with boldness . We must draw on these achievements . <p> The spirit of San Francisco was not utopian . It was grounded in a sober understanding of what was at stake . It held that , even amid deep division , nations could still choose cooperation over conflict and action over apathy . <p> We saw that spirit last September , when world leaders gathered in New York for the Summit of the Future .
