<h> Tech Terror <p> First in Gaza , and then during Israel 's reckless aggression against Iran , the role of technology -- particularly the influence of US-based tech oligarchs -- has come sharply into focus . It was first revealed that Amazon is providing cloud services to the Israeli Defence Forces ( IDF ) , enabling real-time targeting capabilities for Israeli warplanes . Soon after , it emerged that WhatsApp , the Meta-owned messaging app , is being used by Israeli forces to identify and track targets . Meta , which also owns Facebook and Instagram , appears to be providing significant volumes of user data to aid this process . <p> In Iran 's case , reports suggested that WhatsApp played a critical role in locating Iranian leadership figures prior to their targeted assassinations . Now , ironically , the US Congress has banned its members from using WhatsApp -- reportedly to protect them from surveillance and data exploitation via the very same app . <p> In a digital ecosystem dominated by monopolies like Facebook , Google , Amazon , and Microsoft , the illusion of choice grows @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ being exposed as dangerous dependency . And this is no longer just a Western concern -- it is now an existential question for the Global South . <p> Pakistan must urgently reconsider its reliance on communication tools owned and controlled by foreign tech giants . Our government and military data -- our national security infrastructure -- should not be exposed to entities with ambiguous loyalties or opaque data-sharing practices . We have already seen how easily private WhatsApp chats and audio files involving public figures can be leaked or misused . <p> If anything , this is a moment for Pakistan , and for developing countries at large , to invest in secure , locally developed alternatives to WhatsApp -- tools that are not beholden to US interests and can not be co-opted in future geopolitical conflicts . Our sovereignty may well depend on it .
