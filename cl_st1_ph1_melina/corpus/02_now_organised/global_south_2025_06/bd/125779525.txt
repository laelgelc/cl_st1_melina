But in the Gaza Strip , where starvation has been deliberately weaponised , this moral imperative has been grotesquely subverted . Its food distributions are inconsistent and often restricted to arbitrarily chosen zones . In short , it adapts to the structures of deprivation rather than challenging them . <p> This distortion is not merely bureaucratic mismanagement ; it is the logical outgrowth of a system that instrumentalises humanitarianism . The Gaza Humanitarian Foundation is embedded in this regime of control . It decides who eats , when , and how much -- not based on humanitarian need , but on political expediency . And when thousands of starving Palestinians frantically converge on the few permitted distribution points , many are gunned down under the pretext that they pose a threat to Israeli forces lying in wait . Humanitarian aid , by definition , is supposed to be impartial , independent , and based on need . But in Gaza , these principles have been eroded beyond recognition . Aid has become anti-political , refusing to name the aggressor , refusing to call out hunger as a weapon , refusing to demand accountability .
