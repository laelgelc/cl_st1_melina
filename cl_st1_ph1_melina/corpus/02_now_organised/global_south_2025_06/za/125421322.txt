<h> OPINION <h> Fixing the ANC 's geopolitical missteps <p> Shawn Hagedorn says there are no scenarios where SA can spur meaningful job creation through alignment with China , Russia or Iran <p> The ANC 's support for Russia , Iran , and Hamas has n't triggered observable consequences as much as it has sacrificed much-needed opportunities . <p> By scuppering peace talks with Ukraine , Russian President Vladimir Putin encourages US and European leaders to aggressively pressure countries which aid Russia . While South Africa 's trade with Russia is meagre , the ANC favours its BRICS partners , most notably China , Iran , and Russia , while disdaining the West . This is despite the far greater job creation potential from exporting to Western nations than to BRICS members . <p> The ANC 's foreign relations policies serve the interests of ordinary South Africans as ineffectively as the party 's economic policies . The leaders of Saudi Arabia and the UAE have agreed with their US counterpart , and the heads of various top-tier technology companies , to build massive AI data centres in those two key Middle Eastern countries . This contrasts starkly with the ANC 's misguided economic stewardship and foreign alignments . <p> Advancing commercial interests <p> Nearly four out of ten people in the world were poor as recently as 1980 ; today , fewer than one out of ten are . At least 70% of the gains were in Asia . Africa 's liberation movements ousted colonial governments but economic success and effective political representation often remain elusive . <p> The African renaissance touted amid 1990s idealism did n't materialise . The ANC advocated for trade among African countries rather than targeting wealthier regions - as is common among high growth countries . <p> Twin focus <p> --&gt; <p> " **27;2083;TOOLONG " is how social scientists describe debates between what-ought-to-be versus what the evidence supports . Their challenges are not simpler than ours , yet they are exploring boldly . <p> Other national leaders can see that ANC leaders favour party interests over those of ordinary South Africans . This makes the ANC a less reliable , and ultimately , a less valuable partner - as both our economy and political calm look unstable . Unfortunately , however , this path offers sparse potential , directly or indirectly , to create jobs . <p> Building new core competencies <p> The Saudis did not restrict their growth plan to paths that draw upon their core competencies . Rather , building AI data centres is a growth area where they can compete by developing new core competencies . Their competitive advantages include : political stability , reliable and cheap solar energy alongside abundant fossil fuel reserves , and being a regional and digital hub connected to 16 subsea cables . <p> As ANC leaders frequently tout beneficiation , they would have favoured building more refineries . But the Saudis know they need to diversify away from oil , not double down . Hence they are aligning their national economy with a top global growth sector . Such trust building and integrating into global supply chains are central to sustaining high-volume job creation . <p> There are no scenarios where South Africa can spur meaningful job creation through domestic growth or alignment with China , Russia or Iran . Unless ANC leaders want to risk the wrath of a jobs-prioritising electorate , they should pivot their foreign relations to build strong western ties .
