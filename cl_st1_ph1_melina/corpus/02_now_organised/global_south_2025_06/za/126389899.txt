<h> OPINION <h> A rare opportunity for SA -- and Israel <p> William Saunderson-Meyer <p> 27 June 2025 <p> William Saunderson-Meyer on the foreign policy benefits of the neutralisation of the Iranian threat <p> JAUNDICED EYE <p> The Israel-Iran war lasted only 12 days but its consequences will shape global politics for years . One positive is that the battering taken by our ally Iran -- following closely on the heels of the collapse of Syria -- could paradoxically greatly benefit South Africa , if President Cyril Ramaphosa was courageous enough to seize the opportunity . <p> The biggest impediment to a good relationship with the United States has been SA 's support of Iran , a nation whose antipathy towards the US ( the ' Great Satan ' ) and Israel ( the ' Little Satan ' ) , drives virtually everything that this repressive theocracy does . While it has been difficult for Iran to challenge directly the military and economic might of the US , Israel has been acutely vulnerable to its near neighbour Iran 's decades-long determination to destroy it . <p> Iran takes a ' @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ statehood , rejecting Israel 's right to exist and plotting the obliteration of the Jewish state . As Iran 's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei puts it unashamedly , Israel is a ' malignant cancerous tumour that has to be removed and will be removed ' . Hence the oil-rich regime 's hell-or-high-water pursuit of a ' peaceful ' nuclear programme , which SA has defended at every turn . <p> Iran was also the primary supplier of money and arms to proxy terrorist groups operating against Israel . Hezbollah in Lebanon could not survive without Iran , nor could Palestinian Islamic Jihad ( PIJ ) in Gaza and the West Bank . While Hamas conceivably could continue to operate without Iranian backing in Gaza , it would be as a much weakened political entity and its military threat to Israel would be negligible . <p> It is hardly surprising , then , that SA 's increasing alignment with Iran 's ' axis of resistance ' , as well as its self-appointed role as leading spokesperson for the Global South in siding with Russia and China against the Western democracies @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ past dozen or so years . This came to a head in February under the combative presidency of Donald Trump , who issued his punishing executive order against SA , largely because of our ' egregious actions ' in taking ' aggressive positions towards the United States and its allies ' and ' reinvigorating its relations with Iran to develop commercial , military and nuclear arrangements ' . <p> --&gt; <p> The executive order , which summarily terminated trade concessions and aid , will deliver a hard blow -- though ultimately recoverable -- to what remains of our manufacturing sector . More seriously , it posed a potentially fatal threat to our knowledge economy . While trade partners can eventually be replaced , nobody is clamouring to step up and gift billions to support the medical and scientific research that has kept SA 's better universities internationally competitive and , in the field of infectious diseases , globally pre-eminent . <p> Despite these substantial economic costs and President Cyril Ramaphosa speaking fervently about the need to ' reset ' ties with the US -- even claiming , unconvincingly , that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Trump -- the ANC-led unity government has been paralysed with inaction . No new ambassador to Washington has been appointed to replace Ebrahim Rasool , who was declared persona non grata in March . Nearly three months after his appointment as Ramaphosa 's special envoy , Mcebisi Jonas -- chairman of MTN Group that faces US litigation over alleged sanctions violations and support for terrorist organisations through its operations in Iran -- has yet to set foot in America . <p> There are several explanations for this unwillingness or inability to act . However , it boils down to the fact that foreign policy is not determined by pragmatism and national interest , but by ideology and party interest . The foundation of SA 's post-1994 risky friendship with Iran stems from an anti-Western ideological outlook that the ANC has historically shared . This antipathy has been amplified by a powerful Islamist clique within the Department of International Relations and Cooperation that is strong enough within ANC party structures to dictate a far harder line on Middle East matters than some in the Cabinet would like . <p> At least @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is no direct evidence , it is widely believed in Western circles that Iran has at times surreptitiously funded the ANC -- much in the same way as Libyan leader Muammar Gaddafi bought SA 's diplomatic support with large secret donations to the party and its leaders , as former ANC treasurer-general Mathews Phosa documents in his recent memoir . Such political opportunism by a corrupt ANC also has other potential Iranian outlets : access to discounted oil , arms cooperation , and the expansion of nuclear capacity at Koeberg if , in the words of Mineral Affairs and Energy Minister Gwede Mantashe , they ' make the best offer ' . <p> --&gt; <p> The drubbing that Iran has taken in the 12-day war changes everything . Iran 's new precariousness as a functional state , its faltering status as a revolutionary beacon , and its more circumscribed financial circumstances reduce its usefulness to the ANC on all fronts . While institutional habits and ideological inertia may persist , the rational incentives underpinning the relationship have eroded . <p> Whether its nuclear programme has been destroyed , as Trump @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ critics claim , Iran undoubtedly will have reduced capacity for mischief-making in the Middle East while preoccupied with internal reconstruction . Infrastructure has to be rebuilt , lost nuclear and military expertise replaced , and weaponry stocks replenished , all while fretting whether the Ayatollah 's government might be toppled in a popular uprising . <p> As importantly , Iran 's vulnerabilities have been exposed , and its confidence has been shaken . Israel effortlessly thwarted Iran 's much-vaunted missile barrages , while the US demonstrated decisively that it would not sit idle while Tehran lashed out . <p> Regionally , any future attempts to rearm Hezbollah , Hamas , or PIJ will trigger a ferocious Israeli response . The illusion that Iran can act with impunity has been shattered , to the quiet satisfaction of much of the Middle East , including Saudi Arabia , the United Arab Emirates , and even Iran 's nominal ally , Qatar . <p> --&gt; <p> The loop of perpetual Middle East conflict has , for now , been broken . Iran 's terror proxies are isolated , vulnerable , and on the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the strategic initiative lies firmly with Israel . <p> Militarily , the Jewish state has never been stronger , but it is being comprehensively bested on the battlefield of international public opinion over the conflict in Gaza . With Iran momentarily cowed and Syria making kissy noises , this is Israel 's best window yet to resolve the Palestinian question . This is the chance to trade restraint for recognition and to convert tactical dominance into a durable regional peace through revitalised and expanded Abraham Accords . <p> South Africa faces its own fork in the road . It can continue acting as a megaphone for radical Islam and a cheerleader for rogue states or it , too , can seize the moment . Re-establishing ties with Israel , mending its fractured relationship with Washington , and offering support in the quest for lasting peace in the Middle East would not only serve our national interest , it would rescue a South African foreign policy that under Ramaphosa 's timorous captaincy is adrift in an ANC sea of moral incoherence.
