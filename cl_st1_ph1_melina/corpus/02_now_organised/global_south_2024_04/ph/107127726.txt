<h> US military says it destroyed Houthi drones over Red Sea and in Yemen <p> CAIRO -- The US military said Sunday its forces destroyed one unmanned aerial vehicle in a Houthi rebel-held area of war-ravaged Yemen and another over a crucial shipping route in the Red Sea . It was the latest development in months of tension between the Iran-backed rebels and the US . <p> The drones , which were destroyed Saturday morning , posed a threat to US and coalition forces and merchant vessels in the region , said the US Central Command . <p> It said that one drone was destroyed over the Red Sea , while the second was destroyed on the ground as it was prepared to launch . <p> " These actions are necessary to protect our forces , ensure freedom of navigation , and make international waters safer and more secure for US , coalition , and merchant vessels , " CENTCOM said . <p> There was no comment from the Houthi rebels , which control much of Yemen 's north and west . <p> The rebels launched a campaign of drone and missile attacks on shipping in the Red Sea @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , although those have largely fallen short or been intercepted . <p> The rebels have described their campaign as an effort to pressure Israel to end its war on Hamas in the Gaza Strip . The ships targeted by the Houthis , however , largely have had little or no connection to Israel , the US or other nations involved in the war . <p> The Houthis have kept up their campaign of attacks despite more than two months of US-led airstrikes . <p> Earlier this month , CENTCOM said its forces also destroyed four unmanned aerial vehicles in Houthi-controlled areas of Yemen . It also said Houthis fired four anti-ship ballistic missiles toward the Red Sea , but no injuries or damages were reported by US , coalition or commercial ships . <p> The escalation in the Red Sea and the Israel-Hamas war in Gaza impacted the U.N.-led efforts to relaunch political talks to end Yemen 's yearslong conflict , according to the U.N . envoy for Yemen . <p> Hans Grundberg told the U.N .
