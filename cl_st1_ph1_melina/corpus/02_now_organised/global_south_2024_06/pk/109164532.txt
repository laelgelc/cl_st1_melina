<p> MANILA/LONDON : The crew of a Greek-owned vessel damaged in an attack by Yemeni Houthi has been evacuated and the abandoned ship is drifting in the Red Sea , the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations and a Philippine minister said . <p> The search for one missing sailor will continue and there is a plan to start salvaging operations for the Tutor , the Liberia-flagged coal carrier , Hans Leo Cacdac , the Philippines ' migrant workers minister said on Saturday . He said the vessel 's 22 crew members were all Filipinos . <p> " It just boils down to finding our seafarer who is still onboard , " Cacdac told a press conference in Manila . <p> The attack near the Yemeni port of Hodeidah on Wednesday caused severe flooding and damage to the engine room and left the Tutor unable to maneuver . It was the third Houthi attack on a ship manned by Filipino seafarers since last year , with two Philippine sailors dying and 17 still held by Houthis , government data show . <p> Houthis claimed responsibility for the missile strike on Tutor and another vessel , Verbena , in the Gulf @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ also damaged two other ships in the last week , " marking a significant increase in effectiveness , " British security firm Ambrey said . <p> The Houthis have used drones and missiles to assault ships in the Red Sea , the Bab al-Mandab Strait and the Gulf of Aden since November , saying they are acting in solidarity with Palestinians in the Gaza war . They have sunk one ship , seized another vessel and killed three seafarers in separate attacks . <p> The missing crew member was believed to be trapped in the engine room , maritime sources and the Philippines ' migrant workers ministry said . <p> Tutor is not sinking and can be safely towed away , Cacdac said after a meeting with the ship 's manning agency .
