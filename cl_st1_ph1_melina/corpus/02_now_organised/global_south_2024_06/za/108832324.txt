<h> 90% of the world 's most neglected crises are in Africa , says Norwegian Refugee Council <p> Nigeriens holding a flag of Burkina Faso ( L ) and Niger and a sign reading " Together we will make it " . ( Djibo Issifou/picture alliance via Getty Images ) <p> Burkina Faso is the worst crisis in the world for the second time running , according to the Norwegian Refugee Council . <p> Nine of the ten neglected crisis hotspots are in Africa , with one in Central America . <p> The neglect is due to a lack of media coverage , no political will , and low donor attention . <p> Ninety percent of the world 's most neglected crises for 2023 were in the Sahel and Central African regions , with Burkina Faso being the worst for the second time in a row , according to the Norwegian Refugee Council ( NRC ) . <p> Cameroon ranked second , then Democratic Republic of the Congo ( DRC ) , Mali , and Niger , before the only non-African state on the list , Honduras in Central America . <p> The top 10 is rounded out by @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Republic , Chad and Sudan . <p> The NRC classified neglected crisis hotspots as areas where there is a lack of media coverage , where policymakers do not prioritise finding answers , and where donor nations do not provide sufficient funding . <p> " Often the countries that top our list are of little geopolitical interest to world powers , " the organisation said in a statement . <p> Other , arguably worse-hit crisis areas in the world receive attention , such as Israel 's war on Gaza and Russia 's invasion of Ukraine . <p> According to Uppsala University 's Conflict Data Program ( UCD ) , the war in Ukraine saw 71 000 deaths last year , and Israel 's war on Hamas recorded over 22 000 deaths in 2023 , though that number has risen substantially since . <p> UCD notes that most wars took place in Africa , with the civil war in Sudan that broke out in 2023 being the third-deadliest conflict of 2023 . <p> " Those in power need to show the same humanity towards people affected by crises in places such as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " said NRC 's secretary-general Jan Egeland . <p> Media blackout <p> The NRC pointed out that crisis cases get a lot of attention only when their root causes or origins had a direct impact on the developed world . <p> NRC argued the reason was that " this is often tied to how much political attention a crisis gets -- if that is lacking , the media may be less likely to cover a crisis " . <p> One given example was the influx of refugees into Europe around 2015 . <p> The influx resulted in the European media getting interested in what was happening in countries such as Syria where the refugees were coming from . <p> " When we know someone who has fled from a crisis , we are more likely to care about it , " NRC said . <p> Funding neglect <p> The NRC said neglect was a choice made by decision-makers because the details of what is needed are all there for everyone to see . <p> " Neglect is a choice -- that millions of displaced people are cast aside year after @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is not inevitable , " said Egeland . <p> Egeland urged some of the world 's leading private entities and the donor community to come together to plug the funding gap , which is 57% of the crisis needs not met . <p> " We urgently need investment for the world 's most neglected crises .
