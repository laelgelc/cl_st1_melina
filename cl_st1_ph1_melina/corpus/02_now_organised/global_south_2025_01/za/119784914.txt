<h> Chinese UAVs finding a large market in Africa <p> If the United States have made unmanned aerial vehicles ( UAVs , or drones ) mainstream through their extensive use of such armed aircraft in their wars in Afghanistan , Iraq or Somalia , among others , it is surprisingly not the States that commands the lion 's share of the market . <p> China has become the world 's biggest exporter of UAVs , both armed and unarmed . In the last decade , the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute ( SIPRI ) reported that CASC , CAIG and other important Chinese UAV manufacturers sold 282 combat drones to 17 countries , while the United States only traded 12 armed UAVs . <p> Beijing successfully usurped Washington in this domain , producing an extensive array of drones since the early 2010s . Affordable , often reliable and free from ethical constraints , they are starting to become a real best-seller in Africa . With a price tag of $1 million , the Wing Loong I can carry two munitions , usually guided-bombs or missiles , enabling governments to track , observe , and conducts strikes all over their territory . <p> In the last several years , and in Africa only , it was Algeria ( 24 UAVs ) , Morocco ( 14 UAVs ) , Democratic Republic of Congo ( 9 UAVs ) , Egypt ( 4 UAVs ) , Nigeria ( 3 UAVs ) and Ethiopia ( unknown ) that have been seduced by Chinese UAVs . <p> It is often the cheaper CH-4 and Wing Loong I , comparable to the American Predator , which are purchased , the few more capable Wing Loong II present in the continent , deployed in Libya for example , are often donations from @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , which opted for the latter UAV to participate in its fight against Boko Haram , a jihadist movement plaguing Africa 's most populated country . Abuja was one of the first to buy Chinese UAVs , starting with smaller tactical CH-3s before 2014 . <p> CH-4 unmanned aerial vehicles . <p> As most armed groups are short on air defence , UAVs can sometimes have a decisive impact on a conflict , the best example being Ethiopia . Foreign powers started to ask their citizens to evacuate when drones entered into action , destroying columns of pick-ups and tanks only 200 km away from the city . Although it appears the UAVs were also Turkish ( Bayraktar TB2 ) and Iranian ( Mohajer 6 ) , and not only Chinese ( Wing Loong I ) , they proved their value . But these are expensive to buy , to operate and to maintain .
