A widening CAD will fuel domestic inflation by raising imported crude oil prices . This could have secondary effects , including a decline in forex reserves and FIIs pulling out of the stock market . <p> Depreciation of the rupee against the dollar is not recent . An analysis of long-term data suggests that the average annual depreciation of the rupee was 0.4% between 2000 and 2004 . Much of the differential in inflation rates between the US and India can be explained by labour productivity . A look at major tradable export items suggests these are income-elastic - they tend to perform well when there is an upsurge in foreign income . In case of India , there is a change in composition of exports from price-sensitive items , like leather footwear , dairy products and beverages , to more income-sensitive items , such as refined petroleum products , iron and steel , chemicals and machinery . <p> For example , the share of refined petroleum products in export basket increased from around 2% in 1993 to about 21% in 2023 . In fact , India is now the second-largest exporter of refined petroleum in the world , with exports valued at $85 bn and a global market share of 12.6% . <p> Although the weakening rupee helped improve the trade balance between 2000 and 2014 , evidence of such a relationship has become increasingly weak in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a weakening rupee will increase India 's trade deficit may not be accurate . <p> This relationship between exchange rate and merchandise trade deficit is becoming irrelevant due to the shift in Indian exports from price-sensitive to income-sensitive items . A prolonged Russia-Ukraine war and the onset of weak global economic growth , especially in the Eurozone , led to lower demand for income-elastic items , which make up a significant portion of India 's export basket . <p> In China , India 's largest trading partner , GDP growth rate is likely to slow down from 8.1% in 2021 to below 5% in 2025 . Trump 's tariffs could pose a challenge for India 's exports , too . Tariffs may also reduce the US trade deficit , potentially leading to further appreciation of the dollar . <p> On the flip side , Trump facilitating end of the war in Gaza and potentially resolving the Russia-Ukraine conflict , could lead to softer crude prices , higher global income and a positive impact on our income-sensitive exports .
