<p> By Joseph TullochThe Assembly of Catholic Ordinaries of the Holy Land has welcomed the announcement of a ceasefire in Gaza , and expressed its hope that the truce will put a permanent end to the " immeasurable suffering " caused by 15 months of war . <p> In a press release , the Assembly -- which is composed of all the Catholic bishops , eparchs and exarchs holding jurisdiction in Jerusalem , Palestine , Israel , Jordan , and Cyprus -- did , however , stress that " the end of the war does not mean the end of the conflict " . <p> What is needed , the Catholic Ordinaries said , is a " long process " to address the " deep-rooted issues " at the heart of the conflict . <p> In this regard , their press release called on the international community to " develop a clear and just political vision for the post-war period " . <p> Pilgrims and the Holy YearThe Ordinaries also said that they " eagerly await the return of pilgrims to the Holy Places " . <p> Christian pilgrims usually @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Israel , but since the outbreak of war in October 2023 they have almost entirely disappeared -- with disastrous economic consequences for local economies dependent on tourism . <p> Finally , the Ordinaries also referenced the Church 's 2025 Jubilee Year , whose theme is ' Pilgrims of hope ' . <p> At the beginning of a Jubilee Year " dedicated to hope that does not disappoint , " the Ordinaries wrote , they read the ceasefire as " a sign that reminds us of God 's faithfulness.
