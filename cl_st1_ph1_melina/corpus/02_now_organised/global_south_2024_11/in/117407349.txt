This article examines whether gender is as significant a schism in US politics as race , analyzing why Kamala Harris lost the 2020 election . It explores the double bind female candidates face , the impact of social conservatism , and the role of disinformation , concluding that a woman president in the US is unlikely in the coming decades . <p> Still blinking at the female gaze : 47th US president Claire Underwood in House of Cards <h> Sonika Gupta <p> In 2014 , two years into the first Obama administration , comedian Chris Rock said that Barack Obama getting elected as president was n't progress for Black Americans . It was progress for White folks as there had always been Black people worthy of the job . It was a telling statement of US politics and of one of its foundational schisms : race . <p> Seeing the second failed presidential campaign of a female candidate , can we now conclude that gender is another significant schism ? Multiple factors are responsible for Democrats not being able to convince the swing vote . They failed to respond to the increasing sense of abandonment of the working class that has permeated to Black and Latino groups . Crucial unions , historically a Democrat-voting bloc , failed to endorse Harris , unconvinced by her rhetoric on middle-class tax cuts . <p> To compound this , Harris seemed reluctant to sufficiently chalk out an economic programme different from an underperforming Biden administration . Foreign policy factors - such as failure to respond in any manner to the genocide in Gaza , and the ineffective US stance on the war in Ukraine - could possibly have had an adverse impact on the overall public faith in another Democrat administration . <p> Of course , there was never any hope of building much traction on polarising issues of immigration , gender identity and abortion . But two accomplished women having lost to a candidate who normalises sexualising women , was recorded boasting about groping women , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ pause for thought . Significantly , the two female-led presidential campaigns come before and after Trump 's loss to Joe Biden , a male opponent , who was n't viewed as someone offering any new policy agenda . So , where did the women candidates fall short ? <p> Hillary Clinton 's 2016 campaign was saddled right from the get-go with a ' likability ' problem revolving around whether she was likable enough , as a woman , to contest . Both Clinton and Harris have suffered from the double-bind of demonstrating they are assertive yet nurturing , decisive yet compassionate , unemotional yet empathetic . This conundrum is exclusively for female candidates and permeates all levels of leadership across politics , business , academia and sports . <p> It 's a standard that 's deliberately designed to fail . Women who have climbed the professional ladder know they must own their unpopularity , and accept its consequences if they are to be taken seriously . However , in politics , which is essentially a popularity contest , women will invariably hit this glass ceiling that ca n't be challenged @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ American voters hold women candidates to a different - often unachievable - standard ? The answer could partially lie in the paradox of sexual liberation and social conservatism coexisting in the US social fabric that defines its electoral politics . <p> In the 1960s sexual liberation movement , the female body became the vehicle of consumerism as well as emancipation . Emancipatory effects resulted in women taking control of their reproductive , sexual and economic choices . But the consumerist enterprise continues to undermine these choices by subjecting women to the male gaze . <p> Overturning of Roe vs Wade in 2022 was the latest salvo in a battle conservatives in the US have been winning . It 's revealing that the fight to reclaim female bodily autonomy , which Harris made a lynchpin of her campaign , won her fewer women 's votes than Biden in 2020 . She clearly failed to bring together women on the issue of abortion that fragmented women along liberal and conservative lines . <p> Democracies across the world are reeling from adverse effects of disinformation on social media . This is n't a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ political parties . Once a conspiracy theory takes hold in any political ecosystem , it seems impossible to uproot it . <p> However , the kind of conspiracies that floated around about female candidates are revealing . Both Clinton and Harris were accused of witchcraft . This is something only women can be maligned for - and have been for centuries . Like the likability issue , there can be no sensible and logical counter to this . So , the female candidate ca n't respond to it without appearing to strengthen the fallacy . <p> So , will the US ever have a woman in the Oval Office ? At this juncture , it seems that unless both parties nominate female candidates , it 's impossible .
