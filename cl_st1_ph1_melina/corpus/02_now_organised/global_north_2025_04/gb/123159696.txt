<p> Stef Wertheimer , who has died aged 98 , was the billionaire patriarch of one of Israel 's leading industrial families and a powerful advocate for peaceful co-existence between Jewish and Arab communities . <p> Wertheimer was the founder of Iscar Metalworking , one of the world 's largest manufacturers of carbide cutting tools for the automotive industry , in which he employed an integrated workforce of Jews , Arabs and Christians . In 2006 he sold 80 per cent of the business to the US investor Warren Buffett 's master company Berkshire Hathaway for $4 billion . Seven years later Buffett bought the remainder for another $2 billion , making Wertheimer one of Israel 's richest citizens . <p> " I made my money in Iscar , " he said later , " I spend my money on industrial parks . " There were seven such developments ( six in Israel , one in Turkey ) designed to bring work and prosperity to disadvantaged and divided areas . Wertheimer garnered political backing in Israel and solicited aid from the US in support of his vision of " an @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ an advanced quality of life can replace terrorism and poverty " . <p> His flagship project , and the headquarters of Iscar , was the Tefen Industrial Park in Galilee ; others followed at Nazareth and in the Negev desert . In the 1990s he advanced the idea of an industrial park at Rafah in the Gaza Strip , but the " second intifada " put paid to the scheme . <p> Stefan Wertheimer was born on July 16 1926 in the small German town of Kippenheim in Baden-W ? rttemberg , between the Black Forest and the Rhine . In 1937 the family fled the Nazi regime to settle in Mandatory Palestine , where Stef studied at Tel-Nordau school in Tel Aviv until he was 16 , then found work in a camera store . <p> In 1943 he volunteered for the Royal Air Force , serving as a technician for optical equipment . He went on to serve in the Palmach -- the strike force of Haganah , the Zionist paramilitary organisation in Palestine .
