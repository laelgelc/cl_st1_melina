<h> I hope a remake of Threads is as shocking as the original <p> This article is brought to you by our exclusive subscriber partnership with our sister title USA Today , and has been written by our American colleagues . It does not necessarily reflect the view of The Herald . <p> I 'M NOT one for remakes or sequels . <p> Believing that you should never tamper with perfection , I refuse to watch the 2016 film Dad 's Army . <p> I loved The Railway Children , but have no interest in their return , nor in updated versions of St Trinian 's - who could possibly improve on the acting talents of Alastair Sim , George Cole and the wonderful Joyce Grenfell ? <p> But there is one film that , however brilliant and irreplaceable the original was - and it was truly outstanding - I 'm right behind a remake . <p> That film is Threads . Part documentary , the film was written by Barry Hines of Kes fame and depicts a nuclear attack on Sheffield . It sets the scene with the everyday @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ elsewhere in the world , tensions between superpowers rise - pretty much like today . <p> But it escalates , and everyday scenes of local families having breakfast , starting work and shopping , suddenly move to a fight for survival for those unlucky enough to still be alive after the warhead hits . <p> I watched the film when it was first broadcast in 1984 and , decades later , could still picture the little boy bursting into the local supermarket yelling : " They 've started fighting , the Americans and the Russians - me dad says you have to come home now ! " And the elderly couple hiding in their cellar listening to intruders walking around in their bombed house . <p> Threads did n't need Hollywood blockbuster special effects - which I hope will be absent from any remake . It was all the more believable for that reason . <p> The film has been broadcast twice more since then : once in 1985 and again in 2024 to mark its 40th anniversary . Nowhere near enough . I 've written about Threads before in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ screened regularly not only in Britain but across the world , to remind us all of how close we are to nuclear annihilation , of how life as we know it could change in an instant . <p> A nuclear bomb exploding <p> The world needs constant reminders and , while it will never come close to having the impact of the original , a remake of Threads can only help the cause . It may even compel people to watch Barry Hine 's film . People need to be shaken to the core by the threat of nuclear war . <p> When Threads was first broadcast there was greater public opposition to the threat of nuclear war . In the early 1980s I took part with my student friends in various anti-war marches including a large protest at Aldermaston in Berkshire - the main site for the research , development , and production of Britain 's nuclear warheads . Just about every student wore CND ( Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament ) badges and carried CND bags : you rarely see them now . <p> The very fact that the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is terrifying in itself . Nations say they are a deterrent but the way some countries parade them through their cities as you would a carnival float does n't inspire confidence . <p> Any war is bad enough . The scenes from Ukraine and Gaza are horrific and distressing . But nuclear war is on another level . It 's off the scale . Modern nuclear weapons are significantly more powerful than the bombs used on Hiroshima and Nagasaki , with some having explosive yields hundreds or even thousands of times greater . <p> Knowing what nuclear weapons are capable of , you have to ask yourself : is mankind really so stupid as to deploy something that could cause its own extinction ?
