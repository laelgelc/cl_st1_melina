<h> 2024 's peak of antisemitic incidents across Canada weighed and measured by B'nai Brith <p> Henry Topaz flanked by Richard Robertson and David Matas in their presentation in Ottawa on April 7 , 2025 . ( Credit : CPAC ) <p> As expected , antisemitic incidents reached a historic high in Canada in 2024 , with 6,219 reported incidents including firebombing of synagogues , attacks on schools and Jewish-owned businesses , and several arrests of suspects on terror-related charges . <p> B'nai Brith Canada reported its findings at an Ottawa press conference on April 7 , as the organization released its annual antisemitism audit . <p> " These 6,219 incidents represent a 7.4 percent increase from 2023 and an inexcusable 124 percent increase from 2022 . Over the past 18 months , a previously unfathomable new baseline for the occurrence of antisemitic incidents in Canada has been established , " said B'nai Brith 's Richard Robertson . <p> " The atrocities of Oct. 7 , 2023 , opened deep fissures in Canadian society , " the director of research and advocacy said in a press release that accompanied the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a disturbing undercurrent of Jew-hatred driven by a virulent , radicalized minority . " <p> Incidents of harassment , which includes slurs , statements and systemic discrimination , rose by 58 percent since 2022 , to a total of 5,818 . ( The amount based exclusively on online harassment rose to 5,367 incidents , an increase of 161 percent from 2022 . ) <p> While 15 incidents of violence and 386 cases of vandalism were also recorded , online harassment constituted the vast majority of all reported antisemitic incidents -- totalling 86 percent . <p> Incidents increased in many regions , specifically in Quebec -- which recorded 1,651 incidents , an increase of 215 percent over 2023 -- and Alberta with 916 incidents -- an increase of 160 percent from the previous year . <p> Ontario saw the largest single number of incidents of any province , but the 1,782 reports represented a 25 percent decrease from 2023 . <p> " The Jewish community has been subject to bigotry far greater than any other religious or ethnic minority , both on a per capita basis and in absolute numbers , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in Canada is becoming normalized . " <p> The ongoing war in Gaza , triggered by the Oct. 7 Hamas attack on southern Israel , was cited as the contributing factor in the alarming growth of antisemitic attacks . <p> But this war is just of 110 global armed conflicts currently occurring , Matas pointed out . <p> " None of these 110 others present the features we see with the Israel-Hamas conflict , a global spread of bigotry , directed against those who share ethnic or religious identity with one side of the conflict . " <p> The justice system needs to become more effective in prosecuting hate crimes , says Matas , who serves as senior legal counsel for B'nai Brith . While about 30 police forces across the country have hate crime units , similar dedicated resources do n't exist in the prosecution system . <p> Matas attended the national forum on combatting antisemitism held in Ottawa last month , and noted the problems of policing and prosecuting hate crimes was discussed . <p> " What I heard informally is that police will go to the prosecutors @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ going to get a conviction , we 're not going to go ahead , so that stymies them . " <p> Understanding hate crime is a " more sophisticated " problem than for example auto theft , said Matas : " When you 're dealing with hate speech you have to understand the discourse , and its impact and its meaning and lot of it is coded and indirect . " <p> Canadian society needs to be more aggressive in combatting antisemitism , added Robertson .
