<p> On January 19 , 2025 , the people of Gaza woke to a strange stillness as the first phase of the ceasefire between Israel and Hamas took effect . My friend Mahmoud* , an Arabic language professor who lives in Gaza with his family , messaged via WhatsApp , " Finally , a new chapter of life . It feels like a new birth certificate is issued . It 's quiet now , no deafening and ominous sounds of drones , no strikes or constant fears for life . One needs to speak volumes of the horror we have lived through . I just hope we wo n't have to live through this ever again . " <p> Mahmoud and I regularly exchange messages . He shares ideas on how to slow-cook breakfast tomatoes on the balcony over woodfire -- a necessity when the cooking gas runs out . I let him know I am trialling a new Palestinian recipe and seek tips on how to get the fried onions just right for the mejadra dish of lentils , spices and grains . " My kids chat to his kids , and my son has named his PlayStation FIFA Palestine player after Mahmoud 's cute four-year-old . <p> The ceasefire brought small space to breathe and begin the incomprehensible journey of processing the depths of grief and piecing splintered lives together . My friend Mo , a doctor and poet , messaged as the ceasefire took hold , " We have to weep until we have no more tears . " For many , this time allowed pause to find and embrace those still living , to bury the dead , to go home . <p> At the end of January , Mo wrote , " I went back home for one day and night .
