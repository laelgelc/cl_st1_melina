By Romy Ash . <h> Richard Roxburgh on the job of an actor <p> Actor and director Richard Roxburgh first met Peter Greste over sandwiches during pre-production for The Correspondent . The film , which opens this month and in which Roxburgh plays Greste , is based on The First Casualty , a memoir depicting the Australian war correspondent 's headline-making incarceration in a Cairo prison in the last days of 2013 . <p> " If you 're playing someone , it 's a huge privilege to have them in the room , " says Roxburgh of their meeting , which took place in the home of producer and former foreign correspondent Carmel Travers . " I was really conscious of that . I was immediately scanning him and studying him , just in a way that I guess actors do . Not that this was going to be , in any way , an exercise in mimicry or even attempting his @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ his internal landscape , his internal struggles , the horror , the building sense of horror that went with being arrested so strangely , as he was , just filling in for a friend over Christmas in Egypt , which was a place he 'd never been to as a journalist before . But it was still really important to me to get the measure of him . " <p> Greste not only tells his own story -- he contextualises it in what he calls " a global war on journalism " . Greste was arrested in 2013 with his Al Jazeera colleagues , Egyptian -- Canadian producer Mohamed Fahmy and local cameraman Baher Mohamed . The subsequent incarceration occurred at a tense political time , after Abdel Fattah el-Sisi 's military takeover of the democratically elected Egyptian government . This tense environment was in stark contrast to the domestic setting where Roxburgh met Greste . <p> For Roxburgh , the job of an actor is about trying , as much as possible , to actualise in his own imagination the experiences of the characters he plays . Over his @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , he has approached many different roles . In Hollywood he played the villain Count Dracula in Van Helsing as a pompous , glam-rock bloodsucker . After this production , Roxburgh married one of Dracula 's three vampire brides , Italian actor Silvia Colloca . " The first thing she ever said to me -- because I had three brides , one Spanish , one American , one Italian -- the very first words she ever said to me were : ' Hi Richard , I 'm your Italian wife , ' ? " he says . " Which sure enough , came to pass , in due time . " He laughs as he retells the story of their meeting , a delightful , well-rehearsed bit of entertainment gossip that underlies their two decades of marriage . <p> " Whether you 're hitting journalists with bullets and shrapnel ... or subjecting them to threats or ridicule ... it 's all coming from the same place , which is about delegitimising truth . This intimate landscape is the landscape of the film . <p> Roxburgh is in every frame of the film . " I 'd never done anything like that , " he says .
