<p> Pakistan has been very busy lately , drawing a lot of attention from and getting involved with opposing political ends of the Middle East . Within ten days Islamabad received visits from the Saudi foreign minister and the Kingdom 's deputy defense minister , followed by a visit from the Iranian President . While both sides of the spectrum are important for Islamabad , managing relations with the two countries is a challenge . These are not just two countries but opposing political zones , each eager to co-opt Pakistan into their respective circle . <p> How the visits were handled indicates Pakistan 's priorities . One could even argue that there is little comparison between the two bilateral linkages . Visitors from Saudi Arabia were much awaited , as these trips were tantamount to opening doors for Saudi investment in Pakistan . Sources I spoke with indicated that the Saudi foreign minister had come to review and explore opportunities for investment in Pakistan and that some deals are ready to be finalised by May this year . <p> Riyadh 's cooperation is being sought for bailing out Pakistan in the Reko Diq copper-gold mine project , where @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the rest are owned by Tethyan Copper Company . During one of the recent visits of Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif to Riyadh , the latter indicated a commitment to investing an additional $5 billion . Then there is prospective investment in agriculture and safe energy projects . <p> Here is a case of mutual benefit . While Saudi Arabia is keen to explore avenues for investment to escape its dependency on oil capital , Pakistan is keen to see some flow of money and investment that is not Chinese and which would not add to its debt burden . However , it is not just all about money . <h> The choice for Pakistan <p> Pakistan and Saudi Arabia are joined together in their appreciation of American significance for the Middle East and South Asia . In some respects , the Islamabad-Riyadh relationship is not just bilateral but a trilateral linkage in which Pakistan will use Saudi Arabia as a window for greater communication with Washington . Since 2017 , attacks against Chinese workers have increased , with Islamabad seeming slow , almost reluctant , to bolster the level of security provided to its ' all-weather ' friend , whose support is critical for keeping Pakistan financially afloat . The military establishment has consistently refused to allow the Chinese to bring their security . Interestingly , Saudi Arabia will be investing in almost the same areas , particularly Balochistan , where the Chinese face grave security threats . <p> The security problem could also be because the Chinese are more visible due to the presence of their workers .
