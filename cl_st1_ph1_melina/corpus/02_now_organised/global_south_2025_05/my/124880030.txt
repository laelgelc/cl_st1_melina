<h> Patriarch Pizzaballa : ' We can not afford the luxury of giving up ' <p> Joining his appeal for peace with the Pope 's , the Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem , Cardinal Pierbattista Pizzaballa , calls on the Christian community to " do everything possible to bring help " to those suffering in Gaza . <p> Patriarch Pizzaballa taking part in the Palm Sunday procession on a road overlooking the Mount of Olives in Jerusalem ( AFP or licensors ) <p> By Kielce GussiePeace was once again the word of the day on Sunday , May 18 . Cardinal Pierbattista Pizzaballa , Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem , joined his voice with that of Pope Leo XIV , who during his homily and Regina Caeli greeting called for an end to violence in war-torn parts of the world . <p> Answer : Peace from Christ " The answer is peace , " Patriarch Pizzaballa stressed in an interview with the SIR news agency . But , he emphasized , the peace the world needs is not " a political peace or merely the absence of war . " Rather , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ conflict , division , and hatred is the peace " that comes from Christ . " <p> The Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem reflected on Pope Leo 's homily during the Mass of Inauguration , referencing Jesus ' words to Peter : " Do you love me ? " Patriarch Pizzaballa said this became " the paradigm of Christian life , whose result is peace . " <p> He explained that if a person has love for God and their neighbor , then peace comes naturally . This peace , the Patriarch argued , is " the kind we all need , everywhere in the world , but especially in the Holy Land . " <p> An abstract idea ? Patriarch Pizzaballa pointed out that recently the word " peace " has been repeated many times . For many people , especially those living in places ravaged by conflict , the notion of " brotherly love " is difficult to accept . He said it " seems abstract , far removed from reality . " <p> Yet , the Patriarch described the Pope 's message as " a call to the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ own " to stay united . He called for this sense of solidarity to come from love of Christ and " not out of fear of the other . " This kind of love creates a chain reaction , even in the public sphere . <p> Thinking of GazaIn his Regina Caeli address , Pope Leo XIV spoke of the " children , families , and elderly survivors " starving to death due to the lack of humanitarian aid reaching the enclave . In response , Patriarch Pizzaballa stressed that " we can not afford the luxury of giving up or standing still . " <p> He argued that we all have the duty to help the local Christians and he issued an unambiguous directive : " We must do everything possible to bring help . " --Vatican News
