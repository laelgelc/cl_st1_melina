( Iubilaeum25 facebook ) <p> ROME : As the world marked International Workers ' Day on May 1 , two contrasting scenes played out on opposite sides of the globe . <p> In the city streets of Asia and the Middle East , the day unfolded in turmoil -- protests , police crackdowns , and cries for justice echoed from Istanbul to Manila , Tehran to Tokyo . Workers rallied for fair wages , job security , and social protections , many facing repression , violence , or arrest . <p> Meanwhile , in Rome , under the banner of the Holy Year 2025 , the Catholic Church opened the Jubilee of Workers ( May 1 -- 4 ) , offering a spiritual and pastoral witness to the sacredness of labour and the enduring rights of workers . <p> In Turkey , thousands defied government bans to gather at Istanbul 's Taksim Square , a symbol of resistance long restricted by state authorities . Officials , attempting to quell the unrest , admitted inflation had eroded a recently approved 45 per cent wage increase . Subsidies and food aid were promised , but dissatisfaction remained . <p> In Japan , demonstrators focused on the fragility of international markets , calling for gender equity , health care , and peace , especially in Gaza and Ukraine . <p> And in Hong Kong , May Day passed quietly for the sixth consecutive year . The Jubilee of Workers , part of the Church 's 2025 Jubilee of Hope , drew pilgrims , trade union leaders , and working-class families from around the world . Organised by the Dicastery for Evangelisation , the event was centred on St Joseph the Worker , patron of labourers . <p> The mood , however , was solemn . Following the death of Pope Francis on April 21 and his subsequent funeral and nine days of mourning , the Jubilee programme was scaled back . <p> Despite this , thousands of pilgrims gathered to perform the Jubilee rite of pilgrimage , crossing the Holy Doors of the four Papal Basilicas -- a moment of renewed faith and resilience . <p> Pope Francis , remembered for his deep commitment to the poor and working class , once said : " Labour is not merely an economic activity -- it is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ holiness . In one scene , people marched under the sun , risking arrest for the right to earn a living with dignity . In another , prayers rose beneath Michelangelo 's dome , invoking peace , fairness , and hope . <p> Both moments , though starkly different , gave voice to a common truth : the struggle for fair , meaningful , and humane work continues . <p> As the Jubilee of Workers concluded in Rome , and as protest dust settles across Asia and the Middle East , the question persists : Will the world finally listen to its workers and act ? --AsiaNews/Agencies
