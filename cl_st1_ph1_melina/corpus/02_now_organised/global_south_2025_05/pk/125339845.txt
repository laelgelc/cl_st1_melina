<h> Targeting the Voiceless <h> When power or ideology is on the line , morality becomes the first casualty . <p> From the APS massacre to the recent killings in Khuzdar , Mir Ali , and Azam Warsak , one question haunts us : what justifies the murder of children -- too innocent to threaten , too voiceless to resist ? Around the world , they are no longer collateral -- they are targets . And in this age of soaring human rights rhetoric and legal safeguards , the hypocrisy is deafening . <p> Islam regards children as a divine trust -- sacred souls placed in our care . Internationally , the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child ( CRC ) -- ratified by Pakistan -- upholds core principles : the right to life , protection , development , and participation . Article 6 guarantees survival and growth ; Article 38 compels states to shield children from armed conflict . Yet , these promises often ring hollow in the very regions where they are needed most . <p> There is no sight more jarring than a child caught @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Yet the blood of the voiceless has become a bargaining chip in conflicts driven by ego , ideology , and imperialism . From bombed classrooms to refugee camps , children are not merely caught in violence -- they are marked by it . Even the once-crowded Zam Zam and Abu Shouk camps in Sudan -- full of children who had known no home but tents -- now stand erased . <p> History is a grim teacher . When power or ideology is on the line , morality becomes the first casualty . Jewish children perished in the Holocaust . Dresden burned . My Lai 's massacre echoed brutality without pauses . In Rwanda , machetes spared neither child nor conscience . Each time , the world declared " Never Again " -- only to forget again . <p> Today , the forgetting continues . Since renewed conflict began in 2023 , more than 14,000 children have reportedly been killed in Gaza . Rubble buries their names ; shrapnel writes their fates . UN agencies warn of a " graveyard for children . " The world responds with carefully worded condemnations @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ too , carries its burden of broken childhoods . In the 2000s , drone strikes -- justified as surgical -- often tore through homes and schools in Waziristan . According to the Bureau of Investigative Journalism , hundreds of children were among the casualties . No names , just numbers . <p> But the horror does n't end with children being targeted -- they are increasingly used , recruited , and weaponised . Across Africa , rebel militias have turned children into tools of war . The Lord 's Resistance Army in Uganda forced many into roles as fighters , porters , and victims of abuse . <p> During a peacekeeping mission in Liberia , I was struck by the sight of a child soldier -- barefoot , barely clothed , yet clutching an assault rifle and calling himself " General Rambo . " He could n't have been more than 13 . He was a tragedy . A child robbed of childhood , not by choice but by circumstance . Pakistan , too , has witnessed its share of this grotesque exploitation . <p> I still remember vividly : @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . It was 2003 , at a church in Islamabad 's Diplomatic Enclave . The boy , no older than 14 , believed he 'd return home after a " mission . " He was fitted with eight grenades . Five exploded -- killing worshippers . The last one detonated in his hand . He never knew escape was never the plan . The three unused grenades he carried were grim relics of stolen innocence . <p> In 2007 , at Islamabad 's Sitara Market , another young attacker panicked and fled . He tried to remove his suicide vest . That split-second of hesitation was his cry for help -- a child trapped in a role he never chose . Many others never got the chance . <p> Children are not born to kill . They are taught . Indoctrinated . Turned into pawns . This transformation -- from innocent to instrument -- is among the gravest betrayals of our shared humanity . The tragedy is n't just in their deaths ; it 's in how we erase their agency long before . <p> Worse still is the silence @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ off as acceptable loss . Those weaponised by extremists are condemned without inquiry into who recruited them , trained them , discarded them . Rarely do we prosecute manipulation ; even more rarely do we rehabilitate it . Media cycles move on , and so does public conscience -- leaving behind only families who remember , and children who never got to grow up . <p> During my tenure as Inspector General of Police in Sindh , I held just one press conference -- not to claim success , but to apologise . A child had died in crossfire . We had failed to protect . I said sorry , pledged to build capacity , and took disciplinary action . That moment still haunts me . For unless we own such failures , we risk becoming no different from the very forces we are meant to fight . <p> Unfortunately , the state 's response to child victims of conflict has been largely procedural . Counterterrorism focuses on eliminating threats -- not healing them . De-radicalisation centres are scarce and underfunded . Victim compensation , where it exists , is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ assurance . Worst of all , public memory fades fast ( especially in our society ) -- far too fast for grieving families to feel acknowledged . <p> The Constitution of Pakistan promises protection , but when these guarantees fail to move beyond the page , they become moral d ? cor -- irrelevant in practice , insulting in principle . Meanwhile , in the rural and tribal belts of Pakistan where birth rates are highest , countless children -- many orphaned and undocumented -- grow up in extended families , invisible to the very state meant to protect them . <p> And moving forward requires more than grief . Pakistan must invest in child rehabilitation , trauma care , and de-radicalisation . Internationally , all actors -- state or non-state -- must be held accountable for violence against children . <p> And this failure is not ours alone .
