<p> Since 2001 , the United States has projected itself as the global vanguard in the " War on Terror . " It has invaded countries , sanctioned regimes , pushed allies to toe its line , and invoked international law to justify its military and economic actions . The Financial Action Task Force ( FATF ) has become one of its favorite tools -- ostensibly to combat terror financing . <p> But for all the rhetoric , Washington 's actions reveal a different agenda . The war on terror is not rooted in universal moral values or consistent standards . It is a selective war , waged based on strategic interest , not principle . This is not counterterrorism -- it is strategic hypocrisy . <p> Terrorists Today , Allies Yesterday <p> The origins of this duplicity trace back to the 1980s , when the U.S. , through the CIA 's Operation Cyclone , armed and trained Afghan Mujahideen -- among them figures like Gulbuddin Hekmatyar and Jalaluddin Haqqani . These " freedom fighters , " funded via Pakistan 's ISI , later morphed into al-Qaeda and the Taliban @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . While bombing Taliban positions , it continued to pour aid into Pakistan , despite Islamabad 's double game -- harboring militants while claiming to fight them . <p> " Pakistan is playing a double game ... it 's taking billions of dollars in aid from America , while supporting insurgents who kill American troops in Afghanistan . " -- The New York Times , 2011 <p> Syria and the Dirty War <p> In Syria , the hypocrisy grew starker . To oust Bashar al-Assad , the U.S. supported an array of rebel factions -- many of whom were little more than rebranded extremists . A declassified 2012 U.S. intelligence report acknowledged that the West and its Gulf allies saw the rise of a " Salafist principality " as a useful pressure point against Assad . That " principality " would become ISIS . <p> This indirect support for radicalism caused untold suffering across the region . Yet the narrative of fighting terror remained undisturbed . <p> Gaza : Silence on State Terror <p> Nowhere is this double standard more glaring than in Gaza . " -- UN Human Rights Office , 2023 <p> The U.S. claims to oppose terrorism . But when state violence is inflicted by its allies , silence or support follows . <p> The FATF and Financial Double Standards <p> The FATF , meant to monitor global terror financing , has become a geopolitical weapon . Countries like Pakistan , Iran , and North Korea are placed on grey or black lists . Until Washington -- and its allies -- are willing to apply the same moral standards to themselves that they demand of others , the war on terror will remain a geopolitical theatre . <p> True peace and security require consistency , not convenience .
