<p> In ways similar to the invention of gunpowder and the nuclear bomb changing the course of war , advancements in communications technology have changed the way wars happen , or at least the way they are reported . The fog of war has become so dense that it is now almost impossible to know what is going on . Decades ago , there used to be the respected and almost institutional presence of the war correspondent , who was informed in military history , had the courage to be on the battlefront and could report with measured responsibility . Martha Gellhorn was among the first women war correspondents when she reported on the Spanish Civil War in 1937 for Collier 's magazine . Towards the end of the Second World War , when she was refused permission to accompany the allied forces landing on Normandy beach because she was a woman , she used the subterfuge of posing as a member of the medical team .
