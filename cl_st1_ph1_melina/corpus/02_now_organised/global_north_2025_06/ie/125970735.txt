<p> Dear Laila is an intimate , interactive installation experienced by one audience member at a time , which shares the Palestinian experience of displacement and resistance , through the story of one family . <p> I created it in response to my daughter Laila , who was then five , asking where I grew up , and why we could n't go there . <p> As I could n't take her to Yarmouk , the Palestinian refugee camp in Damascus where I grew up , I tried to bring it to her by making a model of our now destroyed family home . <p> The audience member sits in Laila 's shoes and learns the story of the house , through the miniature , an audio piece , objects and photos . The story of the house is the story of our family , which in turn is the story of millions of Palestinians . <p> What do you hope an Irish audience will get from it ? <p> I hope that this personal approach makes audiences feel more connected to the experience .
