<p> As Sir Keir Starmer has discovered , nothing quite prepares you for the sheer volume of decisions that come your way as prime minister . There are times , as I learned working alongside Tony Blair , when it feels like being a batsman in a Test match , but instead of one fast bowler hurling the ball at you , there are a dozen or more . <p> There are big decisions -- and few come bigger than whether to engage in military action -- and there are small decisions , coming at you all the time . Like when President Trump opens a folder , and papers fall out . <p> Split decision ... ignore it ? Wait for an aide to rush over and collect them up ? Wait for Trump to do it ? Or do it yourself ? <p> I doubt there is anyone reading this unaware that Starmer chose the last of these options ... cue an avalanche of comment , satire , insult , political attack from the Tories suggesting the picture of our PM bowing down before Trump was a portrait @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Labour 's fault . <p> Thankfully , even in the cesspit of social media , plenty of people suggested what it really showed was the kind of decency and good manners rarely displayed by the man to whose aid the Prime Minister was coming . My point is that even these small , spur of the moment decisions matter , because of the scrutiny they unleash . The big decisions , however , matter a lot more . <p> But for all the obvious parallels , there are far more differences . Perhaps the biggest of these are the role , or lack of it , of the United Nations ; and the focus , or lack of it , on international law . <p> Doubtless some -- those who insist the invasion of Iraq was illegal -- will read that and scoff , and nothing I can add here to the millions of words said and written about the Iraq war is likely to change their view . Both mattered ; both were taken with the utmost seriousness . <p> This feels very different . The UN voice is barely heard , this the result of the deliberate undermining of it , not just by dictatorial Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping in Russia and China , but by the democratically elected Trump and Benjamin Netanyahu in the US and Israel . <p> The debate on intelligence feels different too . One of the reasons we published intelligence , and presented it to Parliament , was out of a desire to share with MPs and public the evidence behind the reasons for Tony Blair 's growing concern about Saddam Hussein . <p> Today , Israel merely asserts that Iran is on the brink of nuclear weaponisation ; when Trump is reminded that the latest official line from his own intelligence director , Tulsi Gabbard , is that Iran does not possess the ability to launch a nuclear bomb , he simply contradicts her , and says he could n't care less what she thinks . Netanyahu , already indicted for war crimes over his actions in Gaza , consistently ignoring rulings of the International Court of Justice , embroiled in corruption charges at home , yet how rarely do we see any of this even mentioned in the current debate about Iran ? <p> Russia invades Ukraine , yet three years later , not only is the war still going on , but Trump seems to take at face value the idea that Putin -- also indicted for war crimes , also ignoring ICJ rulings -- might be a good mediator to help resolve the Iran-Israel war .
