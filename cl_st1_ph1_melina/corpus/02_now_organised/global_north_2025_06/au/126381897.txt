<p> Australia 's public broadcaster should set the standard for robust journalism , and crucially , backing the people who produce it . <p> Instead , this week 's landmark Federal Court finding that Antoinette Lattouf 's firing from the ABC was an unlawful act signed off by executives bowing to pressure from lobbyists is a body blow to the reputation of the institution that underpins this country 's media . <p> The short version of the events ascribes Lattouf 's dismissal to a Human Rights Watch post she had shared on her social media accounts with the caption " HRW reporting starvation as a tool of war " during a planned five-day stint as fill-in host on ABC 's Sydney Mornings in December 2023 . <p> But , given the ABC 's decision to go to court has provided an X-ray of internal correspondence , depositions and testimony from the very top levels of the public broadcaster , we know the complaints had already started rolling in prior to that post , putting management on high alert . <p> A number of those complaints cited an investigation into the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Lattouf 's hosting slot , carrying the co-byline of Lattouf and our associate editor Cam Wilson . <p> In his judgment , Justice Darryl Rangiah noted that the collective complaints , including those citing Crikey 's story , " caused great consternation amongst the senior management of the ABC " . <p> That consternation " turned into what can be described as a state of panic " . <p> The email trail in its entirety lays that state of panic bare , as a series of executives -- including managing director David Anderson , ABC chief content officer Chris Oliver-Taylor , and then acting editorial director at the ABC Simon Melkman -- initially cite the Crikey article as " problematic . " <p> But upon actually reviewing the story in full , the story is deemed " balanced and journalistically sound " . <p> Oliver-Taylor would later write in an email of notes sent to himself : " The main lightning rod is the contentious journalism in Crikey . It was a ' fact-based , research-based ' article . " <p> The Crikey investigation -- alongside a number of Lattouf @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ part of an upcoming interview granted to Women 's Agenda , and ultimately , a media inquiry from The Australian -- became part of an exchange between executives about " perception " , heading off complaints , and ultimately , an urge to " beat the story " . None of it pertained to actual journalism . <p> As this exchange played out across days , Lattouf remained on air , performing under enormous pressure after being alerted to the complaints , and with producers readying themselves to deploy " the dump button when Antoinette is taking talkback calls , as there 's a risk that a motivated detractor could deceive their way on-air by saying they 're going to talk about something innocuous , and then instead accost Antoinette about her views on Israel/Gaza etc " . <p> Ultimately , ruled Justice Rangiah , the decision to fire Lattouf " was made to appease the pro-Israel lobbyists who would inevitably escalate their complaints about the ABC employing a presenter they perceived to have antisemitic and anti-Israel opinions in such a public position " . <p> Unlike most commercial outlets @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ complaints . Readers expect us to hold our nerve and back our reporters in pursuit of the public interest .
