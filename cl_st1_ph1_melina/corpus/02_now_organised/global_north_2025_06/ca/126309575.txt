<h> Defining antisemitism and a call to Mark Carney : Letters to The CJN , June 2025 <p> What follows are three letters , two to the editor , one a reader 's open letter to Prime Minister Mark Carney . This is the first of what I can only hope are many letters round-ups . The aim with Letters is to offer a forum for public feedback , and to showcase the range of viewpoints in the Canadian Jewish community . These are letters to the editor from readers or podcast listeners , not staff editorials . It is unlikely if not outright impossible that any one reader will agree with the content of all the letters we run . I 'm looking for thoughtful feedback coming from a place of goodwill towards Canadian Jewry , but not any one specific political position . <p> If you are interested in submitting a letter to the editor to be considered for publication on our website , please follow our Submissions guidelines for letters . Thank you . <p> Yet , if academic freedom must consider antisemitism , it is important to discuss the definition that 's being used . The one from International Holocaust Remembrance Association , which is now criticized by the man who drafted it , has 11 examples , seven of which focus on Israel . At least three of those examples clearly constrain evidence-based criticism of Israel . A second definition drafted by academics , the Jerusalem Declaration , is far more expansive , noting " it is not antisemitic , in and of itself , to compare Israel with other historical cases , including settler-colonialism or apartheid . " If one applied this definition to the lived experience of Jewish students on campus , it is reasonable to think that there may be fewer incidents of antisemitism . <p> Therefore , it @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ students on Israel-Palestine and actual antisemitism . But none of these facts , which are contextualized , are actually harmful to Jewish students , even to those who view Israel as part of their identity . I agree , however , that they can be shocking and dissonant to those prone to mythology . <p> Another issue that merits consideration is whether surges in violent conflict between Israel and Palestine harm diaspora Jews . A researcher at Sweden 's Lund University wrote on Nov. 1 , 2023 that " during this century , each violent escalation of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict has led to increased antisemitism in many countries . " This could serve as one explanation why anti-Zionist and non-Zionist Jews choose to criticize Israel .
