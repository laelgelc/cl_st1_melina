<p> The Jewish legacy of Toronto 's Kensington Market , a self-contained downtown village of sorts that grew out of Jewish immigration to the city in the 1900s , is n't confined to its remaining visible touchstones like the Kiever Shul , according to Stuart Clarfield . <p> The director of the documentary film Kensington Market : Heart of the City , screening June 10 at Leah Posluns Theatre for this year 's Toronto Jewish Film Festival ( TJFF ) , included scenes in the film of congregants davening in the synagogue , which was founded in 1912 by Ukrainian immigrants . <p> For Clarfield , it 's the spirt of acceptance , belonging , and safety for immigrant communities in Kensington Market that most embodies the area 's Jewish legacy , he told The CJN in an interview . <p> Everyone has their own version of Kensington Market , says Clarfield , from those who grew up in and around the market , to generations of diverse cultural and countercultural backgrounds that have considered the market home for decades . <p> " Why is it that almost everybody comes @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ no matter where they 're from , whether it 's the punks or the homeless folks that are here , or people camping in the middle of the square , or wealthy people going to Tom 's Place for a suit , there 's this flattening ... everyone 's in the village together , " Clarfield said over coffee . <p> " How is that possible , and why has it continued ?
