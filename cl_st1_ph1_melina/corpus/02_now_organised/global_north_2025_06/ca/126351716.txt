<p> When Toronto 's annual Pride parade struts through downtown this weekend , the Jewish delegation will be visible as ever , according to its organizers -- even though its participants know there could be protests and are planning for that possibility . <p> Last year , some of the leaders of the Jewish organizations that march as a group in the annual parade deliberated sitting out the joyous celebration of the LGBTQ+ community , but ultimately many chose to participate despite a political climate targeting Israel support . <p> Ahead of the 2024 parade , a Pride Toronto statement on Gaza was criticized as one-sided , when it did not note the Oct. 7 Hamas attacks and rising levels of antisemitism locally and mentioned the hostages only briefly , near the end of the three-page letter . Meanwhile , a group called Coalition Against Pinkwashing demanded Pride Toronto cut ties with defence companies supplying the Israeli military , which they said profited from Israel 's war in Gaza . <p> Efforts to derail Pride led to a protest hours into the festivities , with demonstrators blocking the parade route @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the marching contingents were still starting out or in progress , although the Jewish Toronto group had already completed the route . <p> Preceded by the Trans March on Friday and Dyke March on Saturday , the annual Pride parade , one of North America 's biggest , takes over downtown Toronto , including Yonge Street south from Bloor Street , on Sunday , June 29 . <p> Parade groups in rainbow colours and risqu ? hot-weather attire , with floats or other vehicles in the mix , march down the main drag , soaking up sun , friendly blasts from water guns , and the lovefest vibe that has become an annual pre-Canada Day blowout summer event . <p> The possibility , if not likelihood , of protests again this year means both security and visibility remain key considerations for Jewish delegation organizers .
