<p> Police in southern Simcoe County are responding , in their own way , to a serious incident south of the border . <p> On Tuesday , South Simcoe police released a statement about the alleged terrorist attack in Boulder , Colorado -- where Mohammed Sabry Soliman , 45 , is accused of throwing two lit Molotov cocktails into a gathering that was calling attention to Israeli hostages in Gaza -- noting it " takes the safety and security of all our community members very seriously . " <p> " Our thoughts are with those impacted by this senseless act of violence , " the department said in a social-media post . " Members remain vigilant in monitoring our communities to ensure the safety and security of everyone . Hate has no place in our communities , and the South Simcoe Police Service does not tolerate or condone acts of hate against a person or property , for any reason . " <p> South Simcoe emphasized it collaborates year-round with various religious and local leaders to support an " inclusive community that respects and values the diverse cultures and religions @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , the department has an increased visible presence at key places of worship and community centres , and officers actively engage with partner agencies and continuously monitor for any potential threats . <p> " We are committed to ensuring that every community member feels safe , secure and supported , " the department said . <p> The United States Attorney 's Office for the District of Colorado has charged the suspect with one count of a hate crime involving actual or perceived race , religion , or national origin in relation to the incident that occurred near the Boulder Courthouse Sunday afternoon . According to the criminal complaint , Soliman threw the improvised incendiary devices at individuals participating in ' Run for Their Lives , ' which organizes weekly walks related to the Israeli hostages . When he allegedly threw the Molotov cocktails , Soliman is said to have yelled " Free Palestine .
