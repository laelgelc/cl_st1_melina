<p> When I saw that my Bluesky feed was smitten with a New York City mayoral candidate , a state assemblyman named Zohran Mamdani , I thought a few things . First , I figured , if Bluesky is 100% behind a candidate , this person stands no chance . I was seeing things about free buses and rent freezes and thinking , yes , it tracks that progressive-heavy platform Bluesky wants this . Remember the old saying , Twitter is n't real life ? Bluesky is even less representative of anything . Then again , I thought Hillary would win . Do n't go to me for prognostication . <p> Next , I thought , wait a second , I do n't live in New York City ! What did I care who became its mayor , let alone in who won a Democratic mayoral primary ? It felt a bit like having to care about the goings-on of Harvard University . OK , not quite , because I 'm from NYC , and more to the point , because this is an objectively high-stakes election involving a city @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , economic , and political impact . The importance here is more than symbolic . This was still not enough to get me to sit down and read the candidates ' platforms and pick which one I would vote for if I could vote in that election , which I can not . <p> Third , I immediately registered the fact that Mamdani is good-looking . Our society is weird about male beauty , pretending not to see it . Women in particular may be reluctant to speak about it because we get accused of being too frivolous for politics . But I am not afraid to say it : Mamdani is a conventionally attractive man , in the visual tradition of Barack Obama and Justin Trudeau . Do I think his floppy-haired heartthrob looks singlehandedly explain his popularity ? No -- policies , charisma , and timing all play a role . But yeah it mattered that he 's cute . <p> It took me longer than it should have to give any thought any which way about what Mamdani 's candidacy means for Jews , or indeed that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ first Muslim mayor . Numerically , though , a candidate succeeding in New York is neither here nor there . It 's Jewish as cities go , but approximately seven out of eight New Yorkers are not Jewish . A Democrat 's success in New York could indicate Jewish support , but would n't necessarily . <p> If a candidate 's votes came almost exclusively from that non-Jewish majority , then that candidate could be neither here nor there where Jews are concerned , or even catering to disparate groups who 've found common cause in hating Jews . New York Times columnist Michelle Goldberg gives as additional evidence for the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Jews Love Zohran Mamdani , " that " he won most of Park Slope , a neighborhood full of progressive Jews , and held his own on the similarly Jewish Upper West Side . " Again , this could point to Jewish support , but it could also mean that the non-Jews in these areas are not so keen on their Jewish neighbours . Such things have , historically , happened . <p> It is not yet known how Jews voted in this election . There was ranked-choice voting , something I am not enough of a shape rotator to fully understand , but that means that you could put some support to a candidate without them being your top pick . This was also , remember , a Democratic primary . Republican Jews , often but not always Orthodox , were n't voting in it . Just as ' antisemitic but pro-Israel ' is a spot on the political spectrum , so too , evidently , is ' anti-Israel but friend of Jews and not just the three who are antizionists . ' So everything 's dandy , right ? <p> *** <p> The question of whether Mamdani is or is not , personally , an antisemite is not a productive conversation . It is a blood-pressure-raising shouting match between people who jump to the term too readily , sometimes for bigoted reasons ( ' Muslim and born in Uganda ' is awfully close to the xenophobic smears against Barack Obama , except in Mamdani 's case , this is his @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ on ) , and ones who effectively would n't characterize any left-wing antisemitism as such . <p> This much is clear : Mamdani has long has been a pro-Palestinian activist . That is not my assumption based on his heritage but a fact about the views he openly espouses . He is also opposed to antisemitism , and prepared to make common cause with Jewish progressives whose views about Israel are not his own . To my knowledge he did not say , globalize the intifada , rather he refused to denounce the expression . His ( as I interpret it ) one-state-solution ideal for Israel would have the result of ending Israel as a Jewish state , which is ... yeah , not my position , nor that of all but a handful of Jews . His antizionism goes beyond criticism of Israel 's actions in Gaza and the West Bank -- criticisms that I see as if anything necessary from a pro-Israel perspective . <p> We can get into abstract philosophical discussions about whether that sort of antizionism amounts to antisemitism ( and I 'm going to be @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ but also that Mamdani is not , personally , an antisemite ) , and still be nowhere closer to figuring out what sort of mayor Mamdani would be for New York 's Jews . <p> What 's hard to assess from afar , even reading up on it , is how much Mamdani is presenting himself as a breath of fresh air for pro-Palestine voters , and how much this is a marginal topic for him that gets dragged up by his detractors . It could be that this was like during the 2024 US presidential elections , where the Democrats were successfully taken to task for a level of ' woke ' that far exceeded their actual platform . <p> *** <p> The takeaway from this election result is less about antisemitism or geopolitics and more about New York 's receding distinctly Jewish character . Asians are the new Jews , halal is the new kosher , things of that nature . I point this out not to make some kind of sinister point about replacement but to remind that cities evolve .
