<h> Letter : Criticism of Israel lacks context <h> Share this : <h> Criticism of Israel lacks context <p> Editor : I appreciate a recent letter 's recognition of the horror Israelis experienced on Oct. 7 ( " Given Jewish history , how can Israel justify Gaza ? " June 4 ) . The brutality of the Hamas attack -- targeting civilians in their homes and at a music festival -- was shocking . It included murder , rape , dismemberment and the documented glee of the attackers , who live-streamed their atrocities . Like the writer , I am deeply concerned about the suffering of all innocent people , Israeli and Palestinian . <p> That said , I strongly differ with his conclusions . What is unfolding in Gaza is not an unprovoked act of aggression -- it is a war , launched by Hamas , a terrorist organization committed to Israel 's destruction . Hamas embeds itself among civilians , using them as human shields . Under such conditions , even the most restrained military response results in tragic collateral damage . <p> In war , truth is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ condemnation of Israel 's actions disproportionately focused and often rooted in longstanding bias . I do n't agree with every Israeli decision -- any more than I agree with every American one -- but the criticism too often lacks context . <p> The U.S. has worked hard to end this war , frequently pressuring Israel to compromise . But no nation , including our own , would allow a terrorist group committed to its destruction to operate across its border . Expecting Israel to return to the pre -- Oct. 7 status quo is unrealistic . It is Hamas -- not Israel -- that has repeatedly rejected ceasefire and peace proposals . <p> There are no easy solutions . But I believe unbiased historians will ultimately find that Israel , despite flaws and missteps , acted with concern for civilian lives under impossible conditions . The suffering must end -- but that will only happen when Hamas relinquishes its arms and its grip on the Palestinian people .
