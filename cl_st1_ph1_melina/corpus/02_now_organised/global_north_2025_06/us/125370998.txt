<p> Amtrak 's Borealis , the passenger rail line running between St. Paul 's Union Depot and Chicago 's Union Station , recently marked its first anniversary . <p> But there is a bigger reason to celebrate . <p> Ridership has surpassed the 205,800 mark as of April 30 , rolling past original projections from the Minnesota Department of Transportation that showed the line would serve between 125,000 and 135,000 riders annually . The original Amtrak feasibility study from 2015 put that number at 155,500 . <p> Later projections showed as many as 232,000 riders would take the Borealis each year . And with May totals still to come , the 411-mile line may reach that number . <p> None of that is surprising to Brian Nelson of All Aboard Minnesota , a rail advocacy group . <p> " We have known for some time the demand has always been there , " Nelson said . <p> The Borealis debuted on May 21 , 2024 , and departs St. Paul at 11:49 a.m. daily and arrives in Chicago about 7 ? hours later .
