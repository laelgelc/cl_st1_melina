<h> 5 convicted in Amsterdam over violence against Israeli soccer fans <p> A Dutch court convicted five men Tuesday for their part in last month 's violence against Israeli soccer fans in Amsterdam that shocked the world and sparked accusations of antisemitism . <p> The Amsterdam district court found them guilty of a range of crimes from kicking fans of the team Maccabi Tel Aviv in the street to inciting violence in chat groups . <p> The heaviest sentence imposed was six months in prison for a man identified as Sefa O. for public violence against several people . <p> The Maccabi Tel Aviv fans came under " hit-and-run " style attacks in the early hours of Nov. 8 after their European League match against Amsterdam giant Ajax . <p> Images of the violence , which left five fans briefly hospitalized , went around the world and sparked a furious reaction in Israel -- including accusations of a " pogrom . " <p> The most serious case under consideration Tuesday was that of a man identified as O. , who prosecutors said played a " leading role " in the violence . The court saw images of the suspect kicking a person on the ground , chasing targets and punching people in the head and body . <p> The prosecutor said the beatings had " little to do " with soccer , but added that " in this case , there was no evidence of ... a terrorist intent and the violence was not motivated by antisemitic sentiment . " <p> " The violence was influenced by the situation in Gaza , not by antisemitism , " said the prosecutor . <p> The attacks followed two days of skirmishes that also saw Maccabi fans chant anti-Arab songs , vandalize a taxi and burn a Palestinian flag . <p> Police said they were investigating at least 45 people over the violence , including that carried out by fans of the Israeli club . <p> Another man , identified as Umutcan A. , 24 , received a sentence of one month for assaulting fans and violently ripping a Maccabi scarf from one of them . <p> Prosecutors had called for heavier sentences against the men -- up to two years in the case of O. <p> The judge said people convicted of such crimes would normally have to serve community service . " But the court finds that , given the seriousness of the offense and the context in which it was committed , only imprisonment is appropriate , " she said . <p> Only one of the five men was in court to hear the verdicts , an AFP reporter saw . <p> A 22-year-old identified as Abushabab M. , 22 , faces a charge of attempted murder but his case has been postponed while he undergoes a psychiatric assessment . <p> He was born in the Gaza Strip and grew up in a war zone , his lawyer told the court , while M. sat sobbing as his case was being heard . <p> Six more suspects are set to appear at a later stage . <p> Three of these suspects are minors and their cases will be heard behind closed doors . <p> At an emotionally charged news conference the morning after the riots , Amsterdam Mayor Femke Halsema said the city had been " deeply damaged " by " hateful antisemitic rioters . " <p> However , Halsema later said she regretted the parallel she had drawn between the violence and " memories of pogroms , " saying that word had been used as propaganda . <p> Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu described the violence at the time as a " premeditated antisemitic attack . "
