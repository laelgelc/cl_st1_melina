But for these few , there is a way out . <h> Escape From Gaza <p> Photographs and video by Paolo Pellegrin <p> Text by Nicholas Casey <p> Dec. 18 , 2024 <p> This wall marks the border between Egypt and Gaza . More than two million Palestinians are stuck on the other side . <p> Yet docked only 25 miles away off the coast of Egypt is a floating hospital run by the United Arab Emirates . <p> More than 8,000 Gazans have been brought here for treatment since the start of the war in October 2023 . <p> Each patient arrives not only with injuries but with a chapter of this ugly war 's history . <p> On Jan. 24 , Reem Elian says , Israeli soldiers shot up her home in Khan Younis , killing her son , a pharmacist , and injuring her other sons and sister-in-law . <p> After the attack , Elian says , soldiers interrogated the survivors for hours , but when she asked for an ambulance , they refused . She made it to the nearest hospital on foot , bleeding and hopping on one leg . <p> Younger patients hardly know how to process what happened to them . <p> Yazen Khaled became an orphan after an airstrike killed his father , his mother , all of his siblings and some of his neighbors . <p> In Gaza , his leg was amputated without anesthesia . In Egypt , more of his limb had to be removed after it became infected . <p> In May , Israel entered Rafah , and the border was closed , blocking access to the hospital . By the end of the summer , the U.A.E. had found a new way out for the wounded -- a humanitarian flight aboard a converted passenger jet to a housing complex in Abu Dhabi . <p> Every few weeks now , desperate families depart Gaza in the early morning hours , making their way on buses to an airport just across the border in Israel . <p> Evacuating the injured by plane is not without its risks . Patients can go into shock midair . Sudden drops in blood pressure could prove deadly . <p> On a recent evacuation , doctors maneuvered a 10-year-old with autism named Ali Darwish onto the plane . Ali was paralyzed from the waist down after an Israeli strike hit his home . <p> As the plane took off , his grandmother shuddered . She had never flown before . Ali looked scared himself , but he is nonverbal and just sat silently gripping the armrests . <p> Cancer treatment in Gaza was difficult before the war and is impossible now . Oncology patients , along with injured children , are prioritized for evacuation . A man with a brain tumor entered the plane and whispered something to a doctor making the rounds . " He wants to smoke , " the doctor said . <p> The doctor moved on to Shams Awida , an 18-year-old who lost most of her eyesight in an attack that damaged her face . <p> As the air pressure changed after takeoff , her eyes began to hurt . " How are you doing ? " the doctor asked . She was all right , she said -- she could still see a little , enough to recognize his outline . <p> Three hours later , the flight touched down . Earlier that morning they were in Gaza . Now they were in Abu Dhabi , an unfamiliar land . <p> If there is a place to return to a normal life after Gaza , it is here . <p> The childhoods that were shattered by war are taking shape again . For the first time , schools are places of learning , not shelters from airstrikes . Children play on a soccer field between the buildings during the day . <p> Yet trauma is around every corner . Maram Qassim , who is 5 , was pronounced dead after an airstrike destroyed the school where she was taking shelter , killing dozens . <p> She was wrapped in a burial cloth when her mother insisted on seeing her one more time and squeezed her hand . Maram faintly squeezed back -- she was alive . <p> In the U.A.E. , Maram recovered her sight , which she lost in the attack , and some of the movement she lost on one side of her body . <p> Children who lose a leg often refuse to try walking on a prosthetic limb , at least at first . But trainers coax them to try . Other children who have also lost legs form bonds with new arrivals and help them learn to walk again . <p> Mohammed Al Jafari , who is 19 , lost his arm in an Israeli attack near his home in Rafah . " My mother said : ' Please , do n't go out . Something might happen , ' " he said . " It was the worst mistake of my life . " <p> Since arriving in the U.A.E. this year , he has learned to use a prosthetic arm and has enrolled in engineering classes to study artificial intelligence . " He 's a small hacker , " one official said . " In four years , " Al Jafari said , " I 'll be a big hacker . " <p> For the younger children , daily life is often about finding new friends who share their struggles . <p> One morning this April , Sham Musselem was with her uncle in the courtyard of her home when an airstrike hit . <p> Her uncle died in front of her , and she was left with her right arm hanging from her body . It was later amputated . <p> At the Emirates Humanitarian City , she met other girls who lost limbs . They spend hours roaming the compound , just trying to be kids again . <p> But while these Gazans have found safety here , they have not found certainty . They still wonder : Will Israel allow us to return ? And what will happen to those we left behind ? <p> Paolo Pellegrin is a Magnum photographer . His most recent book is " Event Horizon , " a collection of images from conflicts and showing the effects of climate change . Nicholas Casey is a staff writer based in Madrid . He previously wrote about Issa Amro , a Palestinian activist in Hebron , who advocates for nonviolent resistance .
