<h> Former emissary to Saudi Arabia will write about foreign affairs . <p> From the fall of the Assad regime in Syria to Israel 's conflicts in Gaza and Lebanon , the Middle East is in the midst of what could be epochal change . And beyond that region , American interests are at stake in any number of foreign policy matters , from Venezuela to Ukraine . <p> Robert Jordan has spent decades deeply engaged in American concerns abroad . <p> Advertisement <p> Jordan was appointed U.S. ambassador to Saudi Arabia , a month after the attacks of Sept. 11 , 2001 . When Editorial Page Editor Rudy Bush and I had breakfast with Jordan last month , he reminded us that 15 of the 19 terrorists who attacked America that day were Saudi . Jordan was thrown into a crisis with many factions and competing interests . From that tangle of stakeholders , he managed to represent the values of the U.S. and the international community , and to enlist the cooperation of the Saudi government in countering terrorism and adopting reform . <p> Advertisement <p> Now retired from his law practice , Jordan is diplomat in residence and professor of practice at the John G. Tower Center for Political Studies at SMU . He is a member of the Council on Foreign Relations and the Dallas Committee on Foreign Relations and a past president of the Dallas Bar Association . <p> Jordan is a frequent commentator for international news outlets including CNN , Fox News , MSNBC , CNBC , Bloomberg and The New York Times . His memoir , Desert Diplomat : Inside Saudi Arabia Following 9/11 , was published in 2015 . <p> We are thrilled that Jordan has agreed to be a contributing columnist for The Dallas Morning News . We expect he 'll bring readers the same critical clarity about foreign affairs that he brought to the U.S. Embassy in Riyadh .
