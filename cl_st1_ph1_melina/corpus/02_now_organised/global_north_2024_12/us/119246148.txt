<p> On the one hand , there 's no sugarcoating how progressives feel at the close of 2024 : rough . <p> Donald Trump won back the White House with a campaign that was openly bigoted and fascist . And from Joe Biden 's backing of Israel 's ghastly war in Gaza to his ill-fated decision to seek reelection , progressives are n't celebrating his tenure , either . <p> The next few years will see an onslaught of challenges . If you look closely , you 'll see signs people are n't just going to roll over and accept it . Here are five that caught my eye . <p> First , populist anger is boiling over . <p> The American public has had it with economic elites . Union activity has been on an upswing for a few years , with union petition filings in 2024 significantly up over 2023 . <p> After a successful national strike in 2023 , the United Auto Workers won an election in Tennessee this year , a significant breakthrough in the traditionally anti-union South . And more Amazon warehouse workers and drivers are pushing to join unions , breaking open one of America 's most anti-union corporations . <p> Meanwhile , communities in places like Wisconsin fought back against a private equity takeover of nursing care . And nationally , widespread anger positively exploded over the greed of health insurance companies . <p> Trump likes to play a populist on TV . However , if he moves to slash workers ' rights , cut taxes for CEOs , and erode healthcare access , he 'll have a fight on his hands . <p> Second , the peace movement is back . <p> The movement for a ceasefire in Gaza drew support from a vast , diverse coalition of young people -- with additional support from faith communities , unions , environmentalists and others who 'd previously stayed " in their lane . " <p> They have n't succeeded yet , but they 've won broad , bipartisan public support for a ceasefire , arms embargo on Israel , and , more generally , a foreign policy informed by human rights . <p> Democratic politicians , in particular , wo n't be able to avoid this issue . <p> Third , climate wins are adding up . <p> Temperatures continue to rise , natural disasters worsen , and our leaders fail us on climate . The Biden administration 's signature climate law , the Inflation Reduction Act , is unleashing record investments in green jobs , fueling unprecedented growth in renewable power across the United States . <p> Meanwhile , Indigenous-led efforts are quietly reintroducing species to their natural habitats . In Oregon 's Klamath River basin , salmon recently appeared for the first time in 100 years after a dam was removed . <p> The more local support initiatives like these win , the harder they 'll be to reverse . <p> Fourth , some people showed up for their immigrant neighbors . <p> One of the worst moments of the 2024 campaign was the ridiculous slander of Haitian Americans in Springfield , Ohio , by Donald Trump , JD Vance and other right-wing figures . <p> Springfield natives turned out to support their Haitian neighbors . Locals flocked to Haitian restaurants , churches and community centers to show their solidarity , prompting state and local Republicans to speak out against these dangerous lies . <p> There 's a lesson here . While hardline immigration measures can attract support in the abstract , people feel much differently when they realize members of their own communities could be affected . <p> Progressives should defend their immigrant neighbors without apology , not peddle the " Republican-lite " policies favored by national Democrats in recent years . They may win some unlikely allies if they stand up for what 's right . <p> Fifth , even where Democrats lost at the polls , progressive issues won . <p> Forget " red states " and " blue states " -- there 's support for progressive ideas in all 50 states . <p> Just look at the election . Several " red states " passed ballot measures to raise the minimum wage , guarantee paid leave , protect abortion access , and liberalize their marijuana laws . This latest election was no exception -- a trend that 's been building for years . <p> Once you filter out the noise around candidates and ask people about progressive policies directly , even " red state " voters tend to say yes . The key is to make candidates run on those policies -- without watering them down . <p> In short , our politics are a mess right now . Our country is n't " lost " -- only our leaders are . When Americans organize around our common decency , it 's going to get a lot harder for bullies like Trump to walk over us . <p> Peter Certo is the communications director of the Institute for Policy Studies and editor of its OtherWords.org op-ed service .
