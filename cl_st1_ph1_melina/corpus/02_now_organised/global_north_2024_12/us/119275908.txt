<h> War-torn Holy Land is an echo of first Christmas <h> We need a holiday more sturdy than inflatable Frostys . <p> Worshippers pray in the Church of the Nativity , where Christians believe Jesus Christ was born , ahead of Christmas in the West Bank city of Bethlehem , on Tuesday , Dec. 17 , 2024 . ( Mahmoud Illean / AP ) <p> For the second straight year , Christmas looks to be quiet in Bethlehem , if not peaceful . Reuters reports that Manger Square in front of the Church of the Nativity is deserted , its souvenir shops closed against the rockets and rifles that now festoon the Holy Land in place of the traditional Christmas Tree missing from the square . <p> The promise of a cease-fire in the Gaza war , which is leaking out to the West Bank , Lebanon and even as far as Iran , has emboldened a few intrepid Christians to hang wreaths and light hopeful candles . On Nov. 22 , Christian leaders in Jerusalem reversed last year 's directive for churches to forgo public holiday displays . But the birthplace of Jesus is still too dangerous for merrymaking . We may have peace on Earth someday , in some forgotten outpost , but not this year . And not in the place where it was promised . <p> Advertisement <p> And that is precisely why we have Christmas . As contributing columnist Abby McCloskey reminds us nearby , the story of Christmas only matters if the kingdom of heaven interrupts the man-made cycle of hell on Earth . <p> Advertisement <p> Bethlehem has never been a peaceful place . The first Christmas story involved religious oppression , occupying forces , widespread government-enforced infanticide and violent authoritarianism . Shortly after Jesus ' birth , the Holy Family fled the continent , crossing unsecured borders as refugees . <p> In fact , the whole Christian story , like that of many religions , is about war : a cosmic battle between good and evil . And for Abrahamic faiths , that war leaks out of the spiritual realm into geopolitical battlefields -- places with embassies and supply chains and shuttered souvenir shops -- places you can find on Google maps . <p> Christmas , after all , is the celebration of an incursion behind enemy lines . A promise of heavenly peace sneaking into a dirty world at war . A savior who would leave behind eternal security to seed an insurrection against a world stubbornly wrapped in razor wire . <p> Advertisement <p> It 's precisely when peace seems feasible -- when wars are n't raging and mothers are n't mourning and the poor are n't oppressed and the weak are n't overlooked -- that we can start to imagine a world in which humankind might finally get to the bottom of things , might architect something resembling peace on earth , or at least get the arrow pointing in the right direction . <p> Christmases like this one , with 45,000 freshly dead in the land called holy , remind us that we 're out of our depths . <p> If our Christmas celebration is only about a well-cooked ham and a pile of shredded gift wrap , it wo n't be sturdy enough to meet our needs . But , for those who believe , Christmas 2024 might remind us that there is one who is . <p> Dallas Morning News editorials are written by the paper 's Editorial Board and serve as the voice and view of the paper .
