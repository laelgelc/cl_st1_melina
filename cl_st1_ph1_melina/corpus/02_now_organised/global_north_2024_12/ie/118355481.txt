<h> Letters : Those who do not cast a vote when it 's their duty to do so have no excuses <p> A person casts their vote at a polling station in St Anthony 's Boys National School in Cork . Photo : Reuters <p> Letters to the editor <p> Today at 03:30 <p> Yet again , around 40pc of the Irish electorate have refused to take ? seriously their civic duty to vote . <p> One can only wonder why they fail to realise the enormous power each voter has in Ireland , when they exercise their democratic right to vote without hindrance . <p> Are these non-voters even aware of other nations that do their best to put obstacles in the way of democracy ? <p> I 'm talking here of the supposedly " great and free " America . Or look at our neighbours in the UK , which holds tight to its first-past-the-post system . <p> And we can not forget the brutal , direct attacks on the female population in Afghanistan : where the Taliban is now legislating to allow nine-year-old female children to be legally wed . <p> There is no excuse not to cast your vote in Ireland when you have the ability to do so : in fact , it is an unconscionable act . <p> Declan Foley , Melbourne <h> We should cherish power of democracy and never be complacent about its value <p> It 's my contention that the power of democracy , particularly in light of worrying global trends , is an anchor of hope . <p> Although democracy has its shortcomings , our electoral system has always tended to reward consensus and tolerance over extremism and polarisation . <p> However , I believe it would be wrong to be complacent with our own franchise -- the best way to combat concerns is by always actively participating in the democratic process and casting our vote . <p> Voting is an incredible and fundamental right that we are lucky to have in this country . <p> Without this basic right , one would have no control over the ground beneath one 's feet . <p> It 's my assertion that Irish people are incredibly privileged to have this right , and it is important not to ever belittle an opportunity like voting in an election . <p> Making one 's voice and one 's opinion heard is of paramount importance and we should never forget that . <p> John O'Brien , Clonmel , Co Tipperary <h> Elitist lefties pretend they care , but they are only ever in it for themselves <p> Many commentators are saying that Ireland 's leftist candidates/parties erred by failing to make a pre-election pact . <p> Such analysis is disingenuous , or simply idiotic . Irish politics is left of centre . So in any general election , the electorate merely decides what degree of left takes power : slightly left or more full-on left ? <p> And it 's usually the former that wins out : the pernicious , elite left -- those who condescendingly tell us plebs how we should live our lives while they fatten themselves on the cream of others ' endeavours . <p> Those in Ireland 's political establishment make the pigs in Orwell 's Animal Farm seem trustworthy . <p> Brendan Corrigan , Granada , Colombia <h> Attending election counts in this country should not be a case of who you know <p> I attended the Dublin Mid-West count centre at Adamstown , intending to see how the count was going . <p> The security men would not allow me in because I did n't have a pass . Someone came to the door and enquired as to why I wanted to get in . <p> I explained that I was an Irish citizen with an interest in the democratic process . This was n't sufficient to secure access . <p> I was then asked if I knew anyone in the count centre . I was allowed in when I named one of the candidates . I imagine that any citizen should have an entitlement to observe the count after the voting . <p> I often read of Irish politicians acting as observers at elections in dubious democracies . Maybe they need to look closer to home . <p> It is hardly a ringing endorsement of the system here that a citizen has to know someone on the inside before being granted access to the count centre . <p> Joe Maher , Lucan , Co Dublin <h> It 's important to realise that politicians can bring about substantial change <p> At 92 years of age , I have lost count of the number of general elections I have voted in . <p> Politics is important because the actions of our public representatives can bring about significant change . <p> Let 's hope that the politicians in the next government act in such a way so as to change the country for the better , like Donogh O'Malley did in 1966 when , as education minister , he announced free secondary school education -- an act that revolutionised Ireland . <p> Anthony Fitzpatrick , Dublin <h> Parties spending their way to the top -- and they can thank the taxpayer for it <p> It 's great , yet again , to see the top three political parties that spent the most on advertising in the weeks coming up to election day ended up receiving the most votes . <p> Coincidentally , they are also the parties that received the greatest taxpayer funding over the last five years . <p> Just saying . <p> Nick Crawford , Dalkey , Co Dublin <h> Pencil is mightier than the sword , especially when we go to cast our votes <p> I am reminded of the man who was asked by a friend , " how did you vote in the election ?
