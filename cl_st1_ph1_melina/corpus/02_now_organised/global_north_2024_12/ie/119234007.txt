<p> The planning for pager and walkie-talkie attacks had begun 10 years ago before they were set off in September 2024 targeting Hezbollah members , as revealed by two ex-officers from Israel 's spy agency -- Mossad . <p> The two recently retired senior agents from Mossad appeared in a chat show recently -- under pseudonyms -- and spoke at length about the planning that went behind the explosions . <p> The covert remote attack had raised fears globally and it showed Mossad 's preparedness for any eventuality . <p> Moreover , the revelations by the two former spies confirm that the supply chain for the pagers had been rigged in advance . <h> Planning the attack with walkie-talkies <p> In an interview aired on the page of 60 Minutes , the retired spies divulged how Mossad agreed to go ahead with the plan that was formulated almost a decade ago . <p> Michael , name changed due to security reasons , stated that he was part of the group which had pushed for this route . <p> The process had first started by bobby-trapping the walkie-talkies with an explosive device fitted . <p> Then they had the hard job of convincing -- first their bosses to go ahead with the plan , and then the Hezbollah members . The agent stated that they had to set up multiple shell companies to hide any link from tracking back to Israel or Mossad . <p> In total , the Hezbollah members had brought 16,000 walkie-talkies which were built at a Mossad facility in Israel . Some of these were eventually detonated on September 18 -- a day after the pager attacks . <p> However , the agency was not satisfied with this and wanted to go a step further . They then planned the rigged pagers -- in a second installment . <h> Rigged pagers plan conceived in 2022 <p> In 2022 , Mossad began developing pagers with explosives hidden in them . Pagers are a lot more compact than walkie-talkies , so great care has been taken to ensure that they are functional and can still cause damage once triggered . <p> The planning for the pager attacks was revealed by Gabriel , the pseudonym of another former agent who worked for Mossad . <p> Mossad designed a slightly larger variant of the pager -- the size difference had helped them fit in explosives . <p> The spy agency also conducted tests to ensure that the explosives in the pager were just enough to injure the person carrying it , and not the ones standing in proximity . <p> They were tested multiple times in order to make sure they caused minimal damage , as per Gabriel . The intent was to hurt Hezbollah members , not the civilians who were nearby . <p> He also clarified that the pagers were simple devices that could not aid Mossad in tracking the members . They had only two purposes , to function smoothly as pagers -- until they were triggered as mini bombs . <p> The meticulous planning also involved setting the pagers to explode after seven seconds -- an average response time for answering a pager -- which Mossad had arrived at after experiments . <h> Fake pager ads , shell companies to lure in Hezbollah <p> The Hezbollah members had been lured in by building fake advertisements for the products , which showed them as being extra durable , tough , and laden with additional features . <p> The ads did the trick , apart from Hezbollah members . Even others had shown interest in the device , but the agency did n't allegedly sell it to anyone else . <p> Gold Apollo -- the Taiwanese company- had been duped into working with Mossad . Gabriel stated that the shell companies had worked their charm , and Gold Apollo had granted a licensing partnership to the fake unit set up by Mossad . <p> Thereafter , they had everything to make Hezbollah believe that they were buying the devices from Gold Apollo . The spy agency manufactured all the rigged devices on its own and sold 5,000 of these to Hezbollah . <p> The agent states that the whole operation was like the movie ' Truman Show ' -- where everything was controlled without the main character -- Hezbollah in this case -- having any idea about it . <h> Mossad chief 's nod on the day of the attack <p> On the fateful day of September 17 , Mossad head Dadi Barnea gave the go-ahead for triggering the rigged papers , and the attack targeted members of Hezbollah in Lebanon and Syria . <p> This attack caused mayhem and resulted in at least nine individuals getting killed and injuring thousands of others . <p> The incident had occurred during escalating tensions between Israel and the Iran-backed Hezbollah , as the two sides had exchanged fire along the Israel-Lebanon border following the October 7 Hamas attack in 2023 that ignited the war in Gaza . <h> RECOMMENDED ARTICLES <p> The September 17 pager attack was followed by walkie-talkies of Hezbollah members exploding on September 18 , further deepening the crisis for the Iran-backed group .
