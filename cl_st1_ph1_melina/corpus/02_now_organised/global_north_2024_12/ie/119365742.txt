<h> The Years of Living Dangerously : Europe looks ahead to 2025 <p> As 2024 slips through its twilight days , the events of the past fortnight have been characteristic of a very turbulent year . <p> Within days of each other , the French and German governments either collapsed or lost a prime minister , both in their own ways having lurched through episodic destabilisations throughout the year . <p> A deeply volatile Middle East was jolted by the collapse of the Assad regime and the emergence of a political-military force once linked to al-Qaeda . <p> While the ceasefire between Israel and Hezbollah in Lebanon in late November provided a rare moment of respite , the sheer destructive force of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict continues to resonate throughout the region , simultaneously contaminating European domestic politics . <p> Russia continues to deliberately destroy civilian infrastructure across Ukraine and Ukraine 's defences have been buckling in the Donetsk region under the pressure of Moscow 's superior manpower . <p> Russian troops are moving faster than at any time since Vladimir Putin launched his invasion in 2022 . <p> Three weeks after the birth of 2025 , the European Union will be confronted with the vision most capitals were petrified by throughout 2024 : Donald Trump returning to the White House , an outcome that could - potentially - have extremely negative consequences for Ukraine and Europe in general . <p> Donald Trump will return to the White House at the start of 2025 <p> Even back in January , the prospects for Europe in 2024 did not look great . <p> Only 16% of respondents expected a calm and stable outlook for 2024/5 : after the hottest Northern Hemisphere summer in history in 2023 , there was widespread fear about extreme weather events , AI-generated misinformation and disinformation , societal and political polarisation . <p> The cost of living , disruption to supply chains for food and energy further darkened the political mood . <p> " These risks are serious because they limit our ability to tackle the big global challenges we are facing , " European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen told delegates at the Swiss mountain resort . <p> " Changes in our climate and our geopolitical climate , shifts in our demography and in our technology .
