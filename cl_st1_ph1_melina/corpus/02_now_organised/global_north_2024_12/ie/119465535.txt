<h> Record number of Irish deaths abroad reported to Department of Foreign Affairs in 2024 <p> The Department of Foreign Affairs , Iveagh House <p> Darragh Nolan <p> Today at 01:01 <p> There was a record number of deaths of Irish citizens abroad reported to the Department of Foreign Affairs this year as the figure rose above 400 for the first time . <p> Consular assistance was provided to 405 families of an Irish citizen who died abroad in 2024 , up from 381 in 2023 . <p> Overall , the number of consular cases dealt with by the Department of Foreign Affairs has gone up for the third consecutive year , rising by 9pc compared to last year . <p> The biggest increase was seen in the number of missing persons cases , up 32pc . Arrests and welfare cases both rose by 26pc compared to a year ago . <p> The 1,858 new cases opened by the Department of Foreign Affairs this year does not include ongoing efforts to assist Irish citizens caught in crises in the Middle East including in Gaza and Lebanon . <p> Of the new cases , the 405 deaths abroad made up the largest proportion , followed by medical cases ( 301 ) , arrests ( 241 ) and welfare ( 238 ) . Six cases of child abduction were reported . <p> T ? naiste and Foreign Affairs Minister Miche ? l Martin said providing consular assistance is one of the " cornerstones " of the work carried out by the Department . <p> " This year , we have provided assistance in a number of complex cases , such as parental child abductions , and in regions where we have no physical presence . These cases are particularly challenging for citizens and their families , " he said . <p> " I would like to pay tribute to the hardworking staff in our missions overseas who , alongside colleagues based in the Department 's headquarters in Dublin , provide round the clock assistance . This important work also includes authenticating over 56,000 Irish documents for business or personal use overseas , and over 2,900 civil letters of freedom issued to Irish citizens marrying abroad in 2024 . " <p> Support provided to Irish citizens caught in crises in the Middle East included evacuations from Gaza and assistance to leave Lebanon . <p> Since late 2023 , a total of 106 people have left Gaza with support from the Department of Foreign Affairs . <p> " A small number of Irish citizens and dependants remain in Gaza and my Department , through our missions in the region , continues to advocate on behalf of those individuals that have not yet been able to leave , " Mr Martin added . <p> " 28 Irish citizens and dependants were directly assisted by my Department to depart Lebanon aboard flights organised by international partners . All citizens that requested assistance to depart were supported to do so . "
