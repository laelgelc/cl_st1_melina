<h> They are safe in Ireland , but Ukrainian and Gazan families will only be at peace when the bloodshed ends at home <p> For those fleeing war , Christmas wishes are focused on loved ones who remain in danger <p> Olesia Tsyhaniuk and six of her children are spending Christmas in Limerick , but her husband and two oldest children remain in Ukraine <p> Shereen Alsabbagh 's mother Najwa ( left ) and late aunt Suad , who was killed in an Israeli airstrike this month <p> Rescuers carry dogs as they help residents evacuate from the site of a Russian missile strike in central Kyiv , Ukraine . Photo : Reuters <p> Kathy Donaghy <p> Today at 03:30 <p> Olesia Tsyhaniuk 's children are used to spending Christmas on a farm in Ukraine , looking after the chickens , ducks , cows and goats . This year , they will spend their first away from home in temporary accommodation in Limerick . <p> The mother and her six children , ranging in ages from five months old to 15 years , arrived in Ireland earlier this month after fleeing their war-torn country . They left their father , Olesia 's husband , and two older siblings behind . <p> The family lived on a farm in a village outside the city of Odesa and were becoming increasingly fearful for their futures . <p> In recent weeks , with the threat of war constantly present and the children being schooled in the bomb-shelter basement of the local school , the heart-breaking decision was taken for Ms Tsyhaniuk to leave with the children . <p> Those fleeing the war in Ukraine and entering Ireland after March 14 are given temporary state-provided accommodation for a maximum of 90 days . They are given food , services and integration support during that period . <p> Ms Tsyhaniuk 's husband , Oleksii Sr , stayed behind with the couple 's two older daughters aged 18 and 21 . Their 21-year-old is pregnant with her first child . <p> Rescuers carry dogs as they help residents evacuate from the site of a Russian missile strike in central Kyiv , Ukraine . Photo : Reuters <p> Speaking through an interpreter , Ms Tsyhaniuk says she is grateful to be here as their home situation was becoming too dangerous . <p> Life before the war was comfortable . The children loved going to school and helping out with jobs on the farm after classes . As things started deteriorating at home , they became more isolated , venturing off the farm less and less as rockets flew over their heads in the night sky . <p> Despite the precarity of their circumstances , Ms Tsyhaniuk says she is hopeful for the family 's future and is grateful they are safe and well this festive season . <p> I hope Christmas time will bring us strength to become better and to learn new things <p> Even though they are currently living in one room , she says the children are not complaining . " I 'm just looking forward to them being able to do things kids are supposed to do and not going to school in a bomb shelter , " she said . <p> " I hope we will overcome these difficulties . I hope Christmas time will bring us strength to become better and to learn new things . I count on God 's blessings and hope that things will be OK . " <p> Christmas in a new place with lots of children brings its own challenge , but Ms Tsyhaniuk says people have been kind to them and their conditions are good . <p> Like in Ireland , the Christmas holiday period is one of the most important for Ukrainians , a time when they exchange presents and sing songs . She hopes the songs they sing this year will be for peace in their homeland . <p> In her home in Co Roscommon , the only gift Shereen Alsabbagh longs for this season is her mother 's presence and her family 's safety . <p> An Irish citizen since 2022 , Ms Alsabbagh grew up in Gaza , but moved here to study and work . The last year has been the most difficult of her life as she has watched events unfold in Gaza . <p> Every day brings more news of devastation . The phone , her only means of connection with her family back in Gaza , has become an instrument of torture . Every time it rings , she fears the worst . <p> Last January , the Irish Independent spoke to Ms Alsabbagh about her hopes to bring her elderly mother Najwa ( 75 ) to this country . As well as living with the constant fears of attack , her mother has become frail due to lack of exercise and access to food . <p> Ms Alsabbagh 's brother , his wife and the couple 's four children live with her mother . Life over the course of this year has only got worse . <p> " It 's more scary and more frightening than ever , " Ms Alsabbagh , speaking from her home in Ballaghaderreen , where she works with refugees , said . <p> The Department of Foreign Affairs issued a visa for her mother in early February . In May , the Israeli authorities granted approval for Najwa to leave Gaza , by which time the border crossings were shut . <p> Shereen Alsabbagh 's mother Najwa ( left ) and late aunt Suad , who was killed in an Israeli airstrike this month <p> Ms Alsabbagh says her mother is losing hope . " I try to give my mother some hope . I tell her I have prepared her room and I have all the things she likes . It works some of the time but many times it does n't , " she said . <p> Ms Alsabbagh says her family never supported Hamas and yet so many of them have been killed in Israeli attacks . She has lost 150 members of her family on her mother 's side alone . <p> " Home is meant to be a safe place , " she added . " Now it is a grave . My mother 's oldest sister , Suad , was killed earlier this month in an airstrike . They were given an evacuation order but my aunt Suad was 82 . She was weak and had bad knees and was not able to walk . <p> " Her son Wael and his wife Haya and their two adult sons lived with her in Tel al-Hawa neighbourhood of Gaza city . <p> " Wael left the house to get a taxi for his mother and family , but when he arrived back at the house in the taxi , it had been hit by an Israeli airstrike . The bodies of his mother Suad , his wife and two sons are still buried under the rubble . <p> " My mother hears all these stories . Every day and every hour more people are getting killed . <p> " It 's impossible for my mother to get out because Israel has closed all the borders . She was 73 when this started , now she is 75 . Her body is getting weaker . " <p> I need to keep strong , but I 'm so tired of living two lives <p> Ms Alsabbagh wakes up every day with headaches , wondering how much longer her family is going to live with this . <p> " These people are innocent , " she said . " They did n't do anything . You are told to work hard , to be a good person , that you will get good things . But I 'm losing hope in all these things . <p> " Every day , they talk of death . Death surrounds them . It 's like death is the only thing they are certain of . " <p> As families make their preparations to celebrate the season of goodwill , she wonders what to tell her mother about hope , about the future . <p> " I need to keep strong , but I 'm so tired of living two lives : I 'm here but I 'm not here . I 'm from there but I 'm not there , " she adds . <p> A spokesperson for the Department of Foreign Affairs said it was aware of Ms Alsabbagh mother 's case , adding that it " is continuing to advocate on behalf of all Irish citizens and their dependents that wish to leave Gaza " .
