This even exceeds the performance of US defence companies , whose revenue fell by 7.9 percent , while British firms managed to grow at 2.6 percent . <p> The Russia-Ukraine war led to a major shift in the UK government 's security perception . In 2022-2023 , the country bolstered its military infrastructure , marking the highest defence expenditure in the past two decades , even reversing a trend that saw health expenditure on a constant rise since the 1960s . In 2022 , the UK devoted 11.1 percent of its GDP to its health spendings , but a year later it was reduced to 10.9 percent . <p> The UK 's Ministry of Defence ( MOD ) is the primary customer for domestic defence suppliers . For example , QinetiQ and Babcock International each earn over 50 percent of their revenue from projects they signed with the British government . <p> Alongside other nations , the UK has also been a key supporter of Ukraine , committing ? 7.8 billion ( $9.9B ) in military support so far . <p> Between 2019 and 2023 , the war-torn country ranked as the third-largest destination for British military sales , highlighting the vital role that UK defence firms have played in arming Ukraine 's military in the years preceding the full-blown conflict with Russia . <p> Others <p> UK 's GDP versus military spendings from 2002 to 2023 . ( Source : SIPRI ) <p> International trade and exports <p> Experiencing a surge in exports in the past five years , the UK has cemented its position as one of the top players in the global defence market . <p> From 2019 to 2023 , the UK ranked as the 7th largest global exporter in international defence sales . In 2022 alone , UK defence exports generated approximately ? 11.2 billion ( $15B ) , accounting for more than a third of the industry 's total revenue -- a staggering 69.9% increase from previous years . The upward trajectory continued in 2023 , with exports reaching ? 9.5 billion ( $12B ) , according to ADS . <p> This growth is largely attributed to a series of new contracts that have expanded the UK 's reach in the global defence sector . The Middle East , in particular , has become a crucial market for UK exports , with countries like Qatar now leading as the top destination for British defence products . <p> In fact , military spending in the Middle East saw a dramatic 9 percent rise in 2023 , amounting to around $200 billion -- the largest annual increase in a decade , according to SIPRI . <p> Stock market dynamics <p> The performance of defence companies in the stock market is traditionally tied to conflicts and geopolitical tensions . Stock prices generally serve as indicators of both the health of the defence sector and investor confidence . <p> Rolls-Royce has seen its stock price almost quadruple since the start of the Ukraine war . While Rolls-Royce 's defence segment accounts for only 26 percent of its total revenue , the company 's aerospace dominance and ongoing demand for military engines and systems have contributed to consistent upward movement in its stock price . <p> Therefore , BAE Systems stands out as a prime example of the sector 's success with 96 percent of its revenue tied to defence . During the same period , BAE 's stock price has doubled to around ? 1300 ( $1600 ) . <p> Other defence players have also shown impressive growth over the past three years . Babcock International 's stock surged more than two-thirds to ? 500 ( $630 ) , while QinetiQ recorded similar gains , rising to ? 425($540) . <p> Some defence companies experienced volatility despite the rising share prices . The reason being their reliance on non-defence contracts , with Serco , for example , facing some setbacks , particularly following a major loss in its Australian migration agreement . <p> This highlights that companies with a larger proportion of their revenue tied to defence tend to benefit more from the current climate of rising military spending . <p> The FTSE 100 index , which tells us how the UK 's most valuable companies performed , has captured this trend -- that except for defence firms , other sectors only grew at 10 percent during the same period . <p> The government has promised to raise defence spending to 2.5 percent of GDP by 2030 , the highest level since 1997 .
