<p> Israel 's Defence Minister Israel Katz said on Tuesday ( Dec 17 ) that his country 's forces would be free to act in the Gaza Strip even after its war with Hamas ends . <p> " After eliminating Hamas 's military and governmental capabilities in Gaza , Israel would have security control over Gaza with complete freedom of action " for the Israeli military , Katz said in a post on X , noting that it was his own " position " . <p> Katz said that Israel 's future control over Gaza should be " exactly like in Judea and Samaria " , the biblical names for the West Bank which Israel has occupied since 1967 and where its forces conduct frequent raids . <p> The question of Gaza 's post-war governance has remained unresolved , more than a year since Israel vowed to crush Hamas in retaliation for its Oct 7 , 2023 attack . <p> Katz 's predecessor Yoav Gallant had voiced firm opposition to any prolonged Israeli control of Gaza , from which Israel withdrew troops and settlers in 2005 after decades of direct rule . <p> In May , then as defence minister , Gallant said that he would " not agree to the establishment of an Israeli military administration in Gaza " . <p> " Israel must not have civilian control over the Gaza Strip , " Gallant said at the time , urging Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to devise a post-war plan for the territory . <p> The Israeli leader replaced Gallant with Katz , a former foreign minister who Israeli analysts say is more likely to endorse the Netanyahu 's views . <p> Hamas seized full control of Gaza in 2007 , capping months of clashes with other Palestinian factions after it won legislative elections the year before . <p> The Hamas attack that sparked the war resulted in the deaths of 1,208 people , mostly civilians , according to an AFP tally based on official Israeli figures and includes hostages killed in captivity . <p> Israel 's retaliatory campaign has killed at least 45,028 people in Gaza , most of them civilians , according to the figures from the Hamas-run territory 's health ministry which the United Nations considers reliable .
