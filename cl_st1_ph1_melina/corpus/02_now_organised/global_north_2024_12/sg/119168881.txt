<h> Syria 's new leader vows not to negatively interfere in Lebanon <p> DAMASCUS -- Two weeks after seizing power in a sweeping offensive , Syria 's new leader , Mr Ahmed al-Sharaa , has stepped up regional contacts , vowing in a meeting on Dec 22 not to " negatively " interfere in neighbouring Lebanon . <p> Turkey has maintained strong ties with Syria 's new leaders and Ankara 's intelligence chief , Mr Ibrahim Kalin , was in Damascus just four days after Mr Assad 's regime fell . <p> Regional powerhouse Saudi Arabia is also in direct contact with Syria 's new government , having supported the opposition to Mr Assad for years during Syria 's civil war , and will send a delegation to the country soon , Syria 's ambassador in Riyadh said . <p> During his meeting with visiting Lebanese Druze chiefs Walid and Taymur Jumblatt , Mr Sharaa said Syria would no longer exert " negative interference in Lebanon at all " . <p> He added that Damascus " respects Lebanon 's sovereignty , the unity of its territories , the independence of its decisions and its security stability " . <p> Syria " will stay at equal distance from all " in Lebanon , Mr Sharaa added , acknowledging that Syria has been a " source of fear and anxiety " for the country . <p> Mr Walid Jumblatt , long a fierce critic of Mr Assad and his father , Hafez , who ruled Syria before him , arrived in Damascus on Dec 22 at the head of a delegation of lawmakers from his parliamentary bloc and Druze religious figures . <p> The Druze religious minority is spread across Lebanon , Syria and Israel . <p> He met Mr Sharaa -- also known as Abu Mohammed al-Jolani -- at the presidential palace , where the new Syrian leader wore a suit and tie instead of the olive-green military shirt he sported just days ago . <p> Mr Walid Jumblatt accuses the former Syrian authorities of having assassinated his father in 1977 during Lebanon 's civil war . <p> The Syrian army entered Lebanon in 1976 , only leaving in 2005 after enormous pressure following the assassination of former prime minister Rafic Hariri , a killing attributed to Damascus and its ally , Lebanon 's Iran-backed Hezbollah group . <h> ' Insecurity ' <p> Mr Assad was an adherent of the Alawite offshoot of Shi'ite Islam and projected himself as a protector of the country 's religious and ethnic minorities . <p> The seizure of power by the Sunni Islamists of HTS -- proscribed as a terrorist organisation by many governments , including the United States -- has sparked concern , though the group has recently sought to moderate its rhetoric . <p> Despite worries over Syria 's future , global powers , including the US and the European Union , have stepped up contacts with the war-ravaged country 's new leaders , urging them to guarantee protections for women and minorities . <p> The foreign leaders have also stressed the importance of combating " terrorism and extremism " . <p> The supreme leader of Iran -- a key backer of Mr Assad 's administration before it fell to the rebels -- on Dec 22 predicted " the emergence of a strong , honourable group " that would stand against " insecurity " in Syria . <p> A Syrian soldier inspects a makeshift lab used by the Assad regime to manufacture a psychostimulant drug on an industrial scale.PHOTO : AFP <p> Mr Ayatollah Ali Khamenei said Syria 's young men would " stand with strength and determination against those who have designed this insecurity and those who have implemented it , and God willing , he will overcome them " . <p> Mr Assad had long played a strategic role in Iran 's " axis of resistance " , a loose alliance of regional proxy forces , particularly in facilitating the supply of weapons to Hezbollah in neighbouring Lebanon . <p> That axis has suffered heavy blows over the past year with Israel 's decimation of the leadership of Hezbollah in Lebanon and Hamas in Gaza . <p> Mr Khamenei nonetheless denied that these armed groups acted as proxies , adding that : " If one day we want to take action , we do not need a proxy force .
