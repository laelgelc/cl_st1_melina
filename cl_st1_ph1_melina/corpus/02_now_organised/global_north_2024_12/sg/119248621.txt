<p> KUALA LUMPUR : More Malaysians approve of Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim and more also believe the country is heading in the right direction under his unity government , compared to a year ago , according to a study by an independent research firm . <p> It found that 39 per cent of respondents felt Malaysia was going in the right direction as of the end of November 2024 , an improvement from the 36 per cent in November 2023 . <p> " Sentiments show gradual improvement after a prolonged period of negativity , although negative sentiments remain oversized , " said the study by Merdeka Center , which published the results on Monday ( Dec 23 ) . <p> The study found that more than half ( 53 per cent ) of Malaysians believe their country is going in the wrong direction after two years under Anwar 's government , with almost half of them citing economic concerns . <p> This sentiment , however , is a slight improvement from Anwar 's one-year mark in November 2023 , when Merdeka Center found that 54 per cent of respondents thought Malaysia was going in the wrong direction . <p> Meanwhile , Anwar 's approval rating at his two-year mark stands at 54 per cent , a four percentage point improvement from his rating after one year . <p> Merdeka Center polled 1,207 registered voters across the country between Nov 27 and Dec 10 . The respondent mix was 52 per cent Malay , 29 per cent Chinese , 7 per cent Indian , 6 per cent Muslim bumiputera and 6 per cent non-Muslim bumiputera . <p> The latest results show more respondents believe Anwar has improved Malaysia 's image , attracted foreign investors and instilled efficiency and integrity in the civil service . <p> Respondents were split almost down the middle when asked whether Anwar was " working hard in strengthening the people 's economy " . <p> On the other hand , 39 per cent of them said they were dissatisfied with Anwar 's performance as of end-November 2024 , down from 43 per cent a year ago . <p> Merdeka Center co-founder and programmes director Ibrahim Suffian called concerns over economic pressures a " persistent " issue , in part because many Malaysians continue to struggle with rising costs of living . <p> " Although incomes have gradually improved since the COVID-19 period , prices have gone up and many still feel that need to stretch their ringgit , " he told CNA . <p> " Additionally , while people are hopeful for the investment pledges generated during the PM 's trips abroad , these things take time to be realised and trickle down to the average person . " <h> ECONOMY , INFLATION TOP VOTERS ' CONCERNS <p> The premier touted the RM254.7 billion ( US$56.7 billion ) in approved digital investments from January to September 2024 , saying this was expected to create 159,000 new jobs . <p> His government 's New Industrial Master Plan 2030 has also led to a 4.7 per cent increase in the manufacturing sector 's added value to the country 's domestic gross product , amounting to RM4.2 billion . <p> " The Madani government has implemented various plans to continue improving the economy throughout this year for the prosperity of the people and country , " he wrote . <p> Despite this , an overwhelming majority of respondents in the Merdeka Center survey who felt Malaysia was going in the wrong direction said they were concerned about the economy . <p> Most of them cited " unfavourable economic condition in general " as the reason , followed by high costs of living , minimum income and weakening of the ringgit . <p> Anwar may have done a " good job " in managing the economy , but people on the ground still feel the pinch from rising costs , said Azmi Hassan , a senior fellow at the Nusantara Academy for Strategic Research . <p> " The problem is , people are thinking - right now - the prices of goods are very high , their income seems to be static , and business is not growing . " <p> Of those who said Malaysia was going in the wrong direction , other reasons given were weak administration , politics , leadership and racial issues , the study found . <p> Nevertheless , Ibrahim said Anwar 's rating and public sentiment of Malaysia 's direction improved from a year ago as Malaysians appreciated the government 's efforts in reeling in investments . <p> " In addition , the feared petrol subsidy cut in 2025 will not hit most Malaysians , as it only gets levied on the top 15 per cent ( of earners ) , " he added . <p> Fifty-one per cent of respondents felt they were satisfied with the federal government 's overall performance as of end-November , although it gathered a more negative sentiment in four of the eight metrics measured . <p> As of October 2024 , more respondents felt they were dissatisfied with the government 's performance in generating economic growth , addressing cost of living issues , fighting corruption and maintaining the rule of law . <p> Azmi reckoned the improvement in Anwar 's rating was not due to domestic economic performance . " It is most probably because Datuk Seri Anwar has been very vocal on the international stage , not only on geopolitics , but also on the ( external ) economy , " he said . <p> For instance , Anwar has continued to press for Malaysia to become a full member of the BRICS group of emerging economies , and vocally protest Israel 's role in the Gaza war , Azmi said . <p> " I think that 's where people see that Datuk Seri Anwar has been working very hard to diversify our market and also to have a voice ( on the international stage ) , " he added . <p> Source : CNA/hz <h> Sign up for our newsletters <p> Get our pick of top stories and thought-provoking articles in your inbox
