Meanwhile , jets pounded the cities of Idlib and Aleppo , killing at least 15 people , said a group that operates in opposition-held areas . <p> The surge in fighting has raised the prospect of another violent , destabilising front reopening in the Middle East as Israel is fighting Hamas in Gaza and Hezbollah in Lebanon , conflicts that have repeatedly threatened to ignite an even wider regional war . It also risks drawing Russia and Turkey -- each with its own interests to protect in Syria -- into direct heavy fighting against each other . <p> An abandoned Syrian army armoured vehicle sits on a highway on the outskirts of Khan Sheikhoun , southwest of Aleppo.Credit : AP <p> The insurgents announced their offensive on Wednesday , just as a ceasefire between the Lebanese Hezbollah militant group and Israel began , raising hope that tensions in the region might calm . <p> The surprise offensive is a huge embarrassment for Assad , and it comes when his allies -- Iran and groups it backs and Russia -- are preoccupied with their own conflicts . <p> A statement from Assad 's office quoted Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi as saying Tehran was ready to support Damascus in its counteroffensive against the insurgency . <p> Advertisement <p> Loading <p> Araghchi appeared in a photograph with Syrian foreign minister Bassam Sabbagh in Tehran . However , Iranian state media also released a clip purportedly showing Araghchi dining in a restaurant in Damascus . <p> Arab leaders including Jordan 's King Abdullah II and United Arab Emirates President Sheikh Mohammed bin Zayed Al Nahyan have also called Assad to express their solidarity . <p> The insurgents took over most of Aleppo on Saturday and made gains in the surrounding province , including capturing a military academy and a strategic town that lies on the highway linking the city with Damascus and the coast . <p> They also took control of the main water pumping station for the city , which is no longer working , said Syrian Minister of Water Resources Moataz Qattan on the pro-government radio station Sham FM . <p> Elsewhere , rebel commander Colonel Hassan Abdulghani said the insurgents advanced in the countryside around Idlib , putting the province of the same name under their control . <p> They also claimed to have entered the city of Hama , but there was no independent confirmation of that . <p> Loading <p> Abdulghani said 65 Syrian troops were taken prisoner in eastern Aleppo . <p> In Khan Sheikhoun , in Idlib province , military vehicles abandoned by Syrian troops dotted roads . People posed and took pictures of themselves on an abandoned tank on a highway , while insurgents grabbed munitions and shells from them before continuing to push deeper into Syria . <p> The insurgents vowed to push all the way into Damascus , but life in the Syrian capital remained normal , with no signs of panic . In southeastern Aleppo , however , the main road out of the city was gridlocked as people fled the fighting , and petrol stations in the area were short on fuel . <p> US national security adviser Jake Sullivan told CNN 's State of the Union that the US , which has about 900 troops in Syria , is watching the situation carefully . The American forces , which are in the northeast and far from Aleppo , are guarding against a resurgence by the extremist Islamic State group . <p> The group leading the rebel advance is designated a terrorist organisation by the US , and Sullivan said Washington has " real concerns about the designs and objectives of that organisation " . <p> " At the same time , of course , we do n't cry over the fact that the Assad government , backed by Russia , Iran and Hezbollah , are facing certain kinds of pressure , " he added . <p> Syrian state news agency SANA and the Syrian Observatory for Human Rights said that overnight the army pushed back insurgents in the northern countryside of Hama province . <p> Syrian state media said government resupply included heavy equipment and rocket launchers while Syrian and Russian airstrikes targeted weapon depots and insurgent strongholds .
