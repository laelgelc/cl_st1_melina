<h> Great power rivalry <p> Syria : Ahmed al-Sharaa , the leader of the Islamist group in Syria that oversaw the ousting of Bashar al-Assad , has unveiled plans to disband his and other rebel groups and form a national army under the control of the defence ministry . <p> As the United Nations warned that the war " has not ended yet " , Sharaa , who heads Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham ( HTS ) , said Syria must " remain united " and find a place for all sects . He said forced conscription will end and only the army will be allowed to carry weapons . <p> " We have to adopt a state mentality , not an opposition mentality , " he said during a meeting with leaders from the country 's Druze minority . <p> Sharaa has been seeking to gain international legitimacy for HTS , which was once affiliated with Al Qaeda and is designated as a terrorist organisation by the UN , the United States and the European Union , all of which have had direct contact with the group since Assad fled to Russia . <p> HTS and its aligned forces , which are backed by T ? rkiye , control Damascus and most of Syria 's territory , but Kurdish and Turkish-backed forces control large parts of the north . <p> T ? rkiye , which has several thousand troops in Syria , has emerged as the most influential player in the country since Iran and Russia withdrew support for Assad . <p> Yasar Guler , T ? rkiye 's defence minister , said his country 's priority was to defeat Syria 's Kurdish forces and was open to providing military backing to the new government . <p> In northern Syria , Turkish-backed groups have been battling US-backed Kurdish fighters , who have led efforts to counter Islamic State . <p> Geir Pedersen , a UN envoy , warned the UN Security Council that further military escalation in the north could be " catastrophic " , calling for sanctions on Syria to be lifted and the terrorist designation of HTS to be reassessed . <p> " The needs are immense and could only be addressed with broad support , including a smooth end to sanctions , " he said . <p> In southern Syria , Israel has seized territory in the Golan Heights , saying it had set up a buffer zone to prevent rebels using the area to launch attacks . Israel also plans to expand settlements in parts of the Golan Heights , which it seized during a war with Syria in 1967 . <p> Sharaa , whose nom de guerre is Abu Mohammad al-Jolani , criticised Israel 's seizure of territory and attacks on Syrian weapons stocks but said he did not plan to enter " new confrontations " . <p> Meanwhile , Israeli officials and Hamas figures indicated that hopes are growing of reaching a ceasefire deal in Gaza . The deal would involve a 60-day pause in fighting , the phased release of Israeli hostages -- living and dead -- and the release of Palestinian prisoners from Israeli jails . <p> Israel invaded Gaza after Hamas militants entered southern Israel on October 7 , 2023 , killing 1200 people and taking 250 hostages . Israel 's attacks in Gaza have killed more than 45,000 people , according to local officials , who do not distinguish between civilians and militants . <p> Discussing the prospects of a ceasefire , White House spokesperson John Kirby told Fox News " we 're getting closer " . But he added : " We 've been in this position before where we were n't able to get it over the finish line . " <h> The neighbourhood <p> Vanuatu : A 7.3 magnitude earthquake caused damage across Vanuatu and prompted authorities to declare a state of emergency as rescuers struggled to reach people trapped under collapsed buildings . <p> The quake struck near the capital , Port Vila , about 12.47pm , local time , on Tuesday , leaving residents without power , water and communications due to damage to electricity and phone lines . Official reports confirmed 14 people had died and hundreds were injured , but the toll was expected to rise . <p> The quake is believed to be the most destructive in Vanuatu in decades , affecting an estimated 116,000 of the country 's 318,000 residents . Vanuatu sits on the Ring of Fire , a seismically active belt around the Pacific basin . <p> Amanda Thompson , a Vanuatu resident , told BBC News : " We often have earthquakes here but not like this . " <p> Glen Craig , from the Vanuatu Business Resilience Council , told Radio New Zealand the quake had collapsed buildings in the city centre and near the airport , but there was less damage in villages and around resorts . <p> " It 'll be a mass casualty event , but we 're unsure of the number because we ca n't access those buildings , " he said . <p> Australia , New Zealand and other countries have sent search and rescue teams and medical support . <p> " We 're closely monitoring the situation and stand ready to provide further assistance , " Australian Foreign Minister Penny Wong said in a post on X. <h> War zone <p> Ukraine : Vladimir Putin praised the Russian military this week for its successful march across Ukraine , as Moscow said it seized about 4500 square kilometres in 2024 and was gaining about 30 square kilometres a day . <p> Ukraine was believed to be behind the assassination this week of a Russian general , Igor Kirillov , who was killed outside an apartment building in Moscow by a bomb hidden in an electric scooter . Ukrainian officials told various media outlets that Kyiv was responsible for killing Kirillov , a general believed to have given orders to use chemical weapons . Britain imposed sanctions on him in October over reports he used toxic choking agents to disorient Ukrainian troops . <p> In Kursk , about 10,000 North Korean troops have been fighting for Russia to try to retake the territory . A US official said this week hundreds of North Korean troops have been killed or wounded . <p> In his final weeks as US president , Joe Biden eased restrictions on Ukraine 's use of long-range weapons to try to assist Kyiv . <p> Donald Trump , who has criticised US aid to Ukraine , indicated this week he may reverse Biden 's decision . <p> " I might , " he told reporters . " I think it was a very stupid thing to do . " <h> Spotlight : Trump 's crypto conversion <p> United States : During his first term as US president , Donald Trump did not hide his contempt for cryptocurrencies , describing them as " based on thin air " and a " disaster waiting to happen " . He dismissed Bitcoin , the first and best-known cryptocurrency , as " a scam " . <p> Trump 's views on cryptocurrency -- a digital currency that offers an alternative to national currencies -- reflected the consensus among economists . Nobel Prize-winning economist Paul Krugman has written that the only use for crypto assets is to buy other crypto assets or to commit money laundering , extortion or scams ; another laureate , Eric Maskin , has warned that cryptocurrencies could make it harder for central banks to control the money supply and prevent recessions . <p> But Trump has since changed tack . <p> Before the November election , he declared he will make the US " the crypto capital of the planet " . His campaign accepted donations in cryptocurrencies and a pro-crypto paragraph was included in the Republican National Committee platform . <p> Ahead of his inauguration , the president-elect has nominated Paul Atkins , a cryptocurrency advocate , to chair the Securities and Exchange Commission ( SEC ) and David Sacks , a former PayPal executive who is close to Elon Musk , to the new role of artificial intelligence and cryptocurrency tsar . The appointments signalled that Trump intends to ease regulations and end efforts by the SEC to regulate the industry . <p> Trump also wants the US to hold strategic reserves of cryptocurrencies -- a move that would stabilise their value and help to attract investment in them . <p> These various moves have prompted cryptocurrency values to soar . Since Trump was elected , Bitcoin 's value has leapt from about $68,000 to peaks above $100,000 . <p> Meanwhile , Trump faced a legal setback ahead of his inauguration on January 20 after a court rejected his bid to overturn his conviction for falsifying records . Trump 's lawyers claimed he was covered by presidential immunity . <p> Trump can still appeal and eventually bring the case to the nine-member Supreme Court , which includes six Republican-appointed judges who have already ruled that presidents should have broad immunity . <p> But such appeals could take weeks or months , making it increasingly likely that Trump will become the first president to enter office as a convicted criminal . <p> This article was first published in the print edition of The Saturday Paper on December 21 , 2024 as " De facto leader calls for Syria to ' remain united ' " . <p> For almost a decade , The Saturday Paper has published Australia 's leading writers and thinkers .
