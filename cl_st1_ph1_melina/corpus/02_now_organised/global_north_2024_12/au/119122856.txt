And any politician who does not like democracy in action , as long as it is peaceful , maybe we should really question their motivations . " <p> For over a century , the right to protest has been a fundamental pillar of Australia 's democracy . <p> It 's delivered the eight-hour workday , voting rights for women , native title rights and marriage equality -- among many other achievements . <p> Now , in the context of regular pro-Palestinian demonstrations , civil rights advocates like Mejia-Canales say this right is under renewed attack . <h> The post-October 7 climate <p> on 7 October 2023 killed almost 1,200 people , a devastating Israeli assault on Gaza has killed over 45,000 Palestinians and reduced much of the territory to rubble . <p> The series of weekly rallies held across the country in solidarity with the Palestinian people has become the biggest anti-war movement in Australia since the Iraq war and a significant thorn in the side of politicians . <p> But , alongside protests and debate in parliament , a ratcheting up of vandalism , <p> Off the back of the attack , Victoria 's Premier Jacinta Allan has flagged that her government intends to legislate a number of anti-protest measures , including banning the use of face masks at protests , banning flags of groups listed as terrorist organisations and banning the use of glue and lock-on devices at protests . <p> Allan also claimed that antisemitism and hate were thriving within the pro-Palestinian movement . <p> " It would be wrong for me to say that everyone who 's attending these protests is antisemitic . There 's no one saying that . But we know that some are , and they are showing that . We also know that hate and antisemitism are thriving in these environments , " Allan said . <p> Pro-Palestinian protest organisers have denied the link between their protests and incidents like the Adass Israel attack . <p> Palestine Action Group organiser Amal Naser said : " The attack of a synagogue is an abhorrent and racist act that should be condemned and has been widely condemned . " <p> " However , there has been no police investigation or conclusion which suggests that it has anything to do with the protest movement . " <p> Ohad Kozminsky , an executive member of the Jewish Council of Australia , is critical of Victoria 's protest crackdown . Source : Supplied <p> Ohad Kozminsky , executive member of the Jewish Council of Australia -- a group formed in the last year by progressive Jewish academics , activists and lawyers -- said the laws were counterproductive . <p> " There are laws against hate speech . There are laws against firebombing and the destruction of property . We do n't need additional laws to fight that , " Kozminsky said . <p> The proposed measures also include banning protests outside of places of worship , something that Kozminsky also thinks is misguided . <p> " Religious institutions , irrespective of their denomination or their faith are legitimate sites of protest . We saw that perhaps most strikingly in the protests that took place in response to revelations about institutional , historic and contemporary sexual abuse of young people , of children , " he said . <p> Mejia-Canales said : " If the premier thinks that these measures criminalising peaceful protest is going to fix antisemitism and other forms of racism , then she 's deluded . <p> " This will not do that . " <h> A long history of protest crackdowns <p> The Victorian government 's approach fits into a broader context of governments across Australia cracking down on protester rights , Mejia-Canales said . <p> " We are now seeing that Victoria is following the example of NSW and South Australia and Queensland in some respects , trying to rush to implement these really severe anti-protest laws , " he said . <p> The NSW government passed legislation in 2022 that banned protesters from disrupting certain major roads , bridges , tunnels , public transport and infrastructure facilities . <p> Meanwhile , South Australia currently has the toughest financial penalties with fines of up to $50,000 for protesters who intentionally obstruct a public place . <p> Mejia-Canales said there 's nothing new about the government trying to make it difficult for citizens to demonstrate publicly . <p> " It 's been happening anytime that someone seeks to challenge power , " he said . <p> " Queensland banned all protests in the streets in the ' 70s . The suffragists , the women , the very brave women who were traversing the country , seeking petitions to give women the vote , they faced some severe repression in the same way that climate and environment protesters are being repressed today . " <p> A recent study from researchers at the University of Bristol in the United Kingdom has found that Australia is the world leader when it comes to arresting these environmental protesters . <p> Their findings show Australia 's arrest rate was higher than all other countries measured , with more than 20 per cent of protests involving arrests , more than three times the global average of 6.3 per cent . <h> ' Increasingly difficult to be a law-abiding , peaceful protester ' <p> One of those activists who has found herself arrested on a number of occasions is 58-year-old Liz Conor , a climate activist and associate professor of history at La Trobe University . <p> Conor said she finds it odd that the Victorian government is now seeking to ban materials like glue and lock-on devices in an attempt to combat antisemitism as they are well-known for being used primarily by climate activists to target the infrastructure of fossil fuel companies . <p> " I see them as more associated with protests that go directly for that corporate infrastructure , " she said . <p> " So I think that 's pretty interesting that we have a Labor state government bringing in laws that target the very kind of protest techniques that are much more about targeting corporate infrastructure than about public disruption . " <p> Liz Conor joined forces with other climate activists to found The Climate Guardian Angels in 2013 , a group of about 100 activists who sought to bring more media attention to the climate crisis .
