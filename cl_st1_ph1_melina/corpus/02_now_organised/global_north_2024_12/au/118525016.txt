It 's also a long time since the ADF operated without air superiority - and never without cyber dominance . <p> In the UK , I was told that our infantry trainers , sent to train Ukrainian soldiers , had little understanding of the drone and artillery-dominated conflict in Ukraine - or of modern urban warfare , although our basic infantry tactics were a sound basis to build on . <p> So what can we learn from Gaza and Ukraine ? They are very different environments , but common lessons can be learned from both conflicts in the areas of asymmetric warfare , military technology , logistics , and intelligence . <p> In Gaza , Palestinian militants , primarily Hamas , have used guerrilla tactics to challenge Israel 's far superior military capabilities . Tactics such as rocket attacks , sniping from concealed tunnel entrances , ambushes , and basing their command and control in civilian areas , have allowed Hamas to sustain its fight against Israel 's military forces . Despite Israel 's air superiority and seemingly limitless supply of American ordnance , Hamas ' use of hit-and-run tactics , improvised explosive devices , and concealment underground have complicated Israeli operations . <p> Israel 's Iron Dome missile defence system has successfully intercepted most of the rockets fired from Gaza and minimised Israeli civilian casualties . However , Palestinian militants in Gaza are now launching larger and more complex volleys of rockets that challenge the Iron Dome 's effectiveness . <p> In Ukraine , the Ukrainian military has used unconventional warfare to counter Russia 's far larger military force . Ukraine 's use of small mobile units , drones , modern anti-tank weapons , sabotage tactics , and diversionary attacks have disrupted Russia 's conventional , large-scale advances . <p> Both conflicts have underlined the importance of advanced technology in warfare . In Ukraine , drones - both for surveillance and attack - have become integral to Ukraine 's land , sea , and air operations . Meanwhile , commercial off-the-shelf drones have demonstrated the growing role of low-cost multiple-use civilian technologies in modern warfare . <p> Western precision-guided missile systems like HIMARS , Storm Shadow , and ATACMS have proven highly effective in disrupting Russian logistics and command structures . ( The ADF has ordered 42 HIMARS with delivery to begin in 2025 . ) <p> There is much to be learned from the conflict in Gaza . Picture Getty Images <p> On the Russian side , advanced missile systems like Iskander , Kalibr , and Kinzhal have been used for devastating strikes on Ukrainian military and civilian infrastructure . <p> The integration of these missile systems has dramatically shaped the dynamics of the conflict , with missile warfare playing a key role in shaping both sides ' strategies . <p> Russia 's most effective weapons against Ukrainian armoured vehicles include anti-tank guided missiles ( such as Kornet and TOW ) , direct fire ( from T-90 and T-80 tanks ) , and multiple rocket launchers like the Tornado-G . The US gave Ukraine 31 M1A1 Abrams tanks but only about 10 remain serviceable . The remainder have been lost , mostly to Kornet missiles . ( Australia is to donate 49 M1A1s to Ukraine . ) <p> Russia 's inability to quickly resupply troops on the front lines , combined with low morale among its soldiers , has been a constant challenge for Russian commanders . Without efficient supply lines and motivated soldiers , it will always be difficult to sustain effective combat operations . <p> Ukraine , on the other hand , has benefited from significant international support , including arms shipments , financial aid , and humanitarian assistance - and its forces are well motivated . At the same time , trying to support a variety of unfamiliar equipment , including Australia 's Bushmaster-protected mobility vehicle , has complicated operations and proved to be a maintenance nightmare . <p> In Gaza , the IDF 's logistic capabilities , short supply lines , and US support have allowed Israeli forces to maintain continuous operations , despite the challenges posed by Hamas ' exploitation of ruined urban environments , Israeli hostages , tunnel networks , and decentralised command structures . <p> Intelligence has proved once again to be a key factor . In Ukraine , the US has provided real-time satellite imagery , signals intelligence , and data from various surveillance platforms that have helped Ukraine anticipate Russian movements and target high-value Russian assets . <p> In Gaza , Israel 's ability to collect intelligence through human agents , drones , sigint , and electronic surveillance has allowed it to target Hamas ' leadership and infrastructure , even when hidden in civilian areas or outside Gaza . <p> Information warfare has played a key role in both conflicts . Both Israel and Ukraine are vulnerable to withdrawal of external logistic support . Ukraine 's ability to shape global narratives and maintain domestic morale through the use of social media , videos , and personal testimonies has proved crucial . <p> Russia , meanwhile , has used disinformation campaigns to justify its actions and influence public opinion , both at home and abroad . <p> The information war is also important in Gaza . Both Hamas and Israel have utilised social media platforms to shape local perceptions , mobilise support , and influence international opinion . The real-time broadcasting of airstrikes , civilian casualties , and military operations has exposed the conflict to a global audience . Israel 's use of dumb bombs , withholding food aid , and the sheer scale of destruction in Gaza , have raised international concerns about disproportionate use of force and genocide against the Palestinian population - with more than 44,000 civilians killed , mainly women and children . <p> In Ukraine , Russian forces have faced similar accusations of deliberately targeting civilian infrastructure , including hospitals , schools , and residential areas . The siege of cities like Mariupol and Kharkiv , and the indiscriminate use of artillery , have led to widespread civilian casualties , with more than 12,000 killed . <p> Both conflicts offer valuable insights for military planners . It 's clear that success in modern warfare is n't solely about military superiority , but also about adaptability , minimising collateral damage , thinking outside the box , and sustaining domestic and international support . <p> A complication for Australian planners is that the ADF is structured as a balanced joint force for the defence of Australia - although Australia seems unlikely to be at risk from an external actor . Realistically , the ADF 's most likely combat role will be in support of US expeditionary operations , providing Army infantry/SF units , RAN destroyers , and RAAF ground attack aircraft .
