<p> In mid-October , a startling photograph appeared online out of northern Gaza . <p> It showed dozens of Palestinian men stripped to their underwear and sitting or squatting in a street surrounded by ruins in the north of the enclave . <p> WARNING Some readers might find details of this story disturbing <p> The photo was taken during Israel 's intense and deadly siege of the town of Jabalia and its surrounds , and it quickly spread around the world , and added further fuel to arguments Israel was forcing tens of thousands out of the area never to return . <p> The ABC has tracked down one of the men pictured among the destruction , who has shared what happened that day when he was separated from his family by the Israeli military . <p> Locals are generally scared to speak out against the Israel Defense Forces ( IDF ) , because they are worried they will be targeted in future strikes , if identified . <p> Another five residents of Jabalia , speaking on the condition of anonymity , have also shared their harrowing stories of what happened when they were forced out of the area . <p> Here is what they told us . <h> It was time to leave but there was no ' safe space ' <p> Ahmad Mahmoud Ka'karany loves northern Gaza . <p> Before the war between Hamas and Israel , he had been offered many jobs in the south -- but never wanted to leave . <p> Ahmad Mahmoud Ka'karany was forced to take shelter in the crowded Jabalia refugee camp . ( ABC News ) <p> The 65-year-old is a former employee of the Palestinian Authority , which was ousted from power in Gaza by Hamas in 2007 , and was an officer in the Palestinian Presidential Guard before retiring around the same time . <p> The war , now stretching into its second year , has cost him his home and his community . <p> Along with his family , he was forced to take shelter in the crowded Jabalia refugee camp . <p> Sitting just north of Jabalia , it is the largest camp in the Palestinian territory and is home to hundreds of thousands of people . <p> " When you go down to the market , there was no room to walk . It was completely overpopulated , " he said . <p> " There 's no safe place in Gaza , from Rafah to Beit Hanoun there 's not a single safe inch . <p> " Whoever tells you about safe areas , safe places , these are all lies and just distractions . " <p> The reality on the ground in Gaza makes assessing the true death toll an enormous challenge , but despite that , experts say it is not only recorded honestly , but also likely an under-count . <p> " The road was so dusty , the tanks were everywhere , and Israelis were on both sides of the road , " Faiza said . <p> " We could not turn to either side , if you dropped something , you could not pick it up out of fear . " <p> Anyone who tried to rest was shot at , according to Hala . <p> " We threw half of our things away while we were walking because we could not carry them anymore , " she said . <p> Faiza described the desperation and despair of others walking with them . <p> " There were two women who had thrown their old mother to the side of the road , " she said . <p> " The woman was dying , and one of her daughters shouted ' mum , forgive us that we throw you mum , and they kept walking. ? <p> " God have mercy on them ! " ? <h> Arriving in Gaza City , exhausted and anxious <p> As thousands continued to walk under the blazing sun , they arrived at another place where the IDF had dug holes in the ground . <p> Fighting has been raging for weeks in northern Gaza . ( ABC News ) <p> Faiza was among the women who were told to get in . <p> " My daughter started to ask me -- ' will they kill us mum ? ' " ? <p> " I told her ' no dear , sleep on my leg , ' and so she slept over my leg , and we all slept on the ground. ? <p> After being held there for some time , they were directed to move once more. ? <p> In groups of five , the women lined up in front of IDF personnel where they were searched and photographed. ? <p> " The Israelis were taking scans of our eyes , " Faiza said. ? <p> " Those women whose veils were over their eyes were told to lift them up , so their eyes could be scanned. ? <p> " Those who were wearing caps , were told to take them off . " ? <p> Faiza was then separated from her children. ? <p> " We walked and waited for our children , then walked some more and waited until they arrive , " she recounted. ? <p> " We walked so long , it was so tiring -- I have diabetes and high blood pressure , it was so sunny and tiresome for someone with diabetes and high blood pressure . <p> " The sun was hitting my head . I felt dizzy , and I did not have anything sweet or sugary to eat . <p> " I needed to drink water , but I was afraid to drink since there were no toilets . " <p> It was around 4pm when Faiza , along with the many other women and children , made it to Gaza City -- and to relative safety . <p> They were welcomed by other Palestinians , who greeted them with water and food . <p> " We were so tired we could hardly eat , " Faiza said . <p> " Some brought us bread with some za'atar -- they told us to eat something , even if just a bite of this bread . <p> " We sat for a while , then those who have relatives started calling them . <p> " Fouad brought me five bars of sweets so that my sugar will be balanced since I felt I would fall , I could not stand . <p> " I was shaking and shaking so much -- when I ate the sweets , I was better , thank god . " <p> The journey is seared into Hala 's memory . <p> " The road was difficult and horrifying , " she said . <p> " We 've seen death , we 've seen things that we never thought we will ever see . " ? <h> The men left behind <p> Youssef was among those still held at the school in Sheikh Zayed. ? <p> He said some of the men were stripped , dressed in white jumpsuits , and blindfolded as they were taken away. ? <p> Hours later , they lined up to have their photos taken by the IDF. ? <p> About 100 men , Youssef said , were cleared to leave . They then started their own long walk . <p> " An Israeli soldier gave two white flags to two of the men , " he said. ? <p> " He told one to hold it and remain at the front of the line , and the other to remain at the back of the queue . <p> " Those who would trespass the one in the front with the white flag will be shot , and those will go behind the one in the back with the white flag will be shot too . " <p> Then Youssef said they started their slow march south towards Gaza City , with 50 to 100 tanks watching their every move . <p> The IDF has released photos and video of lines of people leaving Jabalia , which appear to verify this account . <p> " The soldiers only gave us a bottle of water -- not one for each man , so we shared it so everyone could drink , " he said . <p> " The strange thing is that all soldiers were speaking Arabic . <p> " We did not know anything about the men they took -- we only saw them dressed in white and blindfolded . <p> " But we could not know where they were taken , if they interrogated them or not , we knew nothing about them . " <p> Youssef said between 100 and 150 men were taken away. ? <h> Waiting for a reunion <p> After a long day of travel , Faiza was still struggling to walk . <p> Help was at hand from some of the residents in Gaza . <p> Jabalia , in the north of Gaza , has suffered intense bombardments by Israel . ( Reuters : Stringer ) <p> " They brought a donkey cart that we climbed in , " she said . <p> " That shook us all over , although we had been through enough already . <p> " But this is what was available , so thank God for everything , that we were back safe . <p> " Men started to return too , and we are waiting for the rest to join us . <p> " Homes were bombed , but it is ok -- we do not want the clothes too , we do not want anything , we only want to men to come back to us safely if God wills it . <p> " All of them , I pray they come back unharmed . " ? <h> The Israeli response <p> The IDF said it was " committed to dismantling the military capabilities of the Hamas terrorist organisation " , and accused the group of hiding its people and operations among civilians . <p> The ABC asked the IDF a series of questions about the claims made by Palestinians forced to evacuate Jabalia . <p> The IDF did not provide specific responses to questions regarding its use of earthmoving equipment , retinal scans and photography . <p> It did respond to questions regarding gender segregation , and the treatment and detention of Palestinians -- but did not provide details about how many men had been detained and released during that specific operation . <p> " During the IDF 's operations in combat zones , individuals suspected of involvement in terrorist activities are detained and interrogated , " it said in a statement . <p> " Those found not to be involved in terrorism are released , as was the case here . <p> " Detainees are treated in accordance with international law . " <p> It said searches of Palestinians suspected of involvement with Hamas was standard practice . <p> " Where needed , IDF ground forces take appropriate security measures to ensure their safety , including searches , " it said in a statement . <p> " In some cases , detainees are required to remove clothing to check for concealed explosives or other weapons . According to IDF protocols , detainees are given their clothing back or provided with alternative clothing as soon as possible after the search . <p> " All soldiers are required to treat detainees with respect . Any instances where protocol was not followed are investigated . " <p> The IDF said its activity around Jabalia , and the refugee camp , had been prompted by intelligence Hamas was using medical facilities including the Kamal Adwan Hospital as hideouts and operations centres .
