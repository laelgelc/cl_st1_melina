<p> NEIL Mackay 's Big Read 's on population ( " A swing to the right on fertility " , December 15 ) addressed the issue of falling birth rates from an entirely one-sided point of view , requiring the reader to take for granted the assumption that declining population is a bad thing . However , there is a completely different perspective which takes account of the existential threat to humanity on our planet caused by a multiplicity of environmental crises , and which sees the prospect of declining population as a godsend and a necessity . <p> Scientists have judged that there are nine critical planetary processes with boundaries which define the safe limits for human pressure to maintain a stable and resilient Earth . They have assessed that for six of these , humanity has crossed these boundaries with increasing risk to human life on Earth . Most attention is focused on climate change , but the threats from five other pressures such as species depletion and extinction , diminishing freshwater health and availability , declining land for healthy natural processes , and pollution , are equally stark . <p> There are two major causes of these crises , our economic system based on massive industrialised production and the enormous increase in the human population in the last 100 years ( from two billion in 1928 to eight billion currently and still growing ) . The mindset of our culture remains wedded to unsustainable levels in both these areas as the article very well demonstrates . Such continuing human excess is simply a physical impossibility ; no amount of moaning by the right or the left or any political persuasion can alter the laws of biology , chemistry and physics . We can either manage a decline towards stability at a much-reduced level of production and population , or it will be imposed on us by catastrophe . <p> There is no sign at all that humanity will turn away from its addiction to economic growth , but declining fertility levels across the world are an indication of some hope for population size . As the Earth 's population is projected to grow to at least 10 billion this century , however , and with continuing unchecked economic growth , the decline in fertility rates can not come too quickly . <p> It is obvious that the stabilisation and then decline of population numbers will come with serious social problems , but the planetary disaster of accelerating overshoot of the Earth 's capacity to contain humanity is of an order of magnitude inconceivably greater . <p> If anyone is in doubt about the reality of this planetary crisis , a look at the work of the Stockholm Resilience Centre ( https : **30;54;TOOLONG ) should help . <p> I WAS disappointed to read that some Scottish companies are still exporting products to Russia following its illegal and unprovoked invasion of Ukraine and in spite of sanctions imposed by the West ( " Scotland exports ? 26m of drink to Russia since Ukraine war " , December 15 ) .
