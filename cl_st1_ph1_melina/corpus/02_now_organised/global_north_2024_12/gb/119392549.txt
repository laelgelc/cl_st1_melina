<p> The Halo Trust works around the world clearing land contaminated after war ( Image : Jonathan Buckmaster ) <p> Boats run by criminal gangs . Refugees stuck in expensive hotels . It 's easy to see why many people want the government to stop illegal migration . <p> As a retired Major General who runs one of Britain 's largest overseas charities , I can see a way to address the issue . <p> Rather than pay billions to keep refugees in hotels , the UK could invest more in programmes to help people stay closer to their homes in safety . <p> The HALO Trust was founded in Afghanistan in 1988 . We are famous for destroying landmines - thanks to Princess Diana , who visited us in Angola in 1997 . <p> But we destroy far more than landmines . We clear unexploded bombs and rockets . And we help secure rifles , pistols , and bullets . In short , we take care of anything that goes bang . How does this relate to the small boats crisis ? <p> Recent statistics tell us that the largest group of illegal migrants coming to the UK is from Afghanistan . Many come here because life is too dangerous for them at home . <p> Landmines and other explosives prevent farmers from growing crops and stop children walking safely to school . What if they did n't have to come here in the first place ? <p> Life in Afghanistan today is tough , particularly for women and girls , who are denied the most basic rights . Deadly landmines , however , are indiscriminate and blight the country for everyone . <p> If you remove these explosives , people can live in safety in their own country , without risking their lives at sea in search of a better future . <p> I have seen entire towns built on former battlegrounds or minefields in Afghanistan . I have talked to displaced Afghan families who have built new homes on land we have cleared of landmines . But handing over safe , cleared land is not enough . People also need livelihoods . <p> The HALO Trust was founded by two former Scottish soldiers who trained local people in Afghanistan to clear landmines to a professional standard . They rightly thought that employing locals was a better idea than paying high wages for Western military personnel to do the same thing . <p> Today , HALO has 12,000 highly trained women and men working in nearly 30 countries . In Afghanistan alone , we employ over 2000 staff . Each employee has about 20 dependents reliant on their salary . <p> So that 's well over 40,000 people able to stay in Afghanistan , supported by a decent local wage . But it does n't end there : we calculate that four million Afghans make a living on land made safe by HALO . <p> The British taxpayer has helped these Afghan families build a life in Afghanistan , rather than risking their lives on illegal boats or subsisting in limbo in hotels in British seaside resorts . <p> It costs the government just ? 2million a year to help HALO support over 40,000 Afghans and keep many , many more safely at home . Compare that sum to the millions of pounds Britain spends every day on housing Afghans here . <p> It 's a similar story for Syria , Iraq , and Libya , where HALO also works . And with Gaza under rubble and over a million people already displaced inside Lebanon , we know that more refugees will want to be on the move . <p> The Labour government has an opportunity to re-frame overseas aid as a form of defence , rather than altruism or ' woke tokenism ' . <p> By funding organisations like HALO to clear up after conflict , Sir Keir Starmer has a thrifty and humane solution that helps tackle migration at source . <p> You can find out more and support The Halo Trust 's life-saving work here .
