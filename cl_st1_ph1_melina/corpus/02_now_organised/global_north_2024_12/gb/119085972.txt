<h> Bereaved Israeli parents are fighting to overturn a legal ban on the use of sperm harvested after their sons ' death in combat <p> Women write to the father of Reef Harush all the time . Unsolicited , they offer to carry and raise the child of his fallen son . <p> " Reef was very romantic , " said Avi Harush , whose 20-year-old son died in combat in southern Gaza in April . " I 'd choose someone for him that would be his type -- someone smart , quiet , stable , intelligent and who likes to go out , like he did . " <p> Reef was one of four soldiers killed during a military operation , but the only one whose sperm was frozen in an attempt to preserve his future generations . " An hour after they told us Reef was seriously injured and being evacuated to hospital , the army called and said he had died on the way , " his father said . <p> Harush died in a military operation in Gaza last April <p> " They offered to freeze his sperm . We said yes immediately when the commander asked . None of the others who died had the chance . So it felt like a gift . " <p> While Reef 's sperm sits frozen in a capsule in hospital free of charge , his parents can not use it under the current law , which does not allow for posthumous in vitro fertilisation to create a child with anyone other than their female partner . It is a law that parents are pushing to change . <h> Advertisement <p> " A lot of women come to me to offer themselves on Facebook and Instagram , " Avi Harush said . " Many , many , many . Since I wrote on Facebook about Reef 's sperm being frozen , 48 women have offered themselves up who want to be the mother of Reef 's child . " <p> After someone dies , there is about a 36-hour window from which to cryopreserve sperm -- although there have been cases where it has been preserved after longer , as even after death living sperm can still be produced . The sooner it is retrieved , the better the quality and more likely it is to lead to successful conception . <p> Even the act of freezing the sperm of a loved one was not a given until the Hamas attacks on October 7 , which led to the current war in Gaza . Since then , some 318 soldiers and civilians have had their sperm frozen for future use by partners or would-be grandparents , should they embark on a legal process to allow them to use it , or should the law be changed in the future . <p> The military began offering the option after Shaylee Atary , whose husband Yahav Winner was murdered in the early hours of October 7 in the border community of Kfar Aza , begged doctors to save what they could of him . She realised , as she sat shiva , the seven days of Jewish mourning , that their new baby was all she had left and she would not have another from him . <p> Shaylee Atary fought to preserve the sperm of her husband , Yahav Winner , who was killed on October 7 <p> " We had such a short time window , " she said . " By the time I realised I wanted another child , I had very little time left . I was able to hold off on crying and the breaking down of my whole reality in my heart for a few more days . <h> Advertisement <p> " I ran screaming to Shura army base , where they kept all the bodies , and then other mothers helped me and screamed it all through the night . I screamed wherever I could because the time was so short . But somebody heard me , because I got lawyers and a court order signed . Then we found the body and got him back to hospital -- all while the fighting was still going on . " <p> Despite the burned condition of Winner 's body , which was found next to their baby 's charred crib , they succeeded in harvesting his sperm . However , the quality was not good enough to preserve it for future use . <p> " Still , I 'm happy that I 've changed the story for other people , that I did n't waste the mourning period by just breaking down and crying -- I spent it on something that is worth doing , " Atari said of her efforts to make the option known . <p> " Maybe , you know , outside of Israel , people do n't need to freeze sperm , but here in Israel , people die a lot . Not only because of wars , but there are a lot of rockets through the years , terror attacks -- there 's not a week that goes by without something like that . " <p> The question of parents harvesting ova and sperm post-mortem with anyone other than their late child 's spouse is not addressed by Israeli law and falls in a moral grey area . There is no formal paper to sign when a young soldier joins the army , nor any Jewish law either permitting or prohibiting its use . <h> Advertisement <p> Seeking permission to use his sperm would be a long process for Reef Harush 's family <p> " Right now , we can go to court , and then we have to prove that Reef wanted to be and had to be a dad , but it 's a process that takes a lot of years , " Avi Harush said . " It can happen . It has happened . But then , there are others who were rejected from using the sperm . The Knesset Israeli parliament is debating it . " <p> Baruch Ben Igal , whose son Amit was killed during a West Bank raid in 2020 , has been waiting for a grandchild ever since he asked the army to keep his son 's sperm for future use -- the first time such a request had been made by a would-be grandparent . <p> " When they knocked on my door , I was alone . Amit was my only child , " said Ben Igal , who signed permission for his son to serve in the military . " I thought when he died , then the army will give me his sperm and we 'll find a woman who wants children and she will give us a grandchild . " <p> He is still waiting .
