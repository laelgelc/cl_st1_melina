<h> Yousaf was unsure of SNP future last week - what changed ? <p> It was a far more relaxed and smiling Humza Yousaf who was interviewed by political correspondent Rebecca McCurdy last week . ( Image : Gordon Terris/NQ ) <p> Humza Yousaf had trepidation for 2025 when I interviewed him last week . I left his Holyrood office more convinced than ever that his time in politics was coming to an end . <p> Since his resignation as Scotland 's first minister , it was rare for Yousaf to accept an interview request from print media in Scotland . <p> In fact , to my knowledge , the in-depth sit down chat I had with the Glasgow Pollok MSP must have been one of the only ones he has agreed to since resigning in May . <p> There has been plenty of discussion around whether Yousaf - and his predecessor Nicola Sturgeon - will try to hold onto their Scottish Parliament seats at the 2026 election . <p> We now know that Yousaf wo n't . At the ripe old age of 39 , he wants to make way for the " next generation " of politicians . <p> Yousaf said this line to me last Thursday as we met in his MSP office on Thursday . He sits in a room hidden away at the end of a corridor filled with offices populated by his SNP colleagues - an odd contrast considering he led the party just seven months ago . <p> It 's an odd statement for someone to make before they turn 40 . But by 2026 , Yousaf will have been an MSP for 15 years - a government minister covering transport , justice and health for 12 of those , and first minister for just more than a year . <p> Yousaf was keen to give nothing away on his political future , only to say that he would make his mind up for the SNP deadline of February 2025 . <p> He told me he looked forward to the future " with hope " , speaking of his ambition to move into conflict resolution , peace-making , or countering the rise of the far right . <p> But equally he admitted having " trepidation " and " anxiety " when considering his future in frontline politics . <p> ( Image : Gordon Terris/NQ ) The former first minister had spoken very candidly to me about the highs and lows of his time in the top job . <p> The unknown future of his relatives in Gaza , leaving office and the ongoing challenges of Operation Branchform were up there with the biggest lows in his career . <h> Why are you making commenting on The Herald only available to subscribers ? <p> It should have been a safe space for informed debate , somewhere for readers to discuss issues around the biggest stories of the day , but all too often the below the line comments on most websites have become bogged down by off-topic discussions and abuse . <p> heraldscotland.com is tackling this problem by allowing only subscribers to comment . <p> We are doing this to improve the experience for our loyal readers and we believe it will reduce the ability of trolls and troublemakers , who occasionally find their way onto our site , to abuse our journalists and readers .
