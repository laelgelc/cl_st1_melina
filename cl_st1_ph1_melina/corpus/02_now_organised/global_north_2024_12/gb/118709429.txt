<h> MY ACCOUNT <h> Four things we need to know about Netanyahu 's trial before forming an opinion <p> The trial could become a critical test for Israel 's increasingly fragile democracy <p> Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu appears before the District Court in Tel Aviv to testify for the first time in his corruption trial ( Photo : Chaim Goldberg /Flash90/ Pool/Anadolu via Getty Images ) <p> The court proceedings mark the first time that a sitting Israeli Prime Minister will be tried as a criminal defendant , with charges centring on claims he gave regulatory favours and diplomatic support to prominent businessmen in exchange for luxury gifts and favourable media coverage . <p> Describing the charges as a politically motivated " witch-hunt " orchestrated by rivals and the media , Netanyahu has pleaded not guilty . <p> The case adds to the Israeli prime minister 's growing list of legal issues , as he navigates an international arrest warrant imposed by the International Criminal Court amid the ongoing bombardment of Gaza . <h> Netanyahu is taking the stand in three criminal cases <p> The trial comprises three separate cases , all of which began in 2020 : <p> Case 1000 , the " Gifts Affair " : Netanyahu allegedly received nearly 700,000 shekels ( ? 153,000 ) worth of luxury gifts , including champagne and cigars , from Hollywood producer Arnon Milchan and Australian billionaire James Packer . In return , Netanyahu is accused of advancing Milchan 's business interests , including helping him secure a US visa . Milchan testified in 2020 , describing the gifts as part of a continuous " supply channel . " <p> Case 2000 : Prosecutors claim Netanyahu negotiated with Arnon Mozes , the owner of Yedioth Ahronoth , between 2008 and 2014 for more favourable coverage in exchange for supporting legislation to curb the influence of a rival newspaper , Israel Hayom . <p> Case 4000 : This case involves claims that Netanyahu granted regulatory favors worth 1.8bn shekels ( ? 394m ) to Bezeq Telecom in exchange for positive coverage of himself and his wife , Sara , on a news website owned by the media company 's chairman , Shaul Elovitch . <h> Critics claim Netanyahu has used Gaza war to delay court hearings <p> The authorities first began looking into Netanyahu 's conduct in 2016 , with the police recommending he should be prosecuted in 2018 , before the trial began in 2020 . <p> Netanyahu said in court on Tuesday : " I have waited eight years for this moment , " before adding , " I am shocked by the magnitude of this absurdity . " <p> He added : " I am the Prime Minister , I am running a country , I am running a war ... I am not occupying myself with my future , but rather with that of the State of Israel . " <p> A man takes cover from an Israeli air strike in Gaza City ( Photo : Omar al-Qataa/AFP/Getty ) <p> His lawyers had sought to delay his testimony , arguing that the Gaza war had disrupted his preparations . Israel 's top court later rejected a request for another delay , saying he had already been given five months to prepare . <p> Some experts claim that Netanyahu may be using the conflict to postpone legal proceedings . <p> Noam Ostfeld , principal analyst at geopolitical risk consultancy Sibylline , told The i Paper : " The war has delayed the possibility for prosecution . It is possible the court could eventually remove him from power , but this is very far away . " <p> He added : " Officials will want to prioritise the war , and prosecuting Netanyahu during war is not beneficial to this aim . It could also benefit extremists on the far right in Israel . " <h> A verdict could take years -- but he could face jail time if found guilty <p> The trial is expected to continue for years , and Netanyahu will likely take the stand several times a week for several months . <p> He is also reportedly hoping for a plea deal , which could again cause further delays . Israeli law allows a Prime Minister to remain in office during any appeals process , which means even if convicted , he could remain in power . <p> Meanwhile , critics fear Netanyahu 's allies could introduce legislation to shield him from prosecution entirely , while some analysts also believe Israeli President Isaac Herzog might pardon him if convicted . <p> However , according to Israeli law , fraud and breach of trust can result in prison sentences of up to three years , while bribery charges can result in up to 10 years in jail and a fine . <h> Netanyahu has attempted to overhaul judiciary ahead of court hearings <p> Following the Israeli election in 2022 , Netanyahu 's Likud party relied on the far-right to form a majority government . This led to a campaign to curb the powers of the court , sparking mass protests in Israel . <p> Netanyahu denied any link between the judicial overhaul and his trial . Analysts claim that the Israeli Prime Minister is beholden to the far-right who prop up his coalition -- with many from this faction eager to clamp down on the judiciary , who often rule against expansions of Israeli settlements into the occupied West Bank . <p> Professor Yossi Mekelberg , associate fellow in the Middle East and North Africa programme , Chatham House told The i Paper : " If the protests and pressure from the media can be sustained , it might draw a reaction from him . <p> But he added : " The far right are not going to change , they are happy to prop up Netanyahu , this is their opportunity to be in power and affect direction of the war , by occupying and expanding Israeli settlements into Gaza . " <p> Israeli public opinion on whether Benjamin Netanyahu should continue serving while on trial remains sharply divided , contributing to a political deadlock that has driven Israel to five elections in under four years . <p> With the next Israeli election not scheduled until October 2026 and a trial that could stretch on for years , Netanyahu 's trial could become a critical test for Israel 's increasingly fragile democracy , with far-reaching consequences beyond its own borders .
