" We were drinking salty water . My daughter , Mira , suffered from dehydration , but I could n't do anything to help her . " <p> Despite their lack of food , water and medicine and the relentless bombardment and siege , Almasri had refused to leave Beit Lahia , saying she had no other safe place to go . <p> ' I would give most of my food to my children , but they were still hungry , always crying ' <p> - Jacqueline Almasri , Gaza resident <p> But last week , following Israeli military evacuation orders , she was forced to flee her latest temporary shelter at the school alongside other evacuees , with nothing but the children in her arms . <p> " Many people were injured due to the quadcopter bullets , " Almasri said . " There were no ambulances , no medicine - only first aid . People tried to cover their wounds and escape . " <p> They walked through Israeli checkpoints near the Indonesian Hospital , where the troops separated men from their families to question them . <p> Although only 15km between Beit Lahia and Gaza City , it took Almasri five hours to walk with her children in tow due to their worn-out shoes , and the rough and uneven road . <p> " I wanted to rest in Salah al-Deen Street along the way , but the snipers were shooting close to us . I was afraid for my children , " she said . <p> They now live in a cramped tent with five relatives on al-Wihda Street . <h> ' Most devastating thing I 've witnessed ' <p> Linah Alradiya , 17 , also sought shelter in Beit Lahia 's schools with six members of her family after fleeing their home near the border of northern Gaza . The schools were overcrowded , with no clean water or sufficient food supplies . <p> " We were drinking salt water after the clean water ran out , " Alradiya told MEE . " No one dared leave the schoolyard because the drones and quadcopters would shoot randomly into the streets . " <p> She added : " When people were injured in the school or the neighbourhood , they received first aid via a displaced nurse in the school , because ambulances and the civil defence were prevented from reaching people in the northern Strip . " <p> Alradiya narrowly survived one such attack last week while helping collect water outside the school . An eight-year-old girl was shot in the neck . <p> " I carried her , crying , to the medical corner , but her bleeding was uncontrollable . She was vomiting blood and passed away in my arms , " Alradiya told MEE . " Her death was the most devastating thing I 've witnessed in my entire life . " <p> Linah Alradiya , 17 , pictured at al-Wihda street in Gaza City before finding shelter at a relative 's tent ( MEE/Ahmed Dremly ) <p> On 4 December , as Alradiya and her sisters were kneading dough to bake bread , quadcopter drones appeared , ordering everyone to leave within five minutes . <p> " The evacuees in the school shouted at the drone to give us an hour to gather our belongings , but it started to drop sound bombs , " Alradiya told MEE . " We left the dough and fled with nothing . " <p> After a harrowing four-hour walk , Alradiya 's family arrived in Gaza City . Her father and three brothers were detained briefly at the military checkpoint but later released . <p> The family now shares a makeshift tent with 20 relatives on al-Wihda Street . They struggle to stay warm during the cold nights . <p> " The war has destroyed my life , " Alradiya said . " I was invited and supposed to compete in an international swimming competition in Tokyo last year . Now , I just hope for a ceasefire to rebuild my future . " <h> ' I prefer to die on my land ' <p> Many residents of Beit Lahia chose to stay put , preferring the risk of death to abandoning their land and homes , despite enduring immense suffering - including fear , hunger and lack of medicine . <p> For Howaida Kilani , 62 , a mother of seven , the consequences have been catastrophic . She lived in Beit Lahia with four deaf daughters , another with cerebral palsy , and two sons . <p> ' She died . My daughter died because of famine , lack of medicine and sheer terror ' <p> - Howaida Kilani , Gaza resident <p> On 11 March , an Israeli air strike destroyed her home while half her family was inside . <p> One of her daughters-in-law was killed , and four other members of her family were severely injured . She and her five daughters evacuated to a relative 's house in another area of Beit Lahia . <p> " From the start of the war , I decided to separate my family into different locations , " Kilani told MEE . " If someone dies , at least the others might have a chance to survive . " <p> When the Israeli ground incursion began in northern Gaza on 6 October , Kilani used sign language to persuade her daughters to evacuate to Gaza City . But they refused . <p> " They did n't want to leave . I also prefer to die on my land in Beit Lahia rather than flee , but as a mother , I was desperate with worry for my daughters , " she said . <p> " She was terrified by the bombings , " Kilani recalled . " We used to give her sedatives to help her sleep , but those ran out , so she stayed awake all night . She starved along with us . Her skin began to develop unexplained infections . " <p> Howaida Kilani ( centre ) with her four deaf daughters pictured in Gaza City ( MEE/Ahmed Dremly ) <p> On the afternoon of 2 November , Kilani 's 26-year-old daughter , Abeer , noticed Fatima 's body had gone cold and rigid . Panicking , the family rushed her to Kamal Adwan Hospital on a donkey cart . But it was too late . <p> " She died , " Kilani told MEE , her voice trembling . " My daughter died because of famine , lack of medicine , and sheer terror . " <h> ' We just want to live ' <p> The loss of Fatima devastated Kilani , who has heart disease and diabetes . The tragedy caused her blood sugar levels to plummet , further deteriorating her health . <p> The family decided to evacuate from their relative 's home to a nearby school , believing it might be safer among other displaced families . But the situation worsened . <p> In the early hours of 4 December , the sound of tanks and relentless gunfire drew closer . <p> " A bullet came through the window of a nearby classroom and struck a 45-year-old mother in the neck , " Kilani recounted . " She died instantly . " <p> Her daughters were inconsolable . <p> " They were crying , terrified . They could n't understand what was happening , " she said . " I explained in sign language that we had to leave immediately . " <p> The family 's journey to Gaza City was harrowing . Along the way , Israeli troops detained Kilani 's husband and two sons , releasing them at night after hours of questioning . <p> " It was like doomsday , " she said . <p> Now , Kilani and her family live in a tent on a patch of land in Gaza City 's Omar al-Mukhtar neighbourhood , struggling to survive with limited supplies and an uncertain future . <p> " I appeal to everyone in the world : help us call for a ceasefire , " she pleaded . <p> " We just want to live - even on the rubble of our house .
