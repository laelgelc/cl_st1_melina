In 2013 , it became the scene of fierce battles between Syrian rebels and then-president Bashar al-Assad 's army , both of which fought alongside allied Palestinian groups . <p> Once in opposition hands , the camp was besieged and became a favoured target of Assad 's air force and the Russian jets aiding him . <p> To make matters worse , by 2015 most of Yarmouk had been overrun by the Islamic State group ( IS ) . Only a few hundred residents remained and Ainah was one of them . <p> " The air strikes were the worst things , but everyone left when Daesh was here , " he says , using the Arabic acronym for IS . " They would kill anyone that stood in their way . " <p> Once one of the nicest parts of Damascus , Yarmouk was destroyed during Syria 's war ( Daniel Hilton/MEE ) <p> Assad 's siege on Yarmouk was infamous . A photograph of residents queueing for food in its shredded street became one of the war 's defining images . <p> " It was a tough time , " says Ainah . " I looked everywhere for food , searching the streets for anything even if it was rotten . " <p> The 60-year-old Palestinian also had to learn to spend a lot of time on his own . Ainah 's family fled this part of Damascus when the bombing began but he insisted on staying behind to look after his property . <p> " I knew if I left my house for one second it would be taken by someone else . Thank God it was not bombed , " he says . " I was alone . It was very difficult to be alone . " <h> Palestinians in Syria <p> Unlike neighbouring countries , in 1956 Syria gave Palestinians almost exactly the same rights as Syrians , allowing them to be employed in all sectors . That allowed Yarmouk to flourish . <p> Assad and his father Hafez , who ruled Syria from 1971 until his death in 2000 , also portrayed themselves as allies of the Palestinian cause , allowing some Palestinian factions to operate from Damascus . <p> In November , an Israeli strike on an office in Damascus ' Mezzeh belonging to the Islamic Jihad killed several of the Palestinian group 's fighters . <p> Posters of their faces decorate crumbling concrete walls along Yarmouk 's main street . <p> Khaled Meshaal , a former leader of Hamas , was based in the camp until he broke with Assad after refusing to condemn the Syrian revolution . <p> And while some Palestinians have fought for the toppled government , many have been its victims . <p> Yarmouk resident Mohammed Mahmoud ( Daniel Hilton/MEE ) <p> More than 4,300 Palestinian refugees have been killed during the war and over 3,000 detained , according to the UK-based Action Group for Palestinians of Syria ( AGPS ) . <p> Ayman Sakhnin , a 52-year-old Yarmouk resident , said Assad treated Palestinians decently before the conflict . " But this war was on everyone , Palestinians and Syrians , " he says . <p> " The revolution is good for people 's rights and it is a beautiful thing . " <h> ' Everything is terrible ' <p> Since Assad retook Yarmouk in 2018 , residents like Sakhnin have begun to return . Some shops on the main thoroughfares are open again , but not all of them have four walls . <p> Sixty percent of the camp 's buildings are damaged or totally destroyed , according to AGPS . <p> Unrwa , the United Nations ' agency for Palestinian refugees , is providing some services for the 8,000 people living among the rubble , like healthcare and education . <p> But residents say they are lacking the absolute basics : water , electricity and enough food to live . <p> So far , a few rebels from the camp have come back home but residents say they are yet to be visited by representatives of the new interim government . <p> " Everything is terrible , " says Mohammed Mahmoud , a 25-year-old whose family were expelled from Safed in what is now northern Israel . <p> Mahmoud is a construction worker but his own home is a ruin . " My house has no windows or doors . It 's like living on the street . " <p> And residents ca n't escape the comparisons with Gaza . Mahmoud says the images of slaughter and destruction from the Palestinian enclave are devastating for them . <p> " The same kind of evil is doing to Gaza what was done to Yarmouk , " says Ainah .
