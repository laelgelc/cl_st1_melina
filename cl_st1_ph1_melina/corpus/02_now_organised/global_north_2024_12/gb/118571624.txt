<h> Scottish university campuses remain powerful engines of enlightenment <p> Edinburgh Futures Institute during the Edinburgh International Book Festival <p> A CAMPUS can be a beautiful and powerful place to be . So all kudos to the Futures Institute ( EFI ) at Edinburgh University . <p> Its campus -- inhabiting the Old Royal Infirmary hospital building -- just won a Special Prize For Interiors in the Prix Versailles , the UN-backed global architecture award . <p> Any of you who swished around the EFI during the International Book Festival this year -- occupying their theatres , teeming cafes and modular hang-out spaces , sweeping up and down dramatic staircases -- will understand this award . <p> In the words of the Prix Versailles judges , " a new public space connects Edinburgh University to the city , unfurling a majestic opening for the benefit of the many " . <p> True -- though the EFI campus , in its flexibility and capacity , is primarily a crucible for multi-disciplinary work and innovation . Many of us will be hoping for extraordinary hybrids and brand-new paradigms to arise from there . <p> Search for " campus " in the news flows of the internet , however , and two aspects immediately rise to the surface . <p> One would be the aspiration that many projects , not just academic , have towards being a " campus " . The other would be the revival of the university campus as a site for protest and resistance . <p> Data centres from Malaysia to Louisiana , by way of a former aluminium smelter in Anglesey , are described in reports this week as " campuses " . Google just launched an " AI campus " for older school students in Camden , London . <p> Many of these plans envision buildings surrounded by trees , parks , public meeting spaces . They summon up the original ideal of the campus -- a congenial space where knowledge is generated . <p> As Wikipedia puts it , the campus tradition begins with medieval European universities , " where the students and teachers lived and worked together in a cloistered environment . " <p> It 's something of a stretch to apply these campus conditions to data centres , with their minimal staffing levels maintaining the infinite whirr of servers . <p> Unless we attribute intellectual power to these enablers of chat-bots and streaming services . <p> Which we should n't ... yet . <p> The term has more pertinence in the big corporate sector -- where locations like the GooglePlex or Apple Park ( below ) in California are rightly titled " campuses " . <p> ( Image : Arne M ? seler ) <p> Indeed , the Silicon Valley ideal is that workers are " cloistered " around their scintillating jobs . The foodbars , the spaces for gaming or chilling out , the sofas for power-napping -- all are intended for you to bring as much of your life-energy to work as possible . <p> ( For a local version of this , look at the promo videos for Barclays ' Glasgow Campus , on the banks of the Clyde . There are quiet rooms for the neurodivergent , fully equipped gyms , compost made in-house for the location 's greenery , crumbly artisanal bakeries ... Why would you ever go home ? ) <p> So there 's a cachet to " campus " that various commercial enterprises seek to invoke -- to my eyes , mostly unjustifiably . <p> As I recall the medieval definition of universities , they were " communities of scholars " . And a " skhole " , in the original Greek etymology , was " the ability to organise your own time , and not learn like a slave " , as Finnish hacker Pekka Himanen once put it . <p> Perhaps you think there 's too much of that " skhole " going around , if you are grumpily disposed towards the current wave of campus radicalism ... <p> Well , tough . We incessantly hear about the career-friendly imperatives of current higher education . However , it seems that gathering young people together to dwell on the bigger ideas , in an easeful and expansive setting , still makes for dissent and rebellion . <p> Thank Socrates for that . <p> Some of the most striking uprisings in history have begun on campuses . Black colleges in the US saw their students protest against Jim Crow laws in the 1910s and 20s . <p> The great uprisings of 1968 in Paris arose from students ' closing down of the University of Nanterre . The Tiananmen Square repression of 1989 was a spillover from campus protests at Peking and Tsinghua universities . <p> Western campuses are currently in upheaval around the massacres of the Israeli state in Gaza . The Columbia University protests this summer were the edgiest , with student occupations of the campus 's central lawn and building being met with violence ( including guns drawn ) from the NYPD . <p> Edinburgh University has also had its share of encampments , hunger strikes and occupations around this issue . <p> ( Image : University of Edinburgh ) <p> The students ' emphasis is on the university 's " severing of research collaborations affiliated with Israel 's genocidal project " ( meaning entities like Leonardo 's , AnyVision , BAE , and Ben-Gurion University ) . <p> The campus as a community of scholars seems to be in full operation at Edinburgh . At the height of events this May , hundreds of university staff signed a letter endorsing all the priority demands of the student protestors around divestment and collaboration . <p> I am generally supportive of student protest . Older generations have pushed the current systems of money and power to the edge of the precipice , in terms of climate breakdown , cost of living , runaway automation , warring geopolitics , the threat of a nuclear exchange . <p> I ca n't blame any young person for putting their training and careers , let alone their bodies and souls , on the line . Again , the possibilities of the campus are there to be seized . <p> What is wrong with trying to assert power from below , over institutions that so often proclaim their ideals to be the highest , yet fall short in the grubbiest ways ? <p> Why should n't kids with the flakiest of futures at least be able ask their elders for moral and ethical consistency ? <p> And who else than students on their campus can establish some distance from the everyday bustle , so they can even perceive the military-industrial complex operating in Scotland ( Angus Robertson failing , in my view , to distance the Scottish Government 's business subsidies from that complex ) ? <p> I do n't discount the tensions here . Of all places , the university campus has to be an engine of truth , verifiability and enlightenment . And represent a genuine diversity of viewpoints and analyses . <p> Decades ago , when Columbia University was gripped with student protests over the Vietnam War in 1968 , the Marxist sociologist Immanuel Wallerstein warned that campuses would be unpropitious for leftist revolution : " The university is and will remain a bastion of the centre ... kept squarely in the centre and in tension with the right because of the strength of the left " . <p> Just as for Edward Said and Noam Chomsky ( below ) , Wallerstein the radical academic saw universities as " a refuge and a point of sortie " meaning an attack from a defended position . <p> Maybe we need more " campuses " , in the richest sense -- ones that combine these two modes . <p> Both as a comfortable , capacious space for discussion , study and prototyping . And as an ethical community whose members dare to exert power over , and seek a different and more just vision for , the institutions around them . As well as beyond them . <p> So it 's lovely to be judged as beautiful by stellar architects such as Daniel Libeskind and David Adjaye .
