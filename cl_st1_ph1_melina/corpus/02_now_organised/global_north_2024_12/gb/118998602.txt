" <h> The rise of sharia <p> Britain 's tolerance of sharia has attracted attention at home and abroad . Concerns first emerged when the Muslim Arbitration Tribunal was created in 2007 under a law that allows parties to agree rules for arbitration and get the decisions enforced by the High Court . <p> Since the tribunal would use sharia , this was seen as Islamic law gaining official recognition . <p> Geert Wilders , founder of the Dutch hard-right anti-immigration Party for Freedom , said in a US speech sponsored by the Washington-based Hudson Institute think tank in 2008 : " In England sharia courts are now officially part of the British legal system . " <p> In practice the tribunal was observed to do comparatively little arbitration , mainly involving mosque disputes . Marital issues , which are legally exempt from arbitration , were instead its main work , acting in a role similar to a sharia council . It helps 11 couples a year . <p> A Ministry of Justice-funded study in 2012 found that all panel members on the sharia councils it surveyed were men . Studies have claimed there are between 30 and 85 sharia councils , each dealing with between 80 and 200 cases a year , nearly all women seeking divorce . <p> There are 3.9 million Muslims in England and Wales , according to the 2021 census , a rapid increase from 1.5 million two decades earlier . The population is concentrated in urban areas such as east London , Slough , Luton , Birmingham , Bradford and Blackburn , which mostly have at least one sharia council . <h> The western sharia capital <p> Scholars have long commented on the greater conservatism of the " sharia rules " being applied in Britain when compared with counterparts in Muslim-majority states , Walker noted . <p> Academic research into sharia councils found evidence of victims of domestic violence being encouraged to change their behaviour or reconcile with spouses , according to Fouzia Azzouz , who studied British Muslim marriage and divorce for her PhD at the University of Bristol . <p> Azzouz wrote : " Common among most sharia councils is to seek to reconcile the couple and save the marriage from breakdown as a necessary step before considering an application for divorce , regardless of whether this is requested or not . " <p> Yet in Gaza , wives have a right to an Islamic divorce without attempting reconciliation if injury or cruelty from husbands is established . <p> In Canada , women 's rights campaigners stopped legally binding decisions being made through " faith-based arbitration " when a Muslim group proposed creating a tribunal using Islamic law . Texas and Oklahoma have sought to ban sharia . <p> The Indian state of Uttarakhand banned polygamy , gave Muslim men and women equal inheritance rights and overruled sharia parental rules , which only gave women custody of a child until the age of seven . <p> Azzouz said : " Sharia councils may be more prominent in Britain . Part of this is due to the familiarity of Muslims in Britain with the sharia council/court phenomenon , which has its origins in south Asia . " <p> Maryam Namazie , a founder of the One Law for All campaign , said : " There are different ways in which sharia law is made available in other countries , including via mosques . <p> " Nonetheless , Britain is the western capital for sharia courts as it is also the western capital for Islamism.
