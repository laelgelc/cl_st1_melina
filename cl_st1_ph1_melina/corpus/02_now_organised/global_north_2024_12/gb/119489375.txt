<p> Yotam , a heavy metal drummer who was kidnapped from their kibbutz in southern Israel , had been shot dead in Gaza . <p> He had somehow broken free from his captors and hidden among the rubble for five days before Israeli soldiers mistook him for a Hamas operative and opened fire . <p> The officers at her home allowed the news to sink in for around half an hour before one whispered in her ear : " Iris , do you want them to take sperm from Yotam ? " <p> " I was very surprised , " she told The Telegraph . " I asked my husband , and we immediately said yes . " <p> Within a few hours , doctors were able to retrieve Yotam 's sperm , 10 vials of which are now frozen in storage a year after his death . With any luck , his child -- Iris 's grandchild -- will be carried to term inside a friend of the family . <p> Yotam Haim was one of three Israeli hostages shot and killed by IDF soldiers , who had mistaken them for Hamas combatants Credit : Debbie Hill/Shutterstock <p> It is now easier than ever for parents to harvest the sperm of their dead children in Israel . In the aftermath of the Oct 7 Hamas attacks , the health ministry waived a requirement to obtain a court order for the practice . <p> Between Oct 7 2023 and this June , sperm has been retrieved from around 170 young men in Israel , including both civilians and soldiers , according to Israeli health ministry figures . This is 15 times the figure for the same period in previous years , according to the BBC . <p> " We have a chance to have a continuation of Yotam . I know he really wanted children , " said Iris . " When we got this idea that we can do it , it really gave me a lot of hope and light ... and room to breathe . Because I said , it was not the end . I know that Yotam 's soul is with us always . <p> " This will be something real that we can feel . I hope it will be really successful and we will have grandchildren . " <p> Iris and the family are now waiting for final court approval for the sperm to be taken out of the freezer and used for insemination . <p> She is one of many to have benefited from the streamlining of the Israeli system for harvesting sperm after the Oct 7 attacks . But not all have succeeded in keeping the bloodlines going . <p> Tuval Haim , left , with his brother Yotam , who was taken hostage by Hamas Credit : MirYam Institute/PA <p> Shaylee Atary woke up on Oct 7 just as Hamas terrorists climbed through her bedroom window in Kfar Aza and murdered her husband Yahav . She escaped through nearby bushes , dodging bullets , her one-month-old baby cradled in her arms . <p> It had been 28 hours since Yahav 's murder when she felt a flash of hope . She remembered that Israeli soldiers killed in action can have their sperm harvested in the hours after their death , which can be frozen and later used in an IVF procedure . <p> " I asked Yahav 's mother if she knew if you could harvest the sperm of a civilian like a soldier can . Everything I did from then was like a marathon , running against a time machine before the sperm died to try and save it , " Shaylee told The Telegraph . <p> After speaking to countless lawyers , going to court several times and making appeals on multiple TV stations , Shaylee got the green light to have Yahav 's sperm harvested . But it was too late , his sperm had died . <p> Her efforts were not in vain , triggering the changes that would go on to benefit many family members seeking to keep something of their loved one alive . <p> The delays were partly because of scepticism and controversy over the practice of posthumous sperm retrieval . <p> In a paper titled Planned orphanhood , Ruth Landau , a bioethics researcher , wrote in 1999 that having a child using the process of sperm harvesting could potentially " reflect the person 's failure to accept the finality of death " . <h> ' It is a controversial situation ' <p> The first parents in Israel to harvest and use their dead son 's sperm were Rachel and Yaakov Cohen , whose son Keivan was killed by a Palestinian sniper in Gaza in 2002 . <p> Mr and Mrs Cohen faced several rejections in court , struggled to find a suitable mother and had nine failed attempts at artificial insemination until they eventually succeeded and a woman gave birth to their son 's child . <p> Dr Jehoshua Dor , who first engaged with posthumous sperm retrieval in Keivan 's case , said he understood the comfort he is providing to some people using this process . However , he added that he initially struggled to come to terms with moral and ethical concerns . <p> " I ca n't just do something because the parents want me to . There 's an autonomy of an individual . This was a big struggle , " he explains in his forthcoming book , Test Tube Babies , which has been seen by The Telegraph . <p> " It is a controversial situation , ethically and religiously . Taking care of a child with no father raises ethical questions . " <p> Yet , knowing the comfort it can provide parents and partners , the process is worth it , he said . It was a landmark moment , recalled Dr Dor , who was involved in helping Ms Cohen find a mother to carry Keivan 's child . Their grandchild , Osher , born from Keivan 's sperm , is now 10 years old . <h> Process of harvesting is ' easy going ' <p> According to Dr Dor , who was involved in the extraction of Keivan 's sperm , the process of harvesting is " easy going " . <p> He told The Telegraph there is generally a 72-hour window for the sperm to be extracted from a dead body , although there is more chance of success within the first 24 hours . <p> " A biopsy is done on the testes , tissue is taken to the IVF lab -- like we do when they are alive , " he explained . " The question when they are dead is : how much damage has been done when you wait many hours ? If the sperm is still alive , can we show the DNA is intact ? Once it 's retrieved , we freeze the sperm and then we wait . " <p> The process of artificial insemination is then the same as in regular IVF treatment when both parents are alive . <p> Despite the birth of Keivan 's child marking a major milestone for sperm harvesting in Israel , the country " was not ready for this amount of killing " on Oct 7 , Shaylee said . <p> The obstacles she faced -- as Mr and Mrs Cohen had 20 years ago -- meant she was unable to fulfil her dream of having a second child with Yahav . <p> " I wanted to get life out of him ... but I could n't , " she said , adding that she had only started to mourn the death of her husband once learning that his sperm did not survive .
