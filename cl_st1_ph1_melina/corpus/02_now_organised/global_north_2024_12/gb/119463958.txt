<h> Former Catterick army major to return to nursing after groping woman <p> A boozed-up medical army major dismissed after groping a junior officer on the dancefloor at a Christmas do will be allowed back into nursing , a panel has said . <p> Robin Dews had been drinking when he reportedly staggered over to the younger female officer at the ski-themed barracks party with a free bar and snow machines in Catterick and sexually assaulted her before dancing back towards the DJ booth on December 7 , 2022 . <p> He approached her with his arms outstretched before touching her buttocks and genital area . <p> He was cleared of a second sexual assault on the same woman outside in the smoking area later that night , The Sun reports . <p> Dews , who had completed tours of Bosnia , Afghanistan and Iraq over 24 years , had denied both counts but was found guilty of the dancefloor assault at a hearing at the Court Martial in Catterick in June 2023 . <p> A month later he narrowly avoided jail with a six-month sentence suspended for 18 months and was dismissed from the forces . <p> The former Senior Nursing Officer at the Catterick base on the Medical Regiment Gaza Barracks was then suspended from practising as a nurse in August last year . <p> He had been awarded a three-star commendation by the Defence Medical Services Director General for his role acting at chief executive level to liaise between the Armed Forces and the NHS during the Covid pandemic , The Telegraph reports . <p> A Nursing and Midwifery Council hearing this month met to decide if he should be allowed back into the medical profession . <p> The panel heard he was genuinely remorseful and he had " engaged exceptionally " with probation , training , and completed more rehab days than required . <p> It was told he previously had an unblemished career both in the Army and nursing , with his wife continuing to serve in the forces and him being a househusband since his sentencing . <p> The panel extended his suspension for two months , but said he will be able to renter nursing after that time . <p> Chair Anthony Griffin said : " The panel is satisfied that a two-month suspension will allow time for further reflection , engagement with rehabilitation , and assurance that you have made sufficient progress to return to practice . <p> " A further review will not be required and given the low risk of reoffending , evidence of rehabilitation , and the proportionality of the sanction , a review of the suspension order upon expiry would not be required .
