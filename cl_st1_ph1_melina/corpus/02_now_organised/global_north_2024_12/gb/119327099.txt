<p> On Cemetery Street in the southern Gaza Strip , Tala al-Seban believes , ghosts and spirits come out at night . They make it hard to concentrate as she tries to do her maths homework or practice her English writing . <p> Before the war children avoided the street in Khan Yunis , but one of the graveyards -- a 200-year-old family burial plot -- has been turned into a makeshift school where children as young as six learn among the tombstones . <p> Tala , who is ten , ca n't help but think about her father , who was killed in the war between Hamas and Israel . " Sometimes I feel like I 'm talking to my dad . We did n't bury him here -- we do n't know where he was buried -- but whenever I see the graves , I feel like one of them might be him . I talk to him in my mind , telling him how much I miss him and how hard life has been without him . " <p> The makeshift schools lack water , power and the most basic equipment for teaching <p> ABED RAHIM KHATIB/ANADOLU/GETTY IMAGES <p> This is the second year running in which children are sitting out their formal studies .
