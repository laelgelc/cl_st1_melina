<p> On the most recent episode of BBC Radio 4 's Feedback , listeners were allowed to let rip about the departure of Mishal Husain . " What on earth is the BBC doing in failing to retain such a stellar professional ? " asked Zoe Herington from Worcestershire . " Mishal is a presenter of the highest quality . There are few , if any , who can match her intelligent , analytical interview technique , the fluidity and clarity of her speech , and her command of the English language . " <p> Herington could have been speaking for Husain 's BBC colleagues , especially those on the Today programme , which she has presented for 11 years and for which her last show will be on December 17 . <p> Since it was announced ten days ago that Husain was leaving the corporation to host a new interview series for Bloomberg , the American news agency , anger has bubbled up within BBC News that more was not done to keep her . Emily Maitlis , who departed from the broadcaster in 2022 , wrote on X : " Insane decision to ever let Mishal go . She is THE BEST " . The BBC 's North America correspondent , Nomia Iqbal , wrote : " To say Mishal Husain is a huge loss for the BBC does n't come close . " <p> Rumours have also spread in W1A about the possible catalysts for her departure : some claim that she was unhappy about Today becoming increasingly casual under its new presenting line-up ; others that she was frustrated with the BBC 's coverage of the war in Gaza . <h> Advertisement <p> The truth , according to her friends , is more prosaic : Husain , 51 , had been saying privately for about two years that she wanted to return to TV from Today -- having always felt that a decade was the perfect innings for the 3.30am alarm call -- and BBC bosses failed to offer her an appealing enough new role to keep her . <p> When she turned 50 last year , her focus had sharpened on the next act in her career . That could have been within the BBC , which Husain had joined in 1998 . <p> The exit of the disgraced anchor Huw Edwards , 63 , who received a suspended prison term in September for making indecent images of children , gave the corporation an opportunity to divvy up his role among its biggest news stars . As recently as the summer , Husain was widely expected to be a main beneficiary , perhaps with an extended role on BBC News at Ten or leading the coverage of general elections and state occasions . Husain recently had a meeting with Tim Davie , the director-general , as well as the chief executive of BBC News , Deborah Turness , but was not offered the lead anchor of the 10pm bulletin , where Clive Myrie is the main presenter . <p> Edwards pleaded guilty to three counts of making indecent images of children <p> KARL BLACK/ALAMY LIVE NEWS <p> " This is an amicable split , and bosses are relieved she 's leaving on good terms , but they were taken by surprise , which they should n't have been , " a senior BBC source said . " Even though she made clear she wanted to go back to TV , news bosses did n't take that seriously , giving election night to Clive and Laura Kuenssberg , who presents the flagship Sunday morning politics show . They made the mistake of wanting to keep her on Today so much that there was n't an incentive to find something else . They took an attitude of , ' What else is there ? ' " <p> Myrie and Kuenssberg led the corporation 's TV coverage on election night <p> BBC <p> A former BBC presenter was more blunt : " Mishal ticks every single box but most of all , she is the most brilliant broadcaster the BBC has in news right now . Her interviews are pitch perfect . She is like the best actors or footballers -- they make time stand still and she seems to have an extra second to deliver , where other presenters can seem rushed or harried . Today staff are tearing their hair out . " <h> Advertisement <p> Part of the reason for such a reaction is that Husain is very popular with fellow journalists at the BBC , where many applaud her behind-the-scenes efforts to help female colleagues affected by the corporation 's gender pay gap . <p> This was n't the first time she had been passed over . In 2022 , Husain applied to replace Andrew Marr as the host of the Sunday morning politics show , feeling -- according to BBC sources -- that these jobs do n't come up very often . The BBC ran a formal audition process and Husain was one of three final candidates alongside Sophie Raworth and Kuenssberg . <p> Andrew Marr interviewing Boris Johnson in 2019 . She also recently won two awards herself : the London Press Club named her broadcaster of the year in October for her election coverage , and she won the Charles Wheeler award for outstanding contribution to broadcast journalism a month later . So why was it impossible to find her another role ? <h> Advertisement <p> " Deborah is very television-focused and I think she did n't rate Mishal highly enough , " a colleague said . " The BBC has been utterly hopeless and complacent . It shows a lack of imagination and appreciation that they would let this superstar of news presenting go . " <p> Many feel that Husain could easily have become the main face of BBC News , but her exit , David Dimbleby 's advancing years and Edwards 's crimes have now left the BBC bereft of a " voice of the nation " figure for national events . Insiders say that bosses hope to continue to bring back Kirsty Young , 56 , and will also use Myrie , 60 , though many felt his election night performance was not his best . <p> In addition , Husain is one of the highest-profile Muslim journalists in the country and some colleagues feel that has brought increased scrutiny of her interviews with Israeli politicians . Like a number of BBC journalists , she is known to have expressed reservations in editorial meetings about the BBC 's coverage of Gaza . <p> " She feels we do n't sufficiently report on the deaths of civilians and we frame too much from the Israeli perspective , " said a senior source on the programme . " She has pushed for more voices from within Gaza , and to use Gaza correspondent Rushdi Abualouf on air more regularly . She has also taken a lot of external heat .
