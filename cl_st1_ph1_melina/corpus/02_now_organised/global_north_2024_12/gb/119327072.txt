<h> From the economy to small boats , the prime minister will have to make tough choices to keep his party in power <p> When Sir Keir Starmer addressed the nation outside Downing Street for the first time he promised his new government would " tread more lightly " on the lives of voters . <p> Yet the past six months have seen No 10 go to war with farmers , businesses , pensioners -- and themselves -- in an extraordinarily turbulent start to the new regime . And next year looks set to be just as eventful . <p> On the domestic front the prime minister faces huge challenges : from finding cuts to Whitehall budgets at the spending review , to dealing with potential public sector strikes while finding a way to bolster sluggish economic growth . <p> With Labour 's support plummeting , Starmer will also have to find a way to counter the growing threat from Nigel Farage 's Reform party before local elections in May -- and make good on his promise to " stop the boats " . <p> Internationally the election of Donald Trump brings with it huge unpredictability , the threat of a potential trade war and a potential diplomatic crisis over Trump 's pledge to end the war in Ukraine.So what are the issues to look out for ? <h> Advertisement <h> The economy <p> Starmer came into office promising to defy the bleak forecasts and turbo-charge the economy , delivering the highest sustained growth in the G7 by the end of the decade . Early in the spring , Starmer is due to travel to Brussels for the first joint summit since Britain left the bloc in 2020 . <p> That meeting will kickstart a period of intense negotiations to rewrite the UK existing trade deal -- and , critically , reduce some of the frictions that have resulted in a ? 27 billion drop in UK exports to the EU since Brexit . <p> However , Starmer 's desire to boost growth in the UK by improvising trading ties with the EU will come at a political cost . <p> Sir Keir Starmer with Ursula von der Leyen <p> THIERRY MONASSE/GETTY IMAGES <p> Brussels has already made it clear that a new deal will involve the UK automatically following EU laws on food and some environmental standards , and accepting the jurisdiction of the European Court of Justice . It is also demanding greater access to UK water for EU fishermen -- a highly emotive issue that tied the hands of the Tory government in previous Brexit negotiations . <h> Advertisement <p> Most difficult for Starmer though is the EU 's demand to give young Europeans the right to come and live , work and study in the UK . The prime minister has insisted he will not do any deal that results in a return to freedom of movement . However , he will need to make some concessions to Brussels on this issue to get a deal over the line . <p> The danger for Labour is that a deal will be weaponised by the Tories and Reform to claim that the government is trying to rejoin the EU by the back door . The opportunity is to boost growth by making it easier for businesses to export to what is still the UK largest trading partner . <p> Challenge rating : 3/5 <h> Net Zero <p> Ed Miliband 's pledge to eliminate fossil fuels from Britain 's electricity supply may be five years in the future -- but what happens in 2025 will largely determine whether or not the goal is achievable . <p> In the new year the government will bring forward critical changes to the planning system which should , in theory , make it easier and faster to build the onshore and offshore wind , solar and electricity grid infrastructure needed to meet the target . But get it wrong and projects could end up being challenged in the courts -- leading to long delays and making the 2030 target unachievable . <h> Advertisement <p> Ed Miliband , the energy security and net zero secretary <p> ZARA FARRAR/NO 10 DOWNING STREET <p> At the same time , the government will also need to contact renewable energy companies to produce more new offshore wind projects than in any other year so far in order to hit the target of doubling capacity by the end of the decade . <p> It will also need to start rolling out its " warm homes plans " that aims to upgrade an estimated five million properties by 2030 making them more energy efficient . <p> Previous schemes have been expensive and had lacklustre take-up . It will be critical to get the warm homes plan right from the start or , again , the wider decarbonisation target will be much harder to achieve . <p> Challenge rating : 3/5 <h> Tariffs <p> On January 20 Trump will be inaugurated as America 's next president , and has promised to sign an immediate executive order imposing a 25 per cent tariff on all products from Mexico and Canada and an additional 10 per cent tariff on China . <p> He has also talked of imposing between 10-20 per cent tariffs on European exports -- potentially linked to whether European countries up their defence spending . <p> Donald Trump plans to bring in tariffs which will affect trade <p> TIMES PHOTOGRAPHER JACK HILL <p> The National Institute of Economic and Social Research has calculated that if tariffs were imposed on the UK they would halve GDP growth -- amounting to a ? 21.5 billion hole in Reeves 's tax and spending plans -- and push up inflation by 3 to 4 percentage points . <p> Even if the UK is spared , Trump 's policy will affect global trade flows , reducing growth and potentially triggering a full-blown trade war . If this happens the UK will not be immune from its consequences and will make it far harder for the government to hit its growth targets . <p> Challenge rating : 4/5 <h> Nigel Farage and Reform <p> In May , Starmer will face Labour 's first proper electoral test since last July 's landslide general election win . <p> Seats on all 21 county councils in England and ten unitary authorities in England will be up for election . There will also be elections for six mayoralties . <p> These contests could be bruising for Labour and party strategists are particularly concerned about the potential for Reform to make sweeping gains in red wall areas that Labour won back off the Tories at the general election . <p> Nigel Farage claims Reform now has more members than the Tories <p> JACK TAYLOR FOR THE TIMES <p> Of the 34 council by-elections held since July , Labour has lost 24 seats while Farage 's party has won seven . Farage is promising to find candidates in most seats up from grabs , with Doncaster seen as prime territory to oust Labour from a traditional heartland . <p> Then there is the prospect of money . We should discover next year whether Elon Musk comes through with a multimillion-pound donation for Reform -- that would make a big difference to its prospects . <p> Challenge rating : 3/5 <h> Ukraine <p> Ukraine is facing its toughest year of the war . Russia is advancing across the front lines and President Putin has issued a harsh set of demands as the price for peace . <p> The war between Ukraine and Russia rages on , with the issue of funding still requiring a solution <p> KOSTIANTYN LIBEROV/LIBKOS/GETTY IMAGES <p> But to do so , Starmer will have to confront uncomfortable questions about Britain 's own defence spending . As the war in Ukraine approaches its third anniversary , Britain and other major European countries still appear reluctant to stomach a new age of rearmament . Yet the remilitarisation of Russia shows no signs of abating . The prime minister has not yet announced a timeline to raise defence spending to his target of 2.5 per cent of GDP . <p> Given Trump 's reported demand that Nato lifts its defence spending target from 2 per cent to as much as 5 per cent of GDP , he may have to go significantly further . <p> Challenge rating : 4/5 <h> The NHS <p> The scale of Labour 's victory owed much to the dire state of the NHS and voters will not be forgiving if the health service does not start to improve . There has been little sign of that in the months since the election and already there are warnings that A&amp;Es are going backwards even from last year 's winter crisis . The key moment of 2025 for the health service will come in May when Wes Streeting , the health secretary , sets out his ten-year plan , designed to move care closer to home , do more to prevent illness and increase use of modern technology . <p> Yet there are tensions in the health service about how much the plan should emphasise choice and competition within the NHS , and how much it should focus on collaboration and planning . <p> We Streeting is due to unveil a plan for the next decade for the NHS <p> REUTERS <p> And the new year may be dominated by rows about the costs of Starmer 's promise to meet the 18-week waiting target for routine operations by 2029 . The goal of treating 92 per cent of patients within 18 weeks has not been met since 2015 and with performance languishing at 59 per cent , NHS bosses say it will require doing less in other areas . For all this talk of trade-offs , Starmer has refused to accept this , dismissing it as " the thinking of managed decline " . <p> In January some of these choices will have to be crystallised in guidance for expected standards in A&amp;E , mental health and elsewhere . Officials and health bosses have begun to grumble that Streeting will not confront the trade-offs , which he insists can be dealt with through reform . Whether they are made explicit or not , patients will start to notice the effects of them in the year ahead . <p> Challenge rating : 4/5 <h> Planning and housebuilding <p> No one can accuse Starmer of avoiding hard choices on planning : he has been explicit that councils will have to build more houses , need to put some of them on the green belt and that environmental protections will not be allowed to stop development . <p> This planning shake-up is designed to hit a goal of 1.5 million more homes over the parliament , which ministers acknowledge is hugely challenging . Just 221,070 homes were built last year and the long lead-time of housebuilding means frustrated ministers are still dealing with the effect of the Tory decision to drop compulsory housing targets . Homebuilding may dip below 200,000 this year and ministers have acknowledged that their changes will not lead to a significant increase in supply until late 2026 . <p> Angela Rayner has big plans to deliver more housing for Britain <p> CHRIS RADBURN/AP <p> In the first half of 2025 ministers are expected to identify sites for the first of their generation of new towns , and local reaction to these will give a flavour of how much resistance Starmer 's building boom will face . So , too , will the outcome of a battle with Labour-run councils who insist his targets are impossible . <p> Angela Rayner , the deputy prime minister and housing secretary , and Matthew Pennycook , the housing minister , have acknowledged that every part of their plan will have to work to have any chance of hitting their target . If there are signs it is faltering over the coming year , ministers will have to decide quickly if they want to go further . If they want further liberalisation of planning , or to wield a stick at developers not building quickly enough , they will have to act in 2025 to make a dent in homebuilding this parliament . <p> Challenge rating : 4/5 <h> Small boats <p> So far this year more than 35,000 people have crossed the Channel in small boats -- a figure which is 20 per cent higher than in 2023 . <p> Up until now Labour has been able to blame the rising numbers on the " failed " policies of the previous Tory government . <p> But in 2025 that excuse will have run its course and the challenge for Starmer and Yvette Cooper , the home secretary , will be to show that " smashing the gangs " is more than a slogan and can actually deliver results . <p> Thousands have made the dangerous crossing over the Channel to reach the UK <p> STEVE FINN <p> In particular there will be huge pressure on the government 's new Border Security Command to prevent the boats before they take to the water in northern France -- and to show progress in disrupting the criminal networks who facilitate the crossings . <p> The difficulty is that the gangs Labour wants to " smash " are not operating in the UK and the new command will have to rely on other countries ' law enforcement bodies to make progress -- who may have other priorities . <p> Then there is the issue of accommodating the asylum seekers already in the UK . Starmer has pledged to end the use of hotels -- but this has actually risen under Labour to about 35,000 .
