<h> If only the UK had followed Norway 's oil fund example <h> If only the UK had followed Norway 's oil fund example <p> The world 's largest sovereign wealth fund is valued at $1.8 trillion , or $200,000 per Norwegian <p> STARMER is in Norway to discuss energy deals that he claims will boost economic growth and reduce energy prices . Wrong on both counts . Growth is declining and UK electricity prices , the highest in the world , are rising again in January . <p> Ever wonder why Norway , a nation of five million people , is the richest country on earth ? It 's simple . Norway retained ownership of its vast energy resources and revenues and created the world 's largest sovereign wealth fund valued at $1.8 trillion , $200,000 per Norwegian . Read this and weep , Scotland . <p> Norway established the fund in 1996 as a " rainy day " fund . Today it owns 1.5% of all globally listed stocks and is four times the size of Norway 's GDP . For years the UK ( Scotland ) produced more oil and gas than Norway , but did n't create a wealth fund . <p> In the 1980s the UK sold its energy assets to private corporations and governments , which is where the profits flowed . This mass privatisation meant the UK lost $400 billion . The UK could have built a sovereign wealth fund of ? 850bn had the UK followed the Norwegian model . That 's ? 13,000 per person . <p> What did the UK do with the ? 67bn it got from privatisation ? It cut taxes . The top income tax rate was slashed from 60% to 40% and corporation tax from 52% to 33% . It also cut public investment from 4% in the 1970s to 1% in the 1990s . <p> Norway is now investing in renewables and owns stakes in several UK projects . It also invests in education , employment and training because it knows its people are its most valuable asset . <p> The lesson is to retain state ownership of public assets for the public good .
