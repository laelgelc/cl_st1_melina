<p> Earlier this month , a workman inserted a key in the lock of a massive bronze door at St Peter 's Basilica to check it was still working , ten years after the door was last opened by Pope Francis . <p> This Christmas Eve , Francis will once again open the Holy Door , signalling the start of a Catholic Jubilee year during which more than 30 million pilgrims are expected to pour into Rome and pass through the door , or one of three other portals also opened at major Roman basilicas . <p> Ever since Jubilee years were introduced in 1300 , Catholics going through the doors can earn an indulgence , which slashes the time they believe they will spend in purgatory for sins they were forgiven for during their lifetimes . <p> So for Federico Ciriaci , a worker at the basilica , making sure the door does not get stuck when the Pope tries to push it open at 7pm on December 24 is crucial . <p> Federico Ciriaci , a staff member at the Fabbrica di San Pietro , the maintenance team at St Peter 's Basilica <p> TOM KINGTON FOR THE TIMES <p> " Ensuring the door works without a problem is quite emotional , " Ciriaci , 41 , said during a visit to the door this week . " I checked the key turned smoothly -- without opening the door -- and confirmed it was working well , " he added . <h> Advertisement <p> Normally held every 25 years , an extra Jubilee was inserted in 2015 by Francis , who has also set up an extra Holy Door this year at Rome 's Rebibbia jail as a sign of his concern for the religious life of prisoners . <p> At the end of each of each Jubilee , the door at St Peter 's is bricked up on the inside , so on December 2 , Ciriaci and his colleagues spent three hours knocking it down and removing 3,200 bricks . Inside the cavity between the wall and the door they found a lead box containing the key to the door as well as two spare keys added for safety . <p> " There were also lots of photos stuffed into the cavity through gaps in the wall by visitors to the basilica over the last decade , " Ciriaci said . <p> The demolition of the inner wall of the Holy Door in 1999 <p> ERIC VANDEVILLE/GETTY IMAGES <p> Until the 1975 Jubilee , workers would merely loosen the brickwork to allow the pope to knock it over with one blow , but that year Pope Paul VI was nearly struck by a brick , prompting the team to take down the wall in advance ever since . <p> Heading back to the offices of the " Fabbrica di San Pietro " -- the name given to the basilica 's maintenance team -- Ciriaci checks all three keys , plus a detachable handle for the door , are ready in a drawer for Christmas Eve . Around him , noisy preparations are under way for the start of the Jubilee , similar to the rushed work going on throughout Rome to clean up fountains and repair pavements before pilgrims arrive . <h> Advertisement <p> Due to a cold , Pope Francis gave his weekly Angelus speech from his residence , instead of from a balcony overlooking St Peter 's Square on Sunday , castigating Israel for the " the machinegunning of children " and the " bombing of schools and hospitals " in Gaza . <p> Inside the basilica , Pietro Zander , an official , said the hands of millions of pilgrims passing through the door in 2015 had worn away some of the gold leaf on the 1950 bronze reliefs , which depict religious scenes . Touching up the worn-away gold had been required , but one part was left unpainted . <p> " We did not redo the feet of Christ on the cross to show how they have been touched by the faithful to show devotion , " he said .
