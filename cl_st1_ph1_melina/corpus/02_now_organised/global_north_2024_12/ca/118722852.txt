<p> Iran 's supreme leader says the Syrian revolution , which removed ally Bashar Assad from power , will only serve as greater motivation for the so-called " axis of resistance . " <p> With Hamas in the Gaza Strip and Hezbollah in Lebanon having been decimated by the Israeli military , Ali Khamenei admitted in a speech Wednesday that the seizure of power in Syria by an Islamist rebel group was a setback . <p> But it is one he claimed was plotted by the United States and Israel , and which would ultimately be avenged . <p> ARTICLE CONTINUES BELOW <p> " The main conspirators , the plotters , the command centre is in the U.S. and the Zionist regime , " Khamenei said , adding that those claiming the anti-Israel alliance has been weakened were " making a big mistake . " <p> Khamenei 's words put a brave face on a bad situation for Iran . <p> For years , the country has relied on allied groups across the Middle East to exert power and influence and counter the effects of punishing sanctions over its nuclear program . <p> In the Gaza Strip , Hamas has been decimated since attacking Israel on Oct. 7 , 2023 , losing most of its military capabilities and dozens of key leaders , including Gaza chief Yahya Sinwar and political head Ismail Haniyeh , who was assassinated last summer in Tehran . <p> In Lebanon , Hezbollah is in disarray after a punishing Israeli incursion that followed the assassination of the group 's leader , Hassan Nasrallah . <p> Syria was a third major column in the Iran-led alliance . <p> During a 13-year civil war pitting the Syrian forces against Sunni Muslim and Kurdish rebels , the Assad regime relied on Iran-backed militias , Hezbollah forces and the Russian military to stay in control . <p> Syria , the only other state member of the axis of resistance , provided a land route allowing Iran to send military supplies and equipment onward to Hezbollah . <p> " It still maintains Iraq and Yemen , which are important players , but they can not be compared to what Iran had before the fall of Assad . " <p> Tehran 's political leaders have urged a transition in Syria that is free of violence and foreign meddling , but they are unlikely to get much of a hearing in Damascus 's newly constituted power centres . <p> In one of his first interviews after seizing power , Abu Mohammed Al-Jolani , the leader of the Islamist rebel group Hayat Tahrir al-Sham ( HTS ) , said there was little risk of further fighting from rebel factions and a population that is exhausted from years of war . <p> Netanyahu said Israel wants relations with Syria 's new leadership , but warned about the repercussions of re-establishing ties to Tehran or attacking the Jewish state itself . <p> " We will respond forcefully , and we will exact a heavy price , " he said . " What happened to the previous regime will also happen to this one . " <p> The rather sudden turn of events leaves Iran isolated , destabilized and probably fearful that its own population take inspiration from the Syrian revolution . <p> It 's also living in the shadow of U.S. president-elect Donald Trump 's return to the White House next month . " At the same time , Iran 's strategic exposure and vulnerability give it strong incentives to acquire nuclear weapons . " <p> Few could have predicted Iran 's strategic reversals just a few months ago , when Hezbollah was still a fearsome threat on Israel 's northern border and the Syrian civil war seemed a distant threat or a chapter from the country 's recent history books . <p> Jonathan Panikoff , a former member of the U.S. National Intelligence Council , noted this in a post written from Qatar , where he was attending last weekend 's Doha Forum and witnessed the abrupt departure of the Iranian delegation as word of the Syrian revolution materialized . <p> A year from now , it could be a different emergency : " Iran is sprinting for a nuclear bomb , viewing it as the only way to enhance deterrence and ensure regime survival in its current weakened state , " he wrote in a blog post for the Atlantic Council , where he runs the Scowcroft Middle East Security Initiative . <p> Or perhaps , he suggested , there could be fallout from a U.S. and Israeli strike on Iranian nuclear facilities to prevent the creation of a nuclear weapon . <p> " Neither of these is the most likely scenario , but the odds of either , or both , are higher today than they were in June .
