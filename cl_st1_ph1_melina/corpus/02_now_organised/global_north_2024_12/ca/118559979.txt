<h> Jewish doctors report rising antisemitism in new survey <p> Jewish Medical Association of Ontario said its members have experienced antisemitism in comments or remarks by colleagues in conversation or at organized events , in clinical settings , at medical schools on university campuses , and in social media . <p> As an Asian and Jewish woman working in the hospital system , Serena Lee-Segal says antisemitism at work is a bigger problem now than her own experience of anti-Asian racism during COVID-19 . <p> " What I now experience as a Jewish health-care worker , the rise in this form of hate , has made me feel deeply uncomfortable and unsafe , " the occupational therapist told a news conference held by the Jewish Medical Association of Ontario ( JMAO ) . <p> " Many of our members have been doxxed and subjected to targeted harassment simply for being Jewish , " association president Dr. Lisa Salamon told a news conference Wednesday . <p> " These attacks have profound mental health impacts and send a chilling message to all Jewish professionals in health care . " <p> The association said its members have experienced antisemitism in comments or remarks by colleagues in conversation or at organized events , in clinical settings , at medical schools on university campuses , and in social media . <p> A Toronto surgeon , who asked the Star to withhold her name for fear of further harassment , said she was recently the target of an " intense , hateful email campaign . " <p> She received messages calling her a " Nazi " and a " white supremacist " among other insults after her email address was widely distributed by unknown doxxers . <p> The JMAO 's concerns , which follow Toronto police reports of an increase in hate crimes with a sharp rise in complaints about antisemitism , come amid a doctor shortage across the country . According to the Ontario Medical Association , the shortage has left 2.5 million people in this province without a family physician . <p> ARTICLE CONTINUES BELOW <p> ARTICLE CONTINUES BELOW <p> The association used the news conference to present results of a survey of members of Jewish medical associations across the country . About 2,000 surveys were sent and 944 medical practitioners filled them out , including 476 in Ontario . Not all answered every question . <p> The survey said just seven of the 944 -- fewer than one per cent -- felt antisemitism was severe in community , hospital or academic medical settings such as university medical schools before Oct. 7 , 2023 . <p> A smaller number , 364 , answered a question on whether they have experienced antisemitism on the job since then , with 294 saying " yes . " <p> Asked if they are considering whether to leave North America in search of a better work environment , 236 of the 944 said " yes . " <p> And asked if antisemitism since the Israel-Hamas war began has been the most **25;3365;TOOLONG challenging time of their life , 192 of 786 who responded to that question said " yes . " Of those 786 respondents , 43 said antisemitism has not changed their life significantly . <p> Dr. Barry Pakes , who teaches at the University of Toronto 's Dalla Lana School of Public Health and organized the survey to take the pulse of the profession , said the results point to a level of distress . <p> ARTICLE CONTINUES BELOW <p> ARTICLE CONTINUES BELOW <p> " When Jewish health-care professionals are afraid and harassed , it sends a dangerous message that discrimination is tolerated in our institutions , " added Pakes , a former York Region medical officer of health . <p> " It weakens our health-care system at a time when it is already under tremendous strain , " added Pakes , who noted he has swapped his kippa , a Jewish skullcap , for a baseball hat in some situations after experiencing " catcalls . " <p> Dr. Samuel Silver , an associate professor of medicine at Queen 's University , warned that in a " toxic environment , " Canada risks losing Jewish medical students to other jurisdictions when they graduate . <p> " It 's unacceptable for whatever happens in the international sphere , however people feel about anything going on in the world , to affect their ability to care for patients , to work with their colleagues , to teach , " said Dr. Ayelet Kuper , chair of the JMAO and an associate medical professor at U of T. <p> " We are now feeling at the very pointy end , and it 's something we have to address in our institutions , " she added . <p> Lee-Segal blamed her union , the Ontario Public Service Employees Union ( OPSEU ) , for " visibly targeting Jews with hatred " as fellow members have flown the union flag at " protests condoning terrorism . " Many Jews consider some statements at pro-Palestinian protests , such as " from the river to the sea , Palestine shall be free " as a call for the eradication of Israel . Palestinian advocates dispute this interpretation . <p> ARTICLE CONTINUES BELOW <p> ARTICLE CONTINUES BELOW <p> OPSEU , whose president JP Hornick appeared at rallies in support of the U of T pro-Palestinian encampment , did not reply to requests for comment on Lee-Segal 's allegations or on complaints filed against the union by Jewish members before the Ontario Human Rights Tribunal two months ago . <p> The encampment at U of T , established by students and activists outraged by Israel 's military campaign in Gaza to pressure the university into restructuring its finances and academic relationships in order to sanction Israel , became a magnet for controversy . <p> Before it was dismantled in early July , there were frequent rallies expressing support for protesters living in tents and solidarity with the people of Gaza . U of T president Meric Gertler told a House of Commons committee in May that there were incidents of offensive signs and language during the encampment and agreed antisemitism was a serious problem on campus . <p> On social media and emails , these doctors are being harassed and professionally compromised . <p> Lee-Segal said she was treating two patients in their eighties in hospital late last month -- one of them a Holocaust survivor -- who was " relieved " to see her wearing a Star of David necklace . <p> " They expressed to me how nervous they were about the care they would receive due to the rise in antisemitism that they were seeing in our streets . " <p> Antisemitic acts accounted for one-third of all reported hate crimes in Toronto in 2023 , the last full year for which statistics are available , and were double the level of the previous year , Toronto police report . Seven per cent of reported hate crimes that year were anti-Muslim . <p> In recent weeks , Toronto police charged two males with shooting at the Bais Chaya Mushka Elementary School for Jewish girls on Oct. 12 , which was Yom Kippur , the holiest day in the Jewish calendar . It was the second time this year the North York School was targeted with gunfire . <p> The Oct. 7 , 2023 surprise attack by Hamas was the largest mass killing of Jews since the Holocaust .
