<p> Earlier this month an Ontario Liberal fundraiser , leader Bonnie Crombie charted out a plan to become more centrist and create distance between her party and their federal counterparts , who are struggling in the polls . <p> As part of her speech , she made another remark that evening that later caught my ear and is garnering some traction on social media . <p> She asserted that if the results of the U.S. election teach us anything , it 's that " identity politics is dead and buried . " <p> Crombie talked about how " old-school party loyalties " should no longer stop the Ontario Liberals from making the changes the party needs to make . " We can not wade into culture wars . " <p> She went on to say the party " will not be baited into fights around identity politics ... that are , quite frankly , not what people who are really struggling actually care about right now . " <p> Crombie later said : " And if we are going to win , we have to stop practicing the kind of politics that makes regular people hate us . <p> " That fuels cultural clashes in our communities that divides us . " <p> Fair enough and certainly this is commentary that political journalists , provincial Liberal party supporters and the general public should bear in mind and continue to track as we approach an Ontario election next year . <p> As public editor and a former political reporter for the Star , I was intrigued by Crombie 's words . <p> First , I 'm not sure her pronouncement about the latest U.S. election results demonstrating that identity politics are " dead and buried " is quite on the mark . <p> The Cambridge Dictionary definition of identity politics describes it as " political beliefs and systems that place a lot of importance on the group to which people see themselves as belonging to , especially according to their race , gender or sexual orientation . " <p> I think it can be reasonably argued that the Republicans have now seized the White House twice in the last three elections by successfully parlaying identity politics -- whether it be past attempts at a Muslim ban , their stance against LGBTQ2S rights , women 's reproductive rights and immigration . <p> These , I would argue , are all identity-related issues . In other words , one side 's identity politics beat the other side 's in that election . <p> I also thought about Crombie 's words in the context of major conflicts happening in the world today , particularly Israel-Gaza and Russia-Ukraine -- conflicts where identity is at the very core . <p> Is it possible or even reasonable to expect that political leaders here in Ontario wo n't get drawn into tumultuous fights like these and others that pertain to identity ? <p> My mind went to the situation involving Sarah Jama , a former NDP MPP and now independent , who caused a storm at Queen 's Park this year over her decision to wear the kaffiyeh in the provincial legislature . <p> The kaffiyeh is a checkered scarf , usually cotton , that is worn in Arab cultures , but now also by supporters to show solidarity with Palestinians . <p> A fierce debate ensued at Queen 's Park this past spring , after the Speaker of the House , Ted Arnott , banned the garment . He did so citing strict rules prohibiting MPPs from wearing clothing or using props that make a statement or present a symbol that is " political . " <p> ARTICLE CONTINUES BELOW <p> ARTICLE CONTINUES BELOW <p> As the controversy unfolded , Jama said that wearing the kaffiyeh is the " least she can do " to draw attention to the humanitarian conditions in Gaza and that Gazans have the right to exist and not be the victims of " genocide . " <p> Saying the ban " needlessly divides people " Ontario 's Premier Doug Ford , as well as Liberals , the NDP and Green Party members tried to have Arnott reverse the ban . <p> But those efforts failed after Ford met strong opposition from members within his own caucus . In the end the Premier said he will abide by Arnott 's ruling . <p> Several months later , the storm over the kaffiyeh has abated with the Speaker amending his ban to allow the scarf to be worn within the building at Queen 's Park -- just not in the legislature . <p> Circling back to Crombie , examples like this and others leave me wondering about her ongoing promise to leave identity politics behind . <p> It will be interesting , as she tries to cut into Ford 's lead in the polls , to see what steps , if any , Crombie takes on this front . <p> After all , as a close friend recently said to me " as long as we all have identities , there will be identity politics .
