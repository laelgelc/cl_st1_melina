<h> John Macdonald : Free speech rules should n't stop at universities <p> Here 's how I would sum up the Government 's changes to the free speech rules for universities . <p> It wants more Posie Parkers and less posey political statements . <p> Which I 've got no problem with - but I do n't think it should stop at universities . I think the Government also needs to look at other public entities , such as local councils , which actually seem to be making more posey political statements than universities . <p> Because , if the Government does n't want universities taking positions on things like the war in Gaza because - whatever position they take - wo n't reflect the views of all staff and students , then the same could apply to local councils , could n't it ? <p> If a council boycotts Israel , for example , there 's no way everyone who works for these councils or who pays rates to these councils will agree , is there ? <p> Let me come back to that . But the gist of all this is that the Government wants two changes to the way universities deal with free speech . <p> For starters : It wants them to stop being so antsy about having guest speakers coming onto campus who might upset a few people with their views . <p> Which has seen some universities pull the plug on certain events . Massey University , for example , stopped Don Brash from giving a speech there once because of what one person described as his " separatist and supremacist rhetoric " . <p> A more recent example is Victoria University cancelling a freedom of speech debate this year because of concerns it would turn into a cesspit of hate speech . <p> So the Government wants no more of that . Because it thinks universities are places where all sorts of ideas and thoughts should be shared and debated . And I agree with that . <p> So that 's what I mean when I say it wants more Posie Parker . <p> The other change it 's making to the regulations that universities operate under , is to stop them taking positions on matters that don'tdirectly relate to their core business of research and teaching . <p> Now this is not something that is going to impact academics who enjoy what 's known as academic freedom - which pretty much means they can think and say what they want . Although some academics have questioned that in recent years , saying that they do n't feel as free to think and say what they want as they used to . <p> But , essentially , what the Government wants to stop is universities - as institutions - taking a view or a stance on international issues , for example . <p> Some of our universities have been under pressure to condemn Israel for what 's going on in Gaza and the Occupied Territories . But , as far as I 'm aware , none of them have given-in to that pressure . <p> The closest example I could find here in New Zealand is an announcement three months ago by Victoria University 's fundraising arm - the Victoria University Foundation - that it would be getting rid of its Israeli government bonds and its shares in companies listed in Israel . <p> So maybe this is a pre-emptive move by the Government , as much as anything . And it says the reason it 's doing this , is that if a university takes a stand on something - it does n't reflect the views of all staff and students , and that is unfair . <p> So , if that 's the motivation , then I reckon the Government needs to come down just as hard on other public entities . Public entities which , at the moment , seem to be going harder on this thing than any of our universities . <p> And I 'm thinking , specifically , about local councils around the country which have been more than happy to pile-in on Israel this year , with decisions to boycott companies which operate in Israeli settlements on Palestinian land . <p> But , if we apply the argument the Government 's using to stop universities taking positions on global issues - because they wo n't necessarily represent the views of all staff and students - then the same can be said of these local councils , ca n't it ? <p> In Nelson , for example , after the council there voted to go with a boycott - there were some pretty fired-up locals . The mayor Nick Smith , who voted against it , got a whole lot of abuse too . <p> And who says everyone working at these councils agrees with the position their employers have taken ? They wo n't . And who says everyone paying rates to these councils agrees with their anti-Israel positions ?
