<p> Honouring the memory of more than 1 million Rwandans who died in just one hundred days of the Genocide against the Tutsi in 1994 , the High Commission of the Republic of Rwanda hosted a solemn commemoration , ' Kwibuka ' or ' To Remember ' , at the Serena Hotel . <p> Kwibuka is an annual commemoration period that starts on April 7 and lasts for 100 days . <p> It serves as a solemn remembrance of the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi , a dark period in Rwandan history . <p> Being the 31st years of genocide , this year 's ' Kwibuka 31 ' saw the presence of many diplomats , government officials , members of the Rwandan community and friends of Rwanda . <p> The Additional Secretary ( Africa ) , Ministry of Foreign Affairs , Hamid Asghar Khan , was the Chief Guest on the occasion . however , the world was not free of conflicts . <p> Today , what is taking place in Gaza is a bitter reminder of the fact that genocidal violence continues and that sections of mankind have not learned any lessons . <p> The @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the rescue workers and medics are being killed by the Israeli forces , but the world , including the UN , is a helpless spectator , he regretted . <p> He called upon the world powers to unite for peace and curb aggression worldwide . <p> UN Coordinator Mohamed Yahya also shared the pain and grief of the Rwandans all over the world and assured the UN would continue to work against hate speech . <p> In her speech , Ms Harerimana Fatou expressed her gratitude to all the guests and reflected on the tragic history of the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi , which took over 1 million lives . <p> She emphasized the importance of remembrance and unity , highlighting Rwanda 's remarkable journey of recovery and reconciliation over the past 31 years . <p> She also paid tribute to the survivors , noting that we should never forget to thank President Paul Kagame &amp;
