<h> In times of peace , cinema fosters empathy . In times of conflict , it can become a weapon . <p> When news broke of the Pahalgam terrorist attack on 22 April , killing at least 26 tourists , there was collective mourning across India . But very quickly , that grief turned into a spectacle of communal rage . This was not the quiet , justifiable anger at systemic failure , but a loud , performative hypernationalism that was out to seek retribution . <p> From YouTube channels to prime-time news , the aftermath of this tragedy followed a chilling pattern we are all too familiar with and resigned to now : incendiary rhetoric and co-opting of grief , to fuel a divisive hunger for vengeance . <p> We saw this happen after Uri in 2016 and after Pulwama in 2019 . ? ?
