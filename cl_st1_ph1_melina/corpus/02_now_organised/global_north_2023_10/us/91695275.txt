<h> War has no grief scoreboard : Death in Israel and Gaza is not a sport <p> When you open the newspaper or scroll through articles online , you will see brief summaries of the war between Hamas and Israel . They might describe Hamas ' brutal invasion of Israel or Israel 's ferocious military response . They might provide some in-depth coverage about a particular individual , family , or town . Invariably , they will focus on numbers . <p> This number of Israelis have been killed , this number wounded , this number kidnapped , and this number released . This number of Palestinians have been killed , this number wounded , this number displaced . <p> Rather than focusing on cause and effect or the human impact of particular events ; rather than leading with compassion for civilians or seeking complexity and nuance ; rather than explaining the broader regional tensions that underlie the unfolding war , the news suggests that numbers tell the whole story . Whoever 's side suffers more losses wins the war of public opinion -- or at least a more favorable slant in the article . But there are no winners in war , especially one that so painfully impacts civilians . The moment there are casualties or injuries -- physical or psychological -- everyone involved loses . And numbers can not adequately convey human pain and suffering . They obfuscate rather than clarify the losses inherent to war . <p> This is not a sporting event . There is no " 12th man " that can give one side home field advantage , and these are not video games with redos . Losses last a lifetime , and injuries to body and spirit endure . Yet we are enticed to engage war as a sport when news leads with a scoreboard , rather than human stories . Doing so is deeply dehumanizing to those who have died , those who have been injured , and those who are in grief right now . Doing so erases their humanity in pursuit of personal gain . <p> In fact , Jewish tradition prohibits us from prioritizing numbers over people . Exodus 30:12 relates the formal prohibition of taking a census , even of the living : " When you take a census of the Israelites ... each shall pay God to redeem themselves that no plague may come upon them . " While it pertains specifically to ancient military service , our sages interpret this more broadly . The census depicts a group based solely on numbers , rather than on the human qualities of which it is comprised . It is therefore forbidden under most circumstances . <p> All the more so , we must stop desecrating the memory of the deceased and adding moral injury to those recovering from emotional and physical harm by grouping individual lives into simple figures . Israel 's military response has been incredibly painful for Palestinian civilians , as urban warfare reduces neighborhoods to battlefields . We risk diminishing our own humanity when we gamify this war or indulge ourselves by reading the news with an intense focus on numbers alone . <p> In our increasingly quantitative world , we rely on numbers , but they should not themselves be a headline -- much less a story unto itself or a way of understanding a complicated , unfolding war . But if statistics hide more than they reveal , we would do well to look beyond them . Now is the time for in-depth reporting on individual stories , which open our eyes to new perspectives and our hearts to all that we might not otherwise feel . <p> We risk closing our hearts when we focus on numbers @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ adage that " a single life is like a whole world " -- a world of ideas , relationships , and creative potential .
