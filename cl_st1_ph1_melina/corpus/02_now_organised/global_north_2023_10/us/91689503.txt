<p> BBC director general Tim Davie was grilled over the corporation 's coverage of the Israel-Hamas war during a meeting on Wednesday night with the 1922 Committee of Conservative Members of Parliament , particularly over the broadcaster 's policy not to call Hamas " terrorists . " <p> Since Hamas ' Oct. 7 attack on Israel , the BBC has received backlash from the public and government alike for its refusal to use the term , despite Hamas having been deemed a terrorist organization by the U.K. <p> According to The Telegraph , Davie was confronted during the meeting -- which the BBC said was part of his regular check-ins with the U.K. 's political parties -- by Minister of Immigration Robert Jenrick , who said he 'd " never been so disappointed , " adding : " I worry that the organization has lost the confidence of many people and in particular the British Jewish community . That loss of confidence began with the BBC 's refusal to call Hamas terrorists . Will you reconsider that and change your editorial policy ? " <p> In response , Davie stuck by the BBC 's policy , pointing to the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that it has been deemed a terrorist organization by the U.K. government . " We are impartial , " a BBC spokesperson added later . " <p> According to the BBC , the spokesperson went on to say that a review of the corporation 's editorial policy is due to occur next year .
