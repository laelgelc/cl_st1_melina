<p> A photographer for an Israeli trance-music festival in southern Israel described the harrowing experience of fleeing Hamas militants opening fire at the event as part of a series of coordinated surprise attacks on Israel on Saturday . <p> Ido Derby , a 20-year-old Israeli photographer who was capturing the Tribe of Nova festival , where at least 260 bodies have since been found , told Insider that missile sirens began to blare at around 6:30 a.m . <p> " We were n't panicked because missiles in our country are n't something unusual , " he said , speaking to Insider from his home in Rishon LeTsiyon , where he fled to after the festival . <p> ( The call , which took place on Monday , was interrupted by a siren midway through as the Iron Dome intercepted missiles from Gaza heading toward central Israel . Derby went to a shelter and called back . ) <p> Shortly after the sirens started blaring on Saturday morning , Derby said he followed protocol , collected his belongings , and got into a car with two friends . <p> They started to drive towards a shelter , not realizing anything @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as many festival-goers were trying to make their way to safety , he said . <p> " Suddenly , we started hearing shooting from our right side , " Derby recalled , adding that he initially thought it might be the Israel Defense Forces in action . <p> As the traffic started to clear , Derby said he considered getting out and walking due to the challenging driving conditions caused by mud and dust from numerous vehicles . <p> However , he said he now knows that would have been the wrong decision . At the time , Derby was unaware of what was rapidly unfolding nearby . <p> His mother called him , crying , to tell him that there were reports of attacks in the area . To reassure her , he told her he was fine but said he started to fear his own demise . <p> " I could now see in the mirror everyone else running for their lives , people falling down , " he said .
