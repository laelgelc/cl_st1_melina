<h> We expect Ukraine to do what NO NATO country could or would do - Lt Col Stuart Crawford <h> Ukraine War Update : As the world 's gaze is deflected to Gaza the grim fighting in Ukraine continues unabated . Zelensky 's forces have not achieved the killer counter-offensive which was hoped and there still seems no end in sight , writes Lt Col Stuart Crawford . <p> Whilst the eyes of the world have been on the Gaza conflict for the past fortnight , the other war in Ukraine grinds on . We are now 21 months into Vladimir Putin 's mooted three-day " special military operation " with no end yet in sight . <p> Not that matters have been standing still while much of the media 's attention has transferred to the Middle East . <p> What we can say , however , now that autumn has arrived with the wet weather which precludes much off-road movement by heavy armoured vehicles , is that the Ukrainian summer counter-offensive has made disappointingly little progress . <p> That is not to denigrate the efforts of the UkrAF , who have fought stubbornly and courageously . Their task @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Russian defence lines without the benefit of air superiority , has proved to be beyond their efforts so far . Hardly surprising , because it is something that no NATO country would try without command of the skies . <p> So progress has been limited in general terms to a small advance here and a small retreat there , nothing like the sweeping manoeuvres that we in the West might have been expecting . No breakthrough has been achieved , and Russian morale , although sometimes shaky , has held , and there has been no collapse on their part . <p> Ukraine has benefitted , though , from additional weaponry from the west . Chief among others has been the arrival of the American ATACMS long range precision missiles . These have allowed the UkrAF to attack further into their enemy 's rear areas and may have been partly instrumental in Russia moving the majority of its Black Sea Fleet from Sevastopol in Crimea to ports further to the east and relatively out of harm 's way . <p> We should note two recent developments on the ground . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ much fought over city of Bakhmut , there has been fierce fighting as Russian forces continue to attack in the vicinity of the town of Avdiivka . Ukrainian sources report that their enemy may have committed some three battalions , or close to 2,000 troops , plus numerous tanks and armoured fighting vehicles to the assault . <p> By all accounts all the Russian assaults have been repelled with heavy losses in vehicles and personnel . Yet the Russians persist in their attempts to capture the town . Avdiivka has now become something of a symbol of Ukrainian resistance and the battle there looks to continue for some time . <p> A more intriguing development perhaps has been the reports that the UkrAF have established at least one toehold over the River Dnipro near Kherson further west . The river has been rightly regarded as a major obstacle to further Ukrainian advances towards Crimea . <p> It would appear that Ukrainian forces crossed roughly 30 kilometres northeast of Kherson City and are fighting to take full control of the settlement of Krynky , which is some two kilometres south of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ control all of Krynky , although others directly refuted this and counter that Russian forces have pushed the Ukrainians out of the settlement . <p> If the UkrAF has managed to maintain a bridgehead across the Dnipro here it could be a significant development , possibly allowing further exploitation by them into Russian-occupied territory using fresh troops . It appears that the Russian troops in the sector are not first rate , the more professional elements having been relocated to the Zaporizhzhia sector further east where the main Ukrainian effort is concentrated . <p> Time will tell whether further progress will be made here , but the season for unfettered movement and manoeuvre will be over until the ground freezes over with the coming of winter . <p> I have given up trying to predict when this war will end . I thought I was being overly pessimistic when I suggested this time last year that it might last until the summer just passed . Now I fear we might find it in more or less the same position this time next year . <p> Much depends on the Ukrainians @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the continuing flow of more weaponry into their armouries from the west . Their Russian enemy also gets a vote here , of course , and the indications are that they 're in it for the long haul too . Historically , Russia has relied on its stubborn resilience and vast reserves of manpower and materiel to win through in the end . <p> In my opinion Crimea remains the key terrain . If Ukraine succeeds in regaining its lost territory here then the door may be open to a negotiated settlement . But Putin will not relish such a blow to his prestige and will not give it up easily . <p> Sadly , it 's all still to fight for , and it does n't look as if it will be over any time soon . <p> Lt Col Stuart Crawford is a defence analyst and former army officer .
