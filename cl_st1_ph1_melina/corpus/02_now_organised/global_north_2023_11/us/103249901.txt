<h> Even the Oppressed Have Obligations <p> After the Hamas attack on Israel October 7 , an old , bad argument resurfaced . In the streets of New York , London , and Paris , and on American college campuses , protesters who consider themselves leftists took the position that oppressed people -- Palestinians in this case , but oppressed people more generally -- can do no wrong . Any act of " resistance " is justified , however cruel , however barbaric , however much these protesters would rage against it if it were committed by someone else . <p> I remember the same argument from the days of the Algerian struggle for independence from France , when the National Liberation Front ( FLN ) launched terrorist attacks against European civilians . The movie The Battle of Algiers shows a bomb being planted in a caf ? where teenagers met to drink and dance . This really happened , and figures as eminent as Jean-Paul Sartre defended such attacks . For them , the Hamas murderers are not ordinary mortals , responsible for what they do ; they are agents of resistance , doing what must be done in the name of liberation . <p> Framed this way , the issue is simple : Oppressed people have a right to resist ; the Palestinians have a right to struggle against the Israeli occupation . But rights come with obligations . What are the obligations of the oppressed and , most immediately , of those who act in their name ? This may not seem like an urgent question , given the horrors of the war now unfolding .
