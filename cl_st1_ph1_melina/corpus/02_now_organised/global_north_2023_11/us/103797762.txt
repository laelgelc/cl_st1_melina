<p> My parents were Palestinian refugees who managed to rent an apartment in the city of Nazareth after their village , Ma'Alool , 10 miles to the west , was destroyed by the advancing Israeli army in 1948 . My father , a cobbler , took his trade to Nazareth 's historic bazaar . From the little shop he rented I was able to watch shoppers as they went about their day . In between brewing cups of coffee for friends and family , my father would remind me to take note of all the people who would pass by : Arabs , Jews , Christians and Muslims . The market thrived when people came together . <p> I was not blind to the second-class nature of my existence in Nazareth , but my parents made sure to lead by example . For example , my father routinely asked that I carry the grocery bags of elderly women to the bus station at least a quarter of a mile away from his shop .
