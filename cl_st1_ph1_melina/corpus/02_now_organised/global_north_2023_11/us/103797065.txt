<p> Amid concerns that the four-day Israel-Hamas ceasefire could allow Hamas to bolster its forces , safeguards are in place to prevent the humanitarian aid being shuttled in Gaza as part of the hostage deal from being hijacked by Hamas forces , White House national security adviser Jake Sullivan said Sunday . <p> " First , Dana , there 's the inspection mechanism , " Sullivan told CNN 's Dana Bash . " The Israelis at a crossing called Nitzana , check all of the trucks before they go in through the Rafah crossing into Gaza . And they check to make sure that it is , in fact , humanitarian supplies and not goods that could help Hamas in its military campaign . " <p> After that , aid trucks move on to U.N . depots and to other vetted humanitarian organizations , who distribute aid directly to people in need , Sullivan said on " State of the Union . " <p> " We have seen this work over the course of the last several weeks as humanitarian assistance has ramped up , " Sullivan said , but U.S. leaders continue to monitor the process closely . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Israeli leadership on this , with the U.N . leadership , and with others to make sure , in fact , the aid is getting to where it belongs , which is the innocent people who are suffering , " Sullivan added . <p> On Saturday , 200 aid trucks were dispatched from Nitzana and 187 had made it into Gaza by Saturday evening , according to the U.N. , sending desperately needed food , water and fuel into the besieged region .
