<h> All the Light We Can not See review : Second World War saga is a shonky , star-studded dud <p> Right now , war does n't seem that glamorous . In the present tense , it seems an unlikely canvas for romance or adventure , and yet , time after time , war -- with the clarifying distance of history -- is used as a narrative prop . Suddenly , teenage heartbreak has huge emotional depth , grief and relief perform their tango , and all the struggles of being human are amplified to their maximum level . In war , everything is overwrought : and that 's precisely the timbre of Netflix 's new four-part drama , All the Light We Can not See . <p> France , the Second World War . Marie ( Aria Mia Loberti ) , a blind teenager , broadcasts snippets of Jules Verne 's novel Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea from her attic in Saint-Malo . Her father Daniel ( Mark Ruffalo ) is gone , and Saint-Malo is awaiting the arrival of the Americans , who might end the Nazi 's occupation .
