<p> MARJAYOUN , Lebanon - From his office overlooking the border with Israel , Dr. Mounes Klakesh can hear the thump of artillery rounds and air strikes landing on nearby Lebanese towns . The increasing frequency of those strikes has the staff of his small hospital on edge . <p> " We 've already had to treat 51 people wounded by explosions in the last month or so . Seventeen of those died , or arrived dead . More than that and we 'd be overwhelmed , " Klakesh said . <p> Klakesh , director of the Marjayoun Hospital in southern Lebanon , said it serves nearly 300,000 people in the area . It has 14 emergency beds and struggles to operate because of a lack of staff and , crucially , lack of fuel . <p> The hospital runs on generators 20 hours a day and has to pay up to $20,000 a month for the fuel .
