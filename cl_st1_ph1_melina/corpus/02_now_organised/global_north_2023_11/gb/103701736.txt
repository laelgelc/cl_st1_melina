<h> The Daleks in Colour , review : Whovians , rejoice ! This remastered Sixties classic is a treat <p> Sixty years ago today , an educational programme for young viewers first aired on the BBC . The idea : a girl and her time-travelling grandfather would visit different periods of history , learning along the way . To hammer home Doctor Who 's edifying credentials , they were chaperoned by two school-teachers . This was sci-fi , yes , but with a grounding in fact . No nonsense . <p> The show 's creator was adamant that there would be no " bug-eyed monsters " . And then -- thank God -- the Daleks came . What a genius their designer Ramond Cusick was . His genocidal pepper-pots look the same six decades later , and still raise goosebumps . But what of the story ? Running to nearly three hours , Terry Nation 's original serial had its longueurs . Reviewers revisiting it over the years have called it " unutterably slow and boring " . <p> Now , for The Daleks in Colour ( BBC Four ) , it has been trimmed to 75 minutes , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ colourised in the palette of 1960s kitsch . They have been a bit over-zealous : the story begins with our heroes exploring a fossilised forest , where " everything 's sort of white and ashen " -- except for the bits that are now pink and green . But for the most part , it works a treat . <p> The Daleks ' sworn enemies -- those peaceful himbos , the Thals -- look more like extras from Flash Gordon , with their blond hair and baby-blue uniforms . What 's been lost in the cut ? Nothing essential , unless you 're a purist . I sort-of missed the dead metal alien discovered in the forest , but its absence lends more tension to the long build-up towards the first glimpse of a Dalek , in the metal city-cum-bunker where they have hidden from the aftermath of a nuclear war . <p> The sets are gorgeous .
