<h> Lesley Riddoch : Break-up of Britain ? event showed we must reassess UK <p> WHEN the going gets rough , a friend constantly reminds me that no-one kicks a dead dog . It took a while to understand that unappealing image , the first time it was rolled it out 15 years ago . But it 's as true , consoling and relevant today . <p> Take the pelters around the Breakup of Britain conference last weekend . <p> There was criticism from Unionist detractors on Twitter/X who scoffed that the 700-odd folk shelling out ? 25 to attend were largely over 50 . <p> Well yes . Tom Nairn ( below ) died earlier this year at the age of 90 and many folk from his generation had come to pay tribute to him . Tom was a political philosopher and major figure in the " new left " politics of the 1960s across Europe who , almost uniquely , advocated nationalism as a progressive force . <p> His friend , the journalist Neal Ascherson , described Nairn 's as " the most forceful and original mind to confront , demask and anatomise the British state " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ was a multinational state and not a united nation " . <p> That was the focus of Saturday 's Break Up event . <p> And yet , despite this hugely relevant democratic grounding , the charitable foundations that normally back events like these , rejected funding applications . It seems voting systems , centralisation and levelling up are all acceptable subjects for polite debate but not the viability of the current UK . That 's just too hot to trot . So , income was lower than hoped for -- which made ticket prices higher . <p> There were also speakers from across the UK and beyond -- how very dare a bunch of Scots aim so high -- and they were put up in a decent hotel , not left scrambling for spare beds with pals . <p> Likewise , the venue was not the usual church hall -- no offence -- but Edinburgh 's beautiful , roomy , central and prestigious Assembly Rooms . Fa kens . You could indeed be cheered by the presence of folk from a generation that 's meant to be wedded to the status quo . <p> Clearly though , detractors were not going to look on the bright side . But that does n't matter . <p> Worried that great speakers from the rest of the UK did the same , including Plaid Cymru 's Leanne Wood , the Greens ' Caroline Lucas and -- most amazingly -- Labour 's Clive Lewis . " <p> Totally . This is the leader Labour needed in 2020 , when Clive stood against Keir Starmer . But the object was to think beyond Scotland as Tom Nairn did in 1977 , when his seminal book The Break Up of Britain was first published . So they change the subject . <p> But Britain is unique today -- not because it is the most democratic , productive , literate or child-friendly country in Europe . All those accolades are regularly won by our small Nordic neighbours . <p> Britain is unique in all the wrong ways . <p> The only country in Europe still using the unfair , archaic and polarising first-past-the-post voting system -- except Belarus . <p> The only country with an unelected chamber larger than the elected one . <p> And one of the most centralised countries -- possessed of some of the largest " local " councils in the developed world . <p> Nairn 's argument almost 50 years ago , was that Labour 's disdain for overhauling Britain 's feudal democratic structures would be its undoing and that driving this broken charabanc for more than one Westminster term at a time would prove impossible .
