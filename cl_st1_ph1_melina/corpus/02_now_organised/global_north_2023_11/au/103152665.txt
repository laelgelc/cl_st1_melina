( ABC News : Che Chorley ) <p> Coalition frontbencher Jason Wood , the Shadow Minister for Community Safety , Migrant Services and Multicultural Affairs , has taken to Facebook to attack the government for " fuelling inflation " with " Labor 's fuel tax " . <p> " Inflation is sky high and it 's getting worse thanks to Labor 's petrol tax , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ with the Voice , " reads the post , which features a satirical image of Prime Minister Anthony Albanese filling his car at the petrol bowser . <p> Currently levied at 48.8 cents per litre , fuel excise is paid to the Australian Tax Office by fuel importers or manufacturers , who can pass on the cost to consumers . <p> Importantly , the ATO explains , the rate increases automatically every six months , regardless of who is in government , " based on the upward movement of the consumer price index ( CPI ) " . <p> Labor has n't introduced a new tax ; rather , it oversaw the conclusion of a temporary cut instituted by the former Coalition government in response to a spike in oil prices after Russia invaded Ukraine . <p> " The Government will help reduce the burden of higher fuel prices at home by halving the excise and excise-equivalent customs duty rate that applies to petrol and diesel for 6 months , " the March 2022 budget said . <p> Highlighting the measure 's " temporary " nature , it added that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " revert to previous rates , including indexation that would have occurred on those rates during the 6-month period " . <p> The then-treasurer , Josh Frydenberg , also ruled out any extension of the cut should the Coalition be re-elected . <p> So , has the reintroduction of regular rates added to inflation ? <p> The answer , according to economists James Giesecke and Jason Nassios , of Victoria University 's Centre of Policy Studies , is " no " . <p> In a statement to CheckMate , they said that while inflation was a process of " persistent increase " in prices , fuel excise would have a " small and transitory damping effect " on this over periods shorter than six months . <p> That 's because the excise rate remains locked in during those six months even as broader inflationary pressures might push up ( pre-excise ) fuel prices . <p> In other words , it holds back inflation -- slightly -- until the next time the excise is automatically indexed to CPI . <p> " This indexing process does not add to overall inflation , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ specific nature of fuel excise on fuel 's contribution to overall inflation , " the academics said . <p> Referring to the ending of the Coalition 's six-month cut , they added : " A once-off increase in the price level is not the same as an increase in the inflation rate , because it is not a persistent force for price level increase .
