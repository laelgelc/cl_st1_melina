<h> More families will be allowed to apply to come to Canada from Gaza : Miller <p> Immigration , Refugees and Citizenship Minister Marc Miller rises during question period in the House of Commons on Parliament Hill in Ottawa on Monday , March 18 , 2024 . THE CANADIAN PRESS/Sean Kilpatrick <p> OTTAWA -- Immigration Minister Marc Miller says Canada will allow more people trapped in Gaza who have extended family members in Canada to apply for temporary refuge -- but he admits the move is " cold comfort . " <p> The government launched a family reunification program in January that offers temporary refuge to parents , grandparents , siblings and grandchildren of Canadian citizens and permanent residents . <p> Immigration , Refugees and Citizenship Canada initially said it would only consider 1,000 applications for the program . <p> Miller says the government is increasing the number of people that will be eligible to come out of Gaza . <p> But none of those whose names have been put forward by the Canadian government have so far been able to cross the tightly controlled border . <p> Miller says Canada is pleading with Egypt and Israel to let @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in Egypt and receive a Canadian visa . <p> " We are indeed increasing the number of people that will be eligible to come out of Gaza . There are a number of matters that are beyond our control , notably the ability to extract people from the Rafah gate , " he said . <p> " We will be relentless in advocating for people to reunifying with their family members in Canada , if but for a temporary basis . " <p> As of March 4 , 986 applications had been accepted into processing . <p> Only 12 of those people had made it out of Gaza , finished the screening process and been approved to come to Canada , Immigration , Refugees and Citizenship Canada said in a statement earlier this month . <p> Immigration lawyers and family members have warned that dozens more people are in limbo -- and unclear on whether they are still eligible for the program -- after making it across to Egypt by other means , including paying private companies to negotiate their exit with Israeli and Egyptian officials .
