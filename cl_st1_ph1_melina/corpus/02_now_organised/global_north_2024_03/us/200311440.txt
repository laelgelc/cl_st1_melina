<h> Aboard the U.S. Aircraft Carrier Battling the Houthis in the Red Sea <p> Nearly every night , it is the same drill . A call comes in that mobile missile launchers have popped up somewhere in Yemen near the coastline , preparing to fire . <p> Except , aboard the aircraft carrier Dwight D. Eisenhower , it is not a drill . <p> For two months , the 5,000 sailors and pilots on board have carried out a near daily and nightly task : find and destroy weapons storage sites , missile systems , air defense systems , radars and missile launchers before the Houthi militia uses the weapons to find and destroy commercial ships in the Red Sea . <p> On the Eisenhower , the fighter pilots are only a tiny percentage of the entire crew , which has a vital supporting role . <p> " The mission could n't happen without them , " Capt . Christopher Hill , the commanding officer of the Eisenhower , said in an interview . " But everyone onboard works to support them . " <p> On a recent Tuesday night , it was the same routine .
