<p> In " Thanks for Letting Us Know You Are Alive , " winner of the Juniper Prize for Poetry awarded by UMass Press , poet Jennifer Tseng lets lines from letters from her late father intermingle with her own words , creating a combinatory , deeply intimate , intergenerational new text . " With each letter/ I disappeared/ A little further/ From the world/ Into my father . " The lines from the letters are an archive of longing and regret . To open a letter , to touch with fingertips a piece of paper a person , a beloved , has also touched , can be overwhelmingly intimate , erasing distance and time , the absent person present in the words written on the page , the poignancy made particularly acute after one of the letter writers is gone . There is grief here , the stickiness of family , of the terrors and comforts of love , of her father " de-siring " himself with each letter , " a little more each time .
