As an international lawyer with a focus on the laws of war , I have travelled widely -- from Moscow to New York , Gaza to Mogadishu . <p> Whether I was working on diplomatic issues ( like access to detainees ) or working operationally in the field ( like tracing and reuniting families ) , the complexity of issues and the resilience of people and communities always took my breath away . <p> I have studied , taught and written about many of these issues , but they always become clearer in real life . Her sister was blind and without a husband ( a particular challenge in that society ) and so the journey had been extraordinarily difficult and dangerous for the family unit . <p> One of her real needs was sleeping mats . She graciously showed me her hut , with its dirt floor , and she explained via the interpreter that the rainy season was approaching and her children and sister could n't sleep once the floor turned to mud . <p> Keeping her family in the best health they could was essential , but there was little to eat and she was very worried about sanitation . <p> Other families in the camp had mats woven from branches of palms found in the nearby vegetation .
