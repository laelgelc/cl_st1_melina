<p> Police were on alert and guards patrolled the corridors and surroundings as councillors and officers met together to officially sign off the Birmingham City Council Budget this week . They were about to agree to the most damaging set of service cuts in living memory , with the impact likely to last for years . <p> The extra security was largely due to abhorrhent individual threats made against councillors , but also in the expectation of a large protest outside from disgruntled Brummies . In the end only about 100 largely subdued protestors gathered under the canopy of the Council House , their chants of ' stop the cuts ' failing to penetrate the huge wooden front doors or reach those in the historic chamber . <p> Inside , the only heckles came from four women angry about the tragedy of Gaza . They left after politely saying their piece . <p> That was a relief for targeted councillors , and also for the lead commissioner sent in to oversee the cuts for the Government , Max Caller . In a previous role , as he told me in this first interview here , he had needed a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as he announced far less deep cuts in Hackney over two decades ago . <p> Even the opposition benches could barely muster a fight . There were words of outrage and blame , and a mention of ' dirty rats and gangsters ' , during a five hour debate , but mostly an air of resignation hung over the historic chamber . Where were the rousing speeches of old , the tub thumping and the fury that this disaster deserved ? <p> Perhaps they knew they were just going through the motions . Whatever their political stripes , the budget would be passed anyway . They had ' no choice ' - for if councillors did n't deliver the budget , Caller and his fellow commissioners were on hand to do it for them . <p> And also true is that everyone present likely felt a degree of responsibility for the mess they had collectively created .
