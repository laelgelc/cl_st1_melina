<h> Topline <p> Qatar announced Tuesday it mediated a deal between Israel and Gaza-based militant group Hamas to allow Israeli hostages to get medicine in exchange for more humanitarian aid being allowed into Gaza -- marking the first successful negotiations around hostages in months . <h> Key Facts <p> In a statement posted to X , the Ministry of Foreign Affairs for Qatar said " medicine along with other humanitarian aid is to be delivered " to civilians in the " most affected and vulnerable areas " of the Gaza Strip in exchange for medication being delivered to Israeli hostages being held in Gaza . <p> The medication will leave Doha , Qatar tomorrow and go to Egypt in preparation for delivery to the Gaza Strip , the statement said . <p> France also assisted in the mediations , according to Tuesday 's statement . <h> Big Number <p> 132 . That 's how many hostages taken by Hamas Israel expects are still being held in Gaza , according to Reuters . Israel estimates 25 hostages have died , and more than 100 were released in the temporary cease-fire in November . <h> Key Background <p> Negotiations around the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ first and only cease-fire in November ended . That cease-fire was originally a four-day pause in fighting to allow for the release of women and children hostages being held by Hamas in exchange for Israel releasing Palestinian women and teens who were being held in Israeli prisons . Fighting restarted on Dec. 1 first after Israel accused Hamas of not upholding its end of the agreement by firing rockets and not releasing all women and children . Hamas argued Israel also broke the truce by launching airstrikes .
