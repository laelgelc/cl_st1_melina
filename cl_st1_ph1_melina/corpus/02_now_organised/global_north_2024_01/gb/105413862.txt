<h> The court sees a risk of genocide in Gaza <p> The international court of justice ( ICJ ) has not ruled on whether Israel has committed genocide -- that will be decided at a later date -- but its provisional ruling provides the clearest indication yet of which direction the judges are leaning . <p> Its 29-page court order , released on Friday , is long , convoluted and filled with legalese , but it does hint that a majority of the judges see a credible risk to Palestinians under the genocide convention . <p> In paragraph 74 , it states that " there is a real and imminent risk that irreparable prejudice " will be caused to the rights of Palestinians in Gaza under the genocide convention . <h> Israel 's actions -- and words -- are being closely assessed <p> In the order , the ICJ said Israel 's military operation " had resulted in a large number of deaths and injuries , as well as the massive destruction of homes , the forcible displacement of the vast majority of the population , and extensive damage to civilian infrastructure " . <p> Critically , it said @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ statements made by senior Israeli officials . These included the country 's defence minister , Yoav Gallant , saying Israel was fighting " human animals " and " we will eliminate everything " , and its president , Isaac Herzog , saying : " It 's an entire nation out there that is responsible . " <p> Paragraph 54 of the order stated it was the court 's view , in light of the above statements , that " at least some " of South Africa 's claims that Palestinian rights needing protection under the genocide convention were " plausible " . <p> Further down , it ordered Israel to " take all measures within its power to prevent and punish the direct and public incitement to commit genocide in relation to members of the Palestinian group in the Gaza Strip " . <h> There was no call for a ceasefire <p> The court agreed with most of the requests made by South Africa , including ordering humanitarian access , but notably absent was its demand for an immediate halt to Israel 's military operation . As well as the political battle that will now be fought on how to enforce the court orders , the judges told Israel to submit a report " on all measures taken to give effect to its order " within a month . <p> Given that the Israeli prime minister , Benjamin Netanyahu , criticised the court for its " willingness " to discuss the case at all -- which he said was a " disgrace that will not be erased for generations " -- it is unclear how the country will respond .
