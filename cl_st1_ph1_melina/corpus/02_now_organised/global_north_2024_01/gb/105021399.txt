<h> Gold firms as Middle East concerns boost safe-haven demand <p> Gold prices gained on Friday as fears of escalating conflict in the Middle East lifted the appeal of the safe-haven metal despite stronger than expected U.S. inflation data boosting the dollar and Treasury yields . <p> Spot gold was up 1% at $2,048.20 an ounce at 1254 GMT , extending its run above the $2,000 level to nearly a month . U.S. gold futures were up 1.7% at $2,053.50 . <p> " Gold is rising on the back of the ongoing geopolitical conflict in the Middle East ... In spite of the stronger than expected CPI print , gold prices have recovered today and have moved up and are likely to move up further , " said Kunal Shah , head of research at Nirmal Bang Commodities in Mumbai . <p> A sudden widening of the war in Gaza is raising questions about how long stock markets can rally and when central banks will cut interest rates . <p> The United States and Britain launched strikes against sites linked to the Houthi movement in Yemen and Saudi Arabia called for restraint in light of the strikes . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 1330 GMT , after data on Thursday showed U.S. consumer prices rose more than expected in December . Excluding volatile food and energy costs , however , the pace of price increases fell to 3.9% from 4% on an annual basis . <p> U.S. Federal Reserve officials signalled inadequate progress on inflation to start cutting rates in March . Traders , however , still see a 71.4% probability of a March cut , according to the CME Fedwatch tool . <p> Higher rates dim the appeal of non-yielding bullion . <p> " Market participants continue to expect a soft landing of the U.S. economy , allowing the Fed to cut rates over coming months , " said UBS analyst Giovanni Staunovo , adding that this should support gold prices . <p> ( Reporting by Anjana Anil in Bengaluru Editing by Mark Potter and David Goodman ) <p> Disclaimer : The content of this article is syndicated or provided to this website from an external third party provider .
