<p> This write-up seeks to examine the effects and impacts of emerging developments in International Criminal Law . The Prosecutor of International Criminal Court ( ICC ) recently filed Criminal charges against Benjamin Netanyahu , the sitting Prime Minister of Israel , for the commission of war crimes , including the use of starvation as a method of warfare , targeting of civilians and restricting the human aid to Gaza . The ICC , while taking cognizance of the matter and after examining the material evidence presented before it , issued warrants of arrest of the Israeli Prime Minister . <p> The court ruled , " in the light of ratification of Rome Statute by Palestine State , the court is competent to proceed in the matter ' ' . <p> Although , it is early to say about the outcome of these proceedings , yet let us understand and examine the nature of allegations against the accused and possible outcomes . The allegations levelled against the accused are the following . <p> - War crimes in Gaza : <p> It has been alleged : Netanyahu and his associates used starvation as a method of warfare by placing deliberate restrictions on the supply of food , water , fuel , electricity , and medicines . <p> Crimes against humanity : The civilian population , particularly children and age old people , of Gaza were targeted by denying water , electricity and food particularly when it was evident that they were non-combatants . <p> Inhuman acts : The acts of war by Israel caused deaths and torture through destruction of medical facilities . <p> Persecutions : People in Gaza were targeted on their identity and nationality . <p> Targeting of civilians : Deliberate military actions were taken against the civilian population of Gaza . <p> The actions of ICC are though within the framework of Rome Statute , yet its actions may confront challenges of jurisdiction and absence of action by the Israeli authorities . <p> Jurisdiction : Israel may argue the ICC lacks jurisdiction because Israel is not a party to the Rome Statute and to the dispute of Palestinian statehood . <p> Absence of intervention by the Israeli courts : Israel preempts ICJ involvement by conducting investigations into the alleged crimes . However , its failure to do so conferred jurisdiction on ICC to proceed in the matter . <p> Political and geopolitical implications <p> Enforcement challenges : <p> * The ICC lacks its own enforcement mechanism and depends on member states to execute arrest warrants . Countries with strong ties to Israel , like the United States and Hungary , have already indicated that they will not cooperate with ICC as they do not recognize the institution ; it undermines the ICC 's authority . <p> * Non-cooperation from powerful allies also highlights the ICC 's limitations and its reliance on political will of the world community . <p> International relations : <p> The charges against Netanyahu could strain Israel 's diplomatic ties with ICC member states . <p> * A number of countries support the ICC 's investigations ; these states emphasize on accountability of rogue nations . It may lead to a deepening divide between Israel and other countries . <p> Developing precedent for accountability : <p> * A conviction of a sitting or former head of government like Netanyahu can set a historic precedent for holding rogue leaders accountable for their illegal actions and atrocities . <p> Impact of ICC 's orders : <p> Ending impunity : <p> The ICC action aims to end impunity for those who commit inhuman acts of atrocities . <p> Ringing the bell of justice : <p> The International Law requires the rights of war crime affectees as recognized by the principles of Nuremberg Trials which were adopted by the UN in 1946 . <p> Global security : <p> The ICC being an arm of the United Nations is contributing towards global security and peace . <p> Past precedents : <p> The ICC 's investigations into the Benjamin Netanyahu affairs is significant though , yet not without precedents . Political leaders have previously been held responsible for their illegal actions by international courts . <p> Examples : <p> Slobodan Milosevic ( Yugoslavia ) : <p> * Charges : Milosevic was indicted by the International Criminal Tribunal for war crimes , crimes against humanity , and genocide during the Balkan wars . <p> Result : He was arrested and extradited to The Hague in 2001 , but he died in custody before a verdict was reached . The action of the ICC amid complex geopolitical challenges demonstrated the international community 's commitment and ability to prosecute high-ranking officials who commit atrocities . <p> The Omar al-Bashir case : <p> Charges : The ICC issued arrest warrants against al-Bashir for genocide , war crimes , and crimes against humanity in Darfur . <p> * Result : Despite the warrants , al-Bashir traveled to several countries without being arrested . After being ousted in 2019 , Sudan indicated willingness to cooperate with the ICC , but no transfer has yet occurred . <p> The Charles Taylor case : <p> * Charges : Taylor was tried by a Special Court for Sierra Leone for war crimes committed during the Sierra Leone Civil War . <p> Result : Convicted and sentenced to 50 years in prison in 2012 . Where international courts and states collaborate , the potential for accountability increases . <p> * Limitations : It demonstrates the difficulties in sustaining a trial without state cooperation . <p> Process followed by the ICC <p> Investigation and charges : <p> * To establish whether there exist grounds to believe occurrence of a crime within court 's jurisdiction , the ICC <p> Prosecutor gathers evidence . <p> * The Complaint to the court has to establish " reasonable grounds " against the accused , with reference to charges of war crimes and crimes against humanity . <p> Implementation of the arrest warrants : <p> The ICC relies on cooperation extended by member states for execution of warrants . Member states are under an obligation to comply . <p> * In Netanyahu 's case , Several ICC member states have already shown non-cooperation , weakening the enforcement prospects , but many other states have shown commitment to enforce ICC orders .. <p> ICC trial process : <p> * The accused must be present in The Hague for a trial to proceed . This includes pre-trial confirmation of charges , the main trial , and sentencing if convicted . <p> * Trials are conducted with strict adherence to international legal norms , including the presumption of innocence and the right to defend . <p> Challenges of harmonization : <p> * Where a country itself conducts credible investigations into the allegations , the ICC restraints itself as the ICC acts as a court of last resort . <p> Undertones for Netanyahu <p> Challenges of international politics : <p> * ICC proceedings and court 's legality has been challenged by the US and Hungary . They oppose the ongoing proceedings . These challenges highlight the limitations of international law and in its enforcement . <p> General disagreement : <p> * The ICC faces pressures but the court maintains its impartiality while reviewing the collection of evidence in politically charged cases , particularly where state cooperation is absent . <p> Impact of such prosecutions : <p> * The actions of ICC provide a powerful message regarding accountability . These cases provide an opening for apprehending the defiant leaders particularly those who can be successfully prosecuted . <p> ICC Proceedings and potential legal Defenses for the accused : <p> An accused can employ several legal and procedural defenses to counter the ICC charges . The accused can challenge both the jurisdiction of the court and substance of the allegations . <p> The writer is a PhD in International Law from Concordia College , LL.M from Washington University in St. Louis School of Law : MPA : University of Southern California .
