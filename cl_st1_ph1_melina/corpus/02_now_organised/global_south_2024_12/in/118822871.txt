( Eric Lee/The New York Times ) <p> The Biden administration has announced a detailed national strategy to tackle Islamophobia and discrimination against Muslim and Arab Americans , outlining over 100 specific actions . This strategy , launched five weeks before President Biden 's departure from office , builds on a similar plan released earlier to combat antisemitism . The initiative reflects the administration 's response to escalating hate crimes and discrimination , particularly in the wake of the Israel-Gaza conflict . <p> The strategy 's release comes amid heightened concerns over anti-Muslim and anti-Arab hate . The October 2023 killing of Wadea Al-Fayoume , a six-year-old Palestinian-American boy in Illinois , was cited as a tragic example of the growing threats faced by these communities . In a foreword to the strategy , President Biden called the attack and others like it " heinous acts " and stated , " Muslims and Arabs deserve to live with dignity and enjoy every right to the fullest extent along with all of their fellow Americans . " <p> The White House highlighted that the plan 's importance has grown due to a noticeable rise in hate crimes , discrimination , and bullying directed toward these groups . Data indicates a significant uptick in incidents , particularly following the recent Israel-Gaza war , which has intensified tensions globally and domestically . <h> Key Objectives and Focus Areas <p> The strategy focuses on four main pillars : <p> Raising Awareness : Enhancing public understanding of anti-Muslim and anti-Arab hate and recognising the contributions of these communities to American society . <p> The plan includes enhanced data collection efforts and educational initiatives aimed at fostering awareness . It calls for sharing best practices for hate crime reporting and clarifies that discrimination against Muslim and Arab Americans in federally funded programs is illegal . Additionally , it urges state , local , and international partners , along with non-governmental organisations , to adopt similar measures . <p> While the strategy has been lauded by some as a necessary step , it has also faced criticism from advocacy groups . The Council on American-Islamic Relations ( CAIR ) commented , " The White House strategy lays out some positive recommendations related to anti-Muslim bigotry , but it has been released too late to make an impact . " CAIR also pointed to the strategy 's failure to address what it termed systemic issues , such as federal watchlists and broader U.S. policies that perpetuate discrimination . <p> Jim Zogby , founder of the Arab American Institute , welcomed the inclusion of anti-Arab hate in the strategy but expressed scepticism about its longevity , predicting that the incoming Trump administration might disown the plan . <p> The strategy mirrors a September 2023 plan to combat antisemitism , marking a continued focus on addressing hate in its various forms . However , its timing -- coinciding with the transition of power to President-elect Donald Trump -- raises questions about its future . Trump 's previous policies , including a travel ban targeting Muslim-majority countries , have been criticised for fostering Islamophobia . In recent statements , Trump pledged to ban entry to individuals questioning Israel 's right to exist and revoke visas of foreign students deemed " antisemitic . " <p> The strategy 's release comes during heightened activism on U.S. college campuses and elsewhere , where pro-Israel and pro-Palestinian groups have clashed following the October 7 Hamas attacks in Israel . Human rights advocates have warned of a troubling rise in antisemitism , Islamophobia , and anti-Arab hate . <p> With tensions escalating and political transitions underway , the effectiveness and sustainability of this strategy remain uncertain . Advocacy groups continue to call for broader systemic reforms to address the root causes of discrimination and hate .
