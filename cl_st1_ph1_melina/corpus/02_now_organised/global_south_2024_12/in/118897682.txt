<p> Top diplomats from the United States , the Arab League , and Turkey convened in Jordan on Saturday to discuss the future of Syria following the fall of Bashar al-Assad 's government just a week ago . <p> The focus of the meet was on shaping the country 's transition in the aftermath of the Assad family 's more than 50-year rule , which has left the region in turmoil . There was , however , no representative from Syria . <p> The sudden collapse of the Assad regime has sparked fresh concerns about regional instability in West Asia which is already facing the ongoing Israel-Hamas conflict in Gaza and the continuing hostilities between Israel and Hezbollah in Lebanon . While a fragile ceasefire between Hezbollah and Lebanon , these developments have made the Middle East 's security situation more precarious than ever . <h> West Asia Latest Developments <p> US Secretary of State Blinken wraps up regional tour in Jordan <p> During a regional tour , US Secretary of State Antony Blinken stressed the need for a " Syrian-led transition " and said that the United States has been in direct communication with the Syrian insurgent group that led the overthrow of Assad . However , Blinken clarified that this group remains classified by the US as a " foreign terrorist organization . " <p> Blinken , wrapping up the tour , reaffirmed a broad consensus among his counterparts in Jordan , Iraq , and Turkey that Syria 's future government must be inclusive , respect human rights , and prioritize the dismantling of chemical weapons stockpiles from the Assad era . " The new Syrian government must be one that is inclusive , respects the rights of all citizens , including minorities , and ensures that state institutions are restored and secure , " he said during a press conference in Aqaba . <p> During a meeting with UN Special Envoy for Syria , Geir Pedersen , Blinken discussed the challenges of Syria 's transition and the essential role the United Nations would play in providing humanitarian assistance . <p> In a joint statement issued after the meeting , diplomats from multiple countries , including the United States , Turkey , and several Arab nations , voiced their support for a Syrian-led transitional process aimed at achieving a peaceful resolution and the restoration of Syria 's sovereignty and territorial integrity . The statement also condemned Israel 's recent military actions in Syria , particularly the seizure of a buffer zone in the Golan Heights , which the diplomats labeled as a " heinous occupation . " <p> A separate statement issued by Arab foreign ministers called for UN-supervised elections based on a new constitution approved by Syrians . <p> Meanwhile , situation remains worse in Gaza as Israeli airstrikes continued to target Hamas positions , resulting in significant civilian casualties . On Saturday , at least 11 people were killed in airstrikes across Gaza , including a deadly attack in Deir al-Balah that claimed four lives . Palestinian officials also reported that an airstrike hit Gaza City 's Majda Wasilla school , a shelter for displaced families , killing at least seven , including a woman and her two-day-old baby . <p> The Israeli military confirmed the strikes , stating they targeted Hamas fighters at a compound , but the mounting toll on civilians raises concerns over the humanitarian crisis in Gaza , which has already left over 2 million Palestinians suffering in dire conditions . <h> Syria 's New Leadership Focuses on Reconstruction , Not Conflict <p> In Syria , the newly established leadership , led by the insurgent group Hayat Tahrir al-Sham ( HTS ) , has faced significant challenges in navigating both internal and external relations . Syria 's de facto leader , Ahmad al-Sharaa , also known as Abu Mohammed al-Golani , has made it clear that his group is not seeking further conflict despite Israel 's aggressive actions . " We are not seeking further conflict but are focused on rebuilding our country , " al-Sharaa told Syrian TV . <p> He stated that the focus should be on rebuilding Syria , which has been ravaged by years of war . He further stressed that " uncalculated military adventures " are not in Syria 's interest and that diplomatic engagement is the key to ensuring the country 's security and stability . <p> Israel has launched hundreds of airstrikes targeting Syrian military assets and strategic weapon stockpiles since Assad 's fall , with no signs of de-escalation . While Israel maintains that its presence is temporary and aimed at securing its borders , it has faced condemnation from several Arab nations , including Saudi Arabia , the UAE , and Jordan . <h> Hezbollah says it lost key supply line in Syria <p> Hezbollah , a long-time ally of the Assad regime , has also been affected by the political shift in Syria . The group 's leader , Naim Kassem , confirmed that the fall of Assad had severed Hezbollah 's main supply line through Syria . <p> In his first public comments in the week since Assad 's fall , Hezbollah leader Naim Kassem in a televised speech Saturday said Hezbollah has lost the military supply line through Syria but the new authority there might reinstate the route . Otherwise , he said , " we might find other ways , " AP reported . <h> Palestinian refugees return to Yarmouk <p> In the refugee camps surrounding Damascus , the return of displaced Palestinians , particularly to the Yarmouk camp , has sparked uncertainty about their future under the new leadership . Once a thriving hub for Palestinians , Yarmouk was devastated during the Syrian civil war . With Assad 's government dismantled , many displaced Palestinians are hopeful about returning but are unclear about their role in Syria 's future . <p> Palestinian leaders are keen to establish a dialogue with the new authorities to safeguard their community 's rights and security .
