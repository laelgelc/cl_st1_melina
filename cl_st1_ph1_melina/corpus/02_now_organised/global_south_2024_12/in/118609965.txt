<h> The abrupt fall of President Bashar al-Assad 's regime ends five decades of Baath Party rule in Syria , following a decisive offensive by Hayat Tahrir al-Sham ( HTS ) and allied factions . While Damascus celebrated , the country faces an uncertain future , marked by deep divisions , displaced populations , and scepticism over HTS 's governance . The regime 's collapse also disrupts regional power dynamics , affecting the strategic interests of Russia , Iran , Turkey , and Israel . The challenges ahead underline the complexity of rebuilding a fragmented nation . <p> APOpposition fighters celebrate in Damascus , Syria <p> In a dramatic turn of events , rebel forces led by Hayat Tahrir al-Sham ( HTS ) captured Damascus , marking the end of Bashar al-Assad 's presidency . The offensive , launched from HTS 's base in Idlib on November 27 , swiftly overtook strategic cities , including Aleppo , Hama , and Homs , before forcing Assad to flee the capital . The fall of Damascus brought jubilant celebrations , with citizens chanting , " We declare the city of Damascus free . " <p> HTS announced the end of 50 years of Baath Party dominance , issuing a statement : " After 50 years of oppression under Baath rule , and 13 years of crimes and tyranny and ( forced ) displacement , we announce today the end of this dark period and the start of a new era for Syria . " <h> A Divisive Legacy <p> Assad , who succeeded his father Hafez al-Assad in 2000 , entered office amid hopes of reform . However , he perpetuated the authoritarian structure of his father 's regime , which suppressed dissent and centralised power . The 2011 uprisings , inspired by the Arab Spring , were met with brutal repression , igniting a civil war that devastated the nation . Over half a million people were killed , six million became refugees , and millions more were internally displaced . <p> Assad 's grip on power was largely sustained through military backing from Russia and Iran . Russian air support and Iranian militias , including Hezbollah , played key roles in quelling opposition . However , recent regional and global developments left Assad vulnerable , enabling HTS 's rapid advance . <h> Challenges of Transition <p> Rebel leader Abu Mohammed al-Golani , now known as Ahmed al-Sharaa , announced the establishment of a transitional authority . Prime Minister Mohammed al-Jalali has been appointed to oversee state institutions during this period . Al-Jalali stated his willingness to " work with any leadership chosen by the Syrian people . " <p> Despite this announcement , many remain sceptical about HTS 's capacity to lead . Originally affiliated with al-Qaeda , HTS has attempted to rebrand itself as a nationalist movement . However , concerns persist about its long-term intentions , with fears of authoritarian Islamist rule under its leadership . <p> The transition also risks reigniting internal divisions among rebel factions , who control different regions of the fragmented country . Without a cohesive vision for governance , Syria faces the possibility of further conflict . <h> Regional Power Shifts <h> Russia : A Strategic Loss <p> The fall of Assad is a significant blow to Russian influence in the Middle East . Since its military intervention in 2015 , Russia had been the Assad regime 's staunchest ally , maintaining key naval and air bases in Tartous and Latakia . These bases , crucial for projecting power in the Mediterranean and Africa , now face uncertain futures as Russia grapples with its ongoing war in Ukraine . <h> Iran : A Fractured Axis <p> For Iran , Assad 's ousting undermines the " Axis of Resistance , " which links Tehran to Hezbollah in Lebanon via Syria . This network has facilitated arms transfers and bolstered Iran 's regional influence . Tehran 's position is further weakened by recent Israeli strikes targeting its assets and Hezbollah 's losses in conflicts with Israel . <p> The loss of Assad also disrupts Iran 's strategy of supporting allied groups in Yemen , Iraq , and Gaza , leaving its regional aspirations in disarray . <h> Mixed Reactions from Neighbours <h> Turkey 's Calculated Neutrality <p> Turkey , which hosts over three million Syrian refugees , denied direct involvement in the HTS offensive . However , analysts suggest Ankara may have provided tacit support , aligning with its interest in stabilising northern Syria and countering Kurdish militias . Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan had long advocated for a negotiated resolution to the Syrian conflict , but Assad resisted such overtures . <h> Israel 's Strategic Considerations <p> Israel views Assad 's fall as both an opportunity and a challenge . On one hand , it disrupts Iran 's supply chain to Hezbollah . On the other , HTS 's rise introduces unpredictability . Israel has bolstered its presence along the Golan Heights to prevent spillovers and safeguard against potential arms seizures by extremist groups . <h> Decades of Turmoil <p> Syria 's civil war , rooted in the 2011 anti-government protests , spiralled into a protracted conflict involving multiple factions and foreign powers . Assad 's regime , supported by Russia and Iran , withstood numerous challenges over the years but at a devastating cost to the nation . The last four years saw relative calm in the conflict until HTS 's surprise offensive reignited hostilities . <p> Syria 's future is fraught with uncertainty . Millions of displaced Syrians , both within the country and abroad , are watching developments with a mix of hope and fear . The lack of a unified leadership among rebel factions and the shadow of HTS 's extremist origins further complicates the transition . <p> While the end of Assad 's rule is a turning point , it does not guarantee peace or stability . Addressing Syria 's deep divisions , rebuilding its shattered infrastructure , and ensuring inclusive governance will require coordinated efforts from both internal stakeholders and the international community .
