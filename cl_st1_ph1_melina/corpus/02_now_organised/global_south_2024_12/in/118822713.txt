<p> Explained : Syrian Crisis and its implications for Iran and regional geopolitics <h> Explained : Syrian Crisis and its implications for Iran and regional geopolitics <h> The end of five decades of rule over Syria by the house of al-Assad represents a decisive turning point in Middle Eastern geopolitics . Perhaps the biggest impact will be on Iran , but no actor in the region will be untouched . <p> The collapse of the Bashar al-Assad regime in Syria is expected to have wide ramifications across the region , presenting in particular , a strategic challenge to Iran 's regional influence and undermining the long-standing effort by Tehran to maintain a " Shiite crescent " across the Middle East . <p> Assad was forced to flee to Russia as Damascus fell on December 8 at the end of a two-week lightning offensive by opposition forces led by the Hayat Tahrir al-Sham ( HTS ) , an armed group designated as a terrorist outfit by the United Nations , the United States , and Turkey . <h> The Iran-Syria partnership , and challenges for Tehran <p> Since the formation of the Islamic Republic in 1979 , Damascus has been Tehran 's principal strategic partner , with Syria serving as an essential corridor for Iranian influence to work throughout the Levant . The partnership facilitated Iranian access to Lebanon , and allowed Tehran to provide financial and military support to Hezbollah , the Shiite militia that the Iranian regime used to further its interests in the region . <p> Advertisement <p> The relationship , which began during the three-decade rule of Hafez al-Assad ( 1971-2000 ) , gained strength during the presidency of Hafez 's son Bashar al-Assad . The strategic alignment between the Ba'athist governments of the Assads and Iran was opposed by the predominantly Sunni Arab states in the region . Syria is part of the Arab world , but the al-Assad family is Alawite , a sect of Twelver Shiism . <p> Iran 's regional influence exerted through the " Axis of Resistance " , its strategic network of non-state militant actors , has experienced significant diminution since Israel began its military operations in the Gaza Strip in response to the October 7 , 2023 attacks . The " strategic depth " frequently emphasized by top Iranian officials including the Supreme Leader , has experienced considerable contraction . <p> Hamas , the perpetrator of the attacks on Israel and one of Tehran 's affiliated organisations , has been significantly downgraded as a military entity . In Lebanon along Israel 's northern frontier , Hezbollah , another component of the " axis " , has suffered a comparable setback , evidenced by the elimination of its supreme commander , Hassan Nasrallah on 27 September 2024 , alongside several key military personnel . And in the southern Arabian peninsula , the Houthi militias in Yemen , who receive support from Tehran , are facing mounting pressure not only from Israeli forces , but also from the military operations of the United States Central Command . However , various armed factions supported by Tehran continue to have substantial influence in Iraq , a country that has increasingly aligned itself with Iranian interests in recent years . <p> The politico-security transformation in Syria both illustrates and exacerbates the diminution of the sphere of Iranian influence . <p> Advertisement <p> Tehran had previously commenced a measured reduction of its military footprint within Syria , predicated on its strategic assessment that a degree of stability was returning to the country , where a bloody civil war broke out in early 2011 . However , the elimination by the US , in January 2020 , of General Qassem Soleimani , a pivotal figure in Iran 's Revolutionary Guard Corps , created an operational void that was sought to be filled by the deployment of Hezbollah commanders operating as Tehran 's proxies . But this tactical reconfiguration encountered significant impediments during the military engagements initiated by Israel , which resulted in the neutralization of a large number of senior Hezbollah leaders and strategists . <p> These successive operational setbacks substantially compromised Iran 's capacity to maintain its strategic position within Syria -- and the fall of the Assad regime has presented Tehran with a range of difficult challenges . To hardliners within the regime , the strategic importance of a nuclear deterrence has grown significantly . However , with the Middle East in imminent danger of exploding flames of war , such an escalation by Iran would carry very significant risks . <p> Geopolitical implications for the countries in the region <p> The collapse of the Assad regime and diminution of Iran 's influence in Syria carry profound implications for the region 's geopolitical landscape , power dynamics , and strategic alignments . <p> * Regional powers including Iran , Turkey , and the Gulf Cooperation Council ( GCC ) states will compete to fill the power vacuum in Syria , and to recalibrate their efforts to safeguard their core interests . <p> Advertisement <p> * Key Gulf actors , notably Saudi Arabia and the UAE , may seek to strengthen their diplomatic leverage through calculated engagement with the diverse Syrian political factions and reconstruction efforts , attempting to balance against Turkish and Iranian regional aspirations . <p> * Israel is expected to take more assertive positions against Iran-affiliated organizations in the region , including Lebanese resistance groups active in Syria , with the aim to prevent Tehran from reconstituting its sphere of influence in Israel 's north . <p> * The instability in Syria could also exacerbate sectarian tensions as Sunni Arab states seek to prevent Iran from regaining influence , while various militant groups , including the Islamic State of Iraq and Syria ( ISIS ) could try to rebuild their operational presence in the region . <p> * Turkey 's military footprint in northern Syria is likely to continue , primarily motivated by concerns over Kurdish autonomy movements and their broader regional implications . <p> Advertisement <p> * Iraq , which faces complex domestic and regional dynamics , may explore a strategic reassessment of its ties with Iran , even as it navigates the spillover effects of the volatility in Syria . <p> * The Western alliance , led by the US and European nations , can be expected to intensify their strategic involvement , at a later stage , if not imminently , through coordinated diplomatic channels and measured interventions . <p> Such engagement will serve the West 's dual objectives of preventing the reemergence of non-state militant organizations , while limiting Russian and Iranian strategic expansion in the Mediterranean region . <p> The upshot : watershed moment , no obvious way forward <p> The developments in Syria represent a decisive turning point in Middle Eastern geopolitics . The dramatic reduction of Tehran 's influence in Damascus signals a major reconfiguration of regional partnerships and an intensification of strategic competition . While regional powers might perceive opportunities to expand their influence , the risk of heightened volatility remains significant . <p> Advertisement <p> The success of multilateral diplomatic initiatives in reconciling divergent national interests will be crucial in determining whether the region progresses toward stability or faces further deterioration of its security architecture . Given the extraordinarily complex and fluid situation , forecasting outcomes remains precarious . <p> Dr. Alvite Ningthoujam is an Assistant Professor at the Symbiosis School of International Studies , Pune . His research interests include the geopolitics of the Middle Eastern region .
