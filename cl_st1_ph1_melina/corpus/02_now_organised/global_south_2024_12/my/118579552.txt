<h> Prioritising policies best for the kids <p> Photo shows Afghan school girls , with Unicef bags on their backs , walking back to their houses along a path in Khost. -- AFP photo <p> UNITED Nations Children 's Fund ( Unicef ) ' s years of contributions towards protecting children 's rights and safeguarding their futures have been transformative across the world . <p> Among these efforts include driving down child mortality rates and expanding vaccination coverage , leading to healthier starts for countless young children . <p> Over the past seven decades , the intergovernmental organisation has been working tirelessly to improve the lives of children across Malaysia . <p> According to Unicef East Asia and Pacific Regional Office deputy regional director Dr Myo-Zin Nyunt , the organisation has been collaborating with the Malaysian government since the 1960s and this relationship has evolved significantly since the two first entered into this partnership . <p> " We signed a basic cooperation agreement with the Malaysian government in 1964 , marking the beginning of a collaborative journey aimed at improving the lives of children across the country . <p> " When it comes to child health , nutrition and education , as well as access to water and sanitation , amongst others , these have greatly improved , " he told thesundaypost . <p> Dr Myo-Zin Nyunt <p> However , he also stressed that there were still barriers to equitable access , especially in the rural and underprivileged areas . <p> " Some regions do better than other regions , and some of the children in the urban poor ( areas ) are not having full access to the services that they need . <p> " There are disparities and as such , we still need to really work closely with the Malaysian government and also all partners to address this disparity and existing inequalities in Malaysia . " <p> Dr Nyunt said in Malaysia , Sarawak had emerged as a leading example of how local governments could prioritise child-friendly policies . <p> " We are very proud to be partnering with Sarawak on the Child-Friendly City Initiative ( CFCI ) , and I really appreciate and would like to congratulate the Sarawak government , the state Ministry of Women , Childhood and Community Wellbeing Development and the local councils for taking leadership in the movement with this initiative . <p> " Last year , Unicef organised a visit to Europe -- namely Spain , Finland and Norway -- to see how cities are becoming more child-friendly , and how they create child-friendly spaces . <p> " So , the minister from Sarawak and her team went there and just after a year or so , we now have seven municipalities that have signed the MoUs ( memoranda of understanding ) to implement the CFCI in Sarawak . <p> " This shows that Sarawak is really moving towards this initiative where a child-friendly city is where we look at how to save a city for children when it comes to road traffic , more green spaces , playgrounds and so on , while also allowing children to participate in the decision-making of the city council . " <p> In addition to the CFCI , Dr Nyunt said that Unicef was collaborating with Sarawak on the ' Child-Sensitive Integrated Social Protection Initiative ' , meant to integrate social assistance for children with early childhood interventions and parenting education . <p> " Another project we are working on is the ' Child Wellbeing Index ' , to see how children are faring in terms of their access to health , education and what the outcomes are . <p> " At the same time , we are also supporting the Sarawak government in carrying out evaluation for their early intervention programme to look at how this programme is really cost-effective , supportive and reaching the outcome , " he said . <p> Dr Nyunt ( third left ) and Minister of Women , Childhood and Community Wellbeing Development Sarawak Dato Sri Fatimah Abdullah , on his left , with other guests posing for a group photo at the ' Second Regional Social Policy Conference East Asia and Pacific ' , held in Kuching recently . -- Photo by Mohd Faisal Ahmad <p> Dr Nyunt also emphasised on the vital role of social policy in supporting human capital development , especially in the post Covid-19 recovery era . <p> " In a post Covid-19 world , all countries are working very hard at economic recovery and growth . But from a social policy perspective , it is very important that in order to have economic growth , we really need the human capital and human capital development starts with a child . <p> " A child needs to be healthy , well nourished , well-educated and well-protected by social protection so that he or she can grow and really contribute later in life for the wellbeing of the family , community and also for the nation . " <p> Dr Nyunt viewed such social policy as ' looking at human capital development through the lens of a child 's right and human 's rights ' , ensuring that no children would be left behind , and that all of them would receive comprehensive services and support as per the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child ( UNCRC ) . <p> " Malaysia is one of the signatories to the UNCRC rights to health , education , nationality , and so on . I think these child rights are enshrined in the human capital development support . <p> " So that 's where social policy will really bring into this as the policy looks at how we finance children , what are the social protection mechanisms that could really help , especially the most vulnerable families , to ensure that they do not fall back into poverty . " <p> On the challenges facing children under the East Asia and Pacific region , Dr Nyunt said : " Unicef faces unique challenges due to economic and social disparities . While there are countries like Malaysia and Thailand , which are of upper middle-income status , there are also low-income neighbouring nations such as Laos , North Korea and Myanmar . <p> " But what is common across the region , whether it is an upper middle-income or low-income country , ( is that ) there are pockets of disparities and inequalities . <p> " Across the region , there are around three to five million children who remain unvaccinated , and they are what we call ' zero-dose children ' -- meaning children who did not receive any vaccine . <p> " These children are the same children who have issues with malnutrition . The children will be stunted . <p> " Their families are in or below the poverty line , ( so ) they may not have access to clean water or sanitation facilities and may not be able to send their children to school . " <p> Workers sorting a shipment of polio vaccines provided with support from Unicef to the Gaza Strip , through the Karm Abu Salem crossing , also known as ' Kerem Shalom ' , at a depot belonging to Gaza 's Health Ministry . -- AFP photo <p> Dr Nyunt said another challenge was that when children were not immunised properly , they would be prone to preventable diseases , which would impact their health . <p> " To address these disparities , Unicef is implementing strategies to reach ' zero-dose children ' by working closely with governments to identify those who have not been immunised . <p> " We are helping the governments to have proper data , or what we call a digital health system , so that they are able to digitalise all these health records . <p> " This will enable us to identify which children have yet to be immunised , and then we can really track and target them . " <p> Dr Nyunt also noted that going to the unimmunised children with the vaccine alone was not sufficient because they had other deprivations , inequalities and disparities . <p> " For instance , we have to bring in the water sanitation services , child protection services , and take those children to educate them and bring awareness on some good nutrition practices . " <p> Dr Nyunt said education inequality was another pressing issue where despite the improvement in enrolment of students in school , learning outcomes did vary . <p> " In the East Asia and Pacific region , I would say that 45 per cent of children at aged 10 are unable to read or write properly at their level . Even in a country like Malaysia , we have around 35 to 40 per cent of children who are not learning properly at their level at aged 10 . <p> " This is one of the major concerns that we really need to address because despite we having almost 100 per cent of children enrolled in schools , they are not learning properly . " <p> He said to address this learning poverty , Unicef was also working closely with governments through the respective education ministries by placing focus on teachers ' quality , classroom resources and targeted support for students who are struggling . <p> On children 's rights , Dr Nyunt said there had been more pushbacks on their rights . <p> " When it comes to the particular rights such as rights to citizenship , rights to freedom of speech , I think these are a big challenge in the region . <p> " This is one area that we work closely with the government , civil society organisations and the children themselves to express how they want their lives to be , how they want their education to be , and how they want their environment to be . <p> " I think listening to the children and also delivering the results for the children that they would like to have a future ... that 's one area that we are working closely on . " <p> Unicef has been collaborating with the Malaysian government since the 1960s , and this relationship has since evolved significantly . -- Malay Mail photo <p> Dr Nyunt said while some countries might have more challenges in the realisation of the rights of a child , there were also countries that were very much supportive of Unicef 's work in this aspect . <p> " In some countries , we face a bit of limitation on how much we can deliver for the rights of children , especially when it comes to freedom of speech and participation . <p> " But in Malaysia , we have states like Sarawak that are committed to initiatives like the CFCI . <p> " One example is the Kuching North City Commission ( DBKU ) , which will be setting up a children 's council and listening to the voices of children . " <p> Dr Nyunt viewed forming a children 's council as a very good step towards supporting the participation of children . <p> " Through the council , there will be discussions with the children because they represent the children 's constitution and so , the constituencies will be discussing and bringing the voices of children back to the city council . " <p> Dr Nyunt expressed optimism for the future , while commending Sarawak 's leadership and commitment to initiatives like the CFCI . <p> He believed that these efforts would serve as models for other regions that had been looking for ways to improve the welfare of children . <p> " We look forward to working closely with the Sarawak government , which is moving things in a very good direction , " he said , adding that by working together , Unicef and its partners could continue to make meaningful strides toward a future where every child would have the opportunity to thrive . <p> Robert Gass , the country representative for Unicef Malaysia , delivering his remarks at the Sarawak Urban Forum 2024 , held in Kuching in October this year . -- Photo via Facebook / UNICEF Malaysia
