<h> OPINION <h> Iqbal Jassat was an Assad defender <p> Joy Theron <p> 18 December 2024 <p> Joy Theron takes issue with the MRN 's executive member 's analysis of the downfall of the Syrian dictatorship <p> In his article titled ' Euphoria of Regime Change in Syria Fraught With Danger due to Elephant in the Room - Israel ' Iqbal Jassat of the Media Review Network appears to revel in the downfall of Bashar al-Assad while predominantly attributing regional turmoil to Israeli actions . <p> However , a deeper look into MRN 's publications and Jassat 's commentary shows a consistent pattern of defending Assad and an apparent reluctance to address the widespread human rights violations committed by Arab and Muslim governments . <p> Support for Assad and Syria <p> Jassat 's piece , " Assad Choice : Mortgage Freedom to America ? " frames the Syrian conflict as a Western-orchestrated plot for regime change , subtly justifying Assad 's rule by critiquing foreign intervention . He notes , " Syria today , like Iraq under the former Saddam Hussein regime , has fallen out of favour with Western interest , despite decades of investment in the cultivation of policies sympathetic to the zionist colonial-settler state known as Israel ! " <p> Similarly , in " Syria -- A Prize For ? " , Jassat casts doubt on whether a post-Assad Syria would serve Western interests , thereby indirectly supporting the idea that Assad 's resistance to these interests was legitimate . He questions , " As the battleground widens allowing for further wanton destruction , it remains to be seen whether Assad 's reign will be short-lived or not and whether his inevitable fall will offer Syria as a prize to the west . " <p> A brief look at the Media Review Network 's website reveals numerous articles of Jassat 's that glorify Arab world tyrants . Figures like Ismail Haniyeh , Yahya Sinwar , and Sayyed Nasrallah are celebrated as icons rather than being condemned for their actions as sheer terrorists . <p> --&gt; <p> Perhaps , rather than consistently targeting Israel , the singular democratic nation in the Middle East that ensures the welfare of its citizens , Media Review Network and Iqbal Jassat should turn their attention to the more troubling human rights records of other countries in the region . <p> Human Rights Abuses by Arab/Muslim Governments <p> Saudi Arabia is notorious for its stringent control over political freedoms , women 's rights , and the harsh treatment of migrant workers under the kafala system . Additionally , the death penalty is often applied without a fair judicial process . <p> In Egypt , since President Sisi 's rise to power , there has been a marked increase in mass detentions , suppression of protests , and widespread allegations of torture . The government has cracked down on any form of dissent , significantly curtailing political freedoms and civil liberties . <p> --&gt; <p> Syria , under the leadership of Bashar al-Assad , had been accused of numerous war crimes including the use of chemical weapons , brutal torture , and enforced disappearances . The civil war devastated the nation , leading to one of the worst humanitarian crises in recent history with countless human rights violations . A true Genocide indeed . <p> The United Arab Emirates is known for its suppression of free speech and political dissent . The kafala system , similar to that in Saudi Arabia , leads to the exploitation of the country 's large migrant workforce , often leaving workers in conditions that can be described as modern slavery . Iran enforces significant gender discrimination and restricts freedom of expression . It has one of the highest execution rates in the world , with many executions carried out for political dissent . The government 's strict adherence to religious law impacts all aspects of civil liberties . <p> In Bahrain , the Shia majority faces sectarian discrimination , and political repression is a common practice . Reports of torture in detention centers are frequent , with the government using harsh measures to quell any opposition or protest . <p> Yemen 's ongoing civil war has resulted in horrific human rights abuses by all parties involved , including the use of child soldiers and indiscriminate bombings . The conflict has not only caused immense suffering but has also led to a severe humanitarian crisis , with famine and disease rampant . <p> --&gt; <p> Iraq continues to struggle with issues like extrajudicial killings , widespread corruption , and persecution of religious minorities . The governance structure is volatile , with different factions vying for control , often leading to human rights abuses . Pakistan faces significant human rights challenges , including the misuse of blasphemy laws which are often weaponized against minorities , honor killings that disproportionately affect women , and the exploitation of child labor , which remains a pervasive issue despite national and international calls for reform . <p> If you think Hamas in Gaza and the Palestinian Authority in the West Bank are any better , you would be wrong . There , citizens are faced with arbitrary arrests , suppression of political opposition , severe restrictions on freedom of expression and assembly , and censorship of media . Both the PA and Hamas have been accused of torture and abuse of detainees . <p> But who 's to care about them ? As the saying goes , No Jews , No News ! <p> Selective Criticism <p> --&gt; <p> Jassat and MRN seem fixated on Israel 's role , now particularly in the Syrian context , while largely bypassing the human rights atrocities perpetrated by Arab and Muslim states . This selective outrage suggests a bias where Israel 's actions are depicted as the primary source of regional instability , sidelining the internal political and social issues within these countries . <p> In his latest piece , Jassat implies that Israeli interference is the chief destabilizing factor in Syria , ignoring Assad 's own contributions to the civil strife and human rights abuses . By suggesting that Assad 's regime was merely a target of Western plotting , Jassat minimizes the suffering of Syrians under Assad 's authoritarian rule , focusing instead on external geopolitical maneuvers . This narrative is a disservice to those who endured his tyranny . <p> The selective criticism of Israel by Jassat and the Media Review Network , while turning a blind eye to the oppressive actions of various Arab and Muslim regimes , provides a skewed view of Middle Eastern dynamics . The real issue , or the " elephant in the room " , as Jassat puts it , might well be the pervasive human rights violations by many of these governments , which deserve equal , if not more , scrutiny . Genuine human rights advocacy requires a comprehensive and fair critique that addresses oppression in all its forms , not just those that fit into a convenient narrative . <p> I 'll end off with a quote dedicated to Iqbal Jassat and the Media Review Network . As your dear friend Imtiaz Sooliman said ' Find a new narrative , this one is dull , boring , and stupid ' .
