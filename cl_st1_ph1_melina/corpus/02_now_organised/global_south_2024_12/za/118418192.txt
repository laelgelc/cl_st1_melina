<h> Perhaps the glass is n't half empty <h> Amid power cuts and crumbling infrastructure , South Africans show resilience . Could the spirit that hosted past world cups inspire unity and hope again ? <p> Hope concept . Picture : iStock <p> I should have wondered whether I was tempting fate the other night when I grabbed a torch to look for something under a couch : long time , no load shedding , I thought ... <p> The next day , City Powerless reminded us that competence does n't always go hand in hand with high tariffs . <p> All told we were off for 19 hours and the ducking and diving was what we 've come to expect from our power uselessly ... I mean utility . Team on site investigating . Later : team on site investigating . Power restored . Oops , it went off again ... <p> What was interesting , this time round , though , was that the complaints on X were far fewer than at the height of our power issues , when City Power took up where Eskom left off in terms of keeping us in the dark . <p> I had been working from home and it was only when my wife got back and announced " the power 's out " that I noticed . <p> We 've got a small solar power system , inverter and battery , which powers most of the lights and plugs so computers , TV and Wi-Fi will run for close to a day if used judiciously . We have a small gas stove and , as a final backup , a petrol generator . <p> When I walked out in the garden that evening , it was quieter , too , because there were fewer generators . <p> South Africans get by somehow . But should we ever , in our permanent " work around " state , be dreaming we could ever hold the Rugby World Cup again ? <p> Former Springbok AJ Venter -- not a " woke " liberal , going by his comments on Gaza anyway -- does n't think we can . World Rugby does n't hate us , he maintains , despite the shabby treatment of Rassie Erasmus being snubbed for the Coach Of The Year Award . <p> AJ says no-one wants to give us the tournament because of crime and the collapse of our infrastructure . I wonder , though . <p> In 1993 , heading back to Joburg from the Free State town of Koppies , nursing three broken ribs from cowardly right wingers who wanted to teach someone from Die Engelse Pers a lesson , I heard Louis Luyt on the radio saying that , in less than 18 months , the World Cup would be coming here . <p> He 's mad -- was my thought . In a country going up in the flames of a civil war ? <p> Yet , the rest is history ... as was the Fifa World Cup in 2010 , when we reached for , and touched , the stars once again . It would be nice to see unity of purpose and a resurgent patriotic spirit .
