<h> Despite unrest , life goes on for Pinoys in Israel <p> JERUSALEM -- Like many overseas Filipino workers ( OFWs ) , 37-year-old Marilou Cabaluna decided to look for a job abroad for the future of her children . <p> Cabaluna , who hails from Cagayan de Oro , arrived here in Israel in July 2022 , two years after her originally scheduled deployment was postponed because of the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic . <p> Like many OFWs in Israel , she works in the hotel industry . It was an opportunity that she had prayed for , she told The STAR in an interview . <p> Back then , her only idea of this country was that it was the site of the Holy Land . Two years later , she became more familiar with the intricacies of the geopolitical tension in the region . <p> " We were educated on why there are a lot of borders , why there are a lot of security personnel , " she said in Filipino , referring to the situation in Jerusalem , considered a holy site for some of the world 's major religions , namely Christianity , Islam and Judaism . <p> A year and three months into Cabaluna 's arrival in Israel , the country suffered one of worst attacks in its history . <p> On Oct. 7 , 2023 , Hamas militants launched a coordinated attack from the Gaza Strip into southern Israel , killing over a thousand and abducting hundreds of others , including some Filipinos and other foreign workers . <p> In the north , where Israel shares a border with Lebanon , militant group Hezbollah also intensified its rocket attacks , mostly toward the city of Haifa , escalating a conflict in existence for decades . <p> The attacks prompted Israel to launch a military offensive in Gaza and in southern Lebanon , a response that is drawing widespread criticism around the world as the number of civilians who had died or have been displaced continues to rise . <p> Jerusalem , where Cabaluna works , is located far from the areas directly affected by the attacks , but it was not spared . <p> On that very day of the initial attack , sirens blared in the hotel where she works . <p> " I feel like crying every time I remember what happened , " she said in Filipino . <p> " There was an alarm , and I felt like it was the end of the world . I was shaking , I do n't know what to do , " she added . <p> It was the first time that Cabaluna heard the siren , which signals an imminent rocket attack . <p> While Israel 's missile defense system is capable of intercepting such attacks , residents are required to move to shelter . <p> " That 's when we heard the blasts , the alarms . I really felt like it 's the end of the world , " Cabaluna recalled . <p> Jerusalem did not sustain any damage from those attacks , but what happened lingers in the memory of many . <p> Streets , which locals said used to be bustling with tourists , are mostly empty even at midday . Fewer people also visit religious sites . <p> " According to those who have been here for a while now , it was the first time that they had experienced an attack , " Cabaluna said . <h> Mental toll <p> The impact of the conflict is more evident in other areas . <p> For instance , in northern Israel , where the country has been in conflict with militant group Hezbollah for decades , rocket attacks are not new . <p> Despite the situation , Tyler de Leon , 24 , said he still decided to proceed with his plan to pursue his master 's degree in biology at the Technion -- Israel Institute of Technology , located just south of the city of Haifa . <p> De Leon 's family , who hails from Bacolod , left for the United Arab Emirates when he was eight . He finished his undergraduate degree in the United States . <p> He arrived in Israel in November 2023 , a month after the attacks . He said it was an easy decision and that he knew that it was going to be safe . <p> " I decided to come here despite what happened during the Oct. 7 attack because I really wanted to continue my research . I need to do this before I go back to the US to pursue my PhD , " he told The STAR . <p> " When I got here , Hezbollah was not firing a lot of missiles , especially in Haifa and in the north . Back then , we did n't have any sirens . The sirens actually started a few months ago , " he added . <p> Despite being physically safe , De Leon recognized the mental toll of the situation . <p> " Before the ceasefire happened , there would literally be rockets and sirens all throughout the day . You would n't even know when they would arrive because they would fire them at random times , " he said . <p> " There were a lot of instances I was sleeping at night , and then we would be awakened by sirens and then go to class the next day ... That would really have a great mental impact on you , " he added . <p> The university where he studies provides various kinds of support to those who are affected , according to De Leon . <p> " I 'm quite used to a lot of mental stress , especially since when I was in college ... It 's fine by me . Most of the students here who go through stress go to counseling service offered by Technion , " he added . <h> Better situation <p> The situation has improved in recent months , especially with the 60-day ceasefire agreement between Israel and Hezbollah , according to the Philippine embassy in Israel . <p> " Filipinos here are generally calm , they are secure and they are satisfied with the situation here ... They are very confident in Israel 's ability to defend itself , its people and its territory , " Charge d'Affaires Anthony Achilles Mandap told The STAR in a separate interview . <p> There are about 30,000 OFWs in Israel , mostly working in hotels and as caregivers . <p> Those who live in conflict areas , including in Gaza , that had been the target of Israel 's intensified attacks , had been evacuated and are either working in other areas or have been repatriated to the Philippines . <p> Mandap said only a little over 1,000 have availed themselves of the government 's repatriation program , with many deciding to stay . <p> Among the latter is Cabaluna , who said she is ready to leave , but only if there is a directive for mandatory repatriation . <p> " If the government says that we have to be repatriated , then we will be repatriated . But right now , we just have to keep fighting . That is our mindset . We never considered going home voluntarily , " she said . <p> " Based on our experience right now , we are safe and there is no threat . You can also see that in people here . Everything is normal and life goes on despite what happened , " she added . <p> Mandap said they might even recommend easing of the Philippines ' travel warning to Israel if the ceasefire holds up and the situation does not deteriorate . <p> The current alert level restricts non-essential travel and bars the deployment of new OFWs into Israel . <p> The Philippine embassy also assured the safety of Filipinos in Israel , saying they are ready to provide assistance , if necessary . <p> " We have contingency plans in place that we can execute at any moment 's notice . We have arranged services that we can provide , including repatriation , if they decide to leave for the Philippines under these conditions , " Mandap said . <p> Cabaluna said she is in regular contact with her family to assure them of her safety . <p> " Over time , you will get used to it , " she added , referring to security concerns . " We 'll just go with the flow -- for now . That 's what 's on our mind .
