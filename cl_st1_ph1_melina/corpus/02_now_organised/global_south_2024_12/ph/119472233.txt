<p> Despite assisting in government efforts to bring home thousands of Filipinos in the ongoing conflicts in the Middle East , the Department of Migrant Workers ( DMW ) was still able to push with its efforts better working conditions and services to overseas Filipino workers ( OFW ) . <p> The agency was able to bring home 2,406 OFWs and their 82 dependents from Israel and Lebanon since the Israel-Hamas conflict erupted in October 2023 . The effect of the conflict spread to southern Lebanon as well as to the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden . <p> Houthi attacked international ships passing through the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden supposedly to denounce the attacks of Israel at the Gaza strip . The said attacks affected Filipino crew members . <p> This prompted DMW to impose stricter rules for shipowners and manning agencies with ships with Filipino crew members , which is passing through the said dangerous waters . The measures include ensuring Filipino sailors are given the opportunity to exercise their right to refuse ( RTR ) sailing in areas with war-like conditions . <p> As of October , DMW said it was able to register 186 Filipino sailors , who refused to sail in the Gulf of Aden and Red Sea . <p> DMW discourages ships with Filipinos from passing through the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden , but for those , which will still insist on going through the risky waterways , they should have , maritime security escorts , as well as armed guards onboard . <p> The agency also helped ensure the safety of OFWs , who were affected by the earthquakes , which shook Japan and Taiwan as well as the floods , which hit the United Arab Emirates and Oman earlier this year . <p> ME deployment framework reforms <p> Aside from responding to the plight of distressed OFWs , DMW was also able to implement reforms , which enhanced the protection of OFWs . <p> This includes the implementation of a white-list framework for foreign recruitment agencies ( FRA ) in the Middle East and partner Philippine Recruitment agencies ( PRA ) . The measure aims to ensure only FRAs and PRAs with good standing are able to hire OFWs . <p> Earlier this month , DMW said the whitelist for the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia includes 432 PRAs , while for Kuwait , it is 183 . PRAs and FRAs , which have violations of Philippine laws and regulations will be included in a black list . <p> DMW also issued its Memorandum Circular ( MC ) No. 5 , series of 2024 containing the new guidelines for Filipino residential support workers and/or cleaners ( RSWC ) , who are bound for the KSA . <p> Unlike domestic workers , RSWC provides cleaning services , while not living within the premises of their employers . The said workers also have an 8-hour working period per day . The move is part of DMW 's initiatives to professionalize the domestic workers sector in KSA . <p> New markets <p> The DMW is also forging new bilateral labor agreements with some European markets , where there is increasing demand for migrant workers , including OFWs . <p> The European countries , which are in talks with DMW for the hiring of OFWs are Austria , Czech Republic , Germany , Romania , Finland , Slovenia , Croatia , and Hungary . Among the said countries , Finland , Slovenia , and Hungary still have no BLA with DMW . <p> Thousands of jobs are expected to become available for OFWs in Europe . In the Czech Republic alone , there are 10,300 vacancies for OFW , while for Austria it is at 200,000 . <p> The growing labor market in Europe , however , comes with a new problem for DMW as there is now an increasing number of OFWs , who are absconding from their employment contract and crossing borders to look for better paying jobs in Europe . <p> The recruitment industry urged DMW to implement measures to prevent the practice since it puts the working conditions of OFWs at risk and also hurts their profits . <p> DMW said it is now reviewing its rules for contract violations and deployment regulations in Europe . <p> South Korean market <p> DMW also registered major development for the South Korea market this year , which involves seasonal workers and caregivers . <p> It took over the seasonal workers program ( SWP ) to South Korea , which was previously handled by local government units ( LGU ) amid reports some of its Filipino participants suffered poor working conditions and illegal recruitment . <p> DMW 37 illegal recruitment cases linked to the said incidents . Since the agency took over the SWP , it was able to deploy 6,100 workers to South Korea . <p> Last May , DMW also started the recruitment of Filipino " caregivers , " who perform childcare , cleaning and laundry , and other support for pregnant women in South Korea under its Employment Permit System ( EPS ) . <p> During the pilot of the program , two of the Filipinos , who participated in the EPS as caregivers violated the terms of their contract . Both were detained by South Korean authorities . <p> Despite the incident , the pilot of the program continues to be implemented by DMW . <p> Additional milestones <p> DMW was also able to achieve several milestones this year since it became fully operational in 2022 . <p> These achievements include the launch of its new logo , Overseas Labor Market Situationer report , and the Bagong Pilipinas One-Stop Agarang Kalinga at Saklolo para sa mga OFW na Nangangailangan ( AKSYON ) Center in its new office Makati this month . <p> It was also announced the expansion of the services of its OFW Hospital in San Fernando City , Pampanga with the ground breaking of its Bagong Pilipinas Cancer Care Center ( BPCCC ) . <p> DMW announced it is targeting to come out with the implementing rules and regulations of Republic Act ( RA ) No. 12021 or the Magna Carta for Seafarers and its updated rules for deployment of Sea-based OFWs in the coming weeks . <p> Samuel Raphael P.
