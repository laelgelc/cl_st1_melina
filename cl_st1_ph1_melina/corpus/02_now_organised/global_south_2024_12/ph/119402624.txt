<h> Jimmy Carter : president , global mediator , Nobel laureate <p> Jerome CARTILLIER <p> 29 December 2024 at 2:47 pm*5-min read <p> Former US president Jimmy Carter -- seen with his wife Rosalynn in Oslo in December 2002 when he was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize -- is better known for his post-presidential achievements ( SIGURDSOEN BJOERN ) ( SIGURDSOEN BJOERN/SCANPIX/AFP ) <p> Jimmy Carter held a unique place in US politics : he was the oldest former president and a Nobel peace laureate , but his one term in office was forever tainted by his inability to end the 1979 Iran hostage crisis . <p> Carter , who died Sunday at the age of 100 , arguably wielded his greatest influence not during his 1977-1981 term in the White House , but in the decades following , when he served as a global mediator , rights activist and elder statesman . <p> The Southern Democrat , who left 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue in January 1981 after a crushing election loss to Ronald Reagan , was perceived as naive and weak in the dog-eat-dog world of Washington politics . <p> Even within his own party , the Georgia native with the broad toothy grin -- a " born-again " Christian who taught Sunday school well into his 90s -- was something of a persona non grata for a long time . <p> ADVERTISEMENT <p> But as the years passed , a more nuanced image of Carter emerged , one that took in his post-presidential activities and reassessed his achievements , like the brokering of a peace deal between Israel and Egypt . <p> He placed a commitment to human rights and social justice at the core of his tenure as the 39th president of the United States . <p> That dedication later served as the cornerstone of The Carter Center , which he founded in 1982 to pursue his vision of world diplomacy , earning broad international praise . <p> Carter represented a new generation of Southern men who were more tolerant and progressive on issues of race . <p> The former president -- who had been both a naval officer and a peanut farmer -- heartily embraced his roots . <p> " I am a Southerner and an American , " said Carter , a virtual unknown on the national political scene when he launched his presidential campaign ahead of the 1976 election . <p> ADVERTISEMENT <p> - From farm to White House - <p> James Earl Carter Jr . -- the full name he rarely used -- was born on October 1 , 1924 in the small farm town of Plains , Georgia , south of Atlanta -- the same town where he lived out his golden years . <p> After seven years in the navy , where he worked on the nuclear submarine program and rose to the rank of lieutenant , he returned home to run the family peanut farm . <p> But eventually , politics came calling . <p> He served in Georgia 's state senate and took over as governor in 1971 . Only a few years later , his unlikely White House bid began . <p> Carter arrived in Washington in January 1977 , sworn in to head a country needing a strong leader to dispel the gloom left over from the Vietnam War , the Watergate scandal and a deep recession . <p> For the first time since 1968 , the Democrats controlled the White House and Congress , so hopes were high as Carter took office . <p> ADVERTISEMENT <p> - ' Extraordinarily difficult ' - <p> He enjoyed a strong first two years , with high approval ratings . <p> A shining moment of his term in office was the historic 1978 Camp David Accords signed by Israel 's Menachem Begin and Egypt 's Anwar Sadat , which ultimately led to a peace treaty the following year . <p> Carter also established diplomatic relations with China following the rapprochement initiated by then-president Richard Nixon , and endorsed solar energy , even installing solar panels on the White House . <p> But his administration hit numerous snags , the most serious being the Iran hostage crisis and the disastrous failed attempt to rescue the 52 captive Americans in 1980 . <p> His handling of the renewed oil crisis in 1979-1980 was also sharply criticized . Images of cars lined up at gas stations were long associated with his presidency . <p> ADVERTISEMENT <p> In the wake of Carter 's defeat at the polls , the Democratic Party weathered a political storm -- 12 years of Republican presidents in Reagan and George H.W. Bush . <p> Even now , few Democrats claim to be picking up Carter 's mantle . <p> In a biography published in 2010 , historian Julian Zelizer , a professor at Princeton University , said Carter had fallen victim to " an extraordinarily difficult set of circumstances that would have challenged any president . " <p> - ' Best ex-president ' - <p> But Carter bounced back in perhaps the most spectacular reinvention of any US leader and was often called America 's " best ex-president . " <p> Carter founded his eponymous center in Atlanta and emerged as a prominent international mediator , tackling some of the most intransigent global dilemmas -- including North Korea and Bosnia in the 1990s . <p> He monitored dozens of elections around the world , from Haiti to East Timor , and went to Cuba in 2002 for a historic meeting with then longtime communist leader Fidel Castro to prod him on human rights . <p> Carter won a host of awards including the 2002 Nobel Peace Prize and the highest US civilian award , the Presidential Medal of Freedom . He won three Grammys for best spoken-word album , a category that included audiobooks . <p> Carter maintained a busy schedule into his 90s . He and wife Rosalynn helped build houses for the charity Habitat for Humanity for decades . <p> Rosalynn died in November 2023 at age 96 . The couple had three sons and a daughter . <p> In August 2015 , Carter revealed he had cancer on his brain and was undergoing radiation treatment . <p> At the time of his diagnosis , Carter said that while the presidency was the " pinnacle " of his political career , " life since the White House has been personally more gratifying .
