<p> Wearing a safety belt , helmet and work boots , Raju Nishad navigates the scaffolding , hammering blocks that will form part of a building in a new neighbourhood in central Israel 's town of Beer Yaakov . <p> While he and other Indians working alongside him do not look out of place on the expansive construction site , they are relative newcomers to Israel 's building industry . <p> They are part of an Israeli government effort to fill a void left by tens of thousands of Palestinian construction workers barred from entering Israel since Hamas 's unprecedented October 7 , 2023 attack . <p> If that attack had not happened , this site , with its slowly emerging high-rise towers , homes , roads and pavements , would have teemed with labourers speaking Arabic -- unlike the Hindi , Hebrew and even Mandarin of today . <p> The Hamas attack triggered the deadliest war yet between Israel and militant group Hamas in the Gaza Strip . <p> It later spread to include other Iran-backed groups including Hezbollah in Lebanon and Huthi rebels in Yemen , and even direct confrontation with the Islamic republic itself . <p> Indians have been employed in Israel for decades , thousands as caregivers looking after elderly Israelis , while others work as diamond traders and IT professionals . <p> But since the war in Gaza escalated , recruiters have launched a drive to bring Indians in for Israel 's construction sector also . <p> Samir Khosla , chairman of Delhi-based Dynamic Staffing Services , which has sent about 500,000 Indians to work in more than 30 countries , has so far brought more than 3,500 workers to Israel , a new market for him . <p> Thousands of kilometres from home , Indian construction workers prepare a meal at their flat in the Israeli coastal city of Tel Aviv . Photo : Menahem KAHANA / AFPSource : AFP <p> Khosla himself arrived for the first time a month after the October 7 attack , after the authorities appealed for foreign workers in the construction industry , which ground to a halt when the Gaza war broke out . <p> " We did n't know much about the market , and there was n't an incumbent workforce from India here , " Khosla said . <p> " We really had to move around and understand the needs , " he said , adding that he believed India was a natural choice for Israel given their " excellent relations " . <p> He now hopes to bring in up to 10,000 Indian labourers , as he has a large pool of skilled Indian workers across all trades . <h> Long-term effects possible <p> In nearby Tel Aviv , a group of Indians live in a small flat where , in addition to the construction skills they brought with them , they have also learned to cook the familiar spicy dishes they miss from home . <p> " In a short time , one can earn more money " in Israel , said Suresh Kumar Verma , 39 . Like Nishad , he is from India 's most populous state , Uttar Pradesh . Verma works on a construction site north of Israel 's commercial capital . <p> " Making money is also necessary ... It 's important to continue working hard for the family 's future . " <p> Some Indian workers can earn in Israel three times what they would back home . Photo : Menahem KAHANA / AFPSource : AFP <p> Israeli researchers believe the number of Indians working in construction still does not match the number of Palestinians who did so before the war , and this is hampering the sector 's overall growth . <p> Before the Hamas attack , around 80,000 Palestinians were employed in construction , along with some 26,000 foreigners , Eyal Argov of the central Bank of Israel said . <p> Now there are about 30,000 foreigners employed , far fewer than the previous overall workforce figures , he said , adding that activity in the current quarter of 2024 is about 25 percent below pre-war levels . <p> " These numbers ( of Indians ) are still very low , " Argov said . <p> While this does not create an immediate " shortage of housing , it may cause delays in the supply of new housing " , he said . <p> " Israel has a growing population , increasing by two percent annually , and this delay might lead to some shortage in the future .
