<h> My hope : For Cork to have a ' green heart ' <p> Even as a teenager , Virginia O'Gara had remarkable sustainability values . <p> Coming from a conservative yet supportive upbringing , as a DIY punk rock-loving Texan teen , she took her first steps as an activist with Food Not Bombs , an all-volunteer movement that recovers food that would otherwise be discarded and shares free vegan and vegetarian meals with the hungry . <p> " There was a tremendous amount of food waste that was occurring , and more money was spent on war than actually taking care of American citizens . When I was 14 years old , I found out about Food Not Bombs . I was really inspired , so we started a Dallas chapter , and we would see hundreds of people outside of the Dallas City Library once a week ... it was a really eye-opening experience . " <p> Soon , O'Gara was spreading her wings , all the while learning more about food and its sustainable management . <p> " I travelled up to San Francisco to work @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 1995 , and I had graduated from high school early .
