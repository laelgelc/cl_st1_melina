<h> Dutch church leaders react with shock to antisemitic violence in Amsterdam <p> The Dutch bishops ' conference and Amsterdam 's St. Nicholas Parish reacted with horror to the violence against Israeli soccer supporters in the city Nov. 7 . <p> Both the bishops and the St. Nicholas parish community stressed that differences of opinion should never lead to violence . <p> " We are shocked by this , " the Dutch bishops wrote in a statement released Nov. 8 . " Any form of anti-Semitism and hatred of ' the other ' is a grave sin against our Creator and does great injustice to our neighbor . " <p> On Nov. 7 , Israeli soccer club Maccabi Tel Aviv played a game against Ajax Amsterdam in the Dutch capital . Some 3,000 Israeli soccer fans had flown in for the occasion . Over the course of the day , however , the atmosphere became increasingly grim as pro-Palestinian rioters began attacking Israelis . <p> The attacks followed multiple social media videos showing Maccabi fans chanting anti-Arab slurs and praising Israeli military attacks in Gaza . <p> The Israeli Embassy @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , hundreds of Maccabi fans " were ambushed and brutally attacked , " according to a post on X , formerly Twitter . The embassy also shared a video of the violence . Other videos circulating online seemed to show people being attacked and sometimes chased by people , some of whom chanting anti-Israel slogans . <p> After nightfall , the situation got out of hand . Several people were punched , harassed and kicked in the process . Five people were taken to the hospital with injuries . Police arrested over 60 suspects during the night . <p> Many of the Maccabi fans left home earlier on planes sent by the Israeli government specifically for the purpose . Typically , because of the Sabbath , Israeli airlines do not fly on Saturdays .
