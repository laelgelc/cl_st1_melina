Here is what it is and how it works <p> From CNN 's Ethan Cohen , Molly English and Matt Holt <p> When Americans cast their votes this election , they are not directly electing the presidential candidates themselves . Voters are casting ballots for competing slates of " electors " who will in turn cast the actual votes for president and vice president on December 17 . Collectively , the electors from all 50 states and the District of Columbia are referred to the Electoral College . <p> The candidate who wins the majority of the popular vote on election night does not necessarily win the White House . To win the presidency , a candidate must win a majority of votes in the Electoral College . <p> How the Electoral College Works : <p> The Electoral College is composed of 538 electors who represent all 50 states and the District of Columbia . <p> Each state has between three and 54 electoral votes . The number of electors from any given state is determined by adding its total number of US senators and US representatives . ( DC gets three electoral votes -- the number it would have if it were a state and the minimum number of electors possible for any state . ) <p> A candidate needs a majority of electoral votes ( 270 ) to win . <p> In 48 states and the District of Columbia , all electoral votes are awarded to the candidate who wins a plurality of the popular vote in that state . This is known as the " winner-take-all " system . <p> Two states -- Maine and Nebraska -- do not award their electoral votes according to a winner-take-all system . These states operate under a " district system , " which awards electoral votes to candidates according to both the statewide vote as well as the vote in each congressional district . The system awards one electoral vote to a candidate for each congressional district carried by that candidate . The remaining two electoral votes are awarded to the statewide winner . ( Maine could therefore conceivably have a 3-1 split of its 4 electoral votes . Nebraska could have a 4-1 split or a 3-2 split of its five electoral votes . ) <p> Electors meet in their respective states on the first Tuesday after the second Wednesday in December to cast separate ballots for president and vice president . This year , electors will meet on December 17 . <p> The Electoral College results are counted and certified by a joint session of Congress on January 6 . The count takes place in the House of Representatives chamber and is presided over by the president of the US Senate ( the vice president ) . Assuming there is a majority , the vice president becomes the first person to officially announce the names of the next president and vice president .
