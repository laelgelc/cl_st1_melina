( AFP ) <p> The World Health Organization and some 50 countries issued a warning Friday at the United Nations about the rise of ransomware attacks against hospitals , with the United States specifically blaming Russia . <p> Ransomware is a type of digital blackmail in which hackers encrypt the data of victims -- individuals , companies or institutions -- and demand money as a " ransom " in order to restore it . <p> Such attacks on hospitals " can be issues of life and death , " according to WHO head Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus , who addressed the UN Security Council during a meeting Friday called by the United States . <p> " Surveys have shown that attacks on the healthcare sector have increased in both scale and frequency , " Ghebreyesus said , emphasizing the importance of international cooperation to combat them . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ international security , " he added , calling on the Security Council to consider it as such . <p> A joint statement co-signed by over 50 countries -- including South Korea , Ukraine , Japan , Argentina , France , Germany and the United Kingdom -- offered a similar warning . <p> " These attacks pose direct threats to public safety and endanger human lives by delaying critical healthcare services , cause significant economic harm , and can pose a threat to international peace and security , " read the statement , shared by US Deputy National Security Advisor Anne Neuberger . <p> The statement also condemned nations which " knowingly " allow those responsible for ransomware attacks to operate from . <p> At the meeting , Neuberger directly called out Moscow , saying : " Some states -- most notably Russia -- continue to allow ransomware actors to operate from their territory with impunity . " <p> France and South Korea also pointed the finger at North Korea . <p> Russia defended itself by claiming the Security Council was not the appropriate forum to address cybercrime . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a reasonable use of the Council 's time and resources , " said Russian ambassador Vassili Nebenzia . <p> " If our Western colleagues wish to discuss the security of healthcare facilities , " he continued , " they should agree in the Security Council upon specific steps to stop the horrific ... attacks by Israel on hospitals in the Gaza Strip .
