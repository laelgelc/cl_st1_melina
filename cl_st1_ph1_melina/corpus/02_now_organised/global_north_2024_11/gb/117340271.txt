It must be done carefully to create viable plants , which must then be observed closely . However , the resultant advances in our understanding of how plants work have been extraordinary . <p> In the research reported in January , plants without the Golgi body could live very well . They looked just as green and healthy as plants with their genes all intact . That is , until they were put into the dark . <p> Plants need sunlight to grow , but most can survive darkness for a while until they start to break down . <p> The healthy version of these plants generally manage to last about nine days ' darkness before they start to senesce -- the leaves going thin and yellow , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ this senescence happened in just three days . <p> Something about the Golgi body was needed to keep them going strong under the stress of living in the dark . <p> Leaf senescence is not necessarily a bad thing . It is the last step in the developmental growth of leaves , which is a process under careful genetic control . <p> In autumn , the brilliant reds and oranges that transform treescapes are the most vivid exhibition of senescence . During this process , green chlorophyll which is responsible for taking in the light of the sun is broken down into other molecules , in many species bright yellow and red ones . <p> Biologists argue about the possible reasons for this change . The change may allow nutrients to be reabsorbed into the tree at a time when sunlight becomes scarce . The bright colours may deter insect pests that would otherwise take advantage of the tree 's weakened state . <p> Some of the ideas about pest-prevention are honest warnings to insects to vacate leaves that will soon fall off , causing the any heedless insect inhabitant @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ out of their camouflaged hiding by the change in environment . Research published last year showed that changes in summer temperature affect the onset of autumn 's colours -- hotter temperatures later in the summer push them back . <p> Human-induced changes to plants and trees do n't stop at climate change .
