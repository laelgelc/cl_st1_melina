<p> Jewish and LGBTQ+ people in Berlin have been advised to be cautious about revealing their identity in certain areas of the city , particularly those with large Arab populations . <p> The city 's police chief , Barbara Slowik , told the Berliner Zeitung newspaper , " There are areas of the city , we need to be perfectly honest here , where I would advise people who wear a kippah or are openly gay to be more careful . " <p> She added , " Certain neighbourhoods , where the majority of people of Arab origin live , also have sympathies for terrorist groups and are often openly hostile towards Jews . " <p> Slowik , however , acknowledged that violent crimes against Jewish people were rare , although stressing that even one act of hate was one too many . <p> The warning comes amid rising tensions in Berlin and across Germany , where antisemitic incidents have surged since the outbreak of the war in Gaza . <p> In one recent incident , a youth football team from Makkabi Berlin , a Jewish sports club , was @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in an Arab-majority neighbourhood . <p> The youths , aged 13 to 15 , were verbally and physically harassed , spat on , and threatened with sticks and knives . The incident occurred on the same night that gangs in Amsterdam filmed attacks on fans of the Israeli football club Maccabi Tel Aviv . <p> Since the start of the Gaza war , Berlin 's police have opened more than 6,000 investigations related to antisemitism , most involving online hate speech or graffiti , according to Slowik . <p> Other incidents include attacks on Jewish individuals , such as a football fan assaulted for wearing a Star of David scarf , and a petrol bomb attack on a synagogue shortly after the October 7 Hamas massacres in Israel . <p> On the day of the Hamas attacks , men were seen handing out sweets in celebration in Neukolln , a neighbourhood with a significant Arab population . However , it is expected to be overshadowed by Germany 's stagnating economy in the upcoming elections . <p> The far-right Alternative for Germany ( AfD ) party has used the need to protect Germany 's Jewish community as a rationale for advocating a much stricter policy of mass deportations , targeting refugees from countries like Syria .
