<h> Colchester Rotary Club plants 1,000 purple crocuses <p> HUNDREDS of purple crocus bulbs have been planted in Colchester 's Castle Park to bring a blanket of purple to the city . <p> The Rotary Club of Colchester marked this year 's World Polio Day with the gesture to raise awareness of polio eradication efforts . <p> In Castle Park , club members planted more than 1,000 purple crocus bulbs , symbolising the ongoing campaign to end polio worldwide . <p> Additionally , they donated crocus corms to 11 schools , encouraging students to participate in the cause . <p> The purple crocuses are a vivid reminder of Rotary International 's role in polio eradication , with the colour purple symbolising the " purple pinkie " used to mark children 's fingers once they receive the polio vaccine . <p> Rotarian Peter Rutland said : " One of the things we do with the crocus corns is because the colour for polio is purple . <p> " Part of the reason for that is when they go and vaccinate children in primarily Asian countries , they get their finger dipped @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ just to commemorate that . <p> " For a number of years now , we have been planting these crocuses in Castle Park . <p> " They 're on one of the slopes looking up to the castle , and each year we plant them around this time . <p> " By February and March , they 're in full bloom , coinciding with Rotary 's anniversary on February 23 . " <p> Since Rotary 's campaign against polio began in 1988 , cases have dropped by 99.9 per cent , with only a few instances remaining in Pakistan and Afghanistan . Rutland explained , <p> Mr Rutland said : " We started in the Philippines , and since then , Rotary has partnered with the World Health Organization and the Bill &amp; Melinda Gates Foundation to vaccinate millions of people . <p> " But we want to eradicate it entirely because , as we 've seen recently in Gaza , it can always come back . " <p> The Rotary Club of Colchester also promotes Purple Pinkie Day in schools , where students can dress in pink or purple @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Rutland described the initiative : <p> " We give the schools crocuses to plant , and on Purple Pinkie Day , they do a non-uniform day , wearing pink or purple , and they donate a small amount of money which all goes towards the End Polio Now Campaign and that is the money we need to vaccinate people all throughout the world really .
