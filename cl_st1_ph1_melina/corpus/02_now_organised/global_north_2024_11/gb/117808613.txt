Hence the publicised age warning for parents planning a family treat : " discretion recommended for under 12s . " <p> There is plenty of warning . The stage is bathed in red and even the balcony rails have been repainted , while an opening address suggests ominously that in fairy tales : " Everything turns out all right in the end . She is fostered by Bob and Mariella Nugent , a social climbing couple who treat her reluctantly as a symbol of their charitable concerns within the bourgeoise community . <p> Wife Mariella ( Diana Pilkington ) is vulgarly obsessed with her appearance in her magic mirror -- a borrowing from Snow White -- while husband Bob ( James Doherty ) is a builder or " purveyor of properties , " as he prefers . They are proud of their weird son , the axe-wielding Clive ( Joseph Edwards ) whose main activity is acquiring and stuffing dead animals . <p> There are scenes of wild hilarity as when Karen is forced to show @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ arranged for members of the Save the Orphans Foundation , and takes off in a manic tango which , to his wife 's horror , involves the initially reluctant Bob . <p> The play moves with Karen between her two worlds : that of the pretentious Nugents and her magical experiences both in the ball ( a borrowing from Cinderella ) , where the Prince is enamoured with her dancing , and the threatening forest . <p> After the nightmarish shadow play of the amputation , there is no happy ending to send us on our way although as she again dances on her prosthetic feet we are encouraged to " write your own ending .
