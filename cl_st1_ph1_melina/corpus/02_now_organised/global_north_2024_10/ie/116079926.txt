<p> Floating on a Dead Sea , an immersive show about life in Palestine 's West Bank before October 2023 , will be staged at the city 's Town Hall Theatre on Tuesday , October 29 , at 8pm . <p> This unique work that weaves dance , film , live music and text , is presented by Catherine Young Dance . It grew out of a residency that choreographer Catherine had in Kerry in 2016 when she created a show with asylum seekers and refugees living in direct provision . <p> " Dance is a great way to integrate new communities , because it needs no words , " she says of the show which was staged for Kerry 's Festival of Welcomes . <p> Participants included a young dancer from Gaza who performed a Palestinian folk dance , known as dabke . It became part of the show , alongside dances from various other countries . <p> That September , Caterine reimagined the show for Culture Night at the University of Limerick , which led to an invitation to participate in the 2017 Ramallah Contemporary Dance Festival in Palestine . <p> Catherine and 12 others travelled to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ two performances , one in Ramallah and the other in Jerusalem . <p> " And that changed everything , " she says . <p> Catherine describes the process of dealing with Israeli security as the dance troupe crossed from the West Bank into Israel for the performance in Jerusalem . It 's clear that the injustice faced by the young Palestinians on that occasion has left its mark . The armed soldiers simply refused to let them through the checkpoint . Catherine and her Irish colleagues were in a separate area , and although she knew the soldiers ' behaviour was wrong , she was powerless . <p> Palestinians have had to live with this injustice and powerlessness every day , even before the latest war , she says . <p> " You think you understand human rights . Then you realise you know nothing and your life changes . " <p> On that trip , she asked one question of their hosts : " What can we do to help ?
