<p> Israel 's airstrikes in Lebanon are inflicting destruction that could set its economy back by years . <p> But the defaulted country 's bonds have climbed to two-year highs , gaining a whopping 44% since late September , as traders snap them up for pennies on the dollar . <p> Investors reckon the weakening of Hezbollah , the Iran-backed military group that controls the south of the country , could precipitate a shake-up of Lebanon 's fractured political system and potentially an economic plan to pull the country out of default . <p> " The reason bonds have rallied is that the market thinks that the Lebanese political class might finally be able to agree a political path forward and an economic reform plan without Hezbollah in the picture , " said Anthony Symond at abrdn . <p> " This would pave the way for the Eurobonds to eventually be restructured .
