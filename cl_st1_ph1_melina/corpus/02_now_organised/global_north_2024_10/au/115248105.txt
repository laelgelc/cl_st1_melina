<p> Almost one year ago to the day , I wrote how an isolated conflict between Israel and Hamas would likely not cause a sustained increase in oil prices . <p> This was because neither Gaza nor Israel produces much oil . But this time , it 's different . <h> What 's changed <p> Iran is a major player in the global market for crude oil . The latest data from the US Energy Information Administration lists Iran as the ninth largest oil producer , accounting for about 4 per cent of world oil production last year . <p> While this may sound like a small share , research has shown events like Iran 's nationalisation of the BP-owned Anglo-Iranian Oil Company in the early 1950s , the Iranian revolution in the late 1970s , and Iran-Iraq war of the early 1980s , all caused crude oil price to rise . <p> The extent to which Israel responds to the latest escalation could therefore have a genuine impact on oil prices in coming days . <h> A complex world <p> Of course , the difficulty in assessing any situation is such events do not happen in a vacuum @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Last week , the Reserve Bank of Australia said inflation is still above target , and returning to target is their number-one priority , despite a highly uncertain economic outlook . <p> There 's no doubt these events only add to the uncertainty of that outlook , and any oil price surge can only add to the current cost of living crisis faced by Australians . <p> Let 's be clear . While Australia does not import any crude oil from Iran , we are heavily reliant on our trading partners for the resource . According to the most recent data from the Department of Climate Change , Energy , the Environment and Water , about two thirds of our oil supply currently comes from South Korea , Singapore , Malaysia , and India . <p> The main channel through which higher oil prices could impact inflation is fuel prices . It is well understood that higher oil prices are associated with higher fuel prices . <p> Research by the Australia Institute , found Australia currently imports about 91 per cent of fuel consumption .
