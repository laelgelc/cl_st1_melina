" <p> It 's not easy for the priests and student priests at Sacred Heart Seminary and School of Theology in Milwaukee to manage the Israeli pronunciation of " Liran , " the first name of the new director of the seminary 's Lux Center for Catholic-Jewish Studies . <p> But Liran Gerassi , an Israeli social entrepreneur living in Milwaukee for a few years , gives them an opportunity to practice saying his name every time he enters their classroom to teach about Israel , Judaism , and Jewish history . <p> He also introduces a Hebrew word of the week , sometimes words they already know such as " hallelujah " or " amen " but did n't realize were Hebrew . <p> " The goal of the Lux Center is to promote Jewish-Catholic dialogue and educate the future priests on their Jewish roots , so they will pass on the reconciliation message to the communities they will serve in the future , " Gerassi says . <h> From The Equalizer to the Lux Center <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> He started The Equalizer in 2009 as a volunteer-run soccer team for Jerusalem boys at risk , mainly Ethiopian immigrants . <p> When Gerassi left the organization 14 years later , it consisted of 500 teams across Israel . Under Gerassi 's leadership , it expanded beyond Israel to developing countries in Eastern Europe , Africa , and Latin America in partnership with local governments , NGOs , and Israeli embassies . <p> " The Equalizer is still going strong .
