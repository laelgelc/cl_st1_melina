<p> Wednesday night , synagogues throughout the city sound the shofar , our ritual ram 's horn , to herald the arrival of Rosh Hashanah , the Jewish New Year . <p> In Jewish tradition , the new moon , the phenomenon of the moon appearing absent from the night sky , signals the beginning of a new month . Thus Jewish liturgy recites Psalm 81 at tonight 's service : Sound the Shofar on the arrival of the new moon , as it is covered over , to mark our day of celebration . <p> Rosh Hashanah always arrives with a new moon , with the moon 's absence , with the moon 's appearing " covered over . " Perhaps there is no greater metaphor for how many Jews feel welcoming this New Year 5785 , especially after our past year was filled with tragedy and pain . On this day of celebration , too much seems " covered over , " difficult to see , hard to appreciate . <p> The cloud of Oct. 7 covered this past year , just as it looms large over this new year .
