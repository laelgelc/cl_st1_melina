While it is emotionally easier to experience things separately , life is often a tapestry of many emotions and feelings woven into one cloth . <p> JUDAISM REMINDS us of that existential reality . At a wedding , we temper our joy by breaking a glass to remember national and personal losses in our lives and our history . There seems to be such a randomness in this Jewish law , but maybe that is the point : Life is not a perfectly wrapped gift , it is much more complex . <p> Judaism 's approach to life is holistic , recognizing the array of emotions our state of mind experiences . There is a challenging tension in not wanting to cheat or dilute all that we experience . If the reading ends on a somber note , a more positive verse read earlier in the same haftarah is repeated . <p> THIS LEADS us to this year during which , as Hebrew University of Jerusalem lecturer Abraham Silver writes , we have felt so much , " heartbreak , devastation , and despair . " <p> The first sentence tells us to " rejoice ( vesamachta ) " and the sentence that follows it tells us to " have nothing but joy ( ach sameach ) . " <p> Why the need to talk about the same holiday in both sentences ? And in that repetition , why the addition of the word " ach " ? What is the meaning/function of ach ?
