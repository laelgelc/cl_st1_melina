<p> After a pensive and somewhat calm month of oil trading in May , market bears returned with a vengeance in June , as both the global proxy benchmark Brent and U.S. ' West Texas Intermediate hit four-month lows in the first trading week for the month . <p> The selloff followed a decision by OPEC+ , a select group of Russia-led oil producers and the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries ( OPEC ) spearheaded by Saudi Arabia , to gradually unwind output cuts at its latest meeting on Sunday . <p> OPEC+ is currently cutting its headline output by 5.86 million barrels per day ( bpd ) . This includes 3.66 million bpd of group-wide cuts and 2.2. million bpd in " voluntary cuts " by eight members - Saudi Arabia , Algeria , Iraq , Kazakhstan , Kuwait , Oman , Russia , Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates . <p> The latter cuts were due to expire at the end of June 2024 while the group-wide ones were due to end in December 2024 . While the OPEC+ decision can be construed as bearish , it was n't he only reason for the slide in prices . <p> Last month failed to bring positive macroeconomic data from either the U.S. or China with any degree of consistency . Additionally , India appears to be the only major crude oil consumer to post a tangible increase in monthly imports . China 's imports actually declined month-on-month . So it would be an oversimplification to pin the price slump on what OPEC+ did or did n't . <p> Given how the producers ' group has acted in recent years , the market had in fact already priced in a rollover of its headline cuts at least until the end of the year .
