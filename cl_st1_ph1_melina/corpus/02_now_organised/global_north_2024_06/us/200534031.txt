<p> LAKEWOOD , Ohio -- It was the fall of 1943 . Helga Meta Else Hingst , my future mother , was 13 years old and surviving in the city of her birth , Berlin , Germany . <p> Her older uncle , Herbert Zeyssig , was dead due to his loyalty to the Nazi regime by fighting with the German Panzers on the Russian front . Her second uncle , Helmut Zeyssig , was dead due to his disloyalty to the Nazi regime , his blood-soaked clothes returned from the Majdanek concentration camp in Poland . <p> For Helga , her mother , grandmother and younger sister , virtually every night was spent in bomb shelters . Hundreds of women , children and old men were killed by British bombing runs almost nightly . During five of those perilous nights that November , 4,330 Berliners perished and more than 400,000 were left homeless . <p> Estimates run as high as 3.2 million German civilian deaths during World War II .
