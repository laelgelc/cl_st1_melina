<p> CAIRO - Egypt remains committed to lowering its energy subsidies to reach cost recovery by December as it works to reduce a wide current account deficit , the International Monetary Fund said on Wednesday . <p> Ivanna Hollar , the IMF mission chief for Egypt , told reporters the commitment to lower subsidies , made in the summer of 2024 , remained unchanged . <p> " The authorities have committed to bring the price of fuel products to cost recovery by end-December 2025 . That commitment has not changed and remains the prevailing commitment to bringing retail fuel prices to cost recovery levels , " she said . <p> The IMF on Monday approved the disbursement of $1.2 billion to Egypt following completion of the fourth review of the country 's $8 billion economic reform programme , after allowing Egypt to waive a primary budget surplus target . <p> Egypt 's current account deficit mushroomed to 5.4% in the 2023/24 fiscal year , which ended in June . <p> " We still expect the current account to be relatively elevated this year , but then for the next fiscal @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of GDP deficit , " Hollar said . <p> " Part of the contributing factors to the elevated current account deficit are not just the obstructions in the Suez Canal , but also the rather difficult situation in the energy sector which is contributing to a higher trade deficit , " she said . <p> Suez Canal fees , a main source of foreign currency , plunged to $931 million in the third quarter of 2024 from $2.40 billion a year earlier , a victim of diverted shipping due to the war in Gaza . <p> Egypt still spends about 10 billion Egyptian pounds ( $197 million ) on fuel subsidies each month despite having raised prices three times last year , petroleum minister Karim Badawi said in October . <p> Prime minister Mostafa Madbouly said in televised remarks on Wednesday the government was continuing in its financial reform programme .
