<h> How Labour can avoid the Harman trap <h> Ministers must answer the question that defeated the Blair-era welfare secretary : why are they cutting benefits ? <p> Nothing in politics is as instantly forgettable as the morning broadcast round . For Downing Street this is more than occasionally the point . If you can recall something a minister said on the radio more than an hour after the fact , they 've probably made some catastrophic mistake . In October 2023 , Sir Keir Starmer learnt that to his continuing cost when he appeared to say that Israel had the right to cut off Gaza 's supplies of food and electricity . Seventeen months of fallout is bad enough . But try 28 years . <p> In December 1997 , Harriet Harman was interviewed by John Humphrys on Today . Labour MPs of a certain age can still quote whole exchanges . It went so badly that Tony Blair 's No 10 briefly threatened to stop co-operating with the show altogether . Was Harman cutting payments to single mothers because she wanted to force them into work ? <p> She could not answer . And if the minister responsible could not explain Labour 's flagship welfare policy in those terms -- wanting to get lone parents into work -- what was it for , anyway ? Saving money ? Performative political machismo ? In other words , did Blair want to cut benefits , or did political and fiscal constrains mean he had to ? That same question now lingers ominously over Rachel Reeves , the chancellor , and Liz Kendall , the work and pensions secretary , before the spending review in three weeks ' time . Both will have to do a better job than Harman of explaining why they are cutting welfare .
