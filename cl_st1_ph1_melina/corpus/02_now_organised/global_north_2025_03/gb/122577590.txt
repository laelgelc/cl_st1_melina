<h> Slashing of benefits will not help address any underlying problems <p> THE debate around the ballooning benefits bill seems to accept that the UK population , uniquely amongst developed countries , has not recovered from the economic inactivity caused by the pandemic . Indeed , if the cost projections are to be believed , the numbers of people needing this financial support will continue to increase . <p> Surely , rather than trying to whip people back into the workplace -- a policy that has been singularly ineffective in the past -- it would be more productive to start by understanding and addressing the underlying issues that are pushing them out of work in the first place . <p> Are we all increasingly unhealthy ? What 's causing the general deterioration in mental health ? Do we know ? What impact does austerity have on the problem ? What about inequality ? How does poorly paid , insecure employment impact the desire to work ?
