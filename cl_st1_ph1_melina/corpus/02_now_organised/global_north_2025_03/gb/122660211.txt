<h> Councillors need to dump virtue signalling &amp; fix our towns and cities <p> This article is brought to you by our exclusive subscriber partnership with our sister title USA Today , and has been written by our American colleagues . It does not necessarily reflect the view of The Herald . <p> It 's very , very easy to take the mick out of your local councillors , puffed-up panjandrums lording it about their communities like the big bloke in the white outfit at the start of The Godfather . <p> And in authorities like Edinburgh and Glasgow , there are opportunities aplenty to play up to the stereotype , particularly in Edinburgh where , as Scotland 's capital city , no meeting of councillors would be complete without the urgent need to send a message of **28;396;TOOLONG and kisses to whatever worldwide cause , conflict or commotion may be du jour . <p> If the tiniest , most tenuous sinew can be found to link such vital global messaging to local services so much the better . I suppose I was once possibly guilty of such indulgence , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the Edinburgh Chamber of Commerce because a rush of blood to someone 's head had led to it accepting a membership application from Sputnik , the Russian Government 's Canning Street propaganda outpost which posed as a news agency , because they wanted the subscription dosh . <p> This was unacceptable because , I said , Putin 's regime was authoritarian and anti-democratic and the Chamber should n't be offering up its access to the council , and by extension us councillors , as a member benefit . It did the trick and Sputnik got the boot , but did it make any difference to the council 's operations ? Not in the slightest . <p> This was around the time of the Salisbury Poisoning , but four years before the Ukraine war and as world politics has since heated up , so too has the temperature in Edinburgh Council steadily risen , to the point where Thursday 's full council meeting was packed with items with little or no relevance to the bread and butter of delivering good council services . <p> It 's not as if the agenda @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ including the planning framework for the next six years , a potential city-wide ban on fireworks , whether to spend around ? 700,000 from the UK Shared Prosperity Fund on skills development , and the small matter of approving the strategy for ? 1.7bn of capital investment over the next ten years . <p> But then came the stuff in which so many councillors are really interested , their own motions . The pun is appropriate . <p> Motion Three for the Lib Dem leader Kevin Lang instructed the Lord Provost to write to Kyiv major Vitali Klitschko to tell him we were right behind him . And that lasting peace would be a good thing . <p> Edinburgh councillors like to grandstand on global issues , says former councillor John McLellan ( Image : free ) Motion Six , by the Green Party 's Young Ones tribute act Alex Staniforth , warned council folk to have nothing to do with Donald Trump and condemned the " emerging fascist state " of the Unted States . <p> Motion Seven by Labour 's Lezley Marion Cameron noted at some length a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Afghanistan and , guess what , asked the Lord Provost to write to Sir Keir Starmer to say we all want Afghan women to have a better life . Which , of course , we do . <p> Motion thirteen was more Trump bashing and further orders for letter writing from the council leader and Lord Provost , this time from the SNP 's Lesley Macinnes . <p> At least Motion Two from Green councillor Ben Parker , about a stone on Bruntsfield Links commemorating a massacre at Al Dawayima during the 1948 Palestine War , actually called for the council to do something , in this case install a park bench nearby . <p> Cllr Parker was back on the anti-Israel beat with Motion Four , calling for the defence firm Leonardo - you know , the one employing around 1800 skilled workers at its Crewe Toll factory to which Greens like chaining themselves - to be banned from testing " communications equipment " ( the inverted commas are Cllr Parker 's , as if they were really trying out small tactical nuclear weapons ) on the Braid Hills @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ communications base at the top . <p> Although closer to home , another motion from the SNP 's Vicky Nicolson , a social worker , about welfare cuts was more letter writing instructions for the council leader to condemn any return to austerity ( no second T ) and to demand " sustainable investment " in social security and public services . In other words , more taxes or borrowing which makes it , er , less sustainable . <p> In similar vein , her SNP colleague Stuart Dobbin ( no childish sniggering at the back , please ) demanded opposition to cuts in the international aid budget , but did n't get the memo about ordering up a letter . <p> Out of eighteen motions , half have nothing , or very little , to do with the core business of providing efficient services , and in fairness that should really include one from new Conservative councillor Neil Cuthbert calling for a report on opportunities for companies in Edinburgh like Leonardo to benefit from increased defence spending . <p> Edinburgh Council is also unique in Scotland for allowing any @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ business through its deputations system , where groups , no matter how small , can ask to speak about agenda items . This week councillors were addressed by the Edinburgh Gaza Genocide Emergency Committee , whoever they may be , about a rather dry report on the governance of arms-length organisations like Edinburgh Leisure and Lothian Buses . Maybe they thought it was something to do with supplying weapons to Israel . <p> Recently I 've been working with councillors in a couple of other areas to prepare motions and amendments for important local issues and rules are in place elsewhere to weed out grand-standing motions just there to keep activists happy , create social media posts or give wannabe parliamentarians speaking practice . Edinburgh Council needs the same . <p> As another report to Thursday 's meeting noted , we now pay each councillor a minimum of nearly ? 26,000 a year but in Edinburgh , at least , it 's quite the reward for political flatulence . <p> John McLellan is a former Edinburgh Evening News and Scotsman editor .
