<p> Sarraj Alsersawi from Gaza , who graduated top of his Master 's class in Electronic and Computer Technology , at the DCU ( DCU/PA ) <p> By Cate McCurry , PA <p> Today at 18:20 <p> A Palestinian student has come top of his class in electronic and computer technology at Dublin City University ( DCU ) . <p> Sarraj Alsersawi achieved the top marks while the conflict in Gaza claimed the lives of many of his friends and threatened his family 's safety . <p> Mr Alsersawi arrived in Dublin from Gaza in September 2023 as part of the Ireland-Palestine Scholarship Programme ( IPSP ) to study for his masters . <p> With a background in mechatronics , Mr Alsersawi earned his undergraduate degree at Al Azhar University , Gaza where he went on to work as a teaching assistant . <p> After the attacks by Hamas on October 7 2023 , Mr Alsersawi said he was constantly worried about the safety of his family . <p> " At one point I lost contact with my family because of the blackout of the internet and communications and everything @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " he said . <p> His parents and brother managed to escape the conflict , finding refuge outside Gaza . However , Mr Alsersawi 's thoughts were also with his friends and colleagues at home . <p> His former University , Al Azhar , was completely flattened by Israeli shells and Mr Alsersawi lost friends , colleagues and students in what he describes as " this genocide " . <p> Mr Alsersawi says DCU , and particularly the programme 's chairman Dr Derek Molloy , were very supportive , offering him the option to defer exams due to his extraordinary circumstances . <p> However , he said the only way to cope was to throw himself into his studies . <p> He added : " I did n't have the luxury to just sit down and do nothing and I thought that maybe excelling in my education would be the best way forward for myself and my family . " <p> Mr Alsersawi got first-class honours in his final exams and came first in his class . <p> " I 'm proud of that , " he says . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ I needed to enter the Irish job market , " says Mr Alsersawi , who has secured a job in Cork with multinational company , Stryker , which develops medical devices . <p> Mr Alsersawi says the job is a great fit for him , as he is passionate about using his qualifications to improve people 's quality of life . <p> " Some people would like to work in the automotive or other industries but for me , the medical device industry adds purpose and meaning to what I 'm doing . " <p> He is enjoying his new role and is currently working on the development of a new electrosurgical unit , which uses high radio frequencies to cut tissue and control bleeding by causing immediate coagulation . <p> Even though his parents and brother are safe , the new year brought more distressing news for the family when they discovered their house was destroyed by Israeli bombs . <p> Through all of this , music has kept him going . Mr Alsersawi is a talented musician who plays the traditional Arabic instrument the Oud and has played @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ essential part of my life and it also helped me immensely during that time to be able to play music . It helps a lot , " he added . <p> Since coming to Ireland , he has played several charity concerts for the Palestine Children 's Relief Fund and has started learning to play the mandolin . <p> " I 'm slowly getting into the trad music scene , " he says . <p> Reflecting on what is next for Gaza , Mr Alsersawi points out that the war has claimed the lives of a large proportion of the region 's professional people including engineers , medics and academics . <p> He feels a sense of responsibility to return home whenever the rebuilding phase begins . <p> " It 's essential for us young people who were able to get a good education at some point to go back and try to help rebuild their communities at some point . <p> " It 's a bit early now , but this is something that I always keep in the back of my mind .
