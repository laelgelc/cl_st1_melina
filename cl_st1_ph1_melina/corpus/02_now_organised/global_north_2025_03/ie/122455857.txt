<h> Donkeys are now the main form of transport in Gaza . Meet the vet keeping them alive <p> In September , Dr Saif Alden -- a veterinary surgeon in Gaza -- received a late-night call . His friend Alaa had gone into labour . <p> " She needed to reach a hospital immediately , but the roads were either destroyed or blocked , and no ambulances could get through , " he says . " Time was running out . " <p> When Alaa 's waters broke , the Israel military offensive in Gaza -- triggered by Hamas terror attacks on 7 October 2023 -- had been ongoing for almost a year . Israel 's decade-and-a-half economic embargo limited the supply of trucks and fuel , so donkey carts hauled produce , building materials and rubbish . <p> When the war started , they became animal ambulances . According to the Palestinian Ministry of Health , the Israeli military offensive killed more than 46,000 people between October 2023 and January 2025 . A further 110,000 were wounded . <p> " Donkeys have become the primary mode of transportation for people in need of urgent medical care , " explains Dr Alden . <p> Nine in 10 Gazans have been displaced ; evacuating and re-evacuating in pursuit of ever-shifting ' safe zones ' . Donkeys facilitated much of this movement . At points during the war , the cost of a gallon of fuel surged to an eye-watering US$28 ( ? 22 ) . Donkey prices shot up accordingly .
