<p> Donald Trump , the inglorious U.S. President who has launched a global trade war , abandoned Ukraine , cozied up to Russian dictator Vladimir Putin and seems determined to colonize Greenland , Canada and Gaza , has actually triggered something glorious for Germany . Virtually overnight , he has been instrumental in the dismantling of the country 's notorious " debt brake " that turned Europe 's biggest economy into a walking zombie . <p> The brake is a hangover from the era of Angela Merkel , who was chancellor from 2005 to 2021 and whose stewardship of the economy was vastly overrated . She made Germany beholden to Russian natural gas ; closed the country 's fleet of nuclear generating plants , which by necessity revived some filthy old coal burners ; coddled the car industry , allowing it to pay scant attention to the shift to battery vehicles ; and pursued a mercantilist economic system that effectively suppressed wages to keep costs down and the export model thriving . <p> Perhaps worst of all , she introduced the debt brake in 2009 in the name of spending @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cost Germany tens of billions of euros to fund bank bailouts . Federal borrowing was limited to a mere 0.35 per cent of GDP and the cap was embedded in the constitution . <p> Nice idea at the time , and a fairly popular one with traditionally debt-fearing German citizens . But the lack of spending in a country that had ample fiscal room , thanks to a low debt-to-GDP ratio and bargain-basement borrowing costs , did the economy no favours over the long term . Her conservative government ran small fiscal surpluses ( at least until the pandemic hit in 2020 ) at the expense of public investment , which as a share of GDP had been in steady decline for decades . <p> Productivity and infrastructure -- everything from roads to digital networks -- suffered from lack of love . Defence spending plummeted . At the end of the Cold War , just before German reunification , West Germany was doling out about 2.4 per cent of GDP on the military .
