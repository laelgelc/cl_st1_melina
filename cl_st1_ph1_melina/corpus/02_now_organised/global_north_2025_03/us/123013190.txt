<p> Muslims from across Long Island -- and across the globe -- commemorated Eid al-Fitr on Sunday with prayers followed by family gatherings and traditional home cooking . <p> Eid is both a religious and a family holiday , as Camron Mohmamd , 20 , of Hicksville , explained . " You pray to God and you thank God , but you also then celebrate with your family , " said Mohmamd , whose family emigrated from Afghanistan in the 1980s . <p> " It 's kind of like a second Thanksgiving almost -- good food , good people , good time , " Mohmamd said . <p> He came with his father and other family members , along with thousands of others of the Muslims faith , to the Mitchel Athletic Complex in Uniondale for morning prayers organized by the Islamic Center of Long Island . <p> There are about 40 mosques on Long Island , but a large , indoor facility at the complex can handle a big crowd , and , as several celebrants emphasized , Eid is all about gathering as a community .
