<p> There were two knock-out sights when I interviewed Jacqueline Fahey , 95 , in the dining room of her Grey Lynn home , a wooden box darkened and surrounded by tropical jungle -- the vast trunk of a Phoenix palm that dominated the picture window , and the sight of Fahey herself , a beautifully boned old bird who glowed with charisma and wit , one of our greatest living painters with really quite large hands . She had her back to the bookcase . Her reading included Freud , William Carlos Williams , Hollywood Babylon , and The Vampire Cinema . " Keep to the subject " , she had written in a note to herself before I visited . In fact I led her off the subject sometimes while her own occasional strayings were always coherent and pointed , as in her feelings about the genocide in Gaza , and an entertaining detour into a memoir of her years in French Bay , Titirangi , but she obeyed her own stricture , and spoke with insight and sadness about the subject of our interview : Tony Fomison @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 's new biography Tony Fomison : Life of the Artist .
