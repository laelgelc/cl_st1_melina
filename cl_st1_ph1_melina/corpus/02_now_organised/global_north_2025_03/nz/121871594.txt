<p> Language matters . Aside from its immediate impact on our perception of great political events , including war , language also defines our understanding of these events throughout history , thereby shaping our relationship with the past , the present , and the future . <p> As Arab leaders are mobilizing to prevent any attempt to displace the Palestinian population of war-stricken Gaza -- and the occupied West Bank for that matter -- I could n't help but reflect on language : when did we stop referencing the ' Arab-Israeli conflict , ' and substitute that with the ' Palestinian-Israeli conflict ' ? <p> Aside from the obvious problem that military occupations should not be described as ' conflicts ' -- a neutral term that creates a moral equivalence -- the removal of ' Arabs ' from the ' conflict ' has greatly worsened matters , not only for Palestinians , but Arabs themselves . <p> Before we talk about these repercussions , that of swapping words and altering phrases , it is important to dig deeper : when exactly was the term ' Arab ' removed ?
