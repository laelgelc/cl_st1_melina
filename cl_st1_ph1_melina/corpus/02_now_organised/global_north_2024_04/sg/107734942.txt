PHOTO : UNSPLASH <p> April 25 , 2024 <p> JAKARTA -- As The Jakarta Post celebrates its 41st anniversary today , journalism may be on the cusp of something revolutionary . <p> The internet and later social media may have created seismic changes in the media industry by disrupting how news and content were distributed and monetized . <p> But if we are to believe proponents of artificial intelligence ( AI ) , the change that is coming will be even more fundamental . <p> If anything , AI , especially generative AI , has the potential to change the nature of journalism itself . <p> Optimists have already been talking about machines @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and conducting basic tasks in journalism , from gathering information to publishing news , whether that be audio , video or text . <p> And we are not talking about something far in the future . <p> Search giant Google has already developed a tool , named Genesis , which will allow publishers to carry out the news-gathering process , combining materials from PDFs , transcripts , audio and video sources using AI . The tool , which has undergone testing by a number of publishers , will also help publishers create things like analyses , summaries or turn text into scripts and audio . <p> Some publishers , like JP Politikens in Denmark , have built their own AI models to help transition the newsroom into a more efficient , AI-powered one . <p> Domestically in Indonesia , with revenue from advertising continuing on a downward trajectory while the cost of manpower keeps rising , many media outlets have started to use AI to do the work . <p> As an industry , journalism has not been doing well over the last 15 years and it is easy to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ savior . <p> But as with other new technologies that promised salvation , AI , especially if we are talking about the current iteration of generative AI like ChatGPT , is not without its pitfalls . <p> There are already admissions from developers of AI that their language models can perpetuate racial and cultural biases . It is easy to imagine what kind of crime stories ChatGPT would spew out if editors simply typed in prompts like " black , guns , robbery . " <p> It seems that leaving the work of journalism to AI in this day and age is still a risky enterprise . <p> At the end of the day , journalism is about writing human stories for human readers , and unless the ultimate goal of a media company is to write as many news stories as possible to be indexed by search engines , human-centered journalism should be at the heart of any journalistic endeavor . <p> We at the Post engage in journalism first to inform you , readers , about updates from Indonesia -- a job that could now be easily replaced @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ information engine , the Post also provides context , background and analysis to audiences the world over who may be newly introduced to Indonesia , one of the world 's largest Muslim democracies and Southeast Asia 's largest economy . <p> We believe this is a job too important to be left to AI and should be in the hands of well-sourced journalists , editors and writers who cover Indonesia on a daily basis . <p> We are all aware that even some of the best-intentioned journalists can make mistakes , as we can see from the " beheaded baby story " published in some of the most prominent news outlets in the West . <p> The war in Gaza has shown us how inherent bias among journalists can lead them to parrot propaganda materials published by parties in the conflict . <p> It is probably not a good idea to add AI into the mix -- at least for now . <p> As we celebrate our 41st anniversary today , we 'd like to raise a glass to journalists , editors and publishers who continue to champion the truth @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ enterprise .
