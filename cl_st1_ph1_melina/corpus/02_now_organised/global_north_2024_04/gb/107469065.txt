<p> SINGAPORE - Global airlines faced disruptions to flights on Monday after Iran 's missile and drone attacks on Israel further narrowed options for planes navigating between Europe and Asia . <p> Iran 's attack on Israel by more than 300 missiles and drones , which were mostly shot down by Israel 's U.S.-backed missile defence system , caused chaos in the aviation industry . <p> At least a dozen airlines have had to cancel or reroute flights over the last two days , including Qantas , Germany 's Lufthansa , United Airlines and Air India . <p> This was the biggest single disruption to air travel since the attack on the World Trade Centre on September 11 , 2001 , according to Mark Zee , founder of OPSGROUP , which monitors airspace and airports . <p> " Not since then have we had a situation with that many different air spaces closed down in that quick succession , and that creates chaos , " Zee told Reuters , adding that disruptions were likely to last a couple more days . <p> The latest routing problems are a blow to an industry already facing a host of restrictions due @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Ukraine . <p> Iran 's airspace is used by airlines travelling between Europe and Asia and those carriers will be restricted to two viable alternative routes , either through Turkey or via Egypt and Saudi Arabia , Zee said . <p> Israel closed its airspace on Saturday , before reopening them on Sunday morning . Jordan , Iraq and Lebanon also resumed flights over their territories . <p> Major Middle East airlines , including Emirates Airlines , Qatar Airways and Etihad Airways , said on Sunday they would resume operation in the region after cancelling or rerouting some flights . <p> It was not yet clear if the latest unrest would impact passenger demand , which has remained robust despite ongoing conflicts in Ukraine and Gaza , said Brendan Sobie , independent aviation analyst . <p> " If the political situation and the conflicts continue to escalate then at some point people will be concerned about travelling , but so far that has n't happened , " Sobie said . <p> ( Reporting by Joe Brock ; Editing by Lincoln Feast . ) <p> Disclaimer : The content of this @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ external third party provider .
