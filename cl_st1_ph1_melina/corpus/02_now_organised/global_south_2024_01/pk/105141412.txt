<h> Red Sea conflict shows importance of route for LNG shipments <p> The critical importance of the Red Sea route for LNG has come to light due to disruptions to shipments from the recent conflict in the region , where between 30 and 40 LNG cargo ships pass through every month . <p> Since the beginning of this week , at least three liquefied natural gas ( LNG ) ships from Qatar appear to have been halted near the coast of Oman due to attacks on vessels using the Red Sea route . <p> Another Qatari LNG carrier , Al Rekayyat , which had not updated its position since Jan. 10 , was also observed to have paused in the middle of the Red Sea . <p> Houthis in Yemen have targeted commercial ships in the Red Sea in retaliation for Israel 's attacks on Gaza since late last year . In response , many shipping companies decided to stop their voyages through this route . <p> In recent weeks , many LNG tankers have opted to sail around the Cape of Good Hope to avoid transit through the Red Sea . <p> Alex Froley , an LNG market @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ information provider , told Anadolu that if Qatar has to send cargoes to T ? rkiye and Europe by circumnavigating Africa rather than through the Suez Canal , journey times could more than double . <p> " ICIS calculates it could take around 29 days for an LNG tanker to reach Turkey by going around Africa , compared with only 12 for going through the Suez Canal .
