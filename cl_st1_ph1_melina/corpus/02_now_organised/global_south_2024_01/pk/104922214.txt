<h> Oil falls over 2pc after price cut by Saudi Arabia <p> Crude oil prices came down over 2 percent on Monday after crude demand concerns outweighed Middle East supply fears and also due to price cut by Saudi Arabia . <p> As of 1235 hours GMT , Brent , the international benchmark for two-thirds of the world 's oil , shed $2.13 ( -2.70 percent ) to reach $76.63 a barrel . Similarly , the West Texas Intermediate ( WTI ) , the main oil benchmark for North America , went down by $2.17 ( -2.94 percent ) to $71.64 a barrel . Brent ended last week higher by 2.23 percent , while WTI closed the week up by 3.01 percent . Both benchmarks shed more than 10 percent in 2023 on a year-on-year basis . <p> On the other hand , the price of Russian Sokol decreased by $1.19 ( -1.62 percent ) to $72.17 . Arab Light prices witnessed a decrease of $0.86 ( -1.05 percent ) to reach $81.17 a barrel . The price for OPEC Basket remained unchanged at $80.71 a barrel . It is now at a level last seen in November 2021 . Arab Light , a high-sulphur crude oil , is the major export grade for Saudi Arabia and any price cuts could indicate lower demand or easing supply tightness in the market . Interestingly , Aramco also cut the price for North American buyers by the same amount , while in previous price adjustments it has cut these prices more modestly . For European buyers , Aramco cut February prices by between $1.50 and $2 per barrel . <p> On Sunday , Libya 's National Oil Corporation declared force majeure at the Sharara oilfield , the country 's largest , after its shutdown by protesters .
