<h> Aid freeze sends humanitarian groups scurrying in Gaza <p> Jerusalem : Israel 's cutoff of food , fuel , medicine and other supplies to Gaza 's 2 million people has sent prices soaring and humanitarian groups into overdrive trying to distribute dwindling stocks to the most vulnerable . <p> The aid freeze has imperiled the tenuous progress aid workers say they have made to stave off famine over the past six weeks during Phase 1 of the ceasefire deal Israel and Hamas agreed to in January . <p> After more than 16 months of war , Gaza 's population is entirely dependent on trucked-in food and other aid . Most are displaced from their homes , and many need shelter . Fuel is needed to keep hospitals , water pumps , bakeries and telecommunications -- as well as trucks delivering aid -- operating . <p> Israel says the siege aims at pressuring Hamas to accept its spinoff ceasefire proposal . Israel has delayed moving to the second phase of the deal it reached with Hamas , during which the flow of aid was supposed to continue .
