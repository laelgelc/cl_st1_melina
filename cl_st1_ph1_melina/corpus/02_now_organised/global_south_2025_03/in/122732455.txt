<p> When the United States starts a trade war with your country , how do you fight back ? For individuals , one option is to wage a personal trade war and boycott products from the US . <p> President Donald Trump has said no nation will be exempt from his tariffs , and this includes both Australia and New Zealand . His tariffs on all steel and aluminium imports , in particular , could hurt the sector in Australia , while New Zealand 's meat and wine exports to the US could also feel the effect . <h> Different Responses from Political Leaders <p> So far , political leaders have responded differently . Canada , Mexico and the European Union have imposed reciprocal tariffs on the US , while Australia has indicated it will not retaliate . <p> But whether governments choose to push back or not , citizens in those and other countries are making their own stands . This includes artists such as renowned pianist Andras Schiff , who has cancelled his upcoming US tour . <h> Tesla Faces Global Backlash <p> Most notably , collective outrage @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ boycott of Elon Musk 's Tesla due to his role in the Trump administration . Sales of new Tesla vehicles are down 72 per cent in Australia and 76 per cent in Germany . The share price has dropped by more than 50 per cent since December 2024 , with calls for Musk to step down as chief executive . <p> Some governments are even encouraging consumer boycotts . The Canadian government , for example , has urged citizens to " fight back against the unjustified US tariffs " by purchasing Canadian products and holidaying in Canada . <p> Canadians are clearly embracing this advice . Road trips to the US have dropped by more than 20 per cent in the past month and US liquor brands have been removed from some Canadian stores altogether . <p> This rise in calls for boycotts of American brands and companies is unsurprising in the Trump 2.0 era , where the lines between government and corporate America have become increasingly blurred . <h> Political Change by Proxy <p> When people want to protest a government policy , but have no political leverage because @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ or brands gives them a voice . These actions are sometimes called " surrogate " or " proxy " boycotts . <p> This form of " political consumerism , " where individuals align their consumption choices with their values , is now one of the most common forms of political participation in western liberal democracies . <p> When France opposed the war in Iraq in 2003 , US supporters of the war aimed boycotts at French imports . Consumers in the US , United Kingdom and elsewhere have boycotted Russian goods over the invasion of Ukraine and targeted Israel over its military action and policies in Gaza and the West Bank . <p> Most famously , protests against the apartheid regime in South Africa from the 1950s through to the 1990s helped isolate and eventually change its government . <p> The current boycotts are not just protesting Trump 's trade war , of course . Indeed , political leaders like Trump often argue that consumer action , rather than government regulation , should be relied on to ensure corporations conform to social expectations . <h> How to Wage a Personal Trade War <p> Consumer boycotts do create change under certain conditions -- typically when there is a contained problem that the targeted corporation has the power to solve . <p> For example , consumer boycotts against Nestl ? in the 1970s over false and dangerous marketing of powdered milk for infants led to changes in the firm 's marketing approaches . Boycotts of Nike products over sweatshop conditions for workers had a direct impact on the company 's bottom line and led to improvements . <p> Things may still need to improve at Nestl ? and Nike , but these boycotts show consumer pressure can catalyse corporate action . However , it is much harder -- though not impossible -- for boycott campaigns to succeed when the target is a government . <p> Consumers boycotting American products can amplify the impact @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , if enough consumers stop buying a bottle of soft drink from the US , major supermarkets like Woolworths and Foodstuffs will stop buying thousands of bottles . <p> There are also other ways to " vote with your wallet . " People can engage in " political investorism " by using their power as a shareholder , bank customer or pension-fund member to express their political views . <p> After Russia 's invasion of Ukraine , for example , investors sought to divest from Russian companies , and superannuation funds were pressured by their members to do the same . <p> As consumers and investors , individuals can wage a personal trade war , sending a clear message . Trump may not be willing to listen to the leaders of allied nations , but if consumer and investor pressure is sustained and spreads globally , he may yet hear the voice of corporate America .
