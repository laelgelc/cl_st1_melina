<p> Crude oil futures hovered near multi-week lows this week , as the market grappled with a complex interplay of supply-demand dynamics and geopolitical uncertainties . <p> While a significant drawdown in U.S. distillate inventories provided some support , concerns over China 's slowing oil demand and potential supply increases from OPEC and the U.S. kept prices in check . <p> The U.S. Energy Information Administration ( EIA ) reported a larger-than-expected decline in distillate stockpiles , which fell by 2.8 million barrels last week . Distillates , which include diesel and heating oil , are critical indicators of industrial and transportation demand . The drawdown suggested a potential uptick in U.S. economic activity , offering a modest boost to oil prices . <p> " The distillate numbers are a bright spot , " said John Kilduff , a partner at Again Capital . Growing expectations that the Federal Reserve may cut interest rates later this year have bolstered hopes for stronger economic growth and , by extension , higher energy demand . Lower borrowing costs could stimulate industrial activity and consumer spending , providing a tailwind for oil prices . <p> However , the timing and magnitude of any rate cuts remain uncertain , leaving traders cautious .
