The key lies in discerning opportunity costs and managing alliances wisely , as the US navigates its role amid evolving global dynamics . Should the US remain a hegemon , using its leadership to preserve a modicum of global order ? Or should it retrench to deal with its own problems , leaving an increasingly multipolar and anarchical world to unrestrained power politics ? <p> I gravitate toward the former camp , also known in Washington as internationalism ( as opposed to isolationism ) . History shows that periods when hegemony is absent or ambiguous ( such as the 1920s and 30s ) tend to end in disaster ( World War II ) . Americans increasingly want their country to invest in solving problems at home , and to set clear priorities abroad . <p> Is the Middle East such a priority ?
