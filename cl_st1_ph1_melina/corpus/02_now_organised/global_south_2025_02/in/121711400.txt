<p> A large portion of the cityscape of fallen structures and heaped debris is completely dark as night falls over northern Gaza . As long as the battery lasts , Rawia Tamboura uses the light on her phone to reassure her little sons who are terrified of the dark because they live within the wreckage of their house . Tamboura has returned to her home after being uprooted for the majority of the 16-month conflict . However , she claims that living without running water , electricity , heat , services , or even the means to clear the debris around them is still a frustrating shell of an existence . Nearly 600,000 Palestinians flooded back into northern Gaza under the now month-old ceasefire in Gaza , according to the U.N . After the initial relief and joy at being back at their homes -- even if damaged or destroyed -- they are now facing the reality of living in the wreckage for the foreseeable future . The U.N . has estimated it will cost some $53 billion to rebuild Gaza after entire neighborhoods were decimated by Israel 's bombardment @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ there is almost no capacity or funding to start significant rebuilding . A priority now under the ceasefire is making Gaza liveable for its population in the immediate term .
