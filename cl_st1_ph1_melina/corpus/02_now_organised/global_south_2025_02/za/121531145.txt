<p> Johannesburg -- US Secretary of State Marco Rubio 's decision to skip the G20 foreign ministers meeting in South Africa on Thursday was not a " train smash " or a boycott , President Cyril Ramaphosa said . <p> Rubio announced earlier this month he would not attend the talks among top diplomats from the Group of 20 , the world 's largest economies , because of the host government 's " anti-American " agenda . <p> " What has ensued now with the secretary of state not attending is , in the end , not a train smash , " Ramaphosa told reporters after he opened the two-day meeting attended by the foreign ministers of countries including Russia , China and India . <p> The United States was represented at the talks by the deputy chief of mission at the American embassy in Pretoria . <p> South Africa , this year 's host of the G20 's rotating presidency , is in the crosshairs of the United States for leading a case at the International Court of Justice accusing Israel of " genocidal " acts in its Gaza @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ month , President Donald Trump signed an executive order cutting off financial support to the country , condemning land reforms aimed at redressing inequalities perpetrated during the apartheid era . <p> US Secretary of Treasury Scott Bessent announced on Thursday that he would also not attend the G20 meeting of finance ministers and central bank governors in Cape Town next week . <p> " The absence of some leaders is not the end of the world , " Ramaphosa said , adding he was confident that the diplomatic process would be able to " iron out wrinkles that may have appeared in our relationship " .
