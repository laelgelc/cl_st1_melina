<h> Nigun Quartet : Reviving the soul of jazz with hassidic melodies <p> There are some who feel that contemporary jazz has lost its way , somewhat . They say that it has become overly cerebral and rigidly structured almost , in fact , like classical music . Indeed , as an Israeli conductor recently pointed out to me , the latter appears to be evolving into freer climes so , perhaps in that respect , the disciplinary tables are turning . <p> However , there are still some acts out there that do display something of the original joie de vivre and unfettered spirit that gave birth to jazz over a century ago . The Nigun Quartet , which will be recording a live album at the HaEzor venue in Tel Aviv on August 21 ( 8 p.m. ) , certainly works off a deeply soulful and joyful base . <p> Ofer Schneider says it is more about the feel of the music among the band members and what they impart to the audience than virtuosity , rhythmic complexity , or chord progressions . <p> " We do n't do concerts , or give jazz shows , " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ The nigunim ( hassidic melodies ) are not originally sung on a stage . " <p> Schneider , one of the founding members of the group which has been around for six years -- that makes for impressive longevity in the jazz band domain -- does n't initially come across as your run-of-the-mill jazz bass player . With his flowing peyot ( sidelocks ) , beard , and stock hassidic garb he looks like he would far more at home in a beit midrash ( study hall ) or yeshiva or some other place of Torah study .
