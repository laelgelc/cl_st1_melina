<h> A world mired in conflict calls for international tribunals that play multiple roles <p> The International Court of Justice in The Hague has been busier than ever in recent years : it has been asked to render judgments and issue advisory opinions on conflicts in Gaza , Ukraine and Myanmar , among others , and it settles border disputes all over the world . What roles does this Court of Justice play in such cases , and how can its authority be explained ? Rosa M ? hrlein examined these questions and will defend her Ph.D . thesis at Radboud University on 28 August . <p> In national legal systems such as that of the Netherlands , the roles and powers of a court , court of appeal or tribunal are fairly clear . It is different for international tribunals , which operate in the international legal system , partly due to the fact that regulations have not been drawn up by a central legislative body . The absence of any compulsory jurisdiction for international tribunals is also often cited as a complicating factor . <h> Delicate balance <p> " An international court therefore constantly faces challenges relating @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ delicate balance : offering a resolution to the dispute without antagonizing the parties , " M ? hrlein writes . <p> In her research , M ? hrlein deconstructs various rulings and advisory opinions by a number of interstate tribunals : the International Court of Justice ( not to be confused with the International Criminal Court ) , the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea and the Appellate Body of the WTO . Based on i ) the legal issues of the dispute , ii ) the application and interpretation of the law in the relevant case and iii ) the answer or conclusion given by these tribunals , she identifies five judicial roles : dispute resolution , legal clarification , judicial lawmaking , legitimation , and control and compliance . <p> " Dispute resolution is often cited as the cornerstone of international law , " explains M ? hrlein . " Understandably so , as interstate tribunals have often been established with the notion that they provide an alternative to armed conflict . " <h> A ' controversial crowbar ' <p> " Judicial lawmaking involves not just applying the law , but also providing interpretations relating to how the law should be understood in case of gaps and in new areas of law . This is a controversial role given that it could see the International Court of Justice encroach too much on territory where only states have competence : the creation of new obligations in international law . Legitimation is also an important role in international rulings and advisory opinions . <p> " One example is the advisory opinion in the Namibia case , where the situation was politically deadlocked and the request for an opinion was supposed to signal a breakthrough .
