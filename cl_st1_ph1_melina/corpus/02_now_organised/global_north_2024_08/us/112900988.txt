<p> Richter , who grew up in Tampa , Florida , the grandson of Holocaust survivors and son of therapists , does take us through his life , but he zooms around themes , exploring , among other things , his family 's experience of the Holocaust ; the concept of epigenetic trauma ; trips to visit sites of tragedy in Germany and Poland ; Black-Jewish relations in the United States ; the concept of tikkun olam , or repairing the world . Along the way , he returns to the question of what it means to be descended from survivors , and what it means to be Jewish in the world today , while asking what we can take from our families ' stories in a world that , at times , seems to have learned very little . <p> Particularly moving are the chapters in which Richter goes back through his ancestors ' recorded memories -- his Grandpa Karl 's recordings and Grandpa Jack 's written memoir -- to share their personal experiences of the Holocaust .
