( EPA/SALVATORE DI NOLFI <p> Right now , record numbers of civilians caught up in armed conflicts are dying , while countless more are injured , displaced or separated from their loved ones . As we commemorate the 75th anniversary of the Geneva Conventions , now more than ever we need to ensure that the laudable commitments of its signatories are not forgotten . <p> For many Australians witnessing these conflicts from afar , our news and social media feeds are filled with reports of the destruction , carnage and brutality of war . Amidst these horrific updates , it would be easy to assume that wars operate entirely outside the realms of law and order -- but this simply is n't the case . Even during the devastation of armed conflict , a universally accepted set of laws exists designed to protect people and objects who are not part of the fight and bring common humanity to the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ world committing to basic and common standards of humanity in wartime with the signing of the four Geneva Conventions of 1949 . The Geneva Conventions set out to protect civilians , including humanitarian workers , from harm . Yet this year alone , 137 humanitarians supporting civilians during armed conflict and other violence have paid the ultimate price for their commitment to humanity . This is unacceptable . <p> Today , every country in the world has agreed to these foundational instruments of international humanitarian law ( IHL ) , also known as the laws of war . From enabling a wounded soldier through a military checkpoint , reconnecting families separated through war and ensuring dignity for the dead , history shows us these laws are a powerful tool in protecting those outside the fight in conflicts all over the world . <p> IHL was not set up in the hopes of forming an imagined utopia . " <p> IHL is designed to be pragmatic and able to work in a world that is complex , everchanging and often cruel . In exchange for setting out realistic parameters to protect people who are not , or who are no longer , participating in hostilities , IHL sets clear expectations on nations to play their part . When nations signed up to the Geneva Conventions , they affirmed their ' earnest wish to see peace prevail among peoples ' and committed to protecting victims of conflict . <p> Humanitarian workers , who represent the very best of humanity , are included in IHL 's remit . When States are unable to provide civilians with their basic needs , impartial and neutral humanitarian actors like the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement step up and act as a lifeline . It is essential that parties to conflict enable the work of humanitarians to safely and effectively help people . <p> As @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the Red Cross , alongside the National Red Cross or Red Crescent Societies , is on the ground responding to more than 100 ongoing armed conflicts .
