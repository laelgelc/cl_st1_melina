<p> Fear is the key . With immigration , it 's the fear of being swamped by teeming millions of the desperately unmoored . With youth crime , it is the spectre of the cold-eyed devil child , coming through your window to murder you in your sleep . <p> The Victorian government has just dogged its promise to raise the minimum age of criminal responsibility in the state from 10 to 14 . It has caved in to pressure from the scientists of talkback radio and the social engineers at the Herald Sun , stoking two of the most primal aspects of the general fear people feel about crime -- that it is always rising , and that lawless children are out of control . <p> The minimum age refers to the age below which a child can not be charged with a crime because the law presumes on an absolute basis that they are incapable of forming criminal intent ( that is , they do n't have a sufficient grasp of the difference between right and wrong to be able to consciously decide that they want to commit a crime ) . <p> The dividing line is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to learn that , for example , in NSW it 's only been 10 since 1987 ; before that , children as young as eight could be prosecuted . <p> Above 10 , the common law has long imposed a qualification , called doli incapax , which presumes that children under 14 can not commit a crime ; however , the presumption can be rebutted in any particular case by evidence that the child really did know what they were doing was seriously wrong . <p> Across Australia , all states have the minimum age at 10 , whereas in the ACT and NT it is currently 12 . The United Nations Committee on the Rights of the Child treats 14 as the minimum acceptable level and has called on Australia to conform .
