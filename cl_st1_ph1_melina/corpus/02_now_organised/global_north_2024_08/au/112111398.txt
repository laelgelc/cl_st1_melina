Times in which people base their dining and ordering decisions on how good dinner looks on social media . In which many of the things we consume are prefaced with back-stories . In which overwrought philosophies , " concepts " and press releases are starting to feel part and parcel of every opening . Circa 2024 , going out to restaurants -- and , indeed , writing about them -- often requires deciphering some level of marketing encryption . <p> Thankfully , not every operator feels the need to over-egg the proverbial pudding . Some are happy to give their business straight-shooting names that tell you in no uncertain terms what to expect . Names , for instance , such as Pauly 's Chicken &amp; Ribs . Here , one might assume , is a restaurant owned by someone called Pauly that sells , among other things , chicken and ribs . Case closed . End of story . Born in 1960 in Gaza in Palestine , after coming to Australia Elyas , like many migrants , made a living by cooking . Bakers love them as steam helps bake bigger breads and pastries with better crusts , but generally people use them to keep things juicy . Or at least they do at Pauly 's , where marinated whole chickens , after getting a preliminary blast of heat and smoke over jarrah coals , get shuffled from the barbie to the oven to finish cooking . <p> Admittedly , these are n't the crisp-skinned , made-for-TV chickens that fuel our deepest Sunday roast fantasies , but plump , well-blistered and meaty bits of bird that have , as they say in the movies , seen things . The " spicy " chickens are glazed with a medium-hot , Portuguese-inspired chilli sauce bright with lemon juice and vinegar . <p> Juiciness and taste aside , the other thing to note about Pauly 's chicken is that it 's big . Whereas the birds being served at some fast-food chains have been hit hard by shrinkflation ( when a product gets smaller while its price stays the same ) the chickens here look and feel meaty and of proper weight . Paul tells me he buys free-range number 13 ( 1.3 kg ) birds from Lilydale . Maybe I got lucky the night I visited . Maybe the chickens at Lilydale have undergone some sort of Hulk-like , gamma-ray-related growth spurt of late . Maybe this is the first time in history a man has lied about coq size by saying it 's smaller than it really is .
