<p> An RAF squadron has ditched its historic nickname after a formal complaint was made saying it is offensive to Muslims . Located at RAF Waddington , 14 Squadron was formed in 1915 and gained its nickname ' Crusaders ' after its airmen flew sorties over Gaza and Palestine during the First World War . <p> However , crews have now been told to strip out any references to the word around their hangar after a crew member filed a formal complaint , reports the Express . The Crusades describe the religious conflict between Christians and Muslims during the medieval era over two centuries , starting in 1095 . <p> The first 30 years of 14 Squadron 's history were closely connected to the Middle East , with pilots more recently involved in the first Gulf War in 1991 and Kosovo in 1999 . It is one of the RAF 's most senior and longest-serving squadrons . The change was triggered by one complaint , reports the Mail on Sunday . <p> One insider said : " If they 'd have asked members of the squadron , rather than dictating this change , almost everyone would have been in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ much part of our history . There was never any prejudice or malice in the name . <p> " Every squadron , every regiment has a past . But if that past does n't suit current thinking it will be erased . " <p> To the dismay of many , senior officers upheld the grievance and crews must now remove any references to ' Crusaders ' . An RAF spokesperson said : " As a modern and diverse service , our focus must be on not giving prominence to any offensive term that goes against the values of the Royal Air Force . <p> " Therefore , 14 Squadron have ceased using their historic unofficial nickname . The traditions and informal nicknames used by the RAF in the earlier days have a place in our history . However , some are no longer appropriate in the 21st Century .
