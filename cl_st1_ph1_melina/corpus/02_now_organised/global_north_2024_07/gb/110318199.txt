<p> Every time I step into a hospital in Gaza , I witness unspeakable atrocities and the most horrendous injuries a human can think of . <p> Like the time I was standing next to a resuscitation room in Al-Aqsa Hospital where doctors sedated a nine-year-old child to ease his suffering as he died . <p> His mother 's screams echoed throughout the building . They still echo in my nightmares . <p> Since the start of Israel 's military bombardment on Gaza in October , I have been working with my colleagues at Medical Aid for Palestinians ( MAP ) to support our emergency response . Every task is met with its own set of challenges , such as impassable roads or the constant threat of bombing . <p> But living here has never been easy . <p> I was born in Saudi Arabia to Palestinian parents , but we moved to Gaza when I was 10 . <p> I led a relatively decent life , despite Israel 's occupation and blockade , which routinely caused shortages of water , electricity , medical care , and freedom of movement .
