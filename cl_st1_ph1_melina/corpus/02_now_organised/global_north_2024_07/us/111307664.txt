( Tyger Williams , The Philadelphia Inquirer , TNS ) TNS <p> If you could talk to a lost loved one over the phone , what would you tell them ? <p> At The Thread , a new art and therapeutic installation in Germantown 's Vernon Park , visitors have the opportunity to answer that question . The Thread 's key feature is a small booth with a detached rotary phone , meant to simulate speaking with the dead or anyone else its caller may be mourning . <p> The project , which opened its first iteration in the Rail Park last year , is meant to make grieving more accessible and to encourage more communal , public grieving . <p> " It 's the experience of holding something tangible in your hand in a way that you have communicated in the past @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ what 's going on in your life , tell them you miss them , laugh , cry , just be connected to them ... there 's something really powerful about your voice aloud , " said Beth Jellinek , a licensed professional counselor and one of the co-creators of The Thread . <p> " I do n't think our general culture does a great job of holding space for grief and processing it . " <p> The Thread opened this year 's installation on Saturday , July 20 , in a ceremony that featured live music , a grief ritual led by Salt Trails Philly , and speeches and open conversation about grief from Germantown community members . Some said they were grieving the deaths of friends and mothers ; others spoke about Gaza and Philly 's gun violence . <p> " We are not alone in grief .
