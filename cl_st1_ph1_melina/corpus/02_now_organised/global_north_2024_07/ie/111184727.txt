<h> She died on July 10 , in what was her 101st year <p> " AN AUNT to everyone and a pure lady " - this was how Nora Higgins from Herbertstown has been remembered . <p> Nora died on July 10 and would have been 101 on October 18 . <p> She spent her whole life in Herbertstown , except for a short period when she was in her early 80 's and she went to live in Dromkeen to nurse her sister who was ill . <p> Nora was the youngest of 12 children born to John and Mary Higgins ( nee Bresnan ) in Rathjordan , Herbertstown and was the last sibling to survive . <p> She always loved farming and was a hard worker and used to walk four miles to and from school each day . <p> Her mind was very sharp right up to the end and she even organised her own 100th birthday party , making a guest list , choosing prayers , renting the Millennium Centre in Caherconlish and getting a meal for around 150 people . <p> To those who did n't call her Auntie Nora , she was known @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ village . <p> Nora came from a staunch GAA family and was a huge Limerick hurling fan and was always very interested in local politics and international news . Many of her prayers in later years were devoted to the children suffering in Ukraine and Gaza because of war . <p> Even into her older years , she remained strong and fit from all the years of working on farms . <p> Nora never smoked , drank , got married or had children . <p> She always said that the secret to a long life was hard work and faith . <p> When she was about 13 , Nora started working for the O'Donoghues who ran the local pub , first on their farm and then in the pub and looking after the children . <p> She never actually officially retired from the pub and even into her 80 's would have gone in for an hour or two now and then . <p> When she was in the pub , she took the angelus very seriously and people were not allowed to order a drink until it was finished @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ life in St Michael 's nursing home in Caherconlish under the care of Dr Fehily and his team and was affectionately known as " the queen " there . <p> Nora was so popular that people had to fit into her schedule and almost needed to make an appointment , as she nearly always had someone visiting her . <p> She had been living on her own and decided to move to the nursing home when she was in her 90 's . <p> Nora loved caring for people and even from the nursing home she would always send Mass cards and birthday cards , never forgetting the all important fiver and every card would come with a little handwritten note . <p> At her funeral , one man from the community remarked that she probably pulled the first pint for many people in Herbertstown and pulled many a pint at the funerals of the same people , over the years . <p> Registration is absolutely 100% FREE and will help us personalise your experience on our sites .
