<p> SINGAPORE -- Despite record heatwaves , floods and storms across much of South-east Asia in 2024 , more people in the region are concerned about bread-and-butter issues such as food security than about climate change , according to a new survey . <p> The proportion of those who see climate change as a serious and immediate threat dropped to 42.5 per cent in 2024 , the latest South-east Asia Climate Outlook Survey by the ISEAS -- Yusof Ishak Institute found . This was down from 49.4 per cent in 2023 , 45.8 per cent in 2022 , and 72.2 per cent in 2021 . <p> Earlier in September , Typhoon Yagi killed hundreds as it swept across northern Vietnam , Laos , Thailand and Myanmar , damaging or destroying crops and livelihoods . <p> The annual survey of regional climate views also found that nearly 70 per cent of respondents reported experiences of food insecurity , compared with 60 per cent in 2023 . <p> Of the respondents who experienced some level of food insecurity , 42.5 per cent blamed it on rising food prices , while 28.8 per cent attributed it to climate change . <p> The @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ nearly 3,000 people online in all 10 Asean nations between July 10 and Aug 17 .
