<p> Toronto screenings of The Bibi Files , the work in progress documentary concerning corruption charges against Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu , are going ahead despite efforts by his government to block them . <p> Alexis Bloom 's film was a late addition to Toronto International Film Festival ( TIFF ) selection by the festival 's head documentary programmer Thom Powers and screens again on Tuesday evening after the initial presentation on Monday . <p> Producer Alex Gibney attended the Monday screening . The Bibi Files reveals previously unseen , leaked interrogation footage of Netanyahu , who has been under investigation by Israeli authorities for eight years and is yet to stand trial . <p> Netanyahu was indicted on breach of trust , bribery , and fraud charges in 2019 . The tapes show Netanyahu responding to allegations that he and his wife Sara accepted expensive gifts like champagne and Cuban cigars from Hollywood producer Arnon Milchan , calling them gifts from a friend . He denied receiving jewellery . <p> While the film is not specifically about the ongoing war in Gaza that has claimed more than 40,000 lives , itself triggered by the Hamas attacks on @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the ongoing conflict looms heavily over the film . <p> Bloom posits that prolonging the war is a tactic so Netanyahu can avoid conviction . <p> Gibney , who won the documentary Oscar in 2008 for Taxi To The Dark Side , told the audience after Monday 's screening that Netanyahu 's depth of " moral corruption " was unprecedented in his experience . <p> Monday night 's screening ended with audience members bearing placards calling for a ceasefire and the release of hostages .
