<p> MOSCOW - Russian President Vladimir Putin visits the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia on Wednesday and will hold talks with Saudi Arabian Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman , a rare trip abroad to discuss oil , OPEC+ and the conflicts in Gaza and Ukraine . <p> The meeting between Putin and MbS , whose countries pump one fifth of the world 's oil , comes after oil prices declined despite a pledge by the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries and Russia ( OPEC+ ) to further curtail supply . <p> Putin is due to hold talks with President Sheikh Mohammed Bin Zayed Al Nahyan in Abu Dhabi and then travel to Saudi Arabia for his first face-to-face meeting with MbS since October 2019 . <p> The Kremlin said they would discuss energy cooperation , including as part of OPEC+ , whose members pump more than 40% of the world 's oil . <p> " Close Russian-Saudi coordination in this format is a reliable guarantee of maintaining a stable and predictable situation in the global oil market , " the Kremlin said . <p> They will also discuss Israel 's war against Hamas in the Gaza Strip @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ issues of stability in the Gulf , the Kremlin said . A Kremlin aide said Ukraine would also be discussed . <p> Relations between Saudi and Russia within the OPEC+ cooperation have at times been uneasy and a deal on cuts almost broke down in March 2020 , when the markets were already shaken by the onset of the coronavirus . <p> Saudi Arabia at the time slashed oil prices for its clients , while Russia said it would be free of any obligations to stick to production quotas . <p> But the two nations managed to patch up their relations within weeks and OPEC+ agreed to record cuts of almost 10% of global oil demand , to prop-up the oil markets . <p> In October , Putin praised MbS and said that if there were differences on extending OPEC+ cuts , then the Kremlin would seek consensus . <p> ( Editing by Guy Faulconbridge/Andrew Osborn ) <p> Disclaimer : The content of this article is syndicated or provided to this website from an external third party provider .
