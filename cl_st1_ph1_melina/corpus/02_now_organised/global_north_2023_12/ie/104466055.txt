Photo : Getty Images <p> Roslyn Dee <p> Today at 03:30 <p> As I left the main church behind me and walked slowly down the stone steps , I was bursting with anticipation . Stepping off the last step and out into the peaceful grotto , I was surprised to find myself , as a non-believer in this holy place , totally overcome with emotion . Was it excitement ? Respect ? Or was it that sense of staring history in the face ? <p> Years later , I still do n't know the answer , but whatever I was feeling in the Church of the Nativity in Bethlehem that day , it came down to one thing : I was looking down at a bronze star on the floor and at an accompanying Latin inscription telling me , " Here , of the Virgin Mary , Jesus Christ was born . For years it has been a thriving , bustling Palestinian city in the West Bank , with a mixed Muslim/Christian population and an economy almost totally driven by tourism . <p> Well , it WAS a thriving , bustling city . This Christmas , though , Bethlehem is not bustling , nor is it rejoicing . Rather , it is a city in mourning -- for itself . <p> And who could blame it ? There are virtually no visitors this week walking in Manger Square , nor is anyone kneeling in quiet reflection in the Church of the Nativity . As a result , the souvenir shops are empty and the city 's economy lies in ruins . <p> There 's nothing holy or sacred , whatever your beliefs , about holding infants hostage or slaughtering them in their thousands <p> The horror of Gaza has put paid to Bethlehem 's Christmas celebrations , delivering a killer punch that has knocked the Christ right out of Christmas @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ shortened to Xmas , but this year , I have to concede , it 's definitely Xmas in Bethlehem . <p> From my own visit , I can still recall watching little boys playing football on one of the back streets , kicking a ball around like children do in cities all across the world . Were they Christian kids or Muslim kids ? I have no idea ; nor does it matter . Just as the baby Jesus was a child of Bethlehem , those boys too were children of Bethlehem , living their lives as children are supposed to , in a place they called home and where they felt safe . <p> What now , though , for those children of Bethlehem , living in fear in the powder-keg atmosphere of what , God help us , is still known as the Holy Land ? Are they safe , are they cherished by those who hold ultimate sway over their lives ? Just look at Gaza -- only 70km away -- and the answer is all too obvious . <p> All those who genuinely believe will be @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that inspirational person born 2,000 years ago . But there 's never been a more important time to pray , too , for all the other children of Bethlehem and beyond . <p> For there 's nothing holy or sacred , whatever your beliefs , about holding infants hostage or slaughtering them in their thousands . This Christmas , therefore , we should remember all of the children -- Christian , Jewish and Muslim -- living in what was , once upon a time , a holy land truly worthy of reverence and respect .
