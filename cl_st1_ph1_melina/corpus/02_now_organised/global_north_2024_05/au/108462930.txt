<h> Racism at universities has been an issue for decades <p> The distressing antisemitism and anti-Palestinian sentiment we are currently hearing of on our university campuses are of significant concern . However , they are far from the first time that racism has been complained of at universities . <p> These are challenging times . Jewish and Palestinian communities have suffered generations of trauma . That very real pain has been compounded to unimaginable levels since the horrific October 7 attacks in Israel , and the devastating military response in Gaza . <p> The instances of antisemitism and anti-Palestinian sentiment on campus are being taken seriously by the Australian Human Rights Commission . Vilification , abuse , and incitement are never acceptable . Threatening people 's safety , for example , abusing people , spitting on them , or any kind of physical harm is unacceptable , and there should be consequences for it . <p> A person aggrieved by an alleged act of racial discrimination can make a complaint to the commission . As with all allegations of vilification or hate , each incident must be examined and considered in its context under the Racial Discrimination Act 1975 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , the commission and other bodies have been sounding the alarm , saying racism in universities warrants comprehensive examination . <p> That 's why I 'm pleased to lead a groundbreaking study into racism at universities announced last week by the Department of Education . The initiative implements part of a key recommendation from the Australian Universities Accord Final Report . <p> Previous research projects have delved into specific areas of concern . From the racism endured by Arab and Muslim students following the 9/11 terror attacks , to evidence showing international students , particularly those of Asian background , have been victimised , exploited , and attacked . <p> There have also been investigations into the claims of non-white academics who say there is a lack of opportunities for them to progress to senior positions . And then there is the experience of First Nations students and staff who are grossly under-represented , and often feel culturally unsafe in the university environment . <p> The new study will build on that work , looking holistically at how prevalent the problem is and how we can dismantle racism at our @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ no-less destructive , interpersonal racism , to explicit acts of discrimination , and any structural barriers that limit people 's rights or progression based on their race . <p> We will hear the experiences of students and staff across universities in the country . These people , whose testimonies will be taken anonymously , will come from all different backgrounds and beliefs . Our approach will be trauma-informed , with the welfare of those speaking a key priority . Among those we will be speaking to will be those involved in or affected by the current protests . <p> In the present climate , some may see it as too idealistic to think we will find a solution to divisions dating back generations . <p> But I am confident that by bearing witness to these first-person testimonies and taking the time to understand the myriad experiences , we will be in a much stronger position to put the systems in place to make sure all feel safe from racism and can flourish .
