<h> Western nations urge Israel to refrain from large-scale Rafah invasion <p> Thirteen nations , usually supportive of Israel , call for restraint , urging against a major offensive in southern Gaza due to concerns for civilian safety . <p> AA <p> " My hope in 1948 was to return , but my hope today is to survive , " a displaced Palestinian says . / Photo : AA <p> Thirteen Western countries , including many traditionally supportive of Israel , have appealed to it not to launch a large-scale military offensive on Rafah . <p> " We reiterate our opposition to a full-scale military operation in Rafah that would have catastrophic consequences on the civilian population , " read the appeal on Friday , sent by the countries ' foreign ministers to their Israeli counterpart Israel Katz and made public . <p> The signatories were Australia , Britain , Canada , Japan , New Zealand , South Korea and EU member states Denmark , Finland , France , Germany , Italy , the Netherlands and Sweden . <p> The ministers welcomed recent measures adopted by Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu 's cabinet to improve the flow of international aid @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> " We urge the Government of Israel to let the humanitarian aid enter into Gaza through all relevant crossing points , including the one in Rafah , " they said . <p> They called on Israel to take " concrete action for the protection of civilians , international and local humanitarian aid workers and journalists " . <p> Finally , they called on Israel to " facilitate further evacuations by issuing exit permits for all our citizens , eligible persons , and Palestinians admitted to relocate for humanitarian or medical reasons abroad " . <p> Eight months after the start of the war , Israel is vowing to intensify its ground offensive in Rafah despite international concerns for the nearly 1 million displaced Palestinians in the southern city . <p> The Israeli siege of Gaza has brought dire shortages of food as well as safe water , medicines and fuel for its 2.4 million people . <p> The arrival of occasional aid convoys has slowed to a trickle since Israeli forces took control last week of the Gaza side of the Rafah crossing .
