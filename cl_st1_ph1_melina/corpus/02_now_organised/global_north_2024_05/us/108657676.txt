<p> JERUSALEM -- Jerusalem on Thursday held its annual gay pride parade under heavy security and with a subdued vibe , given the solemn mood in Israel after months of war . <p> LGBTQ supporters marched the streets of Jerusalem carrying rainbow flags , Israeli flags and yellow ribbons , a symbol for hostages still held in Gaza . Absent were the vibrant cheers and music that usually accompany the event . <p> Organizers estimated that 10,000 people marched . Police said some 2,000 officers were on guard . <p> The turnout was smaller than in previous years . Last year 's parade in Jerusalem was estimated to have drawn 30,000 people . <p> Netanel Shaler , executive director at Havruta , an LGBTQ group for those with a religious leaning , said the goal was to be sensitive to the overall security situation while still allowing people to march . <p> In a video interview with Israeli news website Ynet , Shaler said he was serving as a military reservist and was on a special leave to participate . <p> This year 's parade adopted the slogan " Born to be Free , " and was led in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ abducted during Hamas ' Oct. 7 assault on southern Israel that sparked the war in Gaza . <p> Some protesters appeared on the sidelines holding signs against the parade .
