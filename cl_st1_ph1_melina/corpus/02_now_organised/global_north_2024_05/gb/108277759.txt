<p> Growth is expected to accelerate across the EBRD 's region of operations 2024 as inflation falls . / EBRD <p> By Clare Nuttall in YerevanMay 15 , 2024 <p> The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development ( EBRD ) forecasts growth across its region of operations , comprising Emerging Europe , Central Asia and the Southern and Eastern Mediterranean ( SEMED ) , at 3.0% in 2024 , up from 2.5% in 2023 , as outlined in its latest Regional Economic Prospects report released on May 15 . <p> The growth forecast , however , falls slightly below last September 's projections , with a downward revision of 0.2 percentage points ( pp ) . This revision is partly attributed to the weak performance in early 2024 , particularly evident in Central Europe and the Baltic states , reflecting Germany 's economic stagnation , the development bank said . <p> " Last year was very difficult for some Central European and Baltic countries . This year is going to be better , but some of the figures in Q1 were less strong than we expected , hence the downgrade , " commented EBRD chief economist Beata Javorcik in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ In the Southern and Eastern Mediterranean we have spillovers from the war in Gaza and slower-than-expected progress in Egypt on some of the big government investment projects , " Javorcik added . <p> Despite facing headwinds from global geopolitical tensions , including trade restrictions , the EBRD remains optimistic about the economic trajectory of its regions , projecting a further uptick in growth to 3.6% in 2025 . <p> Titled " Taming Inflation " , the report points to a moderation in inflationary pressures compared to last year , when the region as a whole experienced an economic slowdown attributed to high energy prices stemming from the conflict in Ukraine and the aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic . <p> However , Javorcik pointed out that despite the disinflation , in some countries in the region inflation remains high . <p> Regional differences <p> Despite the slow start to 2024 , in Central Europe and the Baltic states , growth is anticipated to gradually improve , with projections for 2024 at 2.2% and an expected acceleration to 3.1% in 2025 . <p> Growth is set to reach as high as 3.5% @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ several countries compared to the September 2023 forecast . The steepest downgrades across the EBRD 's region of operations are for Estonia ( down by 1.7 pp ) , followed by Czechia ( -1.6pp ) . <p> In the Southeast European EU member countries -- Bulgaria , Greece and Romania -- GDP growth is projected to increase from 2.0% in 2023 to 2.8% in 2024 , supported by accommodative fiscal policies and robust real wage growth . <p> Growth in the Western Balkans is expected to rise to 3.3% in 2024 from 2.5% last year , with Kosovo set to be the fastest growing economy in the Western Balkans region in both 2024 and 2025 . Its forecast for 4.0% growth remains unchanged . <p> Across the wider region , Central Asia will achieve the strongest growth , although it is anticipated to dip slightly from 5.7% in 2023 to 5.4% in 2024 , with a rebound expected in 2025 to 5.9% . <p> The Caucasus will see more moderate growth of 4.1% in 2024 , settling closer to 3.5% in 2025 . The influx to Armenia was equivalent to 2.5% of the population . This is paying dividends , " said Javorcik . <p> Armenia 's population was further boosted by the arrival of around 100,000 ethnic Armenians from Nagorno Karabakh after the war with Azerbaijan . <p> Ukraine 's output expansion is likely to be constrained in 2024 due to significant damage to electricity infrastructure .
