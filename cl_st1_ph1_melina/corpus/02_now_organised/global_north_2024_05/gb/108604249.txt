<h> Yemen 's fishermen left high and dry by conflict , extreme weather <p> BEIRUT ( Thomson Reuters Foundation ) - For Yemeni fisherman Salem Atek , 2024 has been a perfect storm : driven out of the rich waters of the Red Sea by conflict , he has been left foraging for meagre catches closer to shore all while braving extreme weather caused by climate change . <p> In March , warships started shooting near Atek 's boat as he fished in the Red Sea , now a place of peril due to attacks by Yemen 's Houthi group on international shipping , who say they are acting in solidarity with Palestinians in the Gaza war . <p> Traumatised by his narrow escape , Atek decided to sit out the rest of the tuna season . <p> But his troubles were not over - unseasonably high tides then swept into a hut where he stored his fishing equipment near his home in al-Buraiqeh , near Aden , damaging some of his gear . <p> " For the first time in my life the water got into it at this time of the year , " he said . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ we fishermen have our calculations ... but now our calculations are not working . Not only has a civil war on land destroyed boats , ports and processing sites , the new conflict at sea is costing lives and money as fishermen are driven out of their usual grounds . <p> " We are being forced into debt , " Atek told the Thomson Reuters Foundation by phone , saying he could only catch cheaper , smaller fish by the coast while he could snare more expensive fish , such as tuna , further out to sea . <p> A civil war has raged in Yemen since the Iranian-backed Houthis ousted the government from the capital Sanaa in late 2014 .
