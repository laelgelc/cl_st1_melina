<p> Most stock markets in the Gulf slipped on Tuesday amid rising geopolitical tensions in the region , while investors also awaited key U.S. inflation data later in the week . Israeli tanks advanced to the centre of Rafah for the first time on Tuesday , as part of ground offensive in the southern Gaza city that has stirred global condemnation for its continued civilian toll . <p> Saudi Arabia 's benchmark stock index was down for a fourth straight day , falling 1.5% to 11,660 , its lowest level in five months . ACWA Power slid 4.7% and Saudi National Bank , the kingdom 's largest lender , declined 2.1% . <p> The Abu Dhabi benchmark index dropped 1% to 8,742 , its lowest level in more than two years . The conglomerate Alpha Dhabi Holding declined 6% to close at 11.0 dirham per share , its lowest level since listing in June 2021 . Among other losers , First Abu Dhabi Bank , the UAE 's largest lender , fell 2.2% to 11.58 dirham apiece , the lowest in three and half years . Qatar Islamic Bank , the country 's largest Islamic lender , climbed 3.2% and Qatar Navigation rose 1.1% . The Federal Reserve 's preferred measure of inflation , core personal consumption expenditures , due on Friday will also be closely watched for clues on the U.S. central bank 's interest rate outlook . <p> Markets are currently fully pricing in one 25 basis-point Fed rate cut this year .
