Photo : Getty Images <p> Comment : Saturday February 1 was the grim fourth anniversary of the military coup in Myanmar . The date seems to have gone unnoticed back here in New Zealand . Even before war in Ukraine and then Gaza , Myanmar got little attention . Yet it is the most destructive conflict in our region . <p> The figures are shocking ; nearly a third of the population of 60 million is likely to need humanitarian aid this year . At least 3.5 million are internally displaced ; they are escaping indiscriminate bombing and shooting by the army . Around 1.3 million people have fled the country and are living in refugee camps . <p> As opposition to the army grows , its control on the ground has shrunk dramatically . Most border zones are in the hands of local warlords or ethnic resistance groups , and conflict is spilling over into neighbouring states : China , Thailand , India and Bangladesh . Unfortunately , their differences have often been exploited and there is no clarity among those opposing the junta about the way ahead . <h> read more <p> Increasing pressure on the junta has resulted in even more untargeted attacks on civilians . Forced conscription has been reinstated and has led to raids in areas under military control . The conscripts are given very little training and used as cannon fodder . Jails are full of people swept up in raids and conditions are appalling . The annual amnesty mainly sees the release of common prisoners , and a few foreigners who have become an embarrassment for the regime . There are around 20,000 jailed for dissent of one kind or another . <p> Myanmar hit the news recently over the kidnapping of a high-profile Chinese actor who was spirited over the Thai border and forced to work in a scam factory in the Myanmar town of Myawaddy . <p> Those caught up in the raid being led away . While the Chinese and Thai governments are believed to have put pressure on the junta to close down the centres , it is doubtful whether the junta has the ability , or even the political will to do so . Thailand has just cut off electricity , internet and fuel supplies to five locations across the border that host cyber-scam centres . However , these cuts are most likely to hurt innocent locals who already do not receive electricity for more than a few hours a day . <p> All of this is very tough on the small Myanmar community in New Zealand , most of whom came here as refugees . They are desperately worried about family members back in Myanmar , find it increasingly difficult to get funds to them , and are in despair over the New Zealand Government 's unwillingness to make any concession to the gravity of the situation . The parents and six children are now New Zealand citizens , have bought themselves a house , and are all either working or studying . However , the Lings still have a daughter , son-in-law and three young grandchildren back in Myanmar , who are living in real danger .
