<h> Brian Taylor : Donald Trump and self-delusion -- should we boycott him or praise him ? <h> Donald Trump and self-delusion -- should we boycott him or praise him ? <p> Let us , for a moment , try to see the world as Donald Trump sees it . He may be driven by self-belief -- or , perhaps , self-delusion -- but he remains the leader of the supposedly free world . What he says , matters . <p> En passant , let us note that he remains indisputably the US Commander in Chief . It is to be hoped that the American system of military and diplomatic constraint continues to apply , particularly with regard to the nuclear launch protocol . <p> OK , that is extreme . I strive to believe that Mr Trump offers seemingly outlandish proposals in order to secure concessions . He is , after all , the author of a book called The Art of the Deal . <p> I also know that he is surrounded by advisers , both permanent and political , who strain to subdue some of his more @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ literature , from the wonderful novel Scoop , by Evelyn Waugh . <p> In that fine work , Lord Copper is a dictatorial , hectoring newspaper magnate . His aides fear to contradict him as he scatters his views on every subject . If he is right , those aides enthusiastically assent . <p> If , more commonly , he is utterly wrong , then they mutter : " Up to a point , Lord Copper . " I would imagine that comparable phrases are being regularly deployed in the White House at the moment . <p> Not sure whether Benjamin Netanyahu is a fan of Waugh 's work . But you could see a Lord Copper moment written on the Israeli Prime Minister 's face when Mr Trump stood next to him and came out with his proposals for Gaza . You know , as the evacuated Riviera of the Middle East . <p> You could see Bibi picturing the practical impossibilities of the scheme , the global condemnation . Yet , he also thought , maybe this could be depicted as matching broader Israeli aims , maybe this @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a few lines to the effect that the US President dared to tread where others feared to go . Up to a point , Lord Copper , would have covered it too . <p> Perhaps President Trump will pursue his plan . Perhaps he will set up a new colonial department to handle Gaza alongside Greenland and Canada . Perhaps , alternatively , it will subside beneath its own inherent absurdity . <p> Either way , Donald Trump will be unmoved . He alone has the vision . And if he sees Gaza as partly a real estate question , then so be it . <p> But , if he is unmoved , he is not unmoving . Wherein lies hope . Look at the swift switch with regard to trade policy . In one moment , he announces protective tariffs against his neighbours , Mexico and Canada . <p> Then after personal talks with the leaders of those countries -- he just loves those personal talks -- Mr Trump backs down and suspends the tariffs for a month . <p> A family tries to cook and get warm around @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cold winter day in Rafah , Gaza ( Image : Anadolu via Getty Images ) In a way , bizarre . Did he not say " tariffs is the most beautiful word to me in the dictionary ? " He did indeed . <p> So why is it beauty deferred , why glory on hold ? Because he -- and only he -- has struck a personal bargain with Canada and Mexico . He has obtained assurances about clamping down on cross-border movement of people and drugs . <p> Observers say those assurances remain limited . That they merely extend proposals already in place . Not for the President . They are the best deal ever , from the most admirable leader . This is self-centred action , diplomacy via solipsism . <p> Now , this might change again . He might turn upon his neighbours once more , accusing them of bad faith . If they are wise , they will endorse his efforts , praise his unique sagacity -- and cut a continuing deal . <p> The alternative approach , as suggested by some voices in the UK @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ refuse to have anything to do with him . In effect , to boycott America . Might make us feel virtuous . Would terrify business . <p> We remain on the precipice of a globally damaging trade war . Mr Trump says tariffs on the EU are unavoidable . He is annoyed that many American consumers appear to favour German cars over their US counterparts . <p> To which one might say that the American companies should improve their products . If you implement strict protectionism , you tend to end up with inferior goods . Fancy driving a Trabant ? <p> The President says he could bargain with the UK -- although that is not a free pass . It might involve access to our market for American farm goods which are currently restricted . <p> The most intriguing stand-off , though , is with China . The US is levying an additional ten per cent on Chinese imports . China has responded with a calibrated package of measures which remind us of the true nature of underlying global industrial competition . <p> China has announced an inquiry into @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of Google 's global market while Beijing blocks their search services . <p> This is not about the current situation . This is a warning to America 's west coast that China could seek other ways to hinder tech trade , including in AI . America currently has a surplus in services , countering its deficit in manufacturing . <p> That is why there is such interest in the Chinese AI system DeepSeek . This is our uncertain future . Our Prime Minister wants Britain to take a lead . The Scottish Government has just announced a ? 9m package to support AI development here . <p> Further , China has placed controls on the trade in rare earth metals . These are vital components in electronics . Forget gold and silver . These are the new industrial battleground - and China is serving notice . America says Ukraine can fill any supply gap -- in return for military support . <p> So , there is complexity within this trade war . Even though the President talks in simplistic terms . In essence , he is applying 19th Century protectionism @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ may see the late 19th Century as a golden age of emerging US power . Snag is that industry now is closely inter-connected . Hit China and you hit vital components for US firms -- such as electric car batteries where China is dominant . <p> Hit global trade and you hit us . That , plus the UK Budget , may explain why the Bank of England has halved its growth forecast . Let us hope President Trump modifies his personal world view . <p> Brian Taylor is a former political editor for BBC Scotland and a columnist for The Herald .
