<h> ' This time , we stay ' : the Palestinian families vowing not to leave Gaza <p> Saaed Salem 's eyes filled with tears as he surveyed the remains of his north Gaza neighbourhood on a freezing February morning . He was resting in a chair that had somehow survived the war , surrounded by grandchildren and rubble , his hope for the future and the ruins of his past . <p> His family had lost one home in 1948 , when they fled Hirbiya village , now the site of Zikim kibbutz inside Israel , to escape shelling and reports of atrocities by Israeli forces . <p> " We locked our house , took the key , and walked toward Gaza , believing we 'd return in a few days , " said Salem , who was then five . Waiting at the end of an exhausting trek was a new reality of tents and refugee camps and permanent exile in the north of the Gaza strip . <p> " When the truth became clear , that we had abandoned our homes and others had taken them , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ faced death instead . The regret never left us . " He was one of about 700,000 Palestinians forced from their homes in the " Nakba " , or catastrophe , during the 1948 war surrounding the creation of Israel . <p> So when Israeli troops moved into Gaza in October 2023 , more than seven decades later , Salem and his family defied orders for civilians to evacuate to the south of the strip . " We had sworn not to make that mistake again , " he said . <p> They stayed in north Gaza during the war , with about 400,000 others , even as a blockade within a blockade meant the north got even less aid than the south . A global watchdog warned of impending famine there last year . <p> " We endured famine , thirst , bombings , fear , everything . We lived among corpses , under ruins , eating food that was n't fit for animals . But we never left northern Gaza , " he said .
