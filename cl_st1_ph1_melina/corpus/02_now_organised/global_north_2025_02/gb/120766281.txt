<h> Ta-Nehisi Coates is feted by Barack Obama and compared to James Baldwin . But in The Message , his tour of the world 's racial hotspots , he is more interested in himself <p> There has been quite the fuss over The Message since it was published in the US , partly over what the book says but mainly because of what its author is : a public intellectual . Ta-Nehisi Coates was thrust into this most dreaded of positions a decade ago after The Atlantic published his article The Case for Reparations , in which he concluded that black Americans were due reparations for the Jim Crow era , akin to Germany paying Israel for the cost of resettling Jewish survivors after the Second World War . <p> He was instantly catapulted from the status of mere journalist to something far loftier . Toni Morrison compared him to James Baldwin . President Obama gave interviews to him . His 2015 book , Between the World and Me , about the impact of slavery , won a National Book award . <p> But Coates had his critics . Black @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . And why , they asked , was he citing Israel as an example of redemption after oppression ? What about Palestine ? <p> Well , The Message is a response to these critics and Coates , foolishly , does not tell them to get stuffed . Instead , he goes on a global tour of various racisms : Dakar , South Carolina and Palestine . He spends a handful of days in each , which is all that is needed for Coates to claim special insight . " My Gap Yah in Racism " would be a better title for the book . And like a teenager interrailing through Europe , Coates is less interested in the places he visits than he is in himself . <p> Our journey begins in the Senegalese capital , Dakar , and Coates frontloads the chapter with horrifying details of the historical slave trade .
