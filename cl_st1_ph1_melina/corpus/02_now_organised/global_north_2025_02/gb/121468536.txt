<h> The past 60 years have not be as stable and secure as some believe <h> The past 60 years have not be as stable and secure as some believe <p> MY good friend Jonathan Shafi suggests that Scottish independence has become more problematic and unviable because of the international situation ( SNP are n't equipped to deal with global insecurity , Feb 19 ) . This is apparently in contrast to those simpler days when Winnie Ewing won Hamilton and recent stable decades . <p> Sometimes the youngsters need to be reminded of history . <p> The Hamilton by-election in 1967 was just a few years after the Cuban missile crisis when we came so close to nuclear war . It was at a peak period in the appalling Vietnam War . There were still fascist dictatorships in Spain and Portugal until the mid-seventies . In 1968 the Soviet Union , Poland and Hungary invaded Czechoslovakia . A year earlier a military coup in Greece had overthrown the democratic government and it remained in power till 1974 . In 1968 the French state nearly collapsed with the mass student @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ was committing plenty of war crimes in the brutal Algerian war . <p> The next peak period of support for independence was in the mid-70s when the SNP won more than 30% of the General Election vote . We had continuing wars but it was economic crises that dominated , with huge inflation levels . <p> In the 1980s the Thatcher/Reagan governments ratcheted up the nuclear threat and Scotland was told we were going to get a huge expansion at Faslane/Coulport to house the Trident missile system . There were huge anti-nuclear campaigns across Europe in opposition to the US decision to introduce Cruise and Pershing missiles . There was a military coup in Nato member Turkey . And , of course , there was the Falklands War . <p> Meanwhile Scottish society had to cope with losing 40% of its manufacturing jobs in five years . Then the US and UK covertly supported Saddam Hussein in his bloody war against Iran . We had the break-up of the Soviet Union and the terrible violence involved in the break-up of Yugoslavia . <p> I could go on , but let @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , the Iraq war , the Libyan war and Western triumphalism -- a triumphalism that did n't last long . That did not reduce support for independence ; quite the contrary . We had the massive financial crash in 2008 , the impact of which still lingers on . We had a global pandemic . <p> The notion that we are in some unique historical stage of instability does not reflect the reality of the past 60 years . There is one unique factor -- global warming . But we are still at the early stages of that impact . The major environmental crises are still to come . In periods of crisis and disorientation people are looking for new models , new futures . They are not shutting the door to change but they can be more open to the snake-oil salesmen if they do not see convincing alternatives . <p> I take Jonathan 's point -- can the SNP provide a credible , inspiring vision for independence ? To do the radical , to take on big vested interests , to energise and create trust in change -- @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , it requires standing up to power . I ca n't help but feel that they are frightened of departing from the conventional wisdom , scared of big new ideas . <p> For example , you would think that Ireland 's very successful international model would be one they might like to promote . A non-aligned country which has contributed to UN peace-keeping forces for decades , was elected as one of the non-permanent members of the UN Security Council , has played an active role along with South Africa in challenging the legality of Israeli actions in Gaza . There is a long list of positives but it is one the SNP never want to reference .
