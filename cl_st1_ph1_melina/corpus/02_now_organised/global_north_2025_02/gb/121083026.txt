<h> Alison Rowat : My unscientific experiment with the Scotcast podcast <h> I listened to BBC Scotland 's new podcast . It 's a bit of a hit and miss <p> In low growth Britain there is one business that is booming - news . No end of material is working its way through the media machine to websites , newspapers , televisions , and radio . And if you miss any of that , there are always podcasts , the latest of which is BBC Scotland 's Scotcast . <p> Before we look at how the newest broadcast on the block is faring , yesterday brought a reminder that the old guard of politics coverage , the Sunday shows , have an important role to play yet . Who else is going to set the scene for another tumultuous week in the life of Starmer 's toddler government ? <p> This week 's Saturday night scandal going into Sunday morning uproar was the sacking of Labour health minister Andrew Gwynne for offensive comments on a WhatsApp chat . In one message the MP wished one of his elderly constituents @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ interview on BBC1 's Sunday with Laura Kuenssberg was recorded on Saturday , before the Gwynne story broke . Angela Rayner , deputy Prime Minister was therefore spared questions on how the likes of Gwynne ever made it through vetting . <p> Rayner faced uncomfortable questions of her own on plans to demolish Grenfell Tower , on the government 's performance in general and her actions in particular . <p> Some of the families of the 72 people who died in the Grenfell fire were invited to a meeting with Rayner . A number of those present later said the minister had been aggressive and upsetting . Rayner regretted if that was the impression given , and said there was no consensus on what to do with the site and probably never would be . <p> So far Rayner has managed to avoid the criticism levelled at other ministers . There was that business with a house sale and the tax paid on it , but that was before the general election . Since then it has been the Chancellor and Prime Minister who have been given near daily dressings @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ while the MP for Ashton-under-Lyne has sailed on smoothly enough . <p> Rayner did , however , make some notable appearances recently in a new book , Get In : The Inside Story of Labour under Starmer by Patrick Maguire and Gabriel Pogrund . Kuenssberg asked her about two of these . Did Rayner say that Starmer " could n't run a bath " never mind the Labour Party ? And did she use an offensive term to describe Prince Andrew ? " I do n't recognise what was said in that book , " she said . As Kuenssberg noted , these were not denials . <p> Asked why the government was struggling , Rayner said people were frustrated , they wanted change but it took time to turn things around , certainly longer than seven months . <p> Rayner was speaking after a six-hour Cabinet meeting in London on Friday , during which ministers were once again urged by Starmer to get a move on and start delivering . " We can either be the disruptors or the disrupted " he told the gathering . Newscast marked its fifth birthday on February 5 with a special episode from the BBC Radio Theatre at Broadcasting House . <p> BBC Radio Scotland 's Scotcast , meanwhile , is yet to mark its first month in existence . Presented by Martin Geissler , Scotcast and the new TV showThe Seven , were funded from money saved by the axing of The Nine . <p> How is the newbie doing so far ? In a wholly unscientific experiment , I added Scotcast to my podcast list and have been dipping in regularly since the launch episode on January 13 . <p> First point to note is that Scotcast is not to be confused with a Coatbridge building supplies firm that comes top in a Google search for the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ week compared to Newscast 's seven . <p> When Geissler launched the show he said it would be " about the most interesting news in Scotland and other stuff going on here too " . <p> Episodes so far have covered homelessness , an interview with former First Minister of Scotland Humza Yousaf on Trump and Gaza , Brexit , scammers , John Swinney 's plan for the health service and the tourist tax . <p> Scotcast does live up to its brief of covering Scottish news , but is it doing so to any greater depth than existing programmes on TV and radio , and other podcasts ? Providing that bit more is meant to be one of the main points of podcasts , after all . <p> Sometimes the added Scottish value was obvious , as with Humza Yousaf 's interview . And Scotcast does , as promised , bring in other journalists from the newsroom to give their take on the subject discussed . <p> The episode on homelessness had Laura Miller as co-host . The item was about so-called Zoom fatigue , or how everyone is tired of looking at their coupons on a computer screen . <p> " Do you hate your face ? " Geissler asked Miller . After some initial hesitation she admitted avoiding looking at herself on screen . Geissler did some sharing of his own , saying he stopped looking at himself in a mirror three years ago " because all my hair fell out " , and he hates being photographed . <p> " We 've put a lot on the table , " said Geissler wrapping up the podcast , " more than I thought we might .
