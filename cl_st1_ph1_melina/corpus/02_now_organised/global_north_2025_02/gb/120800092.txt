<h> No-platforming Michael Gove for being pro-Israel would be wrong <h> Real politics is about the exchange of opinions and the clash of views , and it was in everybody 's interests that a debate at the University of Glasgow went ahead <p> Eddie Barnes <p> Monday February 03 2025 , 6.00pm , The Times <p> To build a better political culture in Scotland we need more open , civilised political debate . I 'm glad to say that , despite coming under severe pressure , that principle has been met at the University of Glasgow . <p> I had invited Michael Gove and Humza Yousaf to share a platform and speak to students . The idea was for them to discuss their lives in politics . However , a few weeks beforehand , Gove had written a deeply provocative article in the Jewish Chronicle in which he called for the Israel Defence Forces to receive the Nobel peace prize . As a result , as the event got going , we were faced with protesters at the university who said Gove should be barred from speaking . <p> Thanks @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of local police officers , the event went ahead . I would argue that no matter your view on Gaza and Israel , it was in everybody 's interests -- including those of the protesters outside -- that we did so . <p> For one thing , no-platforming Gove would have denied Yousaf the chance to criticise his comments , something he proceeded to do with gusto . More important than that , however , it would have denied Gove the chance to listen to and engage with that criticism . Real politics is about the exchange of opinions and the clash of views . That , had Gove been no-platformed , would not have taken place . <p> More than ever we need to defend these political basics . Social media has turned much of today 's politics into a search for clicks : witness the way in which prime minister 's and first minister 's questions has become a made-for-social media act designed for retweets and likes . <h> Advertisement <p> The risk with this development is twofold . First , it can lead to even greater polarisation @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ than the population as a whole . Second , it leads to the growth of politics-as-performance where the crafting of a 30-second video for X is deemed more impactful than serious policy analysis or genuine political debate . <p> We declared our support for people 's right to protest . That , too , is vital . But organisations such as ours must resist the demands of no-platforming and speak up for open debate . It may be fine to " unfollow " or " block " somebody online , but to defend the real business of politics , it is vital we stand up against the tendency to extend that principle to the real world too . <p> I do n't know if Yousaf or Gove changed each other 's minds last week when they explained their beliefs to our audience . I do know that it 's only through such an exchange of views , delivered passionately but with civility , that change and consensus is possible .
