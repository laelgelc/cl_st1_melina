<h> Scottish independence messaging must catch up with globalisation <p> THE era of untrammelled American globalisation is in its closing phases . <p> This does not mean that the United States , with its sprawling military , is not dominant in the world system . But the strategic calculations around how the superpower situates itself are radically shifting . <p> This is not necessarily a managed process , but a forced one , owing to imperial decline and with it the coming apart of the post-war global order . Of course , the brash approach taken by Donald Trump puts exclamation points after this process . <p> But events today are not reducible to a study of his personal psychology or the populist resentments built up within the domestic political arena . Rather , he and his administration are a reflection of something far more structural : that the United States is now one of several great powers , vying for position , in an emerging multi-polar order . <p> Gone are the days when the supranational institutions of American hegemony were unchallenged . <p> This means that nation-states and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ are having to consider their position in a volatile and fluctuating world order . <p> This includes Scotland , and has profound implications for the intellectual reconstruction of the case for Scottish independence in the 21st century . <p> Before we get to the meaning behind of actions of the new Trump regime thus far , some further scene-setting is required . <p> The foreign policy disasters attendant to the challenges now confronting American power also lie at the door of the erstwhile Republican establishment and the Democratic leadership . It can not be understated how deleterious the invasion and occupation of Iraq , and the humiliating withdrawal from Afghanistan have been to the US militarily and geopolitically . <p> The same can be said of its approach to the Middle East more recently , where Joe Biden led the most overt charge against the architecture of international humanitarian law , in partnership with Israel 's actions in Gaza . <p> Closer to home , while the export of manufacturing to low-waged economies in the Global South allowed for multi-national corporations to achieve higher profits , and American consumers lower @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ This includes fewer manufacturing jobs , often in areas where people have become supportive of Trump 's campaigning . The US trade deficit with China , for example , eliminated around 3.7 million American jobs between 2001 and 2018 . <p> What is striking about recent days and weeks is the way in which the United States has ranged itself against its allies . <p> Denmark has had it far tougher than Iran , for example . In an aggressive and confrontational phone call with Danish prime minister Mette Frederiksen , Trump demanded that Denmark sell Greenland . <p> Remember , Denmark is a Nato member , which makes an already outlandish initiative seem even more deluded . <p> This while the US sparks a diplomatic crisis with Canada , and as Trump says he prefers to deal with individual European states , rather than the European Union , upon which he plans to launch a tariff war . You might call it a modern version of gunboat diplomacy . Meanwhile , Britain is being buffeted by the crosswinds and is denuded of any real agency as far as Keir @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for Trump , in the hope that he is more gracious with Number 10 than he was to the Danes . <p> But do n't believe the claim that this is all simply a passing , outlandish , phase . What we are seeing is a retrenchment of American power . <p> Rather than firing up for new rounds of war in the Middle East -- though this can never be written off entirely -- Europe and its closest neighbours are to be cannibalised . The idea is to subjugate these powers more comprehensively , rather than manage them as orbital allies . <p> Consider that USAID is being shut down . This is one of the most important components of America 's soft-power operation around the world . <p> It shows that the US is retreating , consolidating , and preparing for future great power conflicts , especially with China . <p> Of course , this also opens up new avenues for the tech oligarchy and others to more directly realise their interests . Nothing can be taken for granted about the architecture of the Western system . That includes the European Union , Nato and the concept of the " special " transatlantic relationship with the US . <p> Within this context , new dimensions begin to contour the discussion around Scottish independence . <p> While it is off the agenda in any practical sense , the renewal of the case and a distinctive Scottish political culture must take into consideration the reality of a brave new world . <p> Simply put , the official case , already threadbare and incoherent , needs almost entirely revamped and reimagined . <p> Turning up to a war with a peashooter , First Minister John Swinney says he plans to use Trump 's " affinity for Scotland " in a bid to avoid the prospect of tariffs being applied on imports to the US . <p> If that was not sycophantic enough , Alba 's Ash Regan wrote a letter to tech overlord Elon Musk urging him @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a new " gigafactory " . <p> None of this represents a theory of meaningful Scottish autonomy or guide to action which fits in the new global landscape . <p> It appears that despite the supposed differences between the SNP leadership and the Alba leadership hopeful , both are content with Scotland being a vassal to more powerful states . <p> New thinking is required , and that means first coming to an appraisal of the geopolitical environment . <p> Within this context , there is the potential for fresh ideas to be reinjected into discussions about the meaning of independence and Scotland 's role on the international scene . <p> That thinking will have to come from outside those elements attached to an outdated view of the world , and from those whose ambition for the country is limited to appeasing American power in its revanchist phase . <p> But there is an opening , perhaps the first in some time , for some dynamic and interesting thinking on the national question . <h> Why are you making commenting on The National only available to subscribers ?
