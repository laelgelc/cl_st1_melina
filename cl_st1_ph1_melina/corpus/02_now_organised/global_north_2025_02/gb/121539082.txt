<p> Palestinian families are suing the Canadian government after a temporary scheme that grants visas to Palestinians from Gaza with family ties to the country has been beset by delays . <p> The scheme has come under fire for its complexity and ineffectiveness in issuing visas . <p> Marku and Lee , an immigration and refugee law firm in Toronto , filed a case on 6 February on behalf of 53 Palestinian families who are currently in the Gaza Strip to ask the Federal Court of Canada to process the applications without delay . <p> The firm says half their clients are children , the youngest of whom is seven months old . They expressed concern that the Canadian government had let the applications " sit in limbo " while conditions in Gaza had progressively worsened and the enclave had become uninhabitable since the scheme was launched more than a year ago . <p> They said their clients ' Canadian family members had petitioned on behalf of their relatives within a month of the application , and their applications had not yet proceeded to the next stage .
