<p> Last month , the province of Manitoba announced it would be bringing in two children from Gaza for urgent medical care . Health coverage would first be provided by the federal government , after which the province would assume responsibility for paying for the children 's care . Premier Wab Kinew addressed the question on the minds of many Manitobans : how and why could the province afford to bring in and treat these kids from overseas when so many current residents were struggling to access timely health care ? <p> Mr. Kinew , speaking slowly , made his case : " At the end of our lives , " he began , " when we meet our maker , and the good creator asks us , ' You , who were given so many opportunities , how did you use my grace to ease the suffering of innocents ? " <p> The clip is about two minutes long , and I encourage readers to watch it for themselves since the printed words do n't convey the strength of delivery . Mr. Kinew was confident and assertive , but not didactic or preachy . He did n't assign blame , and he did n't malign Manitobans who might be concerned about the province 's scarce resources . Instead , he made an empathetic appeal to their better nature , providing them with a moral path that they could accompany him along , should they choose . It was , in a word , leadership . <p> Mr. Kinew has all the elements of a great national progressive leader , should he opt to go that route . A defining moment for him as Opposition leader came in 2021 , when Mr. Kinew confronted the newly sworn-in minister of Indigenous reconciliation in the middle of his press conference after he suggested that residential schools were founded with good intentions .
