<h> Editorial Second-cousin sycophancy <p> At the beginning of this spring , Malcolm Turnbull wrote an essay for Australian Foreign Affairs . He warned against a tendency to believe a second Trump presidency would not change Australia 's interactions with the United States , that for all the bluster and distraction the diplomatic relationship could continue as usual . <p> " Trump is unlike any other president of modern times , " Turnbull wrote . " He is not committed to democracy or the rule of law in the way most people understand it . His conduct on January 6 , 2021 is proof enough of that , as is his affection for authoritarian leaders -- not only the illiberal Viktor Orb ? n in Hungary and Vladimir Putin in Russia , but also President Xi Jinping of China and , most improbably , North Korea 's Kim Jong-un . <p> " Trump 's view of the world is dystopian -- think of his ' American carnage ' speech in 2017 , on the occasion of his inauguration as president . ' He was no different on the Middle East : ' They all want to kill each other . ' ? " <p> Donald Trump does not delegate the way ordinary presidents do . He is not interested in briefings . He makes decisions without thinking deeply , sometimes without thinking at all . His announcements , as on Gaza this week , are impulsive , unstructured and entirely his own . <p> Turnbull diagnosed Trump as a narcissist and a bully . He is animated by his wealth and his lust for power . In this he is not singular , although the approach to such figures is . <p> " To succeed with them , you need to stand up to them -- but courteously , " Turnbull wrote . The approach of the former is muted inarticulacy , a commitment not to offer a running commentary , which translates to ignoring obvious outrages . The latter 's approach is a kind of second-cousin sycophancy , the excitement of a little boy up late at a sleepover . <p> Both are an indication of what to expect as the election draws nearer . We have a prime minister afraid to say what is true and an opposition leader thrilling at the prospect of saying what is not .
