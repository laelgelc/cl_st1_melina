What is USAID ? The independent agency was created through the Foreign Assistance Act , which was passed by Congress and signed by then-President John F. Kennedy in 1961 . The goal was to counter Soviet influence during the Cold War and advance America 's interests by strategically providing foreign aid . Broadly , USAID aims to fight disease , reduce poverty and address humanitarian needs . It 's a big mission that makes up a relatively small slice of overall government spending . According to the nonpartisan Congressional Research Service , USAID managed more than $40 billion in fiscal year 2023 , the most recent year for which complete data is available . That 's less than 1% of the overall federal budget . With those funds , the agency provided assistance to approximately 130 countries . USAID oversees a wide range of programs , from HIV clinics in Africa to rapid-response teams for Ebola outbreaks . The agency has also bolstered conservation efforts in the Amazon rainforest and educated Afghan girls under Taliban rule . In recent years , USAID has also provided significant humanitarian , developmental and economic support to Ukraine during the war with Russia . It has also provided humanitarian assistance in Gaza amid the Israel-Hamas war . While many of its goals are bipartisan , critics argue some of USAID 's programs are wasteful and promote a liberal agenda . The Trump administration has pointed to money for transgender services in Guatemala and diversity , equity and inclusion initiatives in Serbia as examples . What is happening to USAID under the Trump administration ?
