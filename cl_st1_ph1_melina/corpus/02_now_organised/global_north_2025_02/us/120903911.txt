( Neither would serve U.S. interests , by the way . ) <p> But the most dangerous favor , by far , would be U.S. assistance in bombing Iranian nuclear sites -- or even threatening to do so in a new campaign of diplomatic coercion . <p> For months , Israel has threatened to strike Iran 's nuclear program and sought U.S. help to do it . President Joe Biden wisely avoided that course . The new Trump administration , however , seems much keener on wielding U.S. military power to coerce the Iranians into abandoning their nuclear ambitions . <p> Commentators have urged Trump to act now , arguing that Iran is less able @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in Lebanon and Syria , and that Tehran 's enhanced uranium enrichment efforts mean it could quickly produce fissile material for nuclear weapons . ( Never mind that U.S. intelligence has assessed that Iran is n't currently pursuing weaponization and remains " several months " to a year away from being able to assemble a bomb , let alone deliver it . ) <p> Instead , Trump should seize this golden opportunity to step back and let the Middle East solve its own problems , for once . Regional actors can contain Iran . They already have . <p> Iran hawks are right that Tehran is in its weakest position in years -- but they miss the crucial point that it got there without U.S. intervention . Israel 's dismantling of Hezbollah in Lebanon and the Turkish-backed ouster of Assad by Syrian rebels have dramatically undermined Iran 's greatest source of leverage -- its ability to mount proxy attacks . <p> Local actors dealt those blows , on their own initiative , for their own reasons . That 's great news , because the U.S. has bigger problems than Iran @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ can not reach the U.S. homeland and which the U.S. can deter with its own robust nuclear and conventional capabilities . A nuclear Iran might threaten its neighbors ( or not , as nuclear deterrence is a powerful force for peace ) , but it poses little threat to the United States . <p> What really matters is great power competition with China . But a new U.S. push against the Iranian nuclear program could endanger Trump 's evident desire to prioritize China over the Middle East , despite widespread agreement in Washington that a pivot to Asia is long overdue . <p> Of course , it 's easy to imagine how a U.S. strike on Iranian nuclear targets could escalate into all-out war , pulling the U.S. further into the region 's dysfunction . <p> But diplomacy could place the United States in a quagmire , too , if it relies on U.S. military force . If Iran defies U.S. threats , Washington might have to carry them out , for fear of damaging its reputation . This would put Trump right back into the war-with-Iran scenario . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ U.S. threats and backs down , the stress on U.S. resources would be significant . That 's because the steps necessary to make coercive threats credible enough to work are themselves costly . <p> Throughout the bargaining process , the U.S. would have to position itself as willing and able to strike , which means dedicating some military bandwidth to Iran contingencies . Even if those contingencies do n't come to pass , the commitment to potentially enforce threats against Iran would reduce U.S. flexibility to act elsewhere . <p> Furthermore , any solution that requires U.S. power is n't sustainable in its absence . Even if U.S. " bunker-buster " strikes destroyed all of Iran 's nuclear facilities -- a big " if " -- Iran could simply use its nuclear knowledge to rebuild , just as it did after the damage wrought by the cyber-weapon Stuxnet .
