<p> After a year-long wait , the much-anticipated film ' Our Land , Our Freedom ' premiered in Kenya at the NBO Film Festival . <p> The documentary follows Wanjugu Kimathi , daughter of renowned freedom fighter Dedan Kimathi , and her late mother , Mukami Kimathi , as they struggle to resettle displaced freedom fighters denied the land they fought for , while also searching for Dedan 's remains . <p> This gripping documentary reveals the violence of colonialism and the unresolved land disputes still haunting Kenya half a century after independence . <p> KBC Digital caught up with the award-winning directors , Meena Nanji and Zippy Kimundu to talk about their eight-year endeavour making the film and the lessons they learned along the way . <p> What inspired you to create this documentary ? <p> Meena : I was born in Kenya but our family moved away when I was very young . Then in 2012 , I read ' The British Gulag ' by Caroline Elkins , and I was shocked by the brutal history hidden behind colonial narratives . <p> Schools , both here and abroad , teach about British achievements without mentioning the violence @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ was truly shocked because I had no idea about detention camps , concentration camps , or brutality . <p> So , I wanted to tell Kenya 's story from a Kenyan point of view , and years later I came to Kenya looking for a partner to work with and in the meantime met Wanjugu Kimathi and she agreed to do a film about the independence struggle . <p> I had sworn never to do documentaries again but then I met a few people to work with and one of them was Zippy and my direction changed . So that was the motivation for doing the project . <p> Zippy : Yeah , when Meena approached me I was trying to adapt Ngugi wa Thiong'o 's Weep Not Child as a fiction film but I could not find enough information . <p> I realized collaborating on this documentary would allow us to tell a powerful , untold story from the perspectives of the freedom fighters . <p> I thought we would finish it within one or two years but it ended up taking eight years . <p> We premiered it @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Amsterdam ( IDFA ) and we have been travelling with it since then across the world and getting really interesting feedback . <p> Why did it take eight years to complete ? <p> Zippy : Initially we wanted to follow the Mau Mau veterans to get their stories , Meena has an experimental background while I have a fictional background so we thought this would be like a cool art project but not in a documentary style . <p> However , as Wanjugu 's focus shifted toward mobilizing land resettlement for freedom fighters , the story evolved . Wanjugu was carrying the mantle for her mother in this struggle so we decided to follow her story as she grew into an amazing community leader . <p> Meena : Exactly , I mean other factors also made it take a long time . Building trust takes time but Wanjugu and her mother Mukami were very generous in introducing us to many different freedom fighter communities around the country . <p> Funding also took a bit of time , our film is completely financed by grants and we have no investors so @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ delayed us since we had to take a break in 2020 . <p> In the end , though , the journey allowed us to capture a richer narrative -- one we could n't have anticipated if the story had not progressed . <p> What insights did you gain from the female freedom fighters ? <p> Zippy : We featured Mukami 's story a lot in this film and other freedom fighters but we know there are a lot of other strong women who deserve a story by themselves . <p> These women played pivotal roles in the struggle beyond caregiving , they were also spies , strategists , and coordinators . <p> Despite their importance , their contributions are often overlooked in history . <p> Meena : Meeting these women was humbling . I am a feminist but I realised how biased I was when meeting these communities , I would approach the men first but then the women sitting there would constantly chime in as we spoke and I realised how passionate they also were about the fight for freedom . <p> They 're in their 80s and 90s @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ intelligence work during the independence struggle was key , they had access where men could n't go . <p> It was eye-opening to see how deeply they still care about the country 's future . <p> So having learnt all this , what is your view on Kenya 's historical education ? <p> Meena : Kenya 's curriculum still reflects colonial biases . It is time this colonial education in Kenya stopped . <p> It 's time we teach history from Kenyan perspectives . Our film , and many others like Battle for Laikipia aim to provide that missing narrative . If we give future generations an authentic account of their past , they can aspire to make Kenya better . Europe is no longer the model -- we must create our own . <p> Why did you choose the title ' Our Land Our Freedom ' ? <p> Zippy : Land is deeply connected to freedom . The issue of land today is rooted in the colonial system . <p> Many fighters were promised land but never received it , while those loyal to the British were rewarded . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to acknowledge the roots of these issues . <p> Meena : Exactly , we were thinking about calling it ' Our Land Is Our Freedom ' but we thought that might be a little provocative . <p> Land is not just property ; it carries a spiritual connection . Losing it is like losing a family member . Without land , people are trapped in wage labour and poverty , breaking a vital bond between land and community . <p> Zippy : Our land is where we bury loved ones . It is inheritance and ancestral connection . <p> How did your collaboration shape the project ? <p> Zippy : ( laughing ) Collaboration is a marriage , there are highs and lows . This film would never have been what it is today if we did n't come together and also bring in other partners . <p> It was part of our life for a long time and despite our different styles , we always prioritized the film , and that 's why we 're celebrating it today . <p> Meena : We also shared a common goal -- @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to see it through . <p> Zippy : Yes , and the trust the freedom fighters placed in us was invaluable , and we had to work hard to honour their stories . <p> What has the response been so far ? <p> Zippy : This is the film 's first screening in Kenya , and we 're excited to see how people react . <p> The purpose was to bring it home but we knew that when we travelled around with it we would gain a bit of support . <p> Globally , it has resonated with audiences who did not know about this history . Many people were deeply moved by the film 's portrayal of the mother-daughter relationship . <p> Meena : I hope the film prompts viewers to rethink colonialism , not just in Kenya but worldwide . Current issues , like the situation in Gaza , are rooted in colonial history . The consequences of colonialism are playing out in real-time . Understanding these connections enriches our understanding of the present .
