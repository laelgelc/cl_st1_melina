<h> Ali Mazrui 's influence persists a decade after his death . He continues to inform , inspire , and stimulate and his contributions to scholarship and policy debates are being recognised globally . <p> Ten years ago , early on 12 October 2014 , the news broke in the town of Vestal in upstate New York . The Kenyan political scientist Ali Mazrui had died . The news quickly spread around the globe . It was echoed by international media outlets and leading newspapers of the world . <p> The New York Times proclaimed : " Ali Mazrui , Scholar of Africa Who Divided US Audiences , Dies at 81 . " In its obituary , The Washington Post also described Mazrui in the headline as a " controversial scholar " . The Guardian and The Times in the UK similarly announced his death . <p> In short , it felt as if a global celebrity had died . Mazrui 's influence persists a decade after his death . He continues to inform , inspire , and stimulate even today , as a minute of Google searches shows . The answer should provide us with inspiring and useful clues about Mazrui 's continued relevance . <p> I think the main reasons pertain to the substance of Mazrui 's discourse , and its style . The latter goes beyond his literary sophistication . <p> In his public and academic discourse , Mazrui correctly anticipated or foresaw some of the major social theories of our time . In addition , and this is even more evident , he had predicted significant international events with pinpoint accuracy . <h> Prolific scholarship <p> Mazrui 's predictions have been the least remarked upon ( but not necessarily the least remarkable ) aspect of his prolific scholarship . It can be argued that in the late 1960s , Mazrui was breaking new ground in social theory by bringing culture to the centre stage in the analysis of global processes . This was long before the " return of culture " to international relations in the 1990s . <p> Also , as early as the 1960s , Mazrui sharply critiqued modernisation theory , which he saw as " a dynamic , if somewhat mistaken , intellectual theorising " . <p> It was only in the 1990s that Samuel Huntington , the articulate proponent of modernisation theory , admitted , in effect , that modernisation theory was indeed a mistake . Subsequently , Huntington became preoccupied with conflicts of cultures and clashes of civilisations . <p> It was this that prompted Mazrui to ask , rhetorically , in 1995 : " Is the Huntington of modernisation not at odds with the Huntington of the clash of civilisations ? " <p> In 1967 , Mazrui invented the term " neo-dependency " in his doctoral dissertation , which was later converted into @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ popular among leftist African intellectuals . <p> Mazrui wrote in 1984 : " Global capitalism is much more obstinate and resilient than its critics assume . " This was long before there was any clear sign that Soviet communism was about to collapse and about 10 years before Francis Fukuyama popularised the idea of the end of history . <p> Even more intriguing are Mazrui 's specific predictions . In 1972 , Mazrui wrote : " When the hold of the white minority in Rhodesia is one day broken , we will almost certainly have a country called Zimbabwe . " Rhodesia gained independence in 1980 and was renamed Zimbabwe . <p> In 1973 , Mazrui maintained that " before long , the question was bound to be asked whether China belonged to the ranks of the weak and underprivileged or was about to join the ranks of the powerful " . This was at least half a decade before Deng Xiaoping opened up China for business -- a process that unleashed it as a rising global power . <p> In 1975 , Mazrui lamented that " we are nowhere @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Russians out of another Czechoslovakia " . So , in 1979 , the Russians invaded Afghanistan . <h> Free from white minority rule <p> In his 1986 TV series The Africans ( Programme 5 ) , Mazrui asserted that South Africa would be free from white minority rule in the 1990s . In 1994 , white minority rule ended in South Africa . <p> In 2013 , I asked him what he thought regarding his predictions about South Africa . Mazrui said : " I was vindicated about the schedule of the end of apartheid , but I was wrong about the method or strategy of ending it . I was glad apartheid ended with less violence than I predicted ... It seems conceivable that Kenya will have a younger President Kenyatta before very long ( Uhuru Kenyatta ) . It was a style he cultivated from the mid-1960s . It was this style that helped propel him to public prominence from an early age . <p> Apart from his artistic use of the English language , the style consisted of some essential elements . Mazrui would identify a public issue that could generate debate . He would then relate that issue to his conviction . <h> Contradictions as a guide to truth <p> Mazrui enjoyed the contradictions of any public discourse , which , after breathing enthusiasm into it , he proceeded to highlight in a dialectical manner . But dialectical here means not the Marxian dialectic of opposing forces in historical materialism -- Mazrui @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of contradictions as a guide to truth . <p> Mazrui later called his style of discourse , which partly relied on the unity of opposites , " radical pragmatism " . It is a style that recognises challenging realities as they are while constructing a radical response to them . It is a style known to stimulate and inspire some , and irritate many others . <p> When asked about the ultimate source of this style of discourse , Mazrui sometimes used to invoke the old saying in his language ( Kiswahili ) : " Wanaokubaliana kwa kila jambo hawana uzungumzi mirefu . " ( Those who agree on everything do not have much to converse about . ) <p> " My life itself is one long debate , " Mazrui told The Christian Science Monitor in 1986 . <p> Ali Mazrui is sorely missed today when the international system is experiencing what he would call its imminent instability for the first time in 500 years . The world is in flux . During such times , some might ask themselves , how would Mazrui have interpreted this or that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that ? <h> Dialectical style of analysis <p> With his dialectical style of analysis , he would have enlightened us about the meaning and significance of , for example , the rise of Donald Trump ( whom he would have probably compared with the Soviet Union 's Nikita Khrushchev and Uganda 's Idi Amin -- Mazrui once described Idi Amin as a peasant bull in the china shop of diplomatic history ) ; the potentially first black and woman president of the US , Kamala Harris ; and the wars in the Middle East as well as in Ukraine , among many others . <p> Comparing Nikita Khrushchev and Idi Amin , Mazrui wrote in the 1970s : " Both brought to the refined diplomatic world ... the rustic embarrassment of inadequate inhibition . Khrushchev was capable of shouting loudly at another head of government or of taking off his shoe and banging it on the table at the United Nations . " <p> Mazrui would have also saluted Cyril Ramaphosa , whom he had described as the " potential president of South Africa " more than 10 years before Ramaphosa assumed that position , for taking Israel to the International Court of Justice over the war in Gaza . <p> However , Mazrui would probably be puzzled by South Africa 's at best ambivalent position vis- ? -vis Russia 's invasion and occupation of Ukraine . <p> Of course , Mazrui is no longer with us . He can no longer impress us with his predictions , which he formulated based on deep comparative historical knowledge , exercise of judgement , and intuition rather than strict standards of scientific verification . <p> Neither can he mesmerise us with his evocative stories , bold predictions , insightful comparisons , interesting paradoxes , and colourful phrases . But all is not lost .
