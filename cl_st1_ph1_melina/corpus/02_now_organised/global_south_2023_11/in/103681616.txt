<h> The Quint DAILY <p> US doughnut chain Krispy Kreme has been involved in a humanitarian fundraiser for Palestinians in Gaza , putting itself on the same side as millions of protesters around the world . This might sound unsurprising in an era when global brands regularly align with popular causes , but virtually none has wanted to touch Gaza . <p> Even Krispy Kreme 's involvement is only through a company programme that is neutral about who it supports . It allows anyone to set up a fundraiser in which donors can make contributions through buying doughnuts . In this case , the fundraisers were the University of California 's Middle Eastern Student Association , who raised around US$24,000 ( ? 19,400 ) for humanitarian aid . <p> Otherwise , the most notable exception to the corporate avoidance of Gaza is the cosmetics brand , Huda Beauty . Set up a decade ago by American-Iraqi beauty influencer Huda Kattan , the Dubai-based company recently pledged US$1 million for humanitarian aid to Gaza . Kattan also publicly backed the freedom of Palestinians , garnering both support and criticism in the process . <p> For brands in general , it @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ wading into such a divisive issue ( though many did express outrage at the original Hamas attack ) . Equally , however , there 's so much global support for a ceasefire that companies could garner a lot of goodwill from taking a stance . <p> Here are some potentially useful lessons from previous examples of brand activism. <h> 1 . Nike <p> The US footwear group has a history of supporting race-related causes . In 2016 , it released a " Dream Crazy " advert starring former NFL player Colin Kaepernick . Kaepernick 's career had floundered over his refusal to stand up for the American national anthem before games , instead taking a knee to protest racism and police brutality . Nike 's ad sparked both praise and calls for a boycott , but sales rose in the weeks that followed . <p> Nike also responded to the killing of George Floyd in 2020 with an online ad entitled " Do n't Do It " . The company 's general support for Black Lives Matter has enhanced its reputation and brand value . Patagonia <p> Patagonia is another company with a history of taking positions on contentious social and political issues . Following the US supreme court 's decision to overturn Roe vs Wade in 2022 , the California-based outdoor-clothing company announced it would cover the travel expenses of employees going outside the US for abortions . It also said it would pay bail support for employees arrested for protesting the decision . <p> It is ranked the most respected brand in the US , having learned to act on its values and donating heavily to social causes . The brand was valued at over US$3 billion in 2022 . Nonetheless , there are no signs of the US government forcing states to make abortion legal . <h> 3 . The effect of the Assange protest would be difficult to disentangle from a concurrent controversy : a fourth of July tweet by the company calling on the US to give back stolen indigenous lands . This prompted boycott calls from conservatives . <p> Other Ben &amp; Jerry 's causes include racial justice , democratic corruption and global warming . Yet it remains the leading ice-cream brand in the UK and US . <p> Cohen and his co-founder , Jerry Greenfield , have also been outspoken about Israel 's treatment of the Palestinians . The Takeaway <p> Clearly , activism executed effectively can benefit a brand @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ are in it for the long haul . Being seen to reliably support causes despite the risks can develop strong bonds with customers . <p> Contrast this with Bud Light 's recent own-goal over supporting the trans movement . This was new territory for the brand and out of step with its middle-American positioning . It led to a conservative boycott that saw sales drop 30% and the company losing its top spot in the US beer market . <p> The dangers could be similar for many companies backing Gaza , but equally the size of global protests suggests that remaining neutral is increasingly risky too . Companies that do decide to take a stand should n't apologise for any pushback , since it only makes them look inconsistent . Brands like Nike and Patagonia are seen as transparent and authentic through standing firm on a supported cause and making it part of their internal values . <p> On the other hand , it would be very unwise for companies to try and use Gaza for commercial gain . That may lead people to question the authenticity of its support @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the long run . <p> One other lesson to stress is that identifying clear goals from this kind of activism is not easy . It is bound to raise awareness , but may do little to change government policies . Of the examples above , only Nike has been on the side of genuine progress , and this would very likely have happened without the company 's support . In other words , brands may well be rewarded for putting their heads above the parapet , but the political benefits are much more debatable . <p> ( This is an opinion piece and the views expressed above are the author 's own .
