<h> The art of resistance <p> What is the value to a society of its dissenting artistic culture ? This question has been on my mind as I 've watched the debate about Arts Council England 's recent updates to its guidance for National Portfolio Organisations ( NPOs ) around managing reputational risk . Many have echoed the sentiments of English PEN , which expressed concern on Twitter about " the chilling effect ( the guidance ) may have on artistic expression " . It 's a discussion that concerns me both as the leader of an NPO , and the author of a forthcoming book about arts and political resistance . <p> I wrote Acts of Resistance because I believe deeply that the arts really can shape our societies for the better -- often by expressing dissent . Over the last few weeks , we 've had compelling evidence of this . " Mr Bates vs the Post Office " is an ITV drama about the Horizon Post Office scandal , which saw over 900 people who ran post offices falsely accused and prosecuted for fraud and theft by one of Britain 's most trusted institutions . Meanwhile former c.e.o . of the Post Office , Paula Vennells , has handed back her CBE , in response to a petition with more than a million signatures demanding just that . <p> Through my research , I have encountered so many cases like this , where an artwork has had a genuine impact on the legal , political and economic structures that govern societies . Take Edward Abbey 's novel The Monkey Wrench Gang , which inspired the 1990s protest movement that led to the cancellation of 300 road-building schemes across the UK ; or the Ukamau Group 's " Blood of the Condor " , which led to the American Peace Corps being expelled from Bolivia over the film 's revelation that they were sterilising Indigenous women without their consent . Instead , it recognises it as an act of love <p> Yet I 've been struck , as well , that many political artists persist regardless of whether their work is likely to make this kind of legible change . They do it anyway , because creating art can be a means to build resilience and forge solidarity with others who share your outlook and will support you . It can be a way to reaffirm your identity , particularly in the face of forces that seek to undermine it . In certain contexts , simply to feel the joy of creativity can be a powerful form of resistance . <p> I 've been incredibly moved by the persistence of many artists and writers living under siege in Gaza . While the bravery to keep creating , even under the falling bombs , is remarkable , the stories of these artists and writers are salutary for those of us in safer countries too , because they provide a reminder that protecting free creative expression is intimately connected with upholding human dignity . <p> The troubling fact is that these reasons for valuing a dissenting artistic culture are the very reasons a state might wish to suppress such expression . Ironically , censorship is the very best evidence for the political efficacy of the arts , and authoritarian states often seem to believe in this even more than some artists do . Why , otherwise , did they blow up the Buddhas in Bimiyan , outlaw music in Mali , imprison Pussy Riot ?
