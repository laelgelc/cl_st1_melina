There has already been a 42% drop in trade volume through the Suez Canal as a result of the attacks in the Red Sea . <p> " This is due to the political dimension that informs the Houthis ' aggression : their as yet unstated demands in Yemen and for Israel to end the war in Gaza , but @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ this case by Iran , " wrote Murphy . <p> " More importantly still , Somali piracy and Houthi harassment are symptoms of a Red Sea region that overflows with inter-state tensions and willing proxies . The Houthis ' current campaign thus could presage further disruptions in the region . " <p> Red Sea rivalries <p> Ongoing rivalries among key players , including Egypt and Turkey , in the Gulf region have resulted in strategic manoeuvring within the Horn of Africa . " Political fragmentation , shifting alliances , and ready recourse to proxy conflict characterises relations between Horn of Africa states , " wrote Murphy . <p> There has been a scramble for naval bases , surveillance posts and commercial ports along the African coast of the Red Sea , an effort driven by the 2014 Yemeni conflict between Houthi rebels and the Saudi-backed government . <p> Saudi Arabia , backed by allies like the United Arab Emirates ( UAE ) , sought operational hubs on the African side of the Red Sea to support military operations in Yemen . Concurrently , Turkey and Qatar pursued the development of Suakin port in Sudan to enhance their naval presence . <p> The strategic significance of the Red Sea has also attracted global powers like Russia , China , France and the US . <p> " This heightened interest has led to increased attention from European and US policymakers regarding the potential threats posed by the Red Sea . China 's establishment of a base in Djibouti , coupled with Russia 's interest in a naval base at Port Sudan , has contributed to a consensus in Western circles that a volatile situation is developing in the region , " wrote Murphy . <p> Global powers <p> The Houthi attacks in the Red Sea , he said , demonstrate " the massive disruptive potential of some geopolitical minnows when it comes to maritime trade @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . But a rejection of outside powers managing Red Sea affairs proved to be the only unifying driver among the regional hegemons , " according to Murphy . <p> This has led to Chinese involvement being , potentially , key to resolving the situation . <p> " In the unipolar era , the US may have been the natural option .
