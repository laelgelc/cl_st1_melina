The courts would strike down speech codes at a number of public universities as violations of the First Amendment . Still , be the school public or private , including both Harvard and Penn , wherever these attempts to limit speech survived , they did so in an uneasy , even paradoxical coexistence with policies that either explicitly or implicitly invoked the First Amendment , which strictly forbids any abridgment of the freedom of speech . <p> Proponents of speech codes were looking to shelter minorities from the abuse of free speech protections by others . There was little apparent concern that these protections might also be weaponized by one student minority against another . <p> Yet that is the precisely the issue now at hand on a number of American campuses .
