There are two key types of co-ownership that you should be familiar with : <p> Joint tenancy : An ownership structure where each co-owner owns an equal share of the property . For example , two friends may have a 50-50 joint tenancy agreement on a single two-bedroom home . <p> Tenancy in common : In this ownership structure , co-owners may own a certain percentage of the property , usually depending on their financial contribution . <p> In a joint tenancy agreement , each co-owner has the right of survivorship , which means that if they pass away @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the other co-owner(s) . In this model , each co-owner can sell or pass their share of the property on at any time . Their share of the property does not automatically go to the other co-owners if they pass away . <h> Mortgages and financial planning <p> While obtaining a joint mortgage can certainly make home ownership more affordable for all parties involved , it also involves more paperwork , as the bank must do homework on multiple mortgage applicants instead of a single buyer . <p> Before agreeing to anything , you and your friends should all get pre-approved for a mortgage to ensure that your credit and income history wo n't be a barrier once the ball starts rolling . <h> Benefits of co-owning a house with friends <p> Owning a home among friends can come with a lot of benefits , including : <p> Your individual share of housing @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ owner <p> Your housing costs may be considerably cheaper than renting a house among friends <p> You 'll have the opportunity to build equity in the property and may be able to sell it for a profit in the future <p> You get to live with the people you enjoy being with the most ( hopefully ) <h> Drawbacks of co-ownership <p> However , there are also some drawbacks and potential problems that could arise , such as : <p> Each co-owner will need to get approved for the mortgage ( this could be a problem if one co-owner has below-average credit or a checkered income history ) <p> Potential for disagreements in the home <p> Selling the home ( or your share of it ) may be more difficult compared to if you were the sole owner <h> Things to consider before buying a house with friends <p> If handled correctly , buying a home with your friends can be a great idea -- especially if all parties involved agree to treat the arrangement fairly and with mutual respect . <p> Here are some things you should think and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <h> Shared property responsibilities <p> Most homes and properties require maintenance . Aside from regular maintenance like landscaping and house washing , you 'll also need to consider periodic ( and often costly ) repairs such as replacing the roof , buying new appliances , or electrical/plumbing repairs . <p> When these issues arise , everybody needs to be on the same page regarding how quickly the issue is fixed and/or how often maintenance items are performed . <p> For example , partner A agrees to split landscaping duties with partner B , and if both partners are unable to do so , they agree to split the cost of hiring a professional landscape maintenance company . <h> Conflict resolution strategies <p> Even the best of friends can become divided when living in close proximity to one another .
