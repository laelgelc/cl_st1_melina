<p> The Royal Ontario Museum 's new show has arrived just in time for the 80th anniversary of the liberation of a concentration camp where 1.1 million men , women and children were murdered , almost all of them Jews . <p> Its only Canadian stop will be in Toronto , at a time when knowledge about the Holocaust is fading , and demonstrators have yelled ' Go back to Europe ' at Jewish people during protests against Israel . <p> The day before the official opening on Jan. 10 , a lone protester marched in front of the ROM , with a sign that read ' Gaza . Not Long Ago . Not Far Away , ' satirizing the name of the show . <p> It 's a fraught moment to launch a multi-million-dollar show about the Holocaust , but the museum CEO and director Josh Basseches says the time is right . <p> The museum surveyed the public before committing to the show and found interest was as high as a blockbuster dedicated to dinosaurs , Basseches said in an interview with The CJN . <p> " If @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 7 , " he said , referring to the Hamas attacks on Israel in 2023 , and the subsequent , ongoing war in Gaza . <p> " It 's a sobering exhibit . Having the opportunity to understand about an event like this at a place like the ROM , which feels for many as a safe , comfortable place to be , makes it something that people want to do , " he said . <p> " The treatment is quite sensitive , it avoids sensationalism . It does n't have some of the most visceral and disturbing issues , because we wanted to make this an exhibition that could engage people of a wide variety of ages , and from any sort of different background ... As we move further from the Holocaust , whether you are Jewish , or not Jewish , the idea of being a witness , of being aware of an understanding of what happened , feels to me profoundly important . " <p> Between 325,000 and 350,000 people are expected to visit this gallery in the months ahead , Basseches said .
