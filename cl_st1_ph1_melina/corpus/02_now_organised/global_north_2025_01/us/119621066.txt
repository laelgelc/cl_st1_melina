<h> The Gorgeous , Unglamorous Work of Freedom <p> Freedom is a word that turns up with embarrassing frequency in rock-and-roll songs . How we love to free-associate about freedom . On occasion , we 're good for a " Chimes of Freedom " ( at least Bob Dylan is ) , but if we 're honest , the freedom musicians are most interested in is our own . <p> The reason I am climbing on this slippery soapbox called " freedom " today is that I 'm being given a presidential medal by that name -- an honor I 'm receiving mainly for the work of others , among them my bandmates and our fellow activists -- and it 's got me thinking again about the subject . When we rock stars talk about freedom , we more often mean libertinism than liberation , but growing up in the Ireland of the 1960s , the latter had its place too . We were mad for freedoms we did n't have : political freedom , religious freedom , and ( most definitely ) sexual freedom . <p> Rock and roll @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , an international language of liberation . That 's how insufferable we were . <p> Outside the studio , it felt like freedom was unstoppable . In Europe , the generation before us had paid for our freedom in blood . We promised we would never forget . Yes , freedom was stalled here , suppressed there , but not forever , we thought . Walls were made to tumble . Bobby Shriver , Jamie Drummond , Lucy Matthew , and I launched One and ( Red ) to help lift that death sentence . That 's freedom right there . <p> So if freedom swaggered -- or even sometimes staggered , carrying a drink and smoking a cheroot -- we kind of forgave freedom , because it got results . <p> But where are we now , as my hero David Bowie sang ?
