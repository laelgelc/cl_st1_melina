<p> Hostages Romi Gonen , Emily Damari , and Doron Steinbrecher , who were released from Hamas custody on Sunday , reported that they had been held in a U.N . refugee camp at some point during their captivity , according to Israel 's Channel 13 . Details were not given as to what camps they were held in , when , or for how long . <p> The revelation is the latest blow to the position of the U.N . Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East , which has faced attacks against its credibility throughout the war in Gaza . <p> The Washington Examiner reached out to the UNRWA for comment . <p> The UNRWA operates eight refugee camps in the Gaza Strip , established in 1948-1949 after the Israeli War of Independence . Most are n't recognizable as " camps " in the traditional sense , having developed over time into urban establishments . Several have populations numbering over 100,000 people . The majority of Gazans are registered as refugees . <p> The UNRWA clarifies on its website that it does n't administer @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Hamas authorities . It employs 13,000 staff across over 300 installations to provide education , medical attention , and other humanitarian aid to locals . <p> Israel has accused some UNRWA staff members of being members of Hamas , alleging last year that 19 staffers were members of the militant group . The agency fired nine staffers after finding links with Hamas , though they found insufficient evidence in the 10 other cases . <p> Gonen , Damari , and Steinbrecher were held in captivity for 471 days . The current ceasefire deal aims to release all remaining hostages over many weeks .
