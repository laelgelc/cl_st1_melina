<h> Celtic Connection 's GRIT Orchestra shows Scotland 's culture potential <p> Glasgow Royal Concert Hall had many returning audience members and loyal Bennett fans ( Image : Laura Pollock ) <p> GRIT orchestra gave Glasgow 's Celtic Connections a performance on Saturday night which was 10 years on from their first show at the festival in 2015 , all in homage to the late Martyn Bennett . <p> This show commemorated 20 years since Bennett 's passing , celebrating his life and legacy in a year that has seen his seminal album Grit winning 2024 's Scottish Modern Classic Award Between each piece , conductor Greg Lawson gave the audience insight into Bennett 's craft , his being , the orchestra and its workings . <p> At times , you 'd have been forgiven for thinking there was a comedy show playing at the Royal Concert Hall , with laughter radiating through the space at Lawson 's storytelling wit . <p> Combined with the orchestra 's renditions of Bennett 's creations , brilliance is the only way to describe the pairing . <p> But Lawson ( above ) is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ each time to conjure an enthralling incarnation of Bennett 's score , from several albums , including Bothy Culture ( 1997 ) and of course , Grit ( 2003 ) . The latter was recorded while Bennett was ill and battling cancer , unable to play his instruments . He passed away in 2005 , aged 33 . <p> The audience last night was grateful to experience his legacy , being part of the lasting and loyal fanbase to the genre Bennet could be considered the father of . <p> To the evolution of modern Celtic fusion , Bennett gifted an immense amount of material and the orchestra has adapted for stage . Starting off with Shputnik In Glenshiel , you were transported to Glensheil and the arrangement towards the end had such a flow , the woman beside me made a noise of appreciation I can only compare to having a bite of a silky-smooth dessert mousse . <p> We had tipped our view over the looking glass of Scottish beats and culture and were free-falling through it with the fiddles and cello bass carrying us beyond . Lawson pointed to out the complexity of translating Bennet 's work as the man had put it all together alone , in his room , wearing a pair of headphones and playing each instrument . <p> Bennett 's comedy shined through in Aye ? , which features the word " aye " muttered in response to his mother on a phone call Bennett accidentally recorded . It humoured him and he put the recording to a transcending tune that led us , with singer Innes Watson holding a prop phone muttering intermittent ayes , into the hills of Scotland . <p> With each song and each story , Lawson took the room further and further into a state of vulnerability , honesty and release . The pipes were the driver of the arrangement , with the drums and bass giving over a pulsing sense of injustice and permission to feel an anger at the world 's treatment of children and innocents in war .
