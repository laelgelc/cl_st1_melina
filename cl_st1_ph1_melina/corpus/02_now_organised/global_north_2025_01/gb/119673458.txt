<h> A bloody civil war risks turning into an international crisis <p> It is an African tragedy . Some 150,000 have died in Sudan 's fighting that has lasted barely two years . Eleven million people have been driven out of their homes . Cholera , rape , ethnic cleansing and torture have become an everyday reality . Some 26 million are in a hunger crisis and an enduring , nationwide famine seems certain , given the ? damage to irrigation systems . <p> It has not been so bad since the Horn of Africa famine of 1984 , nor has the continent seen such savagery since the genocide in Rwanda in mid 1994 . The Rwandan slaughter at least moved the United Nations general assembly to agree on common responsibility to protect people from mass atrocities . <p> That was regarded as a high point of liberal intervention . Now , it seems , we have reached a nadir of concern . Last September some of the world 's richest powers convened at the UN to address the galloping crisis in Sudan .
