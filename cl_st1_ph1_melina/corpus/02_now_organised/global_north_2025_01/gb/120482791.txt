<h> Glasgow 's Jews have been made to feel unwelcome <h> Glasgow 's Jews have been made to feel unwelcome <p> Members of the Jewish community in Glasgow gather in Buchanan Street where they held a vigil in memory of those killed in Israel by Hamas militants on October 7 <p> As an uneasy peace at last descends on Palestine my thoughts have been with Glasgow 's much-loved Jewish and Muslim communities . Both have suffered greatly at what their people have had to endure since Hamas tortured and slaughtered more than 1200 people on October 7 , 2023 . <p> Both of these groups have contributed so much to the economy of Glasgow and to its culture that my city would be a far poorer place without them . These communities , whose DNA is as Glaswegian as my own , have also co-existed peacefully with each other and with more than a degree of harmony and warmth . My fervent hope is that they both continue to prosper and to gift their talents and their cultural riches to Glasgow . <p> I think too that the rest of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ their people suffer in Gaza by making it clear to them that they are welcome here and that their presence makes Glasgow a better place . <p> In particular , my heart is with my Jewish friends . For the first time since their families began to arrive here in numbers fleeing the Nazi pogroms and the attempt to wipe them completely from the face of the earth , Glasgow 's Jews were too often made to feel unwelcome in this city this last year or so . <p> The Friends of Israel stall has been a fixture on Buchanan Street for many years , arguing for greater understanding of the serpentine politics of the Middle East and peaceful co-existence within a two-state framework . Some of those who volunteer at this outpost are directly descended from families who were crushed by the Nazi regime . <p> Yet , for more than a year they were made to feel like outcasts once more . I personally witnessed some of them being spat upon , abused and threatened by people whose ignorance of the Middle East was matched only by their @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that events in Gaza had provided an outlet for a dormant hatred of Israel and its right even to exist . The majority of the abusers were n't Muslims either . <p> For the first time too , many of those with whom I spoke began to consider uprooting their families from the city they had come to love and flee somewhere that might make them feel safer . It made me ashamed . <p> Massacre depravity <p> I was also present last October when a few hundred of Glasgow 's Jewish community gathered at Kelvingrove to remember the hostages still being held by Hamas . In time , the full extent of how barbarously they were treated by their captors , will emerge , but we already know that for some the depravity of the October 7 massacre marked their captivity . <p> Yet , even as they held this peaceful vigil they were tormented by what can only be described as a mob of ' protestors ' who refused to leave them in peace even as they mourned their brothers and sisters in captivity . It was to the credit of these Jewish mourners that they resisted the urge to react aggressively and thus avoid what could have been a violent confrontation . <p> Many of the so-called counter-demonstrators had diverted from their own pro-Palestine march elsewhere in the city that day . This was one of many that had proceeded throughout the year in Glasgow , all of them unhindered , even as they defamed Israel and questioned its right to exist . <p> Holy jewel <p> A much more pleasant and uplifting occasion last year occurred when I visited the Garnethill Synagogue , a Grade-A listed Glaswegian architectural jewel that was built 145 years ago . <p> On the day of my visit , community leaders and volunteers were preparing for a mini-pilgrimage that would proceed through the streets of Glasgow a few days later . <p> It would start from @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the city 's main faith groups -- Christians , Jews , Muslims , Hindus , Sikhs and Buddhists -- would all be represented . From there they would visit some of the principal centres of all the faiths and culture which help make Glasgow great . <p> The Garnethill synagogue was the first purpose-built one in Scotland and is home to the Scottish Jewish Heritage Centre and the Scottish Jewish Archives Centre . It 's also home to the little white-walled Scottish Holocaust Study Centre where Adolf Hitler 's attempt to remove all traces of this Holy people from the earth are chronicled in all its Satanic wickedness , from Kristallnacht to the horrors of the Nazi death camps . <p> Yet nowhere in this building were there any traces of anything that spoke of bitterness or revenge . It merely channelled both the grief of an entire people and a yearning to reach out to the rest of us and to educate us so that this evil can never again be summoned . <p> Holocaust history <p> THE Scottish Holocaust Study Centre also chronicled the story of the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Here you learn that one of Glasgow 's great historical attributes is how it provided a warm welcome to this people in the darkest days of their history .
