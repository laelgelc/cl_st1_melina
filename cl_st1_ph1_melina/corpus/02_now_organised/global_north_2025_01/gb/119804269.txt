<p> On September 5 , 1972 , a balaclava-clad figure appeared on a balcony in the athletes ' village of the Munich Olympics , watched by an estimated 900 million people around the world . That 's a quarter of a billion more than saw Neil Armstrong walk on the moon three years earlier . The Palestinian terrorist group Black September had taken members of the Israeli Olympic team hostage and went on to murder 11 of them in a tragedy that tarnished the Games , and would also trigger an earthquake in live news broadcasting . <p> " It was a breaking point in media history , " says Tim Fehlbaum , the Swiss director whose extraordinary new film , September 5 , dramatises the massacre from the point of view of the American journalists covering the biggest story of their lives . They were sports reporters , not news men , working for the ABC network from a studio with a view of the athletes ' village .
