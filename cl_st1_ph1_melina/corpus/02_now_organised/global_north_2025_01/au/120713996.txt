<h> Hand axes in Iraq up to 1.5 million years old <p> Archaeologists have found more than 850 artefacts on the surface of a dried lake bed in Iraq 's Western Desert including hand axes dating to 1.5 million years ago . <p> Credit : Free University of Brussels . <p> The discoveries were made by a team led by Ella Egberts from the Free University of Brussels in Belgium . <p> " The fieldwork was a huge success , " Egberts says in a news release on the university 's website . <p> The team found 7 sites in a 200km2 area dating to the Palaeolithic period ( 3.3 million to 11,700 years ago . ) <p> Among the finds were Early Palaeolithic hand axes , probably 1.5 million years old , as well as reduction flakes from the Middle Palaeolithic . These flakes were produced by ancient humans using a stone tool manufacturing technique known as Levallois . This type of stone knapping was developed about 250,000 to 400,000 years ago . <p> Egbert says that the finds help explain how early humans used the resources around them @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " broader understanding of human evolution and behaviour on the Arabian Peninsula " . <p> Few fossils of the ancient humans who lived on the Arabian Peninsula have survived , making it difficult to pin down exactly which hominin species was responsible for the stone tools found by Egbert 's team . <p> Homo erectus lived 2 million to 110,000 years ago and were the first early humans to migrate out of Africa .
