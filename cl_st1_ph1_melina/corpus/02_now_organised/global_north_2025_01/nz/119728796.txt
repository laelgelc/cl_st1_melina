As far as commodity trade is concerned , this fear may be exaggerated . <p> The US share of global imports has decreased to about 13% from around 20% in 2000 . Further , as brought out in a report by the Geneva-based Global Trade Alert , in 2022 only a few countries had a very high share of total national goods exports bound for the US and a high level of dependence on exports as a share of national GDP . <p> A closed US market will severely impact a country 's GDP if it ranks high on both those counts . Only Cambodia and Nicaragua fall in this category , though countries like Canada and Mexico are highly dependent on exports to the US . <p> Countries like China and Germany are not . <p> Additionally , for most countries non-US exports grew faster than US exports between 2012 and 2022 . Economics 101 tells us a country 's saving-investment gap equals its trade balance , service trade , net factor income from abroad and international transfers , meaning remittances . <p> If the US commodity trade balance improves due to raised tariffs , then , unless long-term factors like investment and savings behaviours change , the US services trade balance must deteriorate . This is a clear indication that he wants to return the US to the unilateralism of the 1950s , a stance appealing to the voters who supported his Make America Great Again platform . <p> Even before Trump , it was clear that we are now in an era of geo-economics rather than geopolitics . This shift began as far back as the 1990s with the end of the Cold War , the declining importance of oil as an economic weapon for developing countries and the increasing significance of global trade , marked by the establishment of the World Trade Organisation in 1995 . <p> Economic issues now often take precedence over political ones . <p> For instance , in the Ukraine war , Russia was persuaded to lift its blockade on Ukraine 's grain trade to prevent a global surge in food prices , which @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the export of Russian food and fertilisers was facilitated . This ensured continued global oil supplies . <p> Similarly , as Israel escalated its conflict with Iran , the US , among others , urged Israel to avoid targeting nuclear and oil facilities . Strikes on oil infrastructure would have triggered another round of global inflation at a time when the global economic outlook is already bleak , if not outright recessionary . <p> In the world after Trump 's takeover , not much may change , although multilateralism will suffer a major setback . The consequences of this need not be all bad , as several regional political conflicts have their origins in the multilateral ideological struggles of the Cold War era .
