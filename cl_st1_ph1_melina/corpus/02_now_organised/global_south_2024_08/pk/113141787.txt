<p> Oil prices fell by around 1% on Wednesday due to persistent concerns over Chinese demand and elevated risks of a broader economic slowdown , though losses were capped by supply risks in the Middle East and Libya . <p> Brent crude futures were down 76 cents , or 0.96% , at $78.79 a barrel by 1320 GMT . U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude futures fell 77 cents , or 1.02% , to $74.76 . <p> Prices lost more than 2% on Tuesday , having gained 7% over the previous three days to more than $81 a barrel for Brent and $77 for WTI . <p> " Supply risks in Libya have come to the fore but market participants seem sanguine ... demand in China remains weak and the expected second-half rebound has yet to show credible signs of commencing , " Amarpreet Singh , an analyst at Barclays , said in a note . <p> While a decline in U.S. oil and fuel inventories last week supported prices , the potential loss of Libyan oil output and the possible expansion of the Israel-Gaza conflict to include Iranian-backed militants from Hezbollah in Lebanon remain the largest risks to oil @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as a dispute continues between rival government factions over control of the central bank and oil revenue .
