<p> SYDNEY Aug 15 -- The Melbourne Symphony Orchestra said today it had made a mistake in cancelling a performance by a celebrated British-Australian pianist over remarks criticising Israel 's military campaign in Gaza . <p> At a Sunday concert , British-Australian pianist Jayson Gillham dedicated a piece to " the journalists of Gaza " -- some of whom , he said , had been the victims of " targeted assassinations " by Israeli forces . <p> The Melbourne Symphony Orchestra said the use of their stage " for expressing personal views " was inappropriate and abruptly cancelled his Thursday appearance . <p> The orchestra has described Gillham , who previously performed a benefit concert for the Palestine Children 's Relief Fund , as " one of the finest pianists of his generation " . <p> After a fierce public backlash , the orchestra today apologised for its " error " in " asking Jayson to step back from his performance " . <p> But it maintained " that a concert platform is not an appropriate stage for political comment " , and said today 's performance was still being cancelled due to " safety " concerns . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ accused the orchestra of " over-reach " , adding " music and art exist in a political and social context " . <p> The Committee to Protect Journalists has reported that 113 journalists have been killed since the war in Gaza began 10 months ago . <p> Israel has previously referred to journalists killed in strikes as " terrorists " , and bans international reporters from entering Gaza independently . <p> The Israeli military has said it " does not target journalists as such " but has the right to target militant members of Hamas -- the organisation that spearheaded the bloody October 7 attacks on Israel that triggered the war . -- AFP
