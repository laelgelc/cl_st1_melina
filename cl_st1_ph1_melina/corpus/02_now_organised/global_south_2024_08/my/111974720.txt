<p> COPENHAGEN , Aug 7 -- Danish shipping giant Maersk posted today a 45-per cent fall in net profit in the second quarter , as supply chain disruptions due to the Red Sea crisis led to higher operating costs . <p> Months of attacks by Yemen 's Iran-backed Huthis have prompted some shipping companies to detour around southern Africa to avoid the Red Sea route -- which normally carries about 12 per cent of global trade . <p> The Yemeni rebels have been launching drones and missiles at shipping in the Red Sea since last November , saying they are acting in solidarity with Palestinians during the Gaza war . <p> In the April to June period , Maersk posted a net profit of US$798 million ( RM3.58 billion ) , while sales dipped to US$12.77 billion , both slightly lower than analysts ' forecasts . <p> Operating profit also declined , by 26 per cent to US$2.14 billion . <p> Maersk , the world 's second biggest shipping group , no longer transits the Red Sea . <p> " The situation in the Red Sea remains entrenched , which leads to continued pressure on global supply chains . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the year , " Maersk chief executive Vincent Clerc said in a statement . <p> " We have invested in additional equipment in all our businesses to adapt to the situation and continue supporting our customers through the disruptions , " he said .
