<p> Oil prices remained unchanged on Wednesday following a period of declines that saw Brent crude dip to nearly $77 a barrel . <p> The stability comes after decreases driven by persistent concerns about Chinese demand and a reduction in fears of conflict spreading from the Middle East . <p> As of 0806 GMT , Brent crude futures were steady at $77.20 per barrel , while U.S. West Texas Intermediate ( WTI ) crude was slightly lower at $73.10 , down by 7 cents . <p> Brent crude had fallen 6.2% since peaking above $82 the previous Monday , closing at a two-week low of $77.20 by Tuesday 's end . WTI experienced a 7.5% drop over the same period . <p> Oil prices have declined due to ongoing concerns about demand from China , the world 's largest crude importer , and a decrease in fears that escalating conflicts in the Middle East could threaten oil supply . <p> According to figures from the American Petroleum Institute , market sources reported a rise in U.S. crude oil stocks last week . Official U.S. government inventory data is expected to be released on Wednesday at 10:30 a.m. ( 1430 GMT ) . <p> Meanwhile , U.S. Secretary of State Antony Blinken has concluded his visit to the Middle East to facilitate a ceasefire in Gaza . <p> Blinken 's efforts and mediation from Egypt and Qatar have sparked hopes for a U.S. " bridging proposal " that could help narrow the gaps between the warring parties . <p> ING commodities strategists noted that oil prices have been influenced by hopes for a ceasefire between Israel and Hamas and persistent demand concerns . <p> " While weaker Chinese demand has been widely reported , refinery margins globally have been under pressure for much of August , indicating that these demand issues are not limited to China alone , " they said .
