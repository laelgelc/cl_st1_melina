<h> Each of the four 75-year-old Geneva Conventions aims to protect a distinct category of war victim . Judging by the number of deaths of civilians in the Gaza conflict , they are not succeeding . <p> Gaza is reeling after a missile strike launched by the Israeli Defense Forces ( IDF ) targeted a building and mosque inside a school complex in Gaza City on 10 August . The Israeli military said the school was operating as a Hamas command and control post , but the buildings were reportedly also sheltering more than 6,000 displaced people . <p> Palestinian authorities have stated that the attack killed more than 80 people , a figure disputed by the IDF , which claimed that the strike killed 19 fighters , including senior Hamas commanders . <p> 12 August marked the 75th anniversary of the adoption of the four 1949 Geneva Conventions , which remain the core of " international humanitarian law " ( IHL ) . This represents the body of rules under international law that regulates the conduct of war . <p> Each of the four treaties focuses on the protection of a particular category of war victim . Where a school is used for military purposes , however , it can potentially become a lawful military objective . <p> This would be the case if its use makes an effective contribution to military action and if its destruction , capture or neutralisation offers a " definite military advantage " .
