@@1873579 <h> How Bomb Blasts Changed Lives Of Nyanya/Mararaba Residents <p> Immediately after the Nyanya bomb blasts occurred , the federal government quickly drafted the military to mount checkpoints in different entry routes that lead into Abuja from Nasarawa State , making life unbearable for residents living in that axis . Ruth Choji and Blessing Ukemena write on the plights of the people <p> While most Nigerians continue to live and go about their normal businesses , those living within the Nyanya/Mararaba axis of the Federal Capital Territory ( FCT ) have been going through difficult times since the April 14 and May 1 bomb blasts that took lots of innocent lives and destroyed properties running into millions of naira . They are not just afraid of being bombed by terrorists who seemed to have found the area as a soft target , but the fear of fierce-looking security agents is now the beginning of wisdom for them . People living in Nyanya/Mararaba and those coming into Abuja through the Abuja-Keffi Expressway are now subjected to unkind and untold hardship in the name of security checks . Isa Ali , a trader who deals in textile materials , told LEADERSHIP Sunday that " the security men came and went from shop to shop , lifting all our goods and scattering our shops in the name of searching for bombs . Those who tried to complain were beaten and taken away . So many people locked their shops and ran away . The residence of the chief of Nyanya Palace is just about 50 paces from both sites of the bomb blasts . LEADERSHIP Sunday went to find out what security measures have been put in place by the chief and other residents around the vicinity to avert another bomb blast . The chief was said to be indisposed , but his son , who is also the palace secretary , Mr Danladi Gaza , shared his experience with LEADERSHIP Sunday , stating that " on the second bombing , my wife called me and said we should go in and have dinner . As we went in , she dropped the baby and went into the kitchen , and that was when the bomb went off " .
