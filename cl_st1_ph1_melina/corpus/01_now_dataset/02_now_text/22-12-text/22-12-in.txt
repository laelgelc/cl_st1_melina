In 1988 , India became one of the first countries to recognise the newly established state of Palestine . <p> Through these years , India sustained a policy of hyphenating the ties with Israel -- linking them to ties with the Palestinian Authority , wherein a visit of the heads of state to Israel was accompanied by a visit to the Palestinian land as well . <p> However , this stance changed under the National Democratic Alliance ( NDA ) government 's second term wherein Prime Minister Narendra Modi in 2017 for the first time visited Israel but skipped a customary stoppage in Palestine . This has come to be described as a policy of " De-Hyphenation . Thus , de-hyphenation simply suggests a tilt towards an ' independent ' foreign policy wherein bilateral relations with Israel are based solely on its own merits , separate from its relationship with the Palestinians . <p> This shift in India 's stance to the conflict has been visible in India 's votes at international fora . Earlier this year , India also abstained on a resolution demanding a probe into Israeli actions in the Gaza Strip at the UN Human Rights Council .
