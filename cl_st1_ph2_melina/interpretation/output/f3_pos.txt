## Interpretation of f3 (positive)

### Preferred label
**Institutional policing and legal-administrative control of pro-Palestinian campus activism**

This pole encodes news discourse in which Palestine solidarity is situated within universities and represented through protest organization, institutional disruption, police intervention, disciplinary action, and legal or immigration enforcement. It is not simply a general “protest” dimension: the vocabulary specifically connects higher education activism to mechanisms through which universities and the state regulate participants.

### Lexical evidence

The loadings contain four closely connected clusters:

- **Higher education and academic status:** *doctoral, graduate, postdoctoral, campus, graduation, student, administrator, faculty, college, university, PhD, academic, exam, commencement, class, institution, school, ceremony*.
- **Pro-Palestinian mobilization and demands:** *pro-Palestinian, encampment, demonstration, demonstrator, protester, protest, activist, organizer, rally, chant, divest,* and *divestment*.
- **Campus confrontation and policing:** *trespassing, barricade, crackdown, detention, arrest, riot, detain, police, officer, gear, spray, violent,* and *incendiary*.
- **Legal, disciplinary, and immigration control:** *customs, ICE, revoke, immigration, visa, deport, deportation, attorney, constitutional, enforcement, federal, agent, hearing, lawyer, judge, homeland,* and *proceeding*.

The academic-calendar vocabulary—especially *spring, exam, graduation, commencement,* and *ceremony*—locates much of this discourse in the spring 2024 encampment wave and its effects on end-of-year university activities. At the same time, the immigration cluster extends the pole beyond the immediate encampments. Terms such as *ICE, visa, revoke, deportation, customs,* and *homeland*, together with academic-status words such as *doctoral* and *postdoctoral*, suggest later reporting about the immigration status, detention, or possible removal of non-citizen students and academics associated with pro-Palestinian activism.

The pole also includes competing legitimations. *Peaceful, speech,* and *constitutional* invoke protest and free-expression rights, while *trespassing, riot, harassment, antisemitic, hate,* and *violent* reproduce security, public-order, and institutional-safety frames. Thus, the pole identifies a discourse arena rather than one consistent editorial stance.

### Evidence from the examples

The excerpts strongly instantiate the campus-protest component of the loading pattern. They repeatedly describe:

- tent **encampments** and occupied or barricaded university spaces;
- demands that universities **divest** from Israel-linked companies;
- police in **riot gear** dismantling camps and making **arrests**;
- university administrators suspending students or calling in police;
- disruption, relocation, or cancellation of **exams, graduation, commencement,** and other ceremonies;
- disputes over whether demonstrations were **peaceful**, unsafe, antisemitic, unlawful, or protected speech.

For example, the MIT and University of Pennsylvania report combines *campus, student, divest, encampment, crackdown, detention, arrest, commencement,* and *riot gear*. The UC Santa Cruz excerpt similarly joins graduate-student activism and academic labor with barricades, police enforcement, arrests, classes, and commencement. The Columbia examples place institutional administration at the center: university leaders request police intervention, disciplinary measures follow, and graduation ceremonies are cancelled or reorganized.

The European example shows that this discourse is not exclusively American: protests in Berlin, Amsterdam, Cambridge, Oxford, Helsinki, and other cities are framed through academic boycotts, campus occupations, police intervention, and university rules. Nevertheless, the sample is dominated by US institutions, especially Columbia, UCLA, MIT, and other elite or nationally prominent universities.

The examples give less direct illustration of the highly loaded immigration vocabulary. Because loadings represent the full corpus, however, that cluster should not be treated as peripheral. It indicates that the broader pole also includes a subsequent phase in which campus activism becomes connected to visa status, detention, deportation, and federal enforcement.

## Groups driving the pole

The strongest robust group is **global_north_2024_05** (mean 6.67), and all ten supplied high-scoring examples come from this group. This is historically coherent with the peak of the US and European university encampment movement, police clearances, and commencement controversies in May 2024. The high SD of 7.83 indicates that the signal is probably concentrated in a subset of especially protest-focused articles rather than evenly distributed across the entire monthly group.

A second major peak occurs in **March 2025**:

- global_north_2025_03: 5.98  
- global_south_2025_03: 4.19

This is followed by relatively elevated April 2025 means:

- global_north_2025_04: 4.01  
- global_south_2025_04: 2.80

Given the immigration and legal-enforcement loadings, this later peak plausibly reflects renewed reporting about federal action against student activists, including visa revocation, detention, deportation proceedings, and constitutional challenges. That interpretation is strongly supported by the full loading list, although it is not directly demonstrated by the May 2024 excerpts.

The spring 2024 trajectory is also visible outside May. Global North scores rise from 1.64 in March to 3.51 in April and 6.67 in May, remaining elevated in June and July. The Global South series shows a parallel but weaker April–May increase, peaking at 2.91 in May before falling to 1.19 in June. This suggests a temporally shared news event—the international campus-protest wave—with greater concentration in the Global North material.

Across most matched months, Global North means are higher than Global South means. This may reflect the geographical location and institutional salience of the campus events, particularly their concentration in US and European universities, as well as differences in outlet selection, syndicated reporting, or sampling structure. It should not automatically be interpreted as a simple geopolitical difference in attitudes. The high SDs in the peak months also counsel against treating monthly means as uniform group characteristics.

The high global_north_2023_09 mean of 4.04 is unreliable as evidence of a broader early tendency because it is based on only 25 texts; the corresponding Global South group contains only 10. It may reflect a few highly relevant articles or the composition of these very small strata.

## Alternative labels

1. **Campus Palestine protests, institutional discipline, and state enforcement**  
   Broadly captures both the spring 2024 encampment discourse and the later legal/immigration component.

2. **Policing and governance of pro-Palestinian university mobilization**  
   Emphasizes that universities, administrators, police, courts, and immigration authorities are central actors.

3. **From campus encampments to immigration enforcement against activists**  
   Makes the apparent diachronic development explicit, but is somewhat more interpretively specific because the supplied examples illustrate only the first phase.

4. **Pro-Palestinian student activism under institutional and legal sanction**  
   Highlights suspension, arrest, trespass enforcement, visa action, and deportation proceedings, though “sanction” may underrepresent peaceful demonstrations and negotiated divestment agreements.

Overall, **Institutional policing and legal-administrative control of pro-Palestinian campus activism** is the most inclusive label: it accounts equally for the highly coherent May 2024 examples and for the full-corpus immigration, constitutional, and enforcement vocabulary associated with the March–April 2025 peak.
