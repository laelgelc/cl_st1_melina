## Interpretation of Factor f5 (negative pole)

### Preferred label  
**Pre-war border unrest, West Bank raids, and blocked Palestinian statehood**

This pole encodes an event-specific news discourse centred on the Israeli–Palestinian escalation immediately before October 2023. It combines reporting on Gaza border protests and Israeli retaliation with West Bank military raids, restrictions on Palestinian movement and employment, tensions around Jerusalem’s holy sites, and the unrealised prospect of Palestinian statehood.

### Evidence from the loadings

The strongest loadings form several connected lexical clusters:

- **Border protest and retaliation:** *balloon, tire, hurl, incendiary, frontier, barricade, farmland, stepped-up, roil*.  
  These evoke incendiary balloons, burning tyres, thrown explosives, border-fence demonstrations, damage to Israeli farmland, and intensified Israeli raids or airstrikes.

- **Blockade and economic vulnerability:** *laborer, impoverished, unload, rice, beach*.  
  The especially strong loading of *laborer* reflects reporting on Israel’s closure of crossings and suspension of work permits for Palestinians from Gaza. *Impoverished* frames Gaza as an economically constrained or blockaded enclave. Some lower loadings, such as *rice*, may extend this material-economic strand to food and subsistence reporting elsewhere in the corpus.

- **Deferred national and diplomatic resolution:** *hoped-for, handover, mideast, ultranationalist*.  
  In the examples, *hoped-for* occurs in the recurrent formulation “their hoped-for independent state,” while *Mideast* appears in historical summaries of the 1967 war. *Ultranationalist* characterises the Israeli government and its opposition to Palestinian autonomy or territorial concessions.

- **Sacred-site contestation:** *noble, revere, focal*.  
  These words occur in descriptions of the site “revered by Jews as the Temple Mount and Muslims as the Noble Sanctuary,” presented as a recurrent focal point for violence.

The remaining loadings are more heterogeneous. Items such as *tola, riyal, dirham, gold,* and *fluctuate* suggest that the full pole may also contain economic or commodity-reporting material not represented in the excerpts. Likewise, *barge, beach,* and *emaciate* may point to other logistical or humanitarian contexts. Consequently, the pole should not be reduced entirely to the sampled September events, although those events provide its clearest discourse pattern.

### Evidence from the examples

The excerpts consistently instantiate the main lexical clusters:

1. **Gaza frontier protests** involve burning *tires*, protesters who *hurl* explosives, and the launching of incendiary *balloons* toward Israeli *farmland*.
2. **Israeli military responses** include airstrikes, live fire, crossing closures, and *stepped-up* raids in Jenin, Tulkarm, and other West Bank locations.
3. **Economic coercion and precarity** are represented through the barring of thousands of Palestinian *laborers* from the “*impoverished* enclave.” Work permits are explicitly described as instruments for maintaining calm.
4. **Long-running occupation and unrealised sovereignty** appear in historical codas stating that Israel captured the territories in the 1967 *Mideast* war and that Palestinians seek them for a “*hoped-for* independent state.”
5. **Religious and political flashpoints** are conveyed through *revere/revered* and *Noble* Sanctuary, alongside references to nationalist Jewish visits, Palestinian fears about changes to the status quo, and an *ultranationalist* Israeli government.

Thus, the discourse is not simply generic violence reporting. It represents a particular configuration of limited but escalating confrontation: raids, protests, balloons, work-permit restrictions, holy-site tensions, and diplomatic uncertainty before the post-October war became the dominant context.

### Groups driving the pole

Because this is the negative pole, the relevant groups are those with the **lowest mean Factor 5 scores**. The clearest driver is:

- **global_north_2023_09:** mean **0.12**, \(N=25\)

All ten supplied excerpts come from this group, principally US and UK coverage, and their text-level scores range from −6 to −1. Many are Associated Press or Reuters reports, or publications reproducing closely related wire material. This helps explain the repeated lexical bundle involving *balloons, laborers, tires, Noble Sanctuary,* and the “hoped-for independent state.”

The next-lowest group means are:

- **global_north_2023_10:** 1.79  
- **global_south_2023_09:** 2.00, but only \(N=10\)  
- **global_north_2023_11:** 2.01  
- **global_north_2024_10:** 2.07  

The concentration in September 2023 indicates that this is strongly a **pre-October event discourse**. The specific news cycle concerned border demonstrations, incendiary balloons, West Bank raids, work-permit closures, Jewish holidays, and Saudi–Israeli normalisation negotiations. These topics became less central after October 2023 as the scale and vocabulary of reporting changed.

### Temporal and geopolitical tendencies

The dominant tendency is temporal rather than simply geopolitical. September 2023 is the principal locus of the pole, especially in the Global North material. In most subsequent months, means move farther from the negative pole, with particularly high scores in January and November 2024 and in several months of 2025. This suggests that the pole’s lexical scenario was most salient immediately before the October 2023 rupture.

Global North means are lower than Global South means in many corresponding months, which may indicate that Northern reporting more often retained this wire-service style of episodic security coverage, historical background, and holy-site framing. Nevertheless, this should be treated cautiously because:

- the September samples are very small, especially Global South September (\(N=10\));
- all supplied examples come from the Global North September group;
- repeated AP-derived formulations may amplify a source or syndication effect;
- the Global South group sizes are often smaller and more variable.

The pattern may therefore reflect a mixture of genuine geopolitical framing, the historical salience of particular events, and corpus composition.

## Alternative labels

1. **Gaza border protest and West Bank raid discourse**  
   A precise label for the most recurrent event vocabulary, especially *balloon, tire, hurl, frontier, farmland,* and *stepped-up*.

2. **Blockade, border escalation, and holy-site tension**  
   Captures the connection among Gaza’s economic isolation, labour-permit closures, frontier violence, and Temple Mount/Noble Sanctuary contestation.

3. **Pre-October 2023 low-intensity escalation discourse**  
   Emphasises the pole’s strong temporal localisation and its focus on recurrent raids, protests, airstrikes, and restrictions before the later full-scale war.

4. **Episodic conflict reporting with deferred Palestinian sovereignty**  
   Gives greater prominence to *hoped-for*, *Mideast*, and *ultranationalist*, and to the examples’ recurring historical summaries of occupation and unrealised statehood.

Of these, **“Pre-war border unrest, West Bank raids, and blocked Palestinian statehood”** best accommodates both the dominant event vocabulary and the broader political framing found across the excerpts.
