## Recommended label

### **Episodic humanitarian-crisis and conflict-operations reporting**

This label best captures the combination of detailed reporting on civilian harm, rescue activity, aid delivery, detention and other concrete wartime operations.

### Justification

The excerpts consistently report **specific, recent episodes of violence and humanitarian deterioration**: pre-dawn strikes, bodies recovered from rubble, rescuers treating casualties, damaged hospitals, famine warnings, aid blockades and renewed military operations. The recurrent loading word **rescuer** foregrounds civil-defence and emergency-response testimony, while **pre-dawn** gives attacks precise temporal and eyewitness immediacy. **Two-month** repeatedly anchors these events to the collapse of the January–March 2025 ceasefire or to the duration of the aid blockade. Together, these features produce a highly event-centred form of crisis reporting.

The wider loading set supports this interpretation but shows that the pole is broader than the sampled air-strike stories. Humanitarian and emergency vocabulary includes **caregiver, rescuer, medic, desalination, eyewitness** and **forensic**. Operational and coercive processes are represented by **militarise, command-and-control, detention, detain, detainee, deport** and **deportation**. Aid-distribution terms such as **GHF** and **Israeli-backed** are especially salient to the controversy surrounding the Gaza Humanitarian Foundation in 2025. Meanwhile, **flotilla, handover, hostage-prisoner, batch** and various nationality labels point to concrete episodes involving aid missions, releases, detainees and foreign nationals.

Thus, the pole appears to encode reporting through **particular incidents, operational arrangements and affected individuals**, rather than through a single abstract political theme. Some loadings—such as **postdoctoral, doctoral, PhD, yacht, selfie, wireless** and **newsfeed**—do not occur in the supplied examples and form smaller, heterogeneous event-specific clusters. They caution against defining the pole solely as “air-strike reporting.” The more defensible generalisation is detailed episodic reporting on the human and logistical manifestations of the conflict.

## Groups driving the pole

The clearest driver is **global_south_2025_05**. It has the lowest mean score in the table, **0.191**, and all ten high-scoring negative-pole examples come from this group, including outlets from Pakistan, South Africa, India and Kenya. Although the group mean remains positive, individual texts reach negative scores of −2 and −3; the pole should therefore be understood as strongly represented in a subset of May 2025 Global South articles rather than as characterising every article in that group.

This concentration is historically interpretable. May 2025 saw:

- the aftermath of the collapsed two-month ceasefire;
- an intensified Israeli ground and air campaign;
- the prolonged aid blockade and mounting famine warnings;
- debates over an Israeli-backed aid-distribution mechanism;
- extensive civil-defence reporting of civilian casualties.

These developments closely activate both the excerpt vocabulary and highly weighted items such as **GHF** and **Israeli-backed**.

A broader diachronic tendency is also visible. Global South means are relatively low across early 2025—especially **March, February, April, June**, and most markedly **May**. Global North scores likewise decline from December 2024 into 2025, reaching their lowest later-period value in **June 2025**. This suggests that the discourse becomes more associated with the negative pole in the later phase of the war, particularly in Global South reporting. However, the North/South contrast should be treated cautiously: group sizes vary, the means are all above zero, and the excerpts may partly reflect repeated circulation of AFP-style reports across several outlets.

## Other viable labels

1. **Civilian-casualty, rescue and aid-blockade reporting**  
   A more specific label closely matching the excerpts and loadings such as **rescuer, pre-dawn, medic, GHF** and **Israeli-backed**.

2. **On-the-ground humanitarian emergency reporting**  
   Emphasises civil-defence sources, eyewitness detail, hospitals, casualties, hunger and damaged infrastructure.

3. **Concrete wartime operations and humanitarian logistics**  
   Better reflects the full loading list, including aid distribution, detention, deportation, hostage handovers, flotillas and command structures.

Of these, **Episodic humanitarian-crisis and conflict-operations reporting** is the strongest overall label because it accommodates both the tightly focused May 2025 casualty narratives and the broader, more heterogeneous operational vocabulary found in the full-corpus loadings.
