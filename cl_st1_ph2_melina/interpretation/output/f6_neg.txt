## Interpretation of f6 (negative pole)

This pole is best interpreted as **event-specific emergency-response discourse**, especially reporting on **war-zone public health, humanitarian access, and civilian crisis management**. It also contains a secondary cluster concerning **technologically mediated attacks and their aftermath**, suggesting that the factor is partly organised around highly specific news events rather than a single general register.

### Loading evidence

The clearest and strongest lexical cluster is medical and epidemiological:

- *vaccinate* (-.63)
- *vaccination* (-.54)
- *virus* (-.51)
- *vaccine* (-.49)
- *polio* (-.45)
- *dose* (-.43)
- *healthcare* (-.40)
- *caregiver* (-.34)

These words encode a discourse of **disease containment and organised humanitarian intervention**. The vocabulary foregrounds practical procedures—vaccinating children, administering doses, providing healthcare—and therefore presents the conflict through the management of immediate civilian health risks.

A second event-specific cluster includes:

- *walkie-talkie*, *hand-held*, *wireless*, *pager*
- *detonation*
- *Taiwanese*

This likely indexes reporting surrounding the September 2024 pager and communications-device explosions in Lebanon and the wider regional conflict. Although this theme is not represented in the supplied excerpts, the loadings cover the full corpus and therefore indicate an associated discourse of **communication technologies transformed into instruments of violence**.

Other words—*rebuilding, residence, coffin, irreparable,* and *caregiver*—extend the pole towards civilian destruction, death and post-attack recovery. Items such as *caretaker, then-foreign, finalize/finalise,* and *reciprocity* point to administrative or diplomatic reporting. The remaining vocabulary, including currencies and some malformed or boilerplate-associated forms such as *nication, advertisment,* and *well-rounded*, makes the pole somewhat lexically heterogeneous. It should therefore not be treated as a purely medical dimension.

## Evidence from the excerpts

The strongest substantive alignment between loadings and examples occurs in the September 2024 texts. Excerpts 006–010 report the Gaza polio campaign, using loading words such as *virus* and *dose*. Their discourse is operational and humanitarian:

- numerical vaccination targets;
- first and second doses;
- vaccination coverage;
- WHO, UNICEF and UNRWA coordination;
- humanitarian pauses;
- access to displaced children;
- damaged roads and health facilities;
- the risk of disease spreading under wartime conditions.

The campaign is repeatedly embedded in continuing airstrikes, displacement and healthcare-system collapse. Vaccination thus becomes more than a medical topic: it provides a framework for presenting war as a barrier to the protection of children and for arguing that pauses, access or a ceasefire are necessary for basic public-health action. This is the strongest basis for interpreting the pole as **war-zone humanitarian health intervention**.

The September 2023 examples broaden the interpretation. Excerpts 001–002 use *caretaker* in reports about Pakistan’s possible recognition of Israel, national and Palestinian interests, and Muslim-state normalisation. These represent a more administrative-diplomatic strand rather than emergency medicine. Because both are near-duplicate reports and the group contains only ten texts, however, *caretaker* may be a locally influential lexical marker rather than the central meaning of the factor.

Excerpts 003–005 concern West Bank raids, restricted healthcare access and Gaza’s economically damaging export blockade. They contain no identified f6 negative loading words, so they offer weaker direct evidence. Semantically, however, they reinforce themes of civilian vulnerability, constrained access, institutional management and the material consequences of occupation and blockade.

The word *well-rounded* in excerpts 009–010 occurs in repeated publisher self-description rather than in the news story itself. It is therefore likely a website-boilerplate marker and should not be used to define the substantive discourse.

## Groups driving the pole

Because this is the negative pole, the relevant groups are those with the **lowest mean scores**, even though all group means remain positive.

The lowest means are:

1. **global_south_2023_09**: 0.200, but only **N = 10**
2. **global_south_2024_09**: 0.382
3. **global_north_2024_09**: 0.495
4. **global_south_2024_10**: 0.505
5. **global_south_2025_02**: 0.518
6. **global_north_2024_10**: 0.561

The September 2023 Global South result must be treated cautiously. Its very small sample and the duplicated Pakistan normalisation stories suggest that its low mean could reflect sampling composition as much as a stable geopolitical discourse.

September 2024 provides much stronger evidence. Both Global South and Global North groups are relatively low, with the Global South somewhat lower, and the examples directly connect this month to the Gaza polio campaign. The device-related loadings also plausibly reflect other highly salient September 2024 events. Thus, this pole appears strongly **event-indexical**, capturing the particular emergency-health and technological-security agenda of that month.

More generally, both North and South show a broadly parallel movement towards lower scores from July to October 2024, with September as the clearest trough. This parallelism suggests that temporal news salience may be more important than a categorical North/South distinction. Global South means are often slightly lower during this period, but the differences are modest and could reflect outlet mix or sampling structure. The very high means in May–June 2025 indicate a strong movement away from this pole at the end of the period.

## Possible labels

### Preferred label
**War-Zone Public Health and Operational Humanitarian Response**

This captures the dominant vaccination, virus, dose and healthcare loadings and the examples’ emphasis on campaign logistics, humanitarian pauses, institutional coordination and access under bombardment.

### Broader alternative
**Event-Specific Emergency Intervention and Civilian Crisis Management**

This accommodates not only vaccination reporting but also healthcare restrictions, economic disruption, rebuilding, civilian loss and administrative responses.

### Alternative emphasising the heterogeneous event cluster
**Humanitarian Health and Technology-Mediated Crisis Reporting**

This preserves the dominant medical interpretation while acknowledging the substantial *pager–wireless–walkie-talkie–detonation* loading cluster.

Overall, the first label is the most defensible from the excerpts, while the broader alternatives better represent the full loading set. The pole should be understood as a partly event-driven dimension centred on **practical emergency intervention amid conflict**, rather than as an exclusively medical topic.
