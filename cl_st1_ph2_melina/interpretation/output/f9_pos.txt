## Recommended label

**Visa-waiver reciprocity, cross-border mobility, and regional-economic administration**

### Alternative labels

- **Administrative mobility and contested equal treatment of Palestinians**
- **Pre-war US–Israel procedural diplomacy and regional economic policy**
- **Visa regulation, economic exchange, and Middle Eastern normalization**

## Interpretation

The positive pole primarily encodes a **procedural and diplomatic discourse about regulating movement between Israel and the United States**, especially Israel’s September 2023 admission to the US Visa Waiver Program. This discourse presents mobility through formal criteria, agreements and administrative mechanisms, while questioning whether Palestinian and Arab Americans will receive reciprocal and equal treatment.

The most coherent loading cluster is:

- **waiver, visa, reciprocity, memorandum, application, requalify**
- **Arab-American, congresswoman, skeptical, anytime**
- **Saudi, veto, Indian**

These words frame Israeli–Palestinian affairs through bureaucratic eligibility, bilateral agreements and regional diplomacy. In the examples, *waiver*, *reciprocity* and *memorandum* refer to the formal conditions governing Israel’s participation in the Visa Waiver Program. *Skeptical* and *Arab-American* introduce a rights-based challenge to official claims of equal access: advocates doubt whether Palestinian Americans will actually be treated like other US citizens. The references to congresswomen Ilhan Omar and Rashida Tlaib similarly connect border administration to alleged discrimination against Arab and Palestinian Americans.

The excerpts strongly substantiate this interpretation. Excerpts 001–003, 005 and 007 all concern:

- visa-free travel for Israelis;
- administrative deadlines and eligibility requirements;
- US–Israeli security cooperation;
- the principle of reciprocal treatment;
- Israel’s differentiated treatment of Palestinian Americans;
- monitoring, suspension and compliance procedures.

Thus, this is not simply a general travel discourse. It is a discourse of **mobility as a negotiated diplomatic privilege**, evaluated through formal compliance but contested on civil-rights grounds.

## Economic and regional component

The pole also contains a conspicuous economic cluster:

- **dirham, riyal, tola, gram, currency, fluctuate, rate**
- **Saudi, Indian, redevelopment, impoverished, reliance**

The highest-scoring Pakistani texts combine the visa-waiver story with currency-exchange and gold-price material. Their very high scores are partly generated by *dirham*, *riyal*, *tola*, *gram*, *currency* and *fluctuate*. Consequently, the economic aspect should not be ignored, because these are among the strongest loadings in the full model. At the same time, the excerpts suggest that some of this co-occurrence results from **concatenated news-page material or source packaging**, rather than from a single internally unified argument about Palestine.

A secondary economic-administrative theme is nevertheless visible elsewhere. Excerpt 004 discusses World Bank assessments, poverty, fiscal constraints and medical permit applications, while excerpt 006 links Gaza work permits and border openings to employment and cash flows. These examples broaden the pole from international visas to the **institutional regulation of Palestinian mobility and material access**.

*Saudi*, together with *cusp*, *veto* and *Indian*, adds a further diplomatic-economic strand: anticipated Israeli–Saudi normalization, US-mediated agreements and proposed trade infrastructure linking India, the Middle East and Europe. This supports a wider interpretation involving **regional integration through agreements, travel and commerce**.

## Less central security and occupation strand

Some lower loadings—particularly **stepped-up, entrench, impoverished**—occur in reports about military raids, settlement expansion, border closures and Gaza’s economic deprivation. In the examples, *stepped-up* describes both international mediation and Israeli military activity, while *entrench* characterizes the occupation and settlement system. These items suggest that administrative mobility and regional diplomacy are reported against a background of occupation, violence and constrained Palestinian access.

However, this should remain subordinate in the label: the most repeated and internally coherent example pattern concerns visa-waiver eligibility and reciprocity, not military confrontation alone.

## Groups driving the pole

The pole is overwhelmingly associated with **September 2023**, immediately before the post-7 October phase of the corpus:

- **global_south_2023_09: mean 2.80, N = 10**
- **global_north_2023_09: mean 0.24, N = 25**

These are the only groups with positive means, and all supplied high-scoring examples come from them. The Global South September mean is especially high because Pakistani and Indian reports concentrate multiple loading words, including both the visa-waiver vocabulary and appended currency/gold terminology. Its very large SD of 4.76 and very small sample of ten texts indicate that the mean is likely driven by a few unusually high-scoring items—particularly the two Pakistani texts scoring 12 and 11. It should therefore not be treated as evidence of a general Global South discourse without qualification.

The September concentration has a clear historical explanation: Israel’s admission to the US Visa Waiver Program and discussion of Saudi normalization were prominent news events in late September 2023. The pole is consequently best understood as a **temporally specific pre-war diplomatic-administrative formation**, rather than a discourse sustained evenly across the full period.

After September 2023, every monthly mean is negative. Global South groups are generally less negative than corresponding Global North groups, which may indicate somewhat greater retention of economic, administrative or mobility-related reporting in Global South coverage. Nevertheless, no later group positively activates the pole at the aggregate level. Given unequal group sizes and the exceptional size and variability of the September samples, this North/South tendency should be treated as suggestive rather than definitive.

## Overall assessment

The strongest interpretation is **visa-waiver reciprocity and administratively regulated mobility, embedded in US–Israel diplomacy and regional economic normalization**. The pole also captures economic bulletin vocabulary and permit-based access to work and healthcare. Because several high loadings derive from concatenated currency and gold content, it may partly reflect a **September 2023 source or publication-package signature**. A suitably cautious label should therefore retain both the coherent visa/mobility discourse and its regional-economic component.
