## Recommended label

### **Besieged civilian survival and humanitarian-health crisis**

This pole encodes reporting that represents Gaza primarily as a civilian survival emergency produced and intensified by siege, bombardment, displacement, infrastructure destruction, and restricted access to essential supplies. It brings together several closely connected discourses:

- acute hunger, malnutrition, dehydration, and starvation;
- damaged or inaccessible healthcare;
- humanitarian-aid delivery and distribution;
- displacement, shelter, and exposure;
- civilian injury and death amid military operations.

The label captures both the pole’s strongest lexical pattern and the dominant framing of the high-scoring examples.

## Lexical basis

The highest loadings are strongly concentrated in public health and basic survival. These include:

- **Health and disease:** *vaccinate, vaccination, polio, healthcare, health, medical, medicine, patient, surgery, cancer, prevention*;
- **Maternal and neonatal care:** *newborn, incubator, premature, baby, caregiver*;
- **Food insecurity:** *malnutrition, flour, food, hunger, hungry, famine, starvation, meal*;
- **Water and sanitation:** *desalination, dehydration, sewage, water*;
- **Health-system pressure:** *hospital, ambulance, medic, paramedic, facility, emergency, shortage, collapse*.

These terms indicate more than a general “humanitarian” topic. They construct a discourse of bodily vulnerability in which access to food, clean water, vaccination, neonatal care, surgery, and medicine is endangered.

A second lexical cluster concerns relief logistics:

- *truck, convoy, crossing, airdrop, parachute, delivery, distribution, distribute, supply, unload, aid, charity, kitchen,* and *reopen*.

This presents humanitarian survival as dependent on fragile systems of transport, border access, distribution, and institutional provision.

A third cluster places these needs within a coercive military environment:

- *besiege, siege, bombardment, shell, mortar, tank, gunfire, artillery, incursion, offensive, warplane,* and *relentless*.

Civilian and displacement vocabulary—*shelter, refuge, refugee, camp, tent, flee, child, civilian, non-combatant,* and *population*—connects military action directly to humanitarian exposure. Environmental terms such as *winter, cold, temperature, blanket, weather,* and *storm* further extend the pole to precarious living conditions among displaced populations.

Thus, the pole can be understood as the **humanitarianisation of war reporting**: military developments are narrated through their effects on bodies, hospitals, food systems, water infrastructure, shelter, and aid access.

## Evidence from the excerpts

The examples give especially strong expression to the hunger-and-aid component of the broader lexical pattern. Recurrent loading words include *food, flour, water, hunger, starvation, malnutrition, truck, convoy, delivery, distribution,* and *humanitarian*. The texts repeatedly report:

- populations described as close to or already experiencing famine;
- children dying or becoming acutely ill from malnutrition and dehydration;
- aid trucks, maritime corridors, airdrops, and food-distribution points;
- civilians killed or injured while waiting for aid;
- shortages of baby milk, medicine, fuel, and clean water.

For example, excerpts 001–006 connect *starvation* and *food distribution* with convoys, gunfire, Rafah displacement, and impending military operations. This coupling is central to the pole: aid is not presented as routine relief provision, but as an insecure and contested means of survival within an active conflict zone.

The hospital discourse is also prominent. Excerpts 003–005 and 007–008 describe Al-Shifa, Al-Amal, and other facilities as besieged, raided, overcrowded, or only partially functioning. Excerpt 007 most directly realizes the medical loadings, including *newborn, incubator, surgery, patient, ambulance, medicine,* and *emergency*. Hospitals simultaneously function as:

- treatment centres for injured civilians;
- shelters for displaced people;
- sites of shortages and system collapse;
- locations exposed to bombardment, siege, and military raids.

The examples therefore substantiate a discourse of **interlocking humanitarian systems under pressure**: food access, healthcare, water, sanitation, shelter, and transport fail or become dangerous at the same time.

The full loading list also broadens the interpretation beyond the March 2024 excerpts. Strong items such as *desalination, sewage, polio, vaccination, vaccinate, winter,* and *premature* indicate that the corpus-wide pole includes later or differently focused reporting on disease prevention, water and sanitation, cold-weather exposure, and neonatal vulnerability. Consequently, a label restricted to “famine” or “aid delivery” would be too narrow.

## Groups driving the pole

The strongest mean is for **global_south_2024_03** at **10.37**, and all ten supplied high-scoring excerpts come from this group. Their scores of 27–30 make March 2024 Global South reporting the clearest illustrated driver of the pole. This concentration corresponds to the historical salience of:

- escalating famine warnings;
- acute malnutrition in northern Gaza;
- contested aid-distribution deaths;
- maritime and aerial aid initiatives;
- raids on major hospitals;
- the threatened Rafah operation;
- Ramadan-related pressure for a ceasefire and improved aid access.

Other high-scoring Global South groups show that the discourse is not confined to this one month:

- **global_south_2023_12:** 10.09  
- **global_south_2024_02:** 10.05  
- **global_south_2024_05:** 9.37  
- **global_south_2024_06:** 9.61  
- **global_south_2024_12:** 9.47  
- **global_south_2025_05:** 9.69  
- **global_south_2025_06:** 9.21  

This suggests recurring intensification around successive phases of humanitarian deterioration rather than a simple linear trajectory.

Global North reporting also realizes the pole strongly, particularly in:

- **global_north_2024_03:** 9.15  
- **global_north_2023_12:** 9.07  
- **global_north_2024_02:** 8.60  
- **global_north_2024_12:** 8.26  

The shared March 2024 and December peaks suggest that major humanitarian developments generated similar temporal salience across both geopolitical groupings. Nevertheless, Global South means are higher than Global North means in almost every directly comparable month. This may indicate a more sustained or lexically dense Global South emphasis on civilian deprivation, aid, and healthcare. That inference should remain cautious because group sizes differ, the September 2023 strata are very small, standard deviations are substantial, and the corpus is capped rather than demonstrated to be fully balanced.

Both classifications decline around **February 2025**—4.76 in the Global North and 5.10 in the Global South—before rising again, particularly in the Global South in May–June 2025. The broadly parallel movement suggests sensitivity to changing phases of the conflict and humanitarian situation, while the consistently higher Global South scores may also reflect source selection or sampling structure.

## Other viable labels

1. **War-induced deprivation, aid access, and healthcare collapse**  
   Emphasizes the causal association between military conditions and the breakdown of essential civilian systems.

2. **Civilian humanitarian emergency and relief logistics**  
   Gives greater prominence to trucks, convoys, crossings, airdrops, deliveries, and distribution networks.

3. **Humanitarian survival under siege and bombardment**  
   Highlights how hunger, health, shelter, and displacement are embedded in an active military environment.

4. **Gaza’s besieged health, food, and aid infrastructure**  
   A more concrete label foregrounding the three most recurrent institutional domains.

Of these, **Besieged civilian survival and humanitarian-health crisis** is the most inclusive: it accommodates the examples’ strong famine and aid emphasis while also representing the full loading pattern of healthcare, vaccination, neonatal care, sanitation, shelter, displacement, and conflict-related infrastructure collapse.
