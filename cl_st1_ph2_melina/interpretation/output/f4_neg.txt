## Factor f4 (negative pole): interpretation

### Preferred label: **Episodic human-impact and specialised incident reporting**

This pole appears to combine **person-centred accounts of immediate wartime harm** with a broader lexical tendency toward **specific technologies, injuries, measurements, currencies, transport, and other concrete incident details**. It is best understood as an episodic reporting mode rather than a clearly unified ideological position.

### Evidence from the excerpts

The examples, all drawn from `global_north_2023_10`, concentrate on the immediate aftermath of 7 October and the opening Israeli bombardment of Gaza. Recurring discourses include:

- missing people, hostages, and families searching for relatives;
- eyewitness and survivor testimony from kibbutzim and the Nova festival;
- intimate narratives of death, fear, injury, and family trauma;
- Palestinian civilian casualties, infrastructure destruction, and humanitarian deprivation;
- disrupted communications and reliance on phones, Telegram, videos, and social media;
- security repercussions beyond Israel and Gaza.

These are strongly **episodic and individualised** reports. Events are presented through named victims, relatives, workers, survivors, and witnesses rather than primarily through abstract diplomatic or strategic analysis. Emotional immediacy is produced through quotations such as “Where is my child?”, “We’re living a nightmare,” and “I don’t know if my family are living or not living.”

The examples encompass both Israeli and Palestinian suffering. Accordingly, the pole should not be labelled as encoding a single national alignment. Its more consistent feature is the reporting of conflict through **concrete human consequences, unfolding uncertainty, and personal testimony**.

### Evidence from the loadings

The full loading set is considerably more heterogeneous than the excerpts. Several partial lexical clusters can nevertheless be identified:

1. **Explosive and communications technology**  
   *hand-held, wireless, detonation, detonate, battery, selfie, newsfeed, logo, simultaneous*

   This cluster suggests reporting on technologically mediated incidents: mobile or hand-held devices, explosive mechanisms, visual documentation, and the online circulation of information.

2. **Bodily harm and health**  
   *kneecap, bodily, healthcare, prevention, virus, heartbroken, unintentional*

   These items support a human-impact interpretation, especially where reporting focuses on injury, physical consequences, healthcare, or emotional response.

3. **Maritime movement and transport**  
   *voyage, yacht, flotilla, passenger*

   These terms point to specialised event reporting involving travel, aid movement, maritime activism, or transport-related incidents.

4. **Currencies, commodities, and precise measurement**  
   *dirham, riyal, gram, tola, gold, fluctuate*

   This is a distinct economic and measurement-oriented cluster, indicating that some texts at the pole concern highly concrete commercial or regional details rather than the core conflict narrative alone.

5. **International or identity-specific reference**  
   *Taiwanese, Polish, Swedish, congresswoman, athlete, celebrity, commemoration*

   These suggest geographically dispersed, person-specific, and commemorative news stories.

Thus, the loadings support the broader notion of **specialised, concrete event reporting**, but not an exclusively October 2023 or casualty-centred discourse. The factor may gather several low-frequency news domains whose commonality is specificity—particular devices, injuries, people, nationalities, currencies, or modes of transport.

### Relationship between loadings and examples

The connection between the two forms of evidence is limited. Of the listed negative-loading variables, only **one-year** is explicitly identified in the examples. In the first excerpt it appears both in biographical chronology (“enlisted one year ago”) and in an age category (“one-year olds”). None of the stronger loadings, such as *hand-held, Taiwanese, wireless, selfie,* or *detonation*, is identified in the remaining excerpts.

Consequently, the excerpts strongly establish an **early-war human-interest discourse**, while the loadings reveal a wider and more miscellaneous field of specialised incident reporting. The preferred label therefore needs to accommodate both. A narrower label such as “hostage and victim testimony” would fit the examples but would not adequately represent the full loading structure.

## Groups driving the pole

Because this is the negative pole, the most relevant groups are those with the **lowest mean fac4 scores**. No group has a negative mean, so pole membership is relative: the low-scoring groups are closer to the negative pole, not necessarily negative on average.

The lowest means include:

- `global_north_2023_10`: **3.19**
- `global_south_2023_10`: **3.47**
- `global_south_2023_09`: **3.50**, but with only **N=10**
- `global_north_2023_09`: **3.64**, with only **N=25**
- `global_north_2024_10`: **3.78**
- `global_south_2024_10`: **3.84**
- `global_north_2025_06`: **3.85**
- `global_south_2024_01`: **3.97**

The clearest robust association is therefore with **October 2023**, especially Global North reporting. This is reinforced by the fact that all supplied excerpts come from `global_north_2023_10`. The two September groups are also low, but their very small sample sizes make them unreliable as evidence of a general temporal pattern.

October 2023’s position is historically plausible: reporting immediately after 7 October was dominated by breaking-news accounts, eyewitness testimony, missing-person appeals, casualty reports, communication failures, and rapidly developing humanitarian conditions. October 2024 also shows relatively low means in both geopolitical groupings, which may suggest an anniversary-related return to episodic, commemorative, or retrospective incident reporting.

There is no stable Global North–South division. In October 2023 and October 2024, the two regions score quite similarly. Global South means are often somewhat higher during parts of 2024 and especially January–March 2025, meaning those groups are less associated with this negative pole. However, such differences should be interpreted cautiously because group sizes vary and may reflect historical salience, publication mix, or sampling structure as well as discourse preferences.

The very high scores in January–March 2025—particularly in the Global South—indicate that this negative pole is comparatively weak during that period. Scores then fall again by June 2025. The pattern is therefore episodic rather than a smooth diachronic trend.

## Alternative labels

1. **Immediate conflict testimony and concrete incident detail**  
   Captures the eyewitness, hostage, casualty, and family narratives in the examples while recognising the loadings’ emphasis on devices, injuries, transport, and measurements.

2. **Human-interest crisis and mediated-event reporting**  
   Highlights personal suffering and the role of phones, messaging platforms, newsfeeds, selfies, wireless devices, and other media technologies.

3. **Victim-centred breaking-news particularisation**  
   Emphasises the naming of individuals, direct quotation, biographical detail, and focus on specific unfolding events. This fits the examples especially well but is less satisfactory for the economic and maritime loading clusters.

### Overall assessment

**Episodic human-impact and specialised incident reporting** is the most defensible label. The October 2023 examples strongly encode intimate accounts of victims, survivors, missing relatives, civilian destruction, and information uncertainty. The loadings broaden this into a lexically diffuse discourse of concrete, technically or geographically specific incidents. Because most excerpts contain none of the listed loading words, the factor should be treated as a **heterogeneous and provisional discourse dimension**, not as a narrowly coherent thematic category.
