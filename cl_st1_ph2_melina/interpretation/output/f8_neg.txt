## Proposed pole label

### **Syndicated chronological updates on diplomacy, truces, and conflict operations**

A slightly more general alternative is:

### **Time-bounded institutional event-reporting**

A more content-specific, but less comprehensive, option is:

### **Diplomatic-normalisation, hostage, and truce-update discourse**

The first label best captures both the semantic content and the news-production features of the pole.

## Interpretation

This pole represents an **episodic, chronologically organised news discourse reporting discrete diplomatic, military, hostage, and humanitarian developments**. It often presents events through institutional statements, wire-service material, precise durations, and rapidly updated timelines rather than through sustained thematic analysis.

### Evidence from the loadings

Several lexical clusters support this interpretation:

- **Time-bounded sequencing:** *seven-day, week-long, weeklong, six-month, eight-month, nine-month, minute-by-minute, pre-dawn, simultaneous,* and *three-phase*.  
  These forms package developments as defined phases, pauses, operations, or negotiating periods.

- **Military and humanitarian operations:** *command-and-control, anti-ship, airdrop, parachute, pier, tanker, unload, stepped-up,* and *stampede*.  
  These point to operational reporting about combat, aid delivery, transport infrastructure, and battlefield or crowd incidents.

- **Diplomacy, negotiation, and official accountability:** *then-foreign, negotiator, conduit, reprimand, swearing-in, anti-government,* and *highest-ranking*.  
  This cluster suggests reporting centred on officials, diplomatic channels, negotiations, and reactions to particular decisions or errors.

- **Hostages and conflict incidents:** *hostage-prisoner, misidentification, unintentional, trespassing,* and *bodyguard*.  
  These evoke event-specific reporting on exchanges, captivity, security failures, and contested incidents.

- **News-production vocabulary:** *syndicated, newsfeed, publisher,* and *minute-by-minute*.  
  These indicate that the pole partly captures a wire-service or continuously updated news genre.

The loading list is nevertheless noisy. Highly weighted fragments such as *ing, tion, nication, sev, gat,* and *lim* appear to be tokenisation, line-breaking, or source-formatting artefacts. The visible splitting of words such as *normalisa ? tion* in the excerpts supports this explanation. Consequently, f8_neg should not be treated as a purely semantic topic: it also appears sensitive to **source formatting, syndication, and outlet style**.

## Evidence from the excerpts

The examples instantiate two prominent strands of the pole.

### 1. Pre-war diplomatic and administrative developments

The September 2023 examples concern:

- proposed normalisation between Israel and Muslim-majority states;
- Saudi Arabia and the Abraham Accords;
- Pakistan’s position on recognising Israel;
- the diplomatic controversy surrounding Eli Cohen and Libya;
- Israel’s admission to the US Visa Waiver Program;
- unequal treatment of Palestinian and Arab Americans.

The directly attested loading words *conduit* and *reprimand* occur in accounts of damage to Israel–Libya diplomatic communication. *Syndicated* explicitly identifies an Associated Press feed. These examples therefore combine **official diplomacy, administrative policy, criticism, and syndicated reporting**.

The repeated versions of the Cohen–Libya story also suggest that source reuse or republication contributes to the pole. This reinforces a genre-based interpretation rather than indicating an entirely independent discourse in every text.

### 2. War chronology, truces, hostages, and humanitarian operations

The December 2023 examples report:

- the end of a *seven-day* or *week-long* truce;
- the resumption of Israeli bombardment and ground operations;
- displacement, aid shortages, and UN humanitarian warnings;
- hostage releases and captivity;
- Filipino workers affected by the October 7 attacks;
- *minute-by-minute* news updates.

Here, the loading vocabulary directly structures the reports around **duration and event sequence**. In the Filipino survivor story, *Filipino* marks a nationally localised account of the conflict, while *barged* describes the militants’ violent entry into a home. Thus, even apparently miscellaneous loadings can participate in concrete, personalised incident reporting.

Some excerpts—especially the visa-waiver and World Bank reports—contain few or no identified f8_neg loading words. They still point toward institutional and policy reporting, but their evidential value for the factor’s lexical interpretation is weaker. The pole should therefore not be labelled solely as visa, healthcare, or humanitarian discourse. The broader loading inventory favours **chronological institutional conflict reporting**.

## Groups driving the pole

Because this is the negative pole, the most relevant groups are those with the **lowest mean f8 scores**.

The clearest concentration is in the Global South:

- **global_south_2023_09:** mean **−0.10**, \(N=10\)
- **global_south_2023_12:** mean **0.146**
- **global_south_2024_03:** mean **0.175**
- **global_south_2024_04:** mean **0.183**
- **global_south_2024_06:** mean **0.277**

The excerpts strongly reflect the first two of these groups. September 2023 provides the diplomatic-normalisation and visa-waiver material, while December 2023 provides the truce, hostage, military, and humanitarian-update material.

The September result must be treated cautiously because it contains only ten texts and several excerpts reproduce closely related stories from Pakistani outlets. Its very low mean may therefore reflect a combination of genuine pre-October diplomatic salience, outlet practices, repeated wire material, and small-sample instability. December 2023, with \(N=486\), offers firmer evidence that the pole was also associated with early-war truce and operational reporting.

## Geopolitical and temporal tendencies

Global South groups generally have lower scores than corresponding Global North groups across much of 2023 and 2024. This suggests that the pole is especially associated with **Global South news treatments**, including:

- Pakistan-centred diplomatic positioning;
- concern with Muslim-state recognition of Israel;
- nationally localised experiences such as those of Filipino workers;
- republished international wire and institutional reports.

This should not automatically be interpreted as a uniform Global South discourse. It may also reflect the particular countries, outlets, and syndicated feeds represented in each monthly stratum.

Temporally, the pole is most visible in the earlier corpus, especially September–December 2023 and parts of early 2024. Mean scores generally rise from late 2024 into 2025, particularly in the Global North, indicating that this negative-pole configuration becomes less characteristic at the aggregate group level. The large standard deviations in several late Global North groups also indicate substantial within-group heterogeneity. The apparent diachronic decline may reflect changing historical events and reporting priorities, but source composition and uneven group sizes remain plausible contributing factors.

## Overall justification

The pole is best understood as **syndicated, event-driven reporting that tracks diplomatic initiatives and conflict developments through official actors, tightly specified durations, and operational updates**. Its core is not one single topic but a recurring reporting mode spanning normalisation diplomacy, visa policy, truces, hostage releases, military activity, and humanitarian logistics. The presence of malformed tokens and explicit newsfeed vocabulary means that the dimension also contains a noticeable **publication-format and source-style component**.
