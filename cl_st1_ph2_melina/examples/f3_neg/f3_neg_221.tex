The sudden fall of Syria, and the stunning isolation of Iran, were surely not the domino effects envisioned by Hamas when it attacked Israel 14 months ago.
Back then, Hamas ' s terrorist leadership fantasized about the fireball it might ignite by butchering more than 1,200 Israelis, mostly civilians, and taking hundreds hostage.
The slaughter would very likely provoke a furious Israeli counterattack and presage a Middle East conflagration destined to draw the combined might of Lebanon, Iran and Yemen to the side of Gaza ' s Palestinians.
Now, the architect of that strategy of brutality and insanity, Hamas leader Yahya Sinwar, is dead -- as is his Lebanese accomplice who firebombed Israel from the north, Hezbollah leader Hassan Nasrallah.
Both were killed and their combat brigades defeated by Israel, which has survived their co-ordinated attacks to consolidate its position as a regional power.

Today, Syrian president Bashar Assad has lost his army and his people have perhaps regained their country ( though women may yet be the losers under Islamist rule ).
The dictator who hosted Hamas leaders in Damascus, while shepherding Iranian arms shipments to Lebanon, has just sought asylum in Moscow.
All thanks to the chain reaction triggered by the Hamas massacre, culminating with Israel ' s decapitation of Assad ' s borrowed Hezbollah enforcers, whose brutal suppression of Syrian insurgents and civilians is no more.
As for Iran, which steered the anti-Israel"Axis of Resistance"from afar, it can now be said that the wheels are coming off the Islamic Republic.
After relentlessly colonizing and militarizing both Lebanon and Syria, Iran has suddenly been shorn of its vassal states and stands almost alone in its long distance proxy war with Israel ( with only Yemen ' s Houthi insurgents offering unquestioned loyalty ).
To think that all this is the handiwork of Hamas, which dreamed not of freeing Gaza ( which it already ruled, unchallenged, two decades after Israel withdrew ) but liberating all lands"from the river to the sea."Which is to say -- for those who say it without quite seeing it -- from the Jordan River to the Mediterranean Sea, extinguishing what lies between it in present-day Israel.
It can now be said that the late and unlamented Sinwar, who imagined he would live to see the Middle East remade as he imagined it, has achieved the precise opposite in death -- more death than he bargained for : more than 44,000 Gazans are dead ( according to Hamas authorities, with one-third of them combatants, according to Israel ) ; some 3,500 Lebanese are dead ( including Hezbollah members targeted by a bombing campaign that began with Israel ' s covert pager attack ).
Iran, far from presiding over the destruction of Israel, finds itself more vulnerable than ever : its air defenses destroyed after a series of tit-for-tat attacks, its Lebanese proxy fighting force in disarray, its military entrepot in Syria dismantled.
Against that backdrop, two of Iran ' s two major rivals -- not just Israel but Turkey, too -- are flexing their newfound muscle.
