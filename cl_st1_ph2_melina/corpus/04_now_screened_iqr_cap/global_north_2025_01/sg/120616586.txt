<p> GAZA -- The joy of thousands of Palestinian families who made it back home in northern Gaza after a ceasefire with Israel is turning to despair , as the cold reality of uninhabitable , bombed-out homes and dire shortages of basic supplies sets in . <p> Many have begun complaining about a lack of running water that forces them to queue for hours to fill plastic containers for drinking or cleaning . <p> With most homes now heaps of rubble as far as the eye can see , returnees have scoured whatever useful items remain from their property to erect makeshift tents . <p> At night , residential districts laid to waste by Israeli air strikes and shelling sink into darkness for lack of electricity or fuel to operate standby generators . <p> " There is nothing , no life , no water , no food , no drink , nothing @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ There is no Jabalia camp , " said Mr Hisham El-Err , standing next to the rubble of his multi-storey house in the biggest and mostly densely populated of the Gaza Strip 's eight historic cinder-block refugee camps . <p> His extended family is now huddling in tents , which offer scant protection from Gaza 's mid-winter chill . <p> By late on Jan 28 , Gaza 's Hamas authorities said most of the 650,000 people displaced from the north by the war have re-entered Gaza City and the northern edge of the enclave from areas to the south , where fighting is less intense and destructive . <p> Many of those returning , often laden with what personal possessions they still have after months of being shunted around as battlegrounds shifted , trekked 20km or more along the coastal highway . <p> Mr Fahad Abu Jalhoum returned with his family to Jabalia from the Al-Mawasi area in southern Gaza , but the destruction they found was so pervasive they were forced to go back south . <p> " It 's just ghosts without souls ( in the north ) @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ .
