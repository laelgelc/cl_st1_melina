It does not necessarily reflect the view of The Herald . <p> A Palestinian human rights group has lost a legal challenge at the High Court over weapons sales to Israel . <p> In September last year , the UK government suspended arms sales to Israel after a review of its compliance with international humanitarian law in its war in Gaza . <p> However , an exemption was made for some licences related to parts for F-35s , with lawyers for Al-Haq , a Palestinian human rights organisation , telling the High Court in May that this " carve-out " was unlawful and " gives rise to a significant risk of facilitating crime " . <p> Amnesty International UK , Oxfam and Human Rights Watch intervened in the case to submit evidence of what they said was " Israeli authorities ' lack of commitment to international humanitarian law " , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ responsibility to prevent genocide under Article 1 of the Genocide Convention . <p> On Monday the High Court ruled that the issue was not a matter for the courts , as it is up to Parliament to decide on who it sells weapons to and what military agreements it enters into . <p> In a 72-page ruling on Monday , Lord Justice Males and Mrs Justice Steyn said the case was about a " much more focused issue " than the carve-out itself . <p> The judges said : " That issue is whether it is open to the court to rule that the UK must withdraw from a specific multilateral defence collaboration which is reasonably regarded by the responsible ministers as vital to the defence of the UK and to international peace and security , because of the prospect that some UK manufactured components will or may ultimately be supplied to Israel , and may be used in the commission of a serious violation of international humanitarian law in the conflict in Gaza . <p> " Under our constitution that acutely sensitive and political issue is a matter for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to the electorate , not for the courts . " <p> The UK government had argued that it could not suspend licensing for export of F-35 components for use by Israel without having an impact on the multinational ' F-35 programme ' as a whole . <p> The programme is made up of eight nations , Israel is not a member but is a customer . <p> To do so would , it argued , mean suspending licensing to all nations in the programme which " would have a profound and immediate impact on international peace and security , would undermine US confidence in the UK and NATO at a critical juncture , would seriously undermine the credibility of the UK as a trusted partner on the international stage , and would undermine a key capability allowing the UK and its closest allies and partners to address current security challenges " . <p> Al Haq argued that the carve-out was unlawful as it risked obligations to ensure respect for the Geneva Conventions , to prevent genocide and not to facilitate internationally wrongful acts . <p> The group 's case was @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to indirectly support violations of international humanitarian law in Gaza . <p> The government acknowledged a " clear risk " of Israel violating humanitarian law but said the carve out was justified due to its wider importance . <p> In its ruling the court said it could not rule on the UK 's obligations under international law , nor could it adjudicate on Israel 's actions and intentions . <p> It found that all of the grounds for judicial review put forward were either something that the court could not rule on , or were within the powers of ministers to do . <p> Dr Halima Begum , Oxfam GB Chief Executive , said : " The judgment is surprising and deeply disappointing .
