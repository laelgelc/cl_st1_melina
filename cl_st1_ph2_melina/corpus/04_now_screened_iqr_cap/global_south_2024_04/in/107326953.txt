And so they want two things : an immediate hostage deal with Hamas rather than reliance on military operations in Gaza , and early elections in Israel . <h> Military operations in vain <p> Any nation would find it extremely hard to deal with the situation Israel has been in for the past six months . An armed group taking civilians as hostages and ambushing communities . <p> Israel is known to have a policy of not bargaining with ' terror ' groups . But in 2011 , Netanyahu negotiated a significant deal with Hamas for one soldier , Gilad Shalit , by freeing 1,027 Palestinian prisoners . One of those freed was Yahya Sinwar -- the mastermind of the 7 October attack . So , when Netanyahu made the deal , it benefitted his populism . <p> After the 7 October attack , Netanyahu , who was riding high on international solidarity , adopted an ambivalent strategy -- clumsy for some , no strategy for others . He declared that " it is a turning point for all leaders and nations , the civilised world , to decide if we are willing to fight for a future of hope and promise ; or surrender to tyranny and terror " . Destroying Hamas became his and most other Israeli leaders ' mantra , and they were overconfident . The hostages would come home for sure in this process , or so they thought . <p> But Sinwar was a knottier issue than expected . He has escaped the Israeli army for all these months , while allegedly managing more than a hundred hostages in tunnels in Gaza .
