<p> An " equity and justice " conference involving many of the country 's most prestigious private schools devolved into a " festival of Jew hate " that had scared students leaving in tears , according to outraged parents and attendees . <p> Speakers at last week 's National Association of Independent Schools ' annual People of Color Conference accused Israel of " ethnic cleansing " and " genocide " and downplayed the Oct. 7 Hamas attacks that killed 1,200 Israeli civilians -- prompting some frightened kids to hide their Jewish stars as they left early . <p> " There was an overwhelming feeling that we were not welcome , " one Jewish senior from the Milken Community School in Los Angeles told The Post . <p> One of the conference speakers was Dr. Suzanne Barakat , a physician and activist on issues of Islamophobia and social justice . Our Three Winners <p> " We felt small and insignificant , " said the student , who asked for anonymity out of fear of retribution . <p> The NAIS -- an organization of 1,300 independent schools that includes Manhattan 's Tony Dalton , Brearley , Collegiate , Trinity , Fieldston , and the Riverdale Country School -- held the professional development and networking event Dec. 4-7 in Colorado along with its Student Diversity Leadership Conference . Approximately 8,000 students and adults attended . <p> Among the New York prep schools in attendance were the Brooklyn Friends School , Dalton and Horace Mann . A complete list of attendees was not immediately available . <p> Milken Community Schools head of school Sarah Shulkind denounced the apparent Jew hate and ignorance on display at the conference . milkenschool <p> On the second day , Dr. Suzanne Barakat , a physician and activist , claimed Israel was " founded on ethnocentric superiority and an inherently systemically racist framework , " according to transcripts obtained by The Post . <p> The Mideast conflict , she said , stemmed from a " strain " of Zionism created when Jews fled nationalism in Europe in the 1800s . Critics said she made support of the Jewish state sound like a disease . <p> " They began immigrating to Palestine as colonists , " Barakat said , calling it " ethnic cleansing . " The only mention of Hamas was that they led an ' unprecedented assault . ' " <p> In an email to school leaders the day after the speech , NAIS president Debra Wilson said Barakat 's remarks about the war were " unprompted and unexpected . " <p> " While some attendees shared the speaker 's perspective and felt both seen and heard , others were deeply hurt and outraged , " Wilson added . <p> Critics said the apology was also insulting . <p> " Nobody could even bring themselves to say the word ' antisemitism ' in the acknowledgement , " said Sarah Shulkind , Milken 's head of school . <p> " Most horrifying is that the thousands of educators that were in that room teach at the nation 's most elite schools . . . they stood and cheered , " Shulkind added . " It reveals to me two things : virulent , unapologetic Jew hate , and profound ignorance . " <p> Jewish students said they were forced to get up and leave during Ruha Benjamin 's presentation . <p> On the last day of the conference , Princeton Professor Ruha Benjamin donned Palestinian colors and earrings showcasing watermelons , a symbol of Palestinian solidarity , as she addressed students and educators . <p> Benjamin spoke of her virtual reality project " The Phoenix of Gaza " during the last day of the conference . <p> " As I began walking out , I noticed people looking at my star of David necklace , " he continued . " I no longer felt safe at a conference literally built for inclusion . I got out of the conference room as soon as I could and quickly noticed that almost all other Jewish students did as well . " <p> Jewish leaders sent a letter to the NAIS Wednesday demanding an apology and ensure speaker selection procedures that prevent " toxic rhetoric . " <p> In response to them , Wilson said all future presentations would be required to be submitted in full in advance and took the groups on their offer to serve as a resource , especially ahead of a conference in February . <p> " I write to express my profound remorse over the divisive and hurtful rhetoric expressed on stage at last week 's NAIS People of Color Conference in Denver , " Wilson wrote . " There is no place for antisemitism at NAIS events , in our member schools , or in society . " <p> Another Jewish private school mom said she has spent years trying to get people to pay attention to the NAIS . <p> " Finally , after this speech , parents are waking up and they are livid , " she said . <p> Concerns of Jewish families have long been dismissed , one former NAIS member school employee told The Post . <p> " Jewish mothers in particular were categorized as ' crazy ' which meant overly involved and protective , " the former employee said . <p> " Jewish students were not seen or treated as a minority group ... " <p> Barakat and Benjamin did not immediately respond to inquiries from The Post .
