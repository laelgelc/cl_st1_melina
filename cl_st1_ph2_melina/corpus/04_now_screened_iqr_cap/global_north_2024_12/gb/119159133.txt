<h> Opening a Belfast mission is ' feasible ' for Israelis <p> After Binyamin Netanyahu 's government moves to close its embassy in Dublin , there appears to be precedent for a solution in Northern Ireland <p> Jason Johnson <p> Sunday December 22 2024 , 12.01am , The Sunday Times <p> Dana Erlich , the Israeli ambassador to Ireland , said closing the embassy , a rallying point for recent pro-Palestinian protests , was " the correct diplomatic decision " <p> NIALL CARSON/PA <p> Jason Johnson <p> Sunday December 22 2024 , 12.01am , The Sunday Times <p> The prospect of Israel opening a diplomatic mission in Belfast is " entirely feasible " after its government announced the closure of its Irish embassy , according to a leading Jewish figure . <p> Northern Ireland Friends of Israel co-chairman Steven Jaffe said the move by the Israeli administration was prompted by Irish government actions and would mark a " serious demotion of the Republic 's standing " in " many Arab countries " . <p> Jaffe , a London-based Belfast man and a consultant with Magen David Adom UK , said : " Israel has honorary consuls in Glasgow and Cardiff so it is perfectly feasible that a similar presence could be established in Belfast . <p> " There are many connections and friendships between Northern Ireland and Israel which are strong and growing . " <p> He said that before the opening of Israel 's Dublin embassy in 1994 its " interests in the Republic of Ireland were represented by the embassy in London . <h> Advertisement <p> " And so far as I can tell , that is the most likely outcome of this latest move . " <p> Jaffe 's comments came after DUP MP Jim Shannon , a veteran advocate for Israel , told of his hopes that the Jewish state would consider taking a formal presence north of the border , saying it would be " a positive step " . <p> Last Monday Dana Erlich , the Israeli ambassador to Ireland , said closing the embassy , a rallying point for recent pro-Palestinian protests , was " the correct diplomatic decision " amid " a hostile atmosphere " . Israel 's foreign minister Gideon Sa'ar said Ireland had crossed " every red line " and that the government was behind " antisemitic rhetoric " and " extreme anti-Israel policies " . He said the decision to close the embassy followed Ireland backing South Africa 's International Court of Justice action , which accuses Israel of genocide in the hostilities which began after last year 's October 7 attack by Hamas . <p> Simon Harris , the taoiseach , firmly rejecting claims of Irish antisemitism , said the decision to shutter the embassy in Ballsbridge was " deeply regrettable " . <p> President Higgins said it was " very serious business to actually brand a people because in fact they disagree " with the military actions in Gaza , Lebanon and Syria driven by Binyamin Netanyahu , the Israeli prime minister . <h> Advertisement <p> Jaffe said that amid the dispute Ireland 's reputation would take a hit given " many Arab countries , particularly Saudi Arabia and the Gulf states , are looking to grow their connection with Israel " . He said : " In contrast , Hamas has given full-throated support for Ireland 's actions against Israel . " Ireland seems keen to strengthen its representation in Iran which is hard to reconcile with a deep concern for human rights . <p> " The Republic 's very small Jewish community and its larger population of Israeli expats -- most of whom are high-tech workers -- will be dismayed at the circumstances resulting in the embassy 's closure . " <p> Hamas abducted 250 people in the October 7 attacks and killed about 1,200 . Israel 's response has killed almost 45,000 Palestinians , according to Gaza 's Hamas-run health ministry . <p> The nation 's UK embassy , which manages its consuls in Scotland and Wales , was approached for comment .
