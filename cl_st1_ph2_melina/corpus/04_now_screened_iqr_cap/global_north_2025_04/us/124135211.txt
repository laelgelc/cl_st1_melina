<h> Censorship of universities is n't about antisemitism Opinion <h> Share this : <p> FILE -- A student wrapped in an Israeli flag listens to Pro-Palestinian protesters gathered on campus at the University of Texas at Austin , on April 30 , 2024 , in Austin , Texas . ( AP Photo/Eric Gay , File ) <p> It is distressing to see folks suckered by President Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio 's ruse that censorship is needed to protect us from the evils of antisemitism . <p> This is the president who , just last month , told Congress and the world that " I 've stopped all government censorship and brought back free speech in America . " Notwithstanding the hypocrisy ( or outright deceit ) , college students expressing Palestinian sympathies are the target du jure . Those who are foreign students are being arrested and set for deportation . <p> But criticism of the government and policies of Israel is not antisemitism . Note the people of Israel who regularly take to the streets in the thousands -- to save their country from the Netanyahu @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ anxiety of the Jewish community about a rise of antisemitism . But the manipulative strategy is designed to achieve the larger goal of control over universities -- forcing universities to abandon their role as a forum for the airing and examination of all ideas . <p> I come to this discussion as an American Jew who has harbored civil libertarian values for as long as I can remember -- values that crystallized in my first weeks on a college campus . <p> I recall being drawn to the lawn of the City College of New York by the sounds of a rally of ( according to news accounts ) 1,000 students protesting a ban on communist speakers . <p> I reached the rally just as the chair of the political science department , who noted that he was also representing the American Civil Liberties Union , condemned the speaker ban by reciting the argument against censorship from John Stuart Mill 's classic essay " On Liberty . " <p> I was transfixed by the power of ideas . <p> In less than 10 years , I was teaching Mill 's @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ university . By then , I had found an intellectual home in the ACLU , the nation 's foremost defender of free speech . <p> A few years later , I was an ACLU state director defending free speech in synagogues throughout Michigan during what ( in retrospect ! ) has been called " ACLU 's finest hour " -- courageously defending the right of American Nazis to march in the heavily Jewish Chicago suburb of Skokie . The Illinois ACLU lawyer was , not surprisingly , Jewish . <p> I bear the name of the head of what was the European branch of my family , who was murdered by the Nazis . Yet there I was , trying to convince an audience that in America , Jews ( and other minorities ) ultimately benefit by our system of freedom of expression -- in which there may be horrible ideas , but no illegal ideas . <p> Trump and Rubio are stretching to claim that college student protestors expressing sympathy for the Palestinian cause are " distributing Hamas propaganda " or " acting on behalf of , in concert @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . " <p> College protestors certainly disrupted campuses and may have harassed Jewish students , particularly those in counter demonstrations , but these are violations of university rules that should be dealt with by the university . <p> More debate about the war in Gaza , not less , is sorely needed . There is plenty of blame to go around . On Oct. 7 , 2023 , Hamas brutally and viscously murdered 1,200 people , and the fate of hostages remaining in captivity under horrendous conditions is still unknown . But the war also caused the deaths ( and destroyed the homes , schools and hospitals ) of tens of thousands of women and children who had nothing to do with the Oct. 7 attack by Hamas . <p> Many Jewish families like mine are passionately committed to the post-World War II vision of a homeland for the world 's Jews but understand that by sabotaging a two-state solution , threatening land annexation and conducting total war on the people of Gaza , Netanyahu has made Israel less secure , not more . <p> If there is a beacon @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the First Amendment 's admonition to distinguish between odious ideas and illegal actions . <p> So my appeal , especially to my fellow Jews , is that we are best served by holding fast to the values of free speech and ill-served by surrendering the power over what is permissible to say and think to government . <p> Silencing anti-Israel and pro-Palestinian voices to quell antisemitism may be a satisfying short-term sugar high . But we will moan the loss of free speech and academic freedom if we allow federal and state Trumpians to replicate repressive regimes elsewhere . <p> Howard L. Simon served as executive director of the ACLU of Florida from 1997-2018 . He resides in Gainesville and is president of Clean Okeechobee Waters Foundation , Inc.
