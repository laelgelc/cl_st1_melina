<p> Twin Palestinian filmmakers Arab and Tarzan Nasser , whose real names are Mohamed and Ahmed Abou , and who credit themselves on screen as " Nasser Brothers , " make movies whose titles can be somewhat deceiving -- probably on purpose . <p> Their second feature , Gaza mon amour , owed much less to Alain Resnais than to offbeat auteurs like Aki Kaurismaki or Elie Sulieman , weaving a surreal love story centered around a Greek statue with a prominently erect penis . Their latest work , Once Upon a Time in Gaza , is rather far from the historical action epics of Sergio Leone , though it does have a few things in common with Quentin Tarantino in its TV-show-within-a-movie plot . <p> In both cases , titles and plot devices conceal what the Nassers are really interested in , which is depicting their homeland under Hamas rule and the Israeli blockade . We follow the travails of a middling drug dealer , Osama ( Majd Eid ) , whose tiny falafel joint serves as a front for a narcotics operation involving forged opiate prescriptions . His restaurant is held down by Yahya ( Nader Arb Alhay ) , an unassuming young man who yearns for a better life , but , like so many of his fellow residents , is stuck in a dead end . <p> Once Upon a Time in Gaza is fairly tame , even a bit languid , in its opening sequences , until a corrupt cop ( Ramzi Maqdisi ) barges in to get a piece of Osama 's business . Co-credited as directors and production designers , the Nasser brothers seem less interested in making a classic thriller than in conveying how Osama and Yahya remain forever entrapped .
