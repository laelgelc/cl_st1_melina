But the $230 million pier , which should be re-anchored this week after it broke apart on May 28 , has proven to be a hapless operation . It could soon turn disastrous . <p> The pier has been plagued by issues from the start . Three U.S. ships were grounded on a beach in Gaza following a storm the day it broke apart . Several U.S. military personnel were injured at sea days before that . And the landing area near the pier has been attacked on at least two occasions by Hamas . Aid from the pier has also been looted , with only limited amounts of assistance reaching Gazans from it . <p> These same problems -- and potentially many more -- will @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of U.S. lives or , even worse , some kind of deeper involvement in the Israel-Hamas war could follow . This danger is compounded by the fact that in protecting the floating platform and pier -- which is located in a " high risk " area on the Gaza coast -- U.S. personnel are relatively fixed in place . Like the recent Tower 22 attack in Jordan , that makes them attractive targets , according to former U.S. national security officials . <p> Mission creep is a danger , too . That ended in the deaths of 241 U.S. military personnel in a bombing at a Marine Corps barracks . <p> Like Somalia or Lebanon , the Gaza pier could draw the U.S. into a bigger role .
