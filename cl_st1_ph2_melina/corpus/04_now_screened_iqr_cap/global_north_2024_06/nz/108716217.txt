<p> To truly appreciate the recent decision by Ireland , Spain and Norway to recognise the state of Palestine , the subject has to be placed in proper context . <p> On November 15 , 1988 , Yasser Arafat , then Chairman of the Palestine Liberation Organisation , declared Palestine as an independent state . <p> The proclamation took place within important and unique contexts : <p> One , the Palestinian uprising of December 1987 which ignited international support and sympathy with the Palestinian people . <p> Two , growing expectations that the Palestinian leadership needed to match the popular Intifada in the Occupied Territories with a political programme so as not to squander the global attention obtained by the uprising . <p> There were other issues that are also worth a pause , including the growing marginalisation of the PLO as the main political front of the Palestinian struggle . <p> Advertisement - scroll to continue reading <h> Are you getting our free newsletter ? <p> This irrelevance was the natural political outcome of the forced exile of the PLO leadership from Lebanon to Algeria in 1982 , which largely severed the connection between this leadership and an @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ made in Algiers , Palestinians in occupied Palestine and across the world rejoiced . They felt that their leadership was , once more , directly involved in their struggle , and that their Intifada , which , by then , had cost them hundreds of precious lives , had finally acquired some kind of political horizon . <p> The countries that almost immediately recognised the state of Palestine reflected the geopolitical formation at the time : Arab and Muslim countries , which fully and unconditionally recognised the nascent state . Additionally , there were countries in the Global South which expressed their historic solidarity with the Palestinian people . <p> A third category , which also mattered greatly , was represented by countries in Asia and eastern Europe - including Russia itself - which revolved within the Soviet sphere , posing a direct challenge to American hegemony and Western militarism and expansionism . <p> Soon after the Algiers Declaration , the geopolitics of the world received its greatest shock since World War II , namely the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991 and the subsequent fragmentation of pro-Soviet states @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Western hegemony . <p> That , too , had a direct impact on Palestine . Though Arafat and his PLO made their fair share of mistakes and political miscalculations - leading to the Oslo Accords , the formation of the Palestinian Authority and the fragmentation of the Palestinian front itself - the Palestinian leadership 's options , from a strict geopolitical analysis , were quite limited . <p> Back then , the PLO had one out of two options : either to continue with the struggle for freedom and independence based on the national liberation model or to adopt a purely political approach based on negotiations and supposed ' painful compromises ' . They opted for the latter , which proved to be a fatal mistake . <p> Political negotiations can be rewarding when the negotiating parties have leverage . While Israel had the leverage of being the occupying power and receiving unconditional support from the US and its Western allies , the Palestinians had very little . <p> Therefore , the outcome was as obvious as it was predictable . For example , between 2008 and 2011 , more South American countries recognised Palestine , a direct outcome of new and assertive political independence achieved in that part of the world . <p> In 2012 , Palestine was voted by the United Nations General Assembly as a non-member observer state , allowing it to officially use the name ' State of Palestine ' for all political purposes . <p> All Palestinian efforts since then have failed to overcome the power paradigm that continues to exist at the UN , separating the UNGA from those with veto power at the Security Council . <p> The events of October 7 , and the genocidal war that followed , have certainly ushered in a massive global movement that challenged the pre-existing geopolitical paradigm regarding Palestine . <p> If the war , however , had taken place , say @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ plea for solidarity may have been different . New rising global powers have been boldly challenging , and changing , the world 's status quo geopolitics for years . This includes Russia 's direct confrontation with NATO in Ukraine , China 's rise to global power status , the growing influence of BRICS and the more assertive African and South American political agendas . <p> For its part , the Gaza war has also challenged the concept of military power as a guarantor of permanent dominance . This is now obvious in the Middle East and also globally . <p> The latter realisation has finally allowed for new , significant margins to appear , allowing Western European countries to finally accept the reality that Palestine deserves to be a state , that the Palestinian aspirations must be honored and that international law must be respected . <p> Now , the challenge for Palestinians is whether they will be able to utilise this historic moment to the fullest degree .
