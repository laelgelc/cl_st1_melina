<h> ' Israel 's land war in Gaza will delay peace by another generation ' -- Professor <p> Hard experience says a land war wo n't go well -- and faltering international support suggests the world knows it . <p> Article by Paul Rogers , Professor of Peace Studies , University of Bradford . <p> For the past four decades , Israel 's experience of fighting paramilitary militias has been grim . <p> Its last major operation was in southern Lebanon , which started with Operation Peace for Galilee in 1982 . While initially successful , the subsequent occupation of southern Lebanon led directly to the rise of the Hezbollah Shia militias , persistent losses for the Israel Defence Forces ( IDF ) , a withdrawal to a nine-mile security zone within three years and from Lebanon as a whole in 2000 . <p> In 2006 Israeli military units went back into Lebanon to counter Hezbollah rockets but ended with exhausted troops withdrawing in some disarray and the IDF resorting to a massive air war that did huge damage to the infrastructure of much of the country . <p> Then there is Gaza . <p> Before the current war @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 2007 , primarily to control Hamas 's use of rockets and its growing network of infiltration tunnels . The most significant one was Operation Protective Edge in 2014 which involved troops in a ground assault . In the event , they found the fighting extremely difficult , with the elite Golani Brigade experiencing serious casualties from the start . <p> Once again , there was intense use of air power and , as in the other wars , it was civilians that suffered most . Overall , the Israelis lost more than 300 killed in those four wars but for the Palestinians in Gaza , the death toll exceeded 5,300 . <h> Bitter experience <p> Experiences in other wars are relevant to what happens now in Gaza . After the 9/11 attacks there was considerable international support for the US . War against the Taliban seemed inevitable though very few security analysts warned of the US-led coalition falling into a trap . The warnings were ignored and 20 years later the western troops finally retreated in disarray . Then came the Iraq war from 2003 , with warnings once again @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> As to Gaza , there are multiple concerns about what comes next , the current incursions being far short of a full-scale ground intervention . There are sharp differences of belief of what should come next within the military and within Benjamin Netanyahu 's government as well as within Israel as a whole , with much of it heightened by the concern over the loss of international support . <p> There is also concern about the experience of urban warfare elsewhere . Just six years ago , it took nine months for the US-led coalition to take back the Iraqi city of Mosul from Islamic State . The US led the huge air assault , aided by France , the UK and other partners . The ground forces then were Iraqi troops and militias and they lost 8,200 troops . <p> Mosul 2019 / Wikimedia Commons : Enno Lenze <p> A further 10,000 civilians were killed , and the old city was wrecked . It was likened to Stalingrad in early 1943 . <p> Just last year , a small force of Ukrainian soldiers held off a large Russian @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Azovstal steel works in Mariupol using a network of 24 kilometres of Soviet-era tunnels . Hamas has constructed vastly more tunnels under Gaza and is no doubt ready for months of combat . <h> Dwindling international support <p> Three weeks ago , Israel suffered a grievous loss and rightly got huge initial support , but that is already fading . Worse still , there is a terrible inability of the current Israeli government to understand what it is dealing with in a ground invasion of Gaza . But the death toll tells us all we need to know . If we look at the five Gaza wars since 2007 , including the current devastating war , the Israelis have lost 1,700 people , but the Palestinians have lost more than 13,000 -- and the numbers are rising by hundreds every day . <p> For now , the public mood in Israel still supports the Netanyahu government , however unpopular it might have been just a month ago . But the small but persistent campaign by families of the hostages for them to be the priority is having an effect . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ internationally that is a deep concern in Israel and , indeed , in the Biden administration . This was shown by the weekend debate in the UN general assembly when only 12 states supported the US and Israel in voting against a " humanitarian pause " . Also , eight EU states were among the 120 supporting the motion and the 45 who abstained even included Britain . <p> In some previous Israeli military actions , the end has effectively come about when international support for Israel has collapsed or got close to that . If the ground war against Hamas had started within a few days of the shock of October 7 , and if that operation had been successful and Hamas had collapsed , then Netanyahu could have claimed success . <p> That has not happened and will not happen now . Instead , there is every prospect of a bitter war with many thousands more Palestinians killed , tens of thousands of young Palestinians all too ready to fight in the future , and a peaceful resolution of the Israel/Palestine conflict delayed by at least another generation @ @ @ @ @ @ @
