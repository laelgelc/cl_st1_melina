<h> OPINION <h> Fagan , Seekings and the battle for control of the narrative on Gaza <p> Leslie London <p> 24 March 2025 <p> Leslie London queries the publication of a confidential document related to internal disciplinary matter at UCT <p> Jeremy Seekings : Time for transparency <p> I was surprised to see Politicsweb publish what is clearly a confidential document related to an internal disciplinary matter at the University of Cape Town . Normally , one might just shrug this off as a blip on public opinion . But this publication of a legal recommendation for disciplinary action against Professor Jeremy Seekings takes place in the context of extraordinarily acrimonious contestation of ideas at the University of Cape Town ( UCT ) over what the University should be doing regarding the ongoing genocide of Palestinians in Gaza . <p> The Daily Maverick article of 16 October 2024 by Jeremy Seekings and Nicoli Nattrass , which is the basis for the investigation of disciplinary charges against Professor Seekings , attempted to portray a Senate decision in September 2024 not to recommend Professor Anton Fagan for emeritus status as discriminatory and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ decision was because of his position on the war on Gaza . That was not the case . <p> The questions raised in Senate were about the prima facie evidence that , as a result of student complaints about an exam he had set , he took early retirement from UCT because of an imminent prospect of a disciplinary hearing -- and that early retirement would be in both his and the Faculty 's best interest . He confirmed this in an interview with the South African Jewish report in July 2024 . There is no contesting that a disciplinary hearing for Professor Fagan was anticipated . <p> Faced with the idea that an academic could avoid a disciplinary hearing by taking early retirement but return as an emeritus staff member after retirement with never having to account for the complaint , the suggestion was put to Senate that the nomination was premature and we should await a formal report as promised by the University spokesperson . <p> The nominator declined to postpone the nomination and it was put to the vote . In the climate of uncertainty and the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Professor Fagan 's exam ( Seekings and Nattrass dismiss student experience as " hyperbolic " and as " wild claims " ) , the majority of Senate members declined to support his nomination . <p> There were too many questions unanswered about his retirement , and the absence of transparency about what action had been on the table or removed from the table by his departure . He was not refused emeritus status for anything other than unanswered questions about actions which were sufficiently serious to raise the prospect of a disciplinary hearing which his early retirement had avoided . <p> Seekings and Nattrass immediately turn that into grounds for accusation . As soon as the vote had concluded in Senate , one of the authors attacked those who raised concerns about Fagan 's nomination , saying that had Senate waited for the approval of a new policy on Emeritus appointments , the vote would have been fair . <p> --&gt; <p> Yet the article fails to acknowledge that the starting point of discussion in Senate was to ask the nominator to defer the nomination until we had a report @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the table . By that time , a new policy would have been in place . None of this is mentioned in the Seekings and Nattrass article because it does not serve the purpose of the narrative intended . <p> Quite frankly , many UCT academics are sick and tired of the mischief played out by colleagues using the media to mislead the public about what are matters that have been deeply debated in UCT structures . Smearing colleagues as practicing a cancel culture , being commissars , and creating a Manichean , self-righteous , censorious and adversarial culture is neither intellectually accurate nor reflective of any willingness to accept that their peers do n't agree with them that the University should tolerate special rules for some people . <p> But the narrative can not happen unless the press is thirsty to participate . So , I ask the editor of Politicsweb if you are willing to name the source that supplied you with this legal letter , a letter clearly not intended for the public . The selective release of information to the media in the context of a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ be simply answered by resort to press freedom . It is directly undermining of due process in a quasi-legal matter , which should be allowed to run its course , unimpeded by external manipulation . <p> In this case , I think the public is entitled to know who gave you the letter so that it can judge for itself what is going on behind its release . That would be the acid test of whether Politicsweb believes its readership should have full information to assess an opinion piece . Public interest would be best served by transparency , please ! <p> --&gt; <p> Professor Leslie London is a Professor at UCT and a member of UCT Senate . In the interests of transparency , he declares that he proposed one of the two resolutions on Gaza adopted by Senate in 2024 and then approved by UCT 's council , now currently being challenged in court by another UCT academic .
