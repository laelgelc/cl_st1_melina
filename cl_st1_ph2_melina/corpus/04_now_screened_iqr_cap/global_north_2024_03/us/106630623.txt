<p> If you were a therapist , what 's the first thing you would do when faced with a clinical situation you 'd never seen before ? If you 're like most of us , you 'd turn to the psychological literature for guidance . <p> That 's what Naama de la Fontaine , Ph.D. , did . She 's a leading psychologist at Sheba Hospital in Israel and among the first professional staff to receive hostages released from Gaza . <p> But there was no clinical literature to advise her . <p> As de la Fontaine realized , the extraordinary nature of the events on and after October 7th meant that no medical or psychological literature existed on treating cases such as those she witnessed . De la Fontaine and her staff were on their own . They would need to innovate treatment for many new , tragically unprecedented psychological situations suffered by released hostages , some as young as 3 years old . <p> The team at Sheba Hospital had a great deal of experience with trauma , but this was a mass casualty event of unknown dimensions that had no precedent .
