<p> As the Lebanese Christian village of Rmeish marks its first Easter since the Gaza war erupted , residents say a parallel confrontation between Hezbollah and Israel is dragging them into a conflict they did not choose . <p> Like many Christians elsewhere in southern Lebanon , residents are angry and fearful their homes could be caught in the cross-fire and their families forced to flee -- permanently -- from their ancestral villages near the Lebanon-Israel border . <p> Earlier this week , a Rmeish resident confronted a group of armed men trying to launch rockets at Israel from within the village . Some villagers rang church bells to sound the alarm , and the armed men moved off to fire rockets from another neighbourhood , according to mayor Milad al-Alam and Rmeish residents . <p> " What we 've been saying for the last six months is : among our own homes , keep us neutral . Any strike in return would have brought huge losses , " Alam told Reuters . <p> Hezbollah began launching rockets from hilltops and villages in southern Lebanon at Israel on Oct. 8 in support of its Palestinian ally Hamas , which @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that triggered a fierce Israeli land , air and sea offensive on the Gaza Strip . <p> The villagers ' resentment reflects criticism from Christian clerics and politicians opposed to Hezbollah , who have long accused the group of undermining the state through its possession of a controversial arsenal that outguns the national army , and of monopolising decisions of war and peace . <p> " We have nothing to do with this war . Do they ( Hezbollah ) want to displace us ? " said a 40-year-old resident of Rmeish who asked not to be identified , fearing that criticizing Hezbollah could bring reprisals . Iran-backed Hezbollah , which holds sway over much of the Lebanese state , denied its fighters had tried to launch rockets from Rmeish . <p> More than a dozen sects coexist in a precarious balancing act in tiny Lebanon , reflected in a power-sharing system that reserves government posts by religion .
