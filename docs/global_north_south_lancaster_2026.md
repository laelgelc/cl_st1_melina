Global North and Global South discourses in news reporting

The Global North and the Global South are terms that describe unequal
positions within the modern world system that arose from colonial
expansion and capitalist development (Dados and Connell 2012), with the
former associated with capitalist powers and the latter with oppressed
nations. As such, the Global South refers to regions such as Latin
America, Africa, and Asia that have been historically exploited. The
term builds on earlier formulations, such as 'Third World' in the late
twentieth century (Pinheiro 2024). In this sense, the term Global South
does not describe economic underdevelopment alone, but names a
historical pattern of domination through which exploitation has been
produced and maintained at different levels, including the kinds of
knowledge that are deemed legitimate (Santos 2015). The goal of the
current study is twofold: (1) to collect a corpus of news reporting that
represents Global North and Global South news, and (2) to explore the
corpus by carrying an investigation of the discourses around a divisive
issue between the Global North and Global South. We hope to show that
the division is both (a) ideological and (b) marked by distinct
discourses. Mass media in the Global North controls information flow
around the world, defining what is reported and what is omitted, much as
a propaganda model (Herman and Chomsky 1988) consisting of five filters:
ownership, advertising, sourcing, flak, and ideology, which work
together to ensure that news coverage defends Global North interests. To
achieve the first goal, the study constructs a corpus divided into two
sections. The Global South section includes such news sources as *China
Daily* (China), *TASS* (Russia), *Tehran Times* (Iran), *The South
African* and *News24* (South Africa), and *Al Jazeera* (Qatar). The
Global North section comprises sources as *The New York Times* and *The
Washington Post* (United States), *The Times* and *The Guardian* (United
Kingdom), *The Globe and Mail* and *National Observer* (Canada),
*Haaretz* (Israel), and *The Australian* and *The Sydney Morning Herald*
(Australia). All texts are news reports written in English and are
collected through web scraping and through extraction from the NOW
Corpus. To meet the second goal, the analysis focuses on coverage of the
war in Palestine. Relevant texts are selected from the corpus and
analyzed using Lexical Multidimensional Analysis (LMDA; Berber Sardinha
and Fitzsimmons-Doolan 2025). LMDA extends the original Multidimensional
Analysis framework introduced by Biber (1988), by relying exclusively on
lexical variables. The texts are tagged for POS and lemmatized; content
words are extracted, and key lemmas are identified by comparing the
frequency of each lemma in one corpus section (Global North or South)
against the other. Counts are taken for each key lemma and entered in a
factor analysis. The resulting factors are interpreted as discourse
dimensions. The resulting dimensions are interpreted as macro-level
discursive constructs that distinguish between differing ideologies in
the Global North and South. The dimensions will be introduced and
discussed in the paper presentation.
